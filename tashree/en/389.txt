The intellect, then, cannot issue an absolute judgment on an action that aligns with other intellects; humans share in their judgment of an action as being good or bad. Therefore, you find some who deny deviance and others who pretend to support it, some who criminalize trafficking in white slavery and others who benefit from it and devote their lives to it. Thus, humanity or the human intellect does not agree on a value that describes actions as good or bad. Some legislations may describe certain matters as bad and then retract this to classify them as permissible, or even obligatory, as seen in the perspective of some Western nations on deviance.
Thus, even if the action itself is considered "good" in the view of "nature" or when it aligns with human desires, some intellects may deem it bad. Moreover, intellects often cannot detach from desires when considering the values that are hoped for from prohibiting an action; they do not abandon their lusts when viewing adultery, leaning towards its permissibility, even if laws prohibit it, or vice versa. They may not relinquish their perspective on the material benefits that the action brings them, that is, material values, so you find them permitting usury because it benefits capitalists and banks, even though it severely harms the general public and the poor. They permit drugs and stimulants like cannabis to reap billions from its trade. Thus, we find that the issue of setting values aside when judging an action is an impossible matter, as a person must be influenced by certain values to judge an action. Therefore, it is impossible for one to be neutral when judging an action! It is impossible to be "secular."
