However, the organizational methods of these institutions are flexible: for example, the treasury may have a Ministry of Finance and a modern administrative organization – there is no objection to the method as long as it fulfills the required Sharia obligation. The army is mandated to prepare for jihad and protect the nation, but its organization into divisions, brigades, and branches, arming it with the latest technologies, and establishing ranks and medals, etc., are all organizational means. Likewise, education, health, and services: the state is religiously responsible for ensuring education and basic healthcare for its citizens, but the detailed system (curricula, hospital structure) is established according to ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) and experience to achieve those goals. The only prohibition is for the state to adopt a method that contradicts a Sharia ruling: such as making education coeducational, which contradicts the rulings on gender separation at a certain age; or funding healthcare services through usury; these means are forbidden because they violate texts. Aside from that, the matter is broad: each era has its means of administration and development, and Islam has not obligated us to a fixed mold, but has opened for us the door of ijtihad (on the condition of integrity and not exceeding the limits of the lawful and unlawful).

Opinion and technical expertise: understanding the enemy and formulating policies within the framework of the Sharia: the negation of "the legislation of reason" in politics does not mean the elimination of opinion and technical expertise, but rather regulating them within a clear Sharia limit: the Sharia defines the reference, goals, and rulings (what is permissible and what is not, what is obligatory and what is forbidden, the limits of obedience and accountability, and the principles of relations with the enemy and friend), then it leaves to reason a space for expertise in diagnosing reality: understanding the balance of power, reading the enemy's plans, analyzing intentions and capabilities, estimating scenarios, and building executive options that achieve the Sharia ruling in reality. Here, reason does not produce a "new ruling," but rather realizes the political context: does this action actually lead to empowering the enemy? Does this path achieve the intended preservation of the nation and its strength within the rulings of the Sharia? Thus, the expertise in security, intelligence, economics, and military affairs serves as a tool for understanding and implementation, not as a reference for changing rulings or importing legislative systems under the banner of "public interest (maslaha)."

Political decision-making: the policy of guardianship between ruling and means: the political decisions made by the state to manage affairs are divided in our topic into two levels: the level of ruling/system, which is defined by the Sharia and is not invented by reason (such as the source of legislation, the structure of authority and governance, the reference of the judiciary, regulating essential relations between the state and the subjects, and the principles of foreign policy within its rulings), and the level of means/plans, which is left to reason to choose the most suitable according to time and place as long as it does not create a new ruling or contradict a text. The state may outline an economic, media, or security policy to address an emergency situation or to achieve a predetermined legitimate interest; however, the criterion for its validity is not merely its "effectiveness," but rather the meeting of two conditions: (1) that it is an executive means that does not change a Sharia ruling or alter a fixed Sharia description, and (2) that it achieves guardianship in reality according to a credible assessment from experts, while maintaining the right of the nation to hold accountable for deviation from the Sharia, not for differing methods.

In summary, it becomes clear that reason has a large scope in politics: in innovating plans, developing institutions, and improving means, but without contradicting the fixed Sharia principles. Ibn Aqil said: "Politics is what is done in accordance with the Sharia."
