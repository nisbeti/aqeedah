There is no legislative vacuum: ﴾Today I have perfected for you your religion﴿:
We often hear some Muslims say, while deriving a legal ruling on a particular issue, "In matters where there is no text," and we hear others say: "The interests according to the Sharia are of three types: interests that the Sharia has acknowledged, interests that the Sharia has declared invalid, and interests that are unqualified and have not been acknowledged by any text as valid or invalid." We also hear a third group say: "This falls within the realm of concession that the Sharia has remained silent about regarding its ruling." Is there, then, an area of legislative vacuum that requires rational rulings? Upon closer examination, we find that the notion of a legislative vacuum contradicts the texts of the Qur'an and the Sunnah, which clarify that the religion has been completed and that there is no obligatory matter that our Sharia has not addressed. The Sharia did not remain silent about things without clarifying their rulings; rather, it has specified the ruling for everything. There is no incident without a ruling, nor any event without a place for a ruling, and no Muslim has the right to say that the legislator has remained silent about something and has not clarified its ruling after reading His saying: ﴾And We have sent down to you the Book as a clarification for all things﴿ and His saying: ﴾Today I have perfected for you your religion and completed My favor upon you and have chosen for you Islam as your religion﴿. This clarification of rulings may be explicit by naming the thing and its ruling, such as the prohibition of carrion, blood, and the flesh of swine, or it may be through a general text like: ﴾He created for you what is in the earth all together﴿, thus including everything in the earth under the generality of this text as permissible for us, except for what other texts have specified as exceptions. It may also be through establishing a sign, which is the cause; wherever the cause exists, the ruling applies by analogy.
As for what Ibn Majah and al-Tirmidhi narrated from Salman al-Farsi, who said: The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) was asked about butter, cheese, and fur, and he said: "The lawful is what Allah has made lawful in His Book, and the unlawful is what Allah has made unlawful in His Book, and what He has remained silent about is what He has pardoned." And what Abu al-Darda narrated from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) that he said: "What Allah has made lawful in His Book is lawful, and what He has made unlawful is unlawful, and what He has remained silent about is pardon, so accept from Allah His pardon, for Allah does not forget anything." Then he recited this verse: ﴾And your Lord is not forgetful﴿. This was narrated by al-Bazzar. And what al-Bayhaqi narrated through Abu Thalabah from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) that he said: "Indeed, Allah has prescribed obligations, so do not neglect them, and He has set limits, so do not transgress them, and He has prohibited certain things, so do not violate them, and He has remained silent about certain things as a concession for you, not out of forgetfulness, so do not inquire about them." These hadiths are reports of singular transmission, on one hand, and do not contradict the definitive texts, and on the other hand, these hadiths do not indicate that there are things about which the legislator has remained silent in the sense of not clarifying them, but rather indicate that there are things that Allah, Exalted is He, has not prohibited out of mercy for you, thus pardoning them and remaining silent about their prohibition. (They asked about butter, cheese, and fur, which are lawful as is known, and Allah has not prohibited them for you out of mercy for you, not forgetfulness.) Thus, the subject of these hadiths is not the silence about legislating rulings for them, but rather the silence about prohibiting them(293). The intent is to prohibit inquiring about them, not to imply that there are matters for which Allah has not clarified the ruling.
