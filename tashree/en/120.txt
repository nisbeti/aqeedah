The Mujtahid of the School:
The same applies to the Mujtahid of the school; it is not permissible for him to deviate from the fundamental principles of the school within which he is exercising ijtihad, although he may disagree on some details. If he strays from the fundamental principles of the school, he becomes an independent Mujtahid outside of the school, either as an absolute Mujtahid, a Mujtahid of a specific issue, or he may establish a new school.
To begin with, we can briefly state that the four schools are an extension of the schools and jurisprudence of the Companions, and the imams, their students, and the Mujtahids of their schools have established these schools and laid down the rules of ijtihad within them, deriving all of this from the Book and the Sunnah and the method of ijtihad of the Companions and the Tabi'in. Abu Hanifa (may Allah be pleased with him) is the heir of the school of Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, who taught the people of Kufa knowledge, according to a methodological foundation in applying rulings to contemporary issues, which was inherited from him by Alqamah, who inherited it from Ibrahim al-Nakha'i, who passed this knowledge to many scholars, foremost among them Hamad ibn Abi Sulayman, the teacher of Abu Hanifa. As for Imam Malik, he inherited the knowledge of the people of Medina, led by Nafi' al-Madani, who inherited the knowledge of the seven jurists in Medina: Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib, Urwah ibn al-Zubayr, al-Qasim ibn Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr al-Siddiq, Ubaydullah ibn Utbah ibn Mas'ud, Khārijah ibn Zayd ibn Thabit, Sulayman ibn Yasar, and Abu Salamah ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, who inherited the knowledge of the Companions. As for the reasons for following the four schools—especially after the era of linguistic corruption—
the first reason is: the precision of their principles and their coherence such that there is no contradiction within the principles of the school itself; rather, the principles are consistent with a productive way of thinking, and the comprehensiveness of those principles. When applying those principles to issues, we find that the principles are capable of producing subsidiary rulings due to their ability to deal with various types of evidence and issues.
The second reason: that the four imams are authoritative in language and its sciences; their words and language are cited, such as al-Shafi'i and Muhammad ibn al-Hasan.
The third reason: the abundance of branches in which they exercised ijtihad, covering the necessities of Muslim life and the disputes among them regarding issues and problems, making the rulings of the schools suitable to serve as a law for the state in judiciary matters and for the lives of Muslims and their transactions when needed (not exclusively or binding for judges or Muslims).
The fourth reason: the large number of their students among the distinguished scholars of the Ummah, its jurists, and its usuli scholars, who served those schools by establishing, codifying, authoring, elucidating, and reasoning for the issues in a way that has not been achieved for the jurisprudence of other scholars whose schools have faded or whose teachings have been limited to scattered issues that do not form complete schools like those of the four schools, or were not transmitted from them through methods that raise confidence in them to the level of trust in the jurisprudence of the schools, or answer questions such as: Are these the last opinions of al-Awza'i, al-Layth, or al-Thawri, for example, on those issues? Or do they have specifications or restrictions or other opinions regarding them? Unlike the four schools and some other schools that were transmitted, such as the school of Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq, and others.
The fifth reason: the transmission of these schools from them through widely reported methods that can be relied upon for fatwa.
The sixth reason: the piety of these imams, their jurisprudence, their piety, and their knowledge, which was a matter of consensus, appreciation, and testimony from the Ummah and its distinguished scholars. Some of them would pray Fajr with the ablution of 'Isha, and hundreds of books have been written about their merits and virtues.
