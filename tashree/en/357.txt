The ruling is based on the actions of a person that they perform to satisfy a manifestation of instinct or to fulfill a bodily need. Since actions and relationships (transactions) fall under similar rules and categories (such as ways to satisfy the sexual instinct, which includes marriage and divorce, or types of sales), the rulings pertain to human actions and occurrences. Each occurrence has a fixed ruling; for instance, the reality of adultery is different from that of kissing, which is different from that of touching, and each has its own ruling and punishment. The reality of the eclipse prayer is different from that of the funeral prayer. These actions take the form of specific transactions that recur in every time and place, such as the reasons for acquiring wealth. That is, the reasons for possessing wealth, such as work (including hunting, trading, reviving barren land, and working for wages), inheritance, state gifts to the subjects, and others, as well as the reasons for the growth and increase of wealth, are also specific transactions with fixed rulings, such as the rulings on sales, leasing, contracts, gambling, and others. It is noted that transactions are the same; working for wages is the same whether in China or America, in this time or in the past, whether working in a company or as a private worker sewing clothes during the Abbasid state, for example! Whether gambling with dice or what is called "lottery," Islam provides rulings for the actions that will satisfy the manifestations of instincts and bodily needs, and details the way transactions (such as sales and marriage) are conducted. For example, regarding how one possesses (acquires) these things (like a car or onions) that will satisfy their hunger, and how they act with them (such as selling or stealing), detailed rulings are given that permit sales and prohibit usury, theft, and plunder, as well as rulings on zakat, and so on, and rulings on the guarantees of rights arising from those actions (such as the worker's right to wages). These rulings remain fixed until the Day of Judgment; the rulings on sales do not change just because we sell airplanes instead of camels! The term sale encompasses all types of sales, as in His saying, ﴾And Allah has permitted trade and prohibited usury﴿ {Al-Baqarah 275}, the general ruling permitting sale applies to all types of sale; it is a general ruling!
In economics, sociology, and other societal systems, the issue has not been viewed as merely an economic or social problem, but rather as an economic problem related to the human being(492). Thus, we did not need to explore "Keynesian" remedies for the problem of unemployment and the recurring recession that plagues the capitalist economy, nor was poverty addressed by increasing production, but rather through how wealth is distributed among all individuals one by one, to satisfy their basic needs. In the West, they view economic revival through national output, even though those who own the wealth do not exceed five percent of the population, and the rates of unemployment, homelessness, poverty, and lack of healthcare are astronomical. This reflects the impact of viewing the problem as an economic issue detached from the individual human being. Islam, on the other hand, addresses the poverty of the individual and the poverty of the collective, establishing a system of "social solidarity."
