The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) is asked, and his intention is that he presented a delegation and continued in that state to obtain the issues, fearing that he might transition from the status of a delegation to that of permanent residence, thus becoming a migrant, which would prevent him from asking. The meaning of the hadith is that the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) addresses the companions, saying to them: "What the text has stated, take it, and do not ask about anything else, lest it becomes forbidden for you." This is akin to his statement (peace and blessings be upon him) as narrated by Imam Muslim from Abu Huraira, who said: "The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) addressed us and said: 'O people, Allah has made pilgrimage (hajj) obligatory upon you, so perform pilgrimage.'" A man asked: "Every year, O Messenger of Allah?" He remained silent until the man repeated it three times. The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "If I had said yes, it would have been obligatory, and you would not have been able to do it." Then he said: "Leave me as long as I leave you. Indeed, those before you perished due to their excessive questioning and their differing with their prophets. So if I command you with something, then do of it what you are able, and if I prohibit you from something, then leave it." And it was added in the Sahih of Ibn Hibban: and he mentioned that this verse in Al-Ma'idah was revealed regarding that: ﴾O you who have believed, do not ask about things which, if shown to you, will distress you﴿ [Al-Ma'idah 101]. Thus, the meaning of "what he remained silent about" is what he did not prohibit, so do not ask about it, for Allah has excused you from it and lifted it from you, so do not burden yourselves or seek it out!

Take a step back and ask yourself: If some matters related to organizing life were not addressed by the Sharia from the perspective of excusing people, neither by prohibition nor by permissibility, and there is a prohibition against asking about them, what is the position on them? When you encounter one of these actions, what do you do? If the legislator did not legislate, nor permitted you to legislate, nor to ask about them, and these matters pertain to your livelihood, what is the appropriate action? This means that those who seek areas of "legislative vacuum," if the legislator did not legislate, out of mercy and pardon for the people, will they be able to do better than that? To impose rulings based on reason! This implies that a person will not be able to act because there is no address, nor permission for reason to legislate, nor permission to act! And this is impossible. In the hadith of Abu Thalabah from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him): "Indeed, Allah has prescribed obligations, so do not neglect them, and He has set limits, so do not transgress them, and He has prohibited prohibitions, so do not violate them, and He remained silent about things as a mercy for you, not forgetting, so do not ask about them." Here it is clear that there is a prohibition against asking about those things he remained silent about, such as whether we should perform pilgrimage every year? When he said: "O people, Allah has made pilgrimage (hajj) obligatory upon you, so perform pilgrimage," then there is an important clue for understanding in the text that says: "And He remained silent about things as a mercy for you, not forgetting, so do not ask about them." Thus, what is left unaddressed out of mercy for the people means that either He did not prohibit them or that He did not impose burdensome obligations upon them in the obligatory acts, such as using a miswak for every prayer, pilgrimage every year, or coitus interruptus: as Jabir (may Allah be pleased with him) said: "We used to practice coitus interruptus while the Qur'an was being revealed, and the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) did not prohibit us from that," meaning he did not make it forbidden, and the example of the Jews in the story of Al-Baqarah is not far from us!

The time for its prohibition may not have arrived yet, such as when the companions asked Allah to clarify a clear ruling regarding wine, and the time for prohibition came later; it was a ruling that Allah did not forget, but He legislates it whenever He wills. Also, it is part of the prohibition against asking as the Jews used to do, which caused the legislator to impose burdensome legislation upon them, so He prohibited them from asking. Thus, its meaning is: "Obligations have been prescribed for you, so do not ask more lest it becomes strict upon you," as was the case with the questioner who asked about pilgrimage, and He remained silent about prohibiting things out of mercy for you, so do not ask about them lest it becomes strict and He prohibits them upon you. They did not ask about coitus interruptus, for example, nor can it be considered a legislative vacuum because the legislator cannot leave us without guidance in any matter, as is evident from the definitive texts in both authenticity and meaning, and He has not permitted us to legislate on our own, which means that the meaning of the hadith cannot indicate a legislative vacuum.

One might think of the matter of organizing traffic and say that it is one of the issues that the Sharia has not addressed with permissibility or prohibition. We say: Rather, the Sharia has addressed it with permissibility. It is well known that traveling on roads is among the permissible matters.
