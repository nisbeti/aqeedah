Therefore, the scholar Mustafa al-Zarqa (may Allah have mercy on him) said, describing the characteristics of Islamic legislation as being "characterized by extreme precision in the construction of rulings: so much so that the student researching issues of Islamic jurisprudence and the opinions of jurists and their theories feels as if he is before a sensitive scale used to weigh diamonds, which reveals the differences between similar matters, no matter how subtle and obscure"(214).
Ijtihad and Taqlid:
We said: The intent of the legislator is reached through one of two organized and regulated processes, which are either ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) or taqlid (following a jurist). It is time to elaborate on these rulings due to their great importance. We have previously mentioned that the fourth section of the studies of the science of usul al-fiqh (principles of jurisprudence) deals with how to derive evidence (or: methods of investment) (which is the balancing and preference among evidence), and how to derive (extract) legal rulings from evidence (ijtihad), and the state of the one deriving (the absolute mujtahid(215) or the school mujtahid(216) or the issue mujtahid(217)), his conditions and characteristics, and the states of the obligated individuals: namely ijtihad, taqlid, and seeking fatwa(218), (the followers: a following muqallid(219),

(214) Professor Mustafa al-Zarqa, Characteristics of Islamic Legislation.
(215) The absolute mujtahid is one who exercises ijtihad in legal rulings and in the method of deriving legal rulings, whether he has a specific method as is the case in some schools, or does not have a specific method, but naturally follows a certain way of understanding to derive rulings, as was the case with the mujtahids in the era of the Companions (Islamic Personality, Volume 1, by Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani, Chapter: Ijtihad and Taqlid). "This does not mean that the absolute mujtahid has knowledge of every ruling; this is not the intended meaning, and it is not attainable for a human being. The great Companions would refrain from many issues, as Umar did in the case of fighting those who withheld zakat, and as happened with Imam Malik, who was asked about forty issues and said about thirty-six of them: I do not know. The intended meaning is that the absolute mujtahid has the ability in ijtihad that enables him to research, derive, and establish principles and rules in many jurisprudential issues that generally encompass Islamic law as a whole." The Clear in Usul al-Fiqh, Muhammad Hussein Abdullah, pp. 363-364.
(216) As for the school mujtahid, he is one who follows a mujtahid from among the mujtahids in the method of ijtihad, but he exercises ijtihad in rulings and does not follow the Imam of his school. This school mujtahid has no conditions other than knowledge of the rulings of the school and its evidence, and he can follow the rulings of the school and oppose them with an opinion of his own within the same school. (Islamic Personality, Volume 1, Chapter: Ijtihad and Taqlid). That is, he "follows his Imam in the principles established by the Imam and exercises ijtihad based on them to derive new practical legal rulings that his Imam did not derive. He operates within the rules and methodology established by his Imam in ijtihad." The Clear in Usul al-Fiqh, Muhammad Hussein Abdullah, pp. 363-364.
(217) This does not have specific conditions or a specific method; rather, anyone who has knowledge of some legal knowledge and some linguistic knowledge [which we mentioned in the chapter: Principles of Ijtihad] that enables him to understand the legal texts is permitted to exercise ijtihad in a single issue. It is permissible for him to follow the opinions of the mujtahids in a single issue and their evidence and reasoning, arriving at a certain understanding of the legal ruling that he believes is the legal ruling, whether it agrees with the opinion of the mujtahids or contradicts them. It is permissible for him to follow the legal evidence in a single issue and understand from it what he believes is the legal ruling, whether this issue has been previously discussed by the mujtahids or not. It suffices for the issue mujtahid to be knowledgeable about what pertains to that issue and what is necessary within it, and it does not harm him to be ignorant of what is unrelated to it, whether it pertains to jurisprudential or foundational issues or otherwise. (Islamic Personality, Volume 1, by Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani, Chapter: Ijtihad and Taqlid) [and we will explain and elaborate on this shortly, if Allah wills, with important details].
(218) The one seeking a fatwa is not a muqallid, because the muqallid is one who takes the legal ruling and acts upon it, whereas the one seeking a fatwa is one who learns the legal ruling from someone who knows this ruling, whether that person is a mujtahid or not, and whether the seeker learns it to act upon it or merely for knowledge. (Islamic Personality, Volume 1, by Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani, Chapter: Ijtihad and Taqlid).
(219) The following muqallid is "one who has some recognized knowledge in legislation, but he cannot exercise ijtihad with it, so he follows another in taking the ruling while also taking the evidence for the ruling, [and the principles upon which the evidence is based], and the general muqallid is one who does not have some recognized knowledge in legislation, so he asks about the ruling of the action or thing he wants to do, following whoever he asks without knowing the evidence from which the ruling was taken." The Clear in Usul al-Fiqh, Muhammad Hussein Abdullah, p. 369, with modifications.
