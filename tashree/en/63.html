And Allah, Exalted is He, has confined the right to legislate to Himself alone(120), and has exclusively designated His Most High Self for it, revealing it in a Book and a Sunnah that He inspired to His Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), and has prohibited others from it (which is what is termed as sovereignty (hakimiyya)(121)). Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾Indeed, the judgment is only for Allah﴿ [6:57, 12:40, 12:67], and He said: ﴾And whatever you disagree about, its ruling is to Allah﴿ [42:10]. The term "judgment" in the language means prohibition, and hence it is said that a verdict is a judgment because it prevents from what is not adjudicated, meaning that the judgment prevents people from acting except according to what has been adjudicated. Therefore, only Allah has the right to prevent the ruled from acting except in accordance with His Sharia(122), for legislation establishes the meaning of servitude to Allah!

And Allah, Exalted is He, has permitted the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) to express the revelation that was sent down to him in his own tongue (peace and blessings be upon him), to clarify the legislative meanings, as He said: ﴾And We have sent down to you the Reminder that you may make clear to the people what was sent down to them﴿ [16:44]. This means that he (peace and blessings be upon him) was granted the right to formulate the meanings that were revealed to him as legislation. What is legislated in the Sunnah that was revealed to the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) is legislation just like the legislation in the Qur'an, equally so, and its source is revelation. What he formulates is infallible by revelation, and thus there is a watchful observation from revelation to achieve utmost precision in conveying Allah's intent, and he was infallible, speaking only the truth. These are guarantees that the words of the Sunnah were formulated under the care of revelation to convey the meanings that were revealed to him.

The intellect is not a legislator. Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾Or do they have partners who have legislated for them in religion that which Allah has not permitted?﴿ [42:21], and He said: ﴾And whatever you disagree about, its ruling is to Allah﴿, and He said: ﴾So if you disagree about something, refer it to Allah and the Messenger﴿, and He said: ﴾And We have sent down to you the Book as a clarification for all things﴿. The Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "Every matter not in accordance with our command is rejected." This indicates that the origin is to follow the Sharia and adhere to it.

The legislation of Allah is the guarantor of achieving justice and establishing the balance of equity on earth. If a matter lacks legislation connected to revelation, this opens the door to the existence of oppression or error in judgment and treatment derived from human legislation in that matter. (121) Sovereignty is one of the characteristics of Lordship by Allah's exclusivity in legislation, and one of the characteristics of Divinity by His exclusivity in servitude and sanctification through adherence to what He has legislated and not taking others as lords who legislate in religion what Allah has not permitted, or change His rulings. ﴾Indeed, the judgment is only for Allah; He commanded that you not worship except Him. That is the correct religion, but most people do not know﴿ [12:40]. This verse combines the right of obedience and the right of worship. It is a duty upon the servants to obey Allah in what He commands and to worship Him. Lordship, by its characteristics, entails legislative sovereignty, and whoever rules by other than what Allah has revealed rejects Allah's Lordship and its characteristics on one side, and claims for himself the right of Lordship and its characteristics on another side.

(122) From here, the legislation of servants among themselves involves taking each other as lords besides Allah!
