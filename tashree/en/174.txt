Fifth: The mechanism of applying solutions to circumstances and incidents according to the methodology of achieving the appropriate context and through regulated procedures
And the application of those solutions to those circumstances and incidents according to the methodology of achieving the appropriate context, and through regulated operational procedures for the process of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) based on well-established methodologies for inference and deduction. If followed correctly, it encompasses the new incidents that occur and continues to revolve within the same balanced systems of society without deviating from them, and its solutions for similar incidents remain firmly established throughout the ages and eras.
For example: "Any industry takes the ruling of what is produced, because the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) prohibited the production of wine, which is an industry. Anas reported: 'The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) cursed ten regarding wine: the one who presses it and the one who has it pressed...' This is a hadith. And Ibn Umar said: 'Wine is cursed in ten ways: "I cursed wine itself, its drinker, its server, its seller, its buyer, its producer, and its press..."' This is a hadith. Thus, the industry of pressing is permissible whether it is pressing grapes, oranges, apples, or others. Because the industry, in itself, is permissible, and among the industries is the industry of pressing, which falls under the general permissibility of industries. Then the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) came and prohibited the industry of pressing wine, and its prohibition here is because it is a pressing that produces wine. He gave the pressing the ruling of what is produced, which means giving the industry the ruling of what it produces.
This is not specific to wine, but is general for every prohibited item. The production of hashish, opium, heroin, and similar substances is forbidden because these things are prohibited, and the production of crosses is forbidden because taking the cross is prohibited, and so on. Based on this, it is inferred from the two hadiths regarding the curse on the one who presses wine and the one who has it pressed a rule (the industry takes the ruling of what it produces), and this inference is clear in its reasoning. The prohibition of pressing wine is not a prohibition of pressing in general, but rather a prohibition of pressing wine; thus, pressing is not forbidden, but pressing wine is forbidden. The prohibition of the industry comes from the prohibition of the production it yields, and this is evidence that the industry takes the ruling of what it produces. Based on this rule, the rule (the industry takes the ruling of what it produces) makes the production of prohibited items, such as the production of crosses, forbidden, and the production of items that fall under public ownership, such as oil extraction from public ownership, means that individuals do not have the right to engage in the production of something prohibited like statues and crosses, nor to produce something that falls under public ownership to become their individual property, thus losing its characteristic of being public ownership, such as the extraction of iron."(281) Therefore, the newly inferred rulings remain within the framework of achieving the same economic systems, preventing individuals from extracting iron from mines that do not deplete because it is public property of the Muslims, which the state organizes its extraction and production and benefits all Muslims from it. The effect of this maintains the state's ability to distribute wealth among the subjects (Muslims and dhimmis) in a just manner, allowing the economic system to continue achieving its goals in society even with the newly inferred rulings.
