As for the idea of utility, it is a concept that emerged in the field of economic thought literature, in an attempt to justify ownership and capital, and the transformations of the European bourgeoisie towards trade and then industry, along with efforts to maximize profit and minimize expenditure, etc. This idea, which was outlined by the thinker Jeremy Bentham, was one of the tributaries that led to the emergence of the concept of pragmatism, which represented one of the most important sources for building the Western civilizational formation. Furthermore, the same idea of utility represented the environment in which the concept of rationality or reasonableness grew, which some thinkers relied upon in their establishment of the concept of human rights(417).
The contractual state embodies all these foundations, and ultimately it is the tangible representation of the concept of rights. The practical translation of any contractual situation is represented in the rights that arise from the contract for one party and the obligations for the other party in the same contract. However, in the Islamic perspective, we do not imitate the West to claim that the foundation of human rights is contractual as in the West, and we provide evidence for this, because the contract in the West is based on the premise that "individuals have relinquished all their rights unconditionally in favor of the collective, but this relinquishment is met with individuals regaining new rights and freedoms that align with the new society, determined by the general will, which presupposes the existence of these rights and freedoms, as the state exists only to protect them, thus justice prevails, and every individual enjoys equal rights and freedoms, standing on equal footing with one another, as Rousseau expressed"(418). It goes without saying that the West has failed to embody the general will as a guarantee for its most important principle that underpins the legitimacy of the modern state, and to realize the Western constitutional theory based on the principle that "the nation is the source of authority," a matter we discussed in detail in our book The Black Box of Western Thought. Thus, political parties, lawyers, judges, and influential figures in the state are the ones who determine these rights, not the "general will," and consequently, these rights are not based on the realization of the principle of "contract" as they theorize.
This is a significant principle in differentiating between rights in terms of contract in Islam and in Western thought. The meaning of contract, therefore, differs from the subject of the contract in Sharia regarding the sale of a commodity, for example, and the rights that arise from it, or contracting for marriage and the rights and obligations that arise from it, and so on.
Moreover, we cannot claim that utility is an Islamic foundation for rights, similar to what those who formulated the "Universal Declaration of Human Rights in Islam" did, by citing theories of interests and objectives, nor can we make "struggle" a framework under which "conflict" falls, thereby importing the foundational templates of Western human rights to shape an Islamic rights theory, thus importing the entire Western value system, both its strengths and weaknesses!
Since Western thought has established the concept of rights based on conflict, whether the conflict is foundational to the rights situation or a method of exercising it. Human rights were established in the West due to the perception of a state of conflict among all.
