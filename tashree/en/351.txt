The human need for legislation and a system of life: concepts about things, and concepts about life
When a person looks at the things that Allah has made subservient to him in the universe, he reflects upon them, and concepts about them are formed in his mind (concepts about things) that are almost identical among all people. For example, onions are eaten, poison kills if consumed, walls protect from the heat, and marriage satisfies the instinct of procreation. However, the required behavior towards these things—whether it is permissible to eat onions or pork, whether it is permissible to drink water or wine, or to cultivate land, or to sell a commodity like wine, for instance, or to satisfy the instinct of procreation through marriage or adultery—this behavior requires concepts about life that form a measure for the individual in his actions and behaviors. That is, these concepts about life dominate the individual's concepts about things to regulate and organize them, allowing the person to rise above the animalistic nature that is concerned only with satisfying appetites indiscriminately, and with behavior that lacks controls and organization suitable for social living. These concepts about life only exist after resolving the major dilemma.
Since humans are social by nature, they need to organize the benefit derived from things in a way that makes their behavior—meaning their actions in life that satisfy their instincts and organic needs—acceptable to the group to which they belong, both intellectually and emotionally. Thus, society needs a system.
Moreover, organizing society alleviates the burden of responsibilities on individuals regarding the satisfaction of their basic and luxury needs, ensuring their ability to access goods and services in a manner they can afford, enabling them to live and thrive.
Therefore, although any person can act towards these things according to their whims, this does not lead to the elevation of humanity and its intellectual advancement; rather, they may flounder from one extreme to another. Their freedom to act as they wish may cause harm to others or themselves, or result in a temporary benefit followed by subsequent harm, or vice versa. Living without society is not valid, nor is society valid without organization and protection of that organization.(486)
Many people navigate life aimlessly, performing their actions without a measure to guide them. Consequently, you see them committing reprehensible acts that they believe to be good, while refraining from performing good acts that they perceive as bad. Thus, it is essential for a person—whether Muslim or non-Muslim—to have a measure by which to evaluate their actions, so they can understand the true nature of an action before engaging in it. What are the determinants and characteristics that one must possess to arrive at a sound, good judgment that achieves goodness? What are the criteria that clarify to us the correctness or incorrectness of those measures?(487) And why is it necessary to have specific measures, characteristics, and determinants that govern judgment to bestow upon it the attributes of correctness, invalidity, righteousness, or corruption?
