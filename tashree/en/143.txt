(246) See the section: Consultation and Seeking Opinion in Islam in the book The Islamic Personality, Volume One by Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani (may Allah have mercy on him).
(247) Leadership in Islam is individual, surrounded by the framework of consultation (shura), accountability, responsibility, and justice!
(248) The sultan in Islam is for the community. It is said in the language: "He empowered him," meaning he granted him authority and power. "He imposed upon him" means he enabled him and made him a ruler over it. "He dominated him" means he exercised control, enabled, and dominated. The sultan is power and coercion, and the meaning of sultan is one who has the authority to control and dominate in the state. For governance, kingship, and sultanate are synonymous, as they represent the authority that enforces rulings. The sultan can only be realized through two matters: the first is the care for public interests, meaning the general interests of the people, (the system of governance and state systems) through specific rulings, and the second is the power that protects the subjects and enforces the rulings, meaning security.
(249) Security is the absence of expecting harm in the future, and security is the opposite of fear, meaning the attainment of tranquility in life and the removal of fear. If the believers in the land of Islam are secure and not afraid, and their security is guaranteed by the inherent security of the state, then security has been achieved, ﴾And He will surely exchange for them, after their fear, security﴿. This can only be accomplished by possessing a sufficient amount of power that deters the enemies of the nation—both internally and externally—from being able to harm the Muslims or Islam. To achieve this, it is essential for the sources of power in society to unite in support of the state, which consists of three categories: a- The military and those of strength, b- The influential figures in society, including decision-makers, political intermediaries, scholars, dignitaries, merchants, and tribal leaders, for example, c- The third category is the general populace of Muslims.
The intention is not that they all gather on that matter, but rather that their gathering results in a strength that propels the community behind them to serve as a support for the state.
(250) Sovereignty in Islam belongs to the Sharia (Islamic law), and sovereignty means the entity that possesses the supreme authority in the state, exercising its will and directing it, in all relationships, and even in matters, and to whom all actions emanating from it are subject. See: Islam and the Foundations of Governance by Dr. Mahmoud Khalidi.
