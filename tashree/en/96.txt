Evidence for the obligation of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) as a communal obligation:
We will observe the following evidence that indicates the ruling of ijtihad and that it is a communal obligation in general, and then we will study the aspects of reasoning based on it:
First: The command to ask in case of ignorance: His Exalted saying: ﴾So ask the people of the message if you do not know﴿ indicates the necessity of referring to the people of knowledge to understand the legal rulings. Ijtihad is essential to provide legal answers to various issues.
Second: The obligation of clarification and not concealing: His Exalted saying: ﴾You must clarify it to the people and not conceal it﴿ indicates the obligation of ijtihad for the people of knowledge to clarify the legal rulings and explain them to the people.
Third: The command to refer disputes to Allah and His Messenger: His Exalted saying: ﴾And if you disagree about anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger﴿ indicates the necessity of researching the legal texts and deriving rulings when disputes arise, which is the foundation of ijtihad.
Fourth: The Sharia is complete and comprehensive: His Exalted saying: ﴾This day I have perfected for you your religion﴿ and ﴾And We have sent down to you the Book as a clarification for all things﴿ establishes that the Sharia encompasses all rulings, but reaching them in new situations requires ijtihad.
Fifth: The Prophet's (peace and blessings be upon him) approval of ijtihad: The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said to Muadh, “If you do not find it, then exert your opinion,” and his approval of the ijtihad of Amr ibn al-As in the Battle of Dhat al-Salasil. These texts are evidence of the legitimacy and necessity of ijtihad.
Sixth: Qiyas (analogy) as a tool of ijtihad: His Exalted saying: ﴾And if you disagree about anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger﴿ implicitly refers to qiyas as a tool for deriving rulings in the absence of explicit texts.
Seventh: The obligation necessitates knowledge of the ruling: A legal command cannot be executed without knowing its ruling. Thus, ijtihad is necessary to understand the texts and apply them to various situations.
Eighth: The principle "What is necessary for the obligation to be fulfilled is itself an obligation": One cannot know the legal rulings without ijtihad, which makes ijtihad an obligation to fulfill the commands of Allah and His Messenger.
Details:
[Among the evidence that ijtihad is a communal obligation is His Exalted saying: ﴾And We did not send before you except men to whom We revealed. So ask the people of the message if you do not know﴿, "Thus, Allah commanded those who have no knowledge to ask those who are more knowledgeable than them(197). Although it was revealed in the context of responding to the polytheists when they denied that the Messenger was human, its wording is general, and the consideration is for the generality of the wording, not the specificity of the reason. It is not about a specific subject so that it could be said it is limited to this subject; rather, it is general in the request for those who do not know to ask those who do. It is a request from the polytheists to ask the people of the Book to inform them that Allah did not send to the previous nations except a human. This is a piece of information they were ignorant of, so they were asked to inquire from those who know it. The word 'ask' is general, meaning ask to know that Allah did not send to the previous nations except a human; it is related to knowledge and not to faith. And the people of knowledge, even]

(197) Narrated by Jabir (may Allah be pleased with him) that a man was struck on the head by a stone and then had a wet dream, so he asked his companions: "Do you find for me a concession for tayammum (dry ablution)?" They said: "We do not find for you while you are able to use water." So he performed ghusl (full ablution) and died. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "It would have sufficed him to perform tayammum and tie a cloth around his head and wipe over it and wash the rest of his body," and the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "Did they not ask when they did not know? The cure for ignorance is to ask," narrated by Abu Dawood, Ibn Majah, and Ahmad. The Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) guided them to ask about the legal ruling. Al-Taysir fi Usul al-Tafsir, Ata Abu al-Rashta, Chapter: Taqlid.
