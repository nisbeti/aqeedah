Originally: what is benefit (or let us say: interest) or harm? Some matters are subject to relative estimation, which has reflected in the disturbances and contradictions in Western legislations, and has negated the ability of human legislations to be suitable for every country and every time. Some secular states may permit a certain behavior, while others prohibit it, or allow it at times and prohibit it at others. If the rational estimation of interest were the same and constant, differing rulings would not be permissible. Thus, the angle from which laws are derived is of great importance. It is also essential to define the objectives and standards that the law seeks to achieve, and this has also shown clear contradictions in Western legislations, as we will demonstrate, God willing, which has caused laws to oscillate from one extreme to another. Therefore, we will highlight an important alternative to utilitarianism and the determination of freedoms that ensures the suitability of treatments for every time and place, with some detail regarding these foundations and their relationship to behavior and societal relations, which requires the reader to have some patience to explore its depths.
Here, we will establish, by the will of God, the Islamic way of addressing the problems and issues that confront humans in life with a comprehensive and radical treatment for all their problems, stable and unchanging(240), and making the treatment renewed by applying it to new problems and issues, drawing from the same well of evidence and sources according to the same method of deduction, not flexible (i.e., not fluctuating or changing), while it expands to provide solutions for all human problems, suitable for individuals in every time and place, complementing the systems of life and society, leading to the achievement of its goals.
We will notice the focus on two fundamental dimensions of the issue: the individual dimension, which relates to regulating the behavior of the individual, his righteousness as an individual, and his upliftment as an individual. The basis for examining this is the organization of satisfying instincts and organic needs according to concepts that regulate his behavior and actions as an individual.
The second dimension is the societal dimension, which relates to the relationships of individuals with others in society, their relationships with authority, and the relationships of authority with society. This dimension is formed as a result of the individual's need to fulfill interests that he cannot achieve alone, necessitating his gathering with others to fulfill them. From this gathering arises a certain system that regulates those interests and organizes societal relations.
