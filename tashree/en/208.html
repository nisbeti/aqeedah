(310) The right: Professor Mustafa al-Zarqa said: "The right is a prerogative established by Sharia that grants authority or obligation," end quote, (Islamic jurisprudence in its new form, an introduction to the general theory of obligations in Islamic jurisprudence). This bond grants the creditor authority over the debtor's body, not over their property, and this distinction was what set apart real rights from personal rights. Real rights grant a person authority over a thing, while personal rights grant a person authority over another person. (Including the right of execution or enslavement or disposal, and when the Westerners recognized the corruption of this theory, they sought to mitigate the authority, limiting it to enforcement, which is, for example, imprisoning the debtor, and then enforcement became on the debtor's property, not on their person, by seizing their property, for example), and in reality, the relationship does not arise between a person and a thing, but rather it is a relationship between person and person, with the subject being the thing. Thus, the relationship between two persons exists in real rights! Conversely, in their definition of personal rights, we do not see a denial of the existence of a relationship between a person and a thing, because the subject of the relationship is the thing for which the bond between the two persons was established, so there is a bond between the person and the thing in personal rights, which, like real rights, is an element of financial liability, which its owner can dispose of by sale or mortgage, regardless. Therefore, this division contradicts reality!

Then a difference arose in obligations, leading to two schools: one that emphasizes the personal aspect to retain the original perspective of Roman law, which is what Latin codifications followed, and the other that emphasizes the financial aspect and liberates it from the personal aspect, which is what Germanic codifications followed. The theory of obligations is considered the backbone of civil law as a whole, dividing rights into personal and real, and considering the psychological inclination that takes the inner will, and considers the intent of the contracting parties, or the material inclination that takes the apparent will and stops at the expression of will.

(311) Rights of Allah: everything related to public benefit and its relation to Allah, Exalted is He, is intended to elevate His status. The right of Allah encompasses both public benefits in the Hereafter and this world, and the only exceptions are private worldly interests. Examples of public benefit include the prohibition of adultery, which relates to the general benefit of preserving lineage and protecting marital sanctity, and preventing animosities and disputes among people. Among the exclusive rights of Allah that no one can waive or relinquish are:

The tithe, land tax, punishment for theft, adultery, drinking alcohol, disciplinary measures, and the deprivation of a killer from inheritance or bequest. Jihad in the way of Allah.

The rights of Allah have been legislated for the public good without any personal interest tainting them, and thus they do not lapse with the waiver of anyone. Their collection and enforcement are for the public good, and in summary, the right of Allah, Exalted is He, is His worship, obedience to His commands, and avoidance of His prohibitions.

(312) The rights of individuals are those rights that pertain to individuals and are not subject to public order, except in organizing their resolution and establishing the rights brought before the judiciary. The rights of an individual refer to those related to their religion, self (blood, honor, and dignity), intellect, lineage, and wealth, protecting the personal interest of the individual rather than the collective, whether the right is general, such as preserving health, children, and property (the sanctity of others' wealth), achieving security, suppressing crime, repelling aggression, and enjoying the public facilities of the state; or whether the right is specific, such as safeguarding the owner's right to their property, the seller's right to receive the price and the buyer's right to acquire the sold item, the right of a person to compensation for their damaged property, the return of usurped property, blood money, the wife's right to maintenance from her husband, the mother's right to custody of her child, the father's right to guardianship over his children, the individual's right to engage in work, and the right of preemption.
