Why not make the angle of securing benefits and preventing harms the cause (illa) of the Sharia rulings?
Therefore we should consider that the Sharia rulings came to regulate and organize actions, conduct and relationships with specific rulings. They did not make the angle from which this regulation and organization is viewed the angle of benefits and harms, as a human being estimates them according to what he likes and dislikes, or what harms and benefits him. This is especially so since the estimation of these benefits and harms is, for the intellect, speculative and subject to rapid change: what you think is a benefit turns out to be harmful, and what you think is harmful from your limited view may not be harmful. So how will you judge that it is a benefit or a harm? It is an extremely difficult angle, especially when the ruling concerns a community, where you find that views of benefits and harms are many and contradictory. That is why Islam concerned itself with regulation and legislation to set out the ruling on actions, so that this regulation establishes people's lives according to specific rulings that provide solutions to their problems and new cases and regulate the relationships that arose from needs and interests they wished to fulfil and harms they thought they wished to avoid. Those rulings made clear to them what they must do. What they have determined will necessarily be the sound and correct ruling that regulates relationships in the right way, and the benefit will lie in following those remedies, not in putting those remedies on trial by the scale of the intellect with its narrow view of its own benefits and harms. If the ruling suits its desires it says: the benefit has been achieved in that remedy!
