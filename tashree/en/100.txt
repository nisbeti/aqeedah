Abu Dawood narrated from Amr ibn al-As: "I experienced a wet dream on a cold night during the Battle of Dhat al-Salasil, and I feared that if I performed ghusl (ritual purification) I would perish, so I performed tayammum (dry ablution) instead. Then I prayed the Fajr (dawn) prayer with my companions. They mentioned this to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), and he said: 'O Amr, did you pray with your companions while you were in a state of janabah (major ritual impurity)?' I informed him of what prevented me from performing ghusl, and I said: 'I heard Allah say: ﴾And do not kill yourselves; indeed, Allah is ever Merciful to you﴿.' The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) laughed and did not say anything. This is an acknowledgment from the Messenger of Allah of Amr's reasoning and deduction."
The argument for the legitimacy of taqlid (following a qualified scholar):
"However, the reality of the obligated individuals is that they differ in understanding and perception, and they vary in learning [and abilities and skills], and they differ in terms of knowledge and ignorance. Therefore, it is impossible for everyone to derive all the legal rulings from the evidence; it is impossible for all obligated individuals to be mujtahids (independent jurists). Since the goal is to understand the message and act upon it, understanding the message, that is, ijtihad, is an obligation upon all obligated individuals. And since it is impossible for all obligated individuals to understand the message by themselves [directly] due to their differences in understanding and perception and their differences in learning, the obligation of ijtihad is one of sufficiency; if some fulfill it, the obligation is lifted from the others. Hence, it is obligatory for the Muslim obligated individuals to have among them mujtahids who derive the legal rulings.
Accordingly, the reality of the obligated individuals and the truth of the legal ruling is that there should be mujtahids and muqallids (those who follow) among the Muslims. For whoever derives the ruling directly from the evidence is a mujtahid, and whoever asks the mujtahid about the legal ruling for a matter [or asks someone who knows the legal ruling even if they are not a mujtahid] is a muqallid, whether the questioner asks to learn and act, or to learn and teach others, or just to learn."(204)
