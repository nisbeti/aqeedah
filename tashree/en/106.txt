Therefore, in order to achieve the reality of the matter, if the discussion is about establishing a state, it is essential to understand the components of the state, its emergence, and its definition, and so on. If the research is about a herb that is to be judged whether it is intoxicating or not, scientific laboratory research must be conducted to analyze its compounds and their effects on the user, and so forth. This is the understanding of reality.
Secondly: The next stage is understanding the obligation in reality, which is purely a legal inquiry with no involvement of reason. In the example of the herb, it is necessary to study the concept of wine in Islam: what is it? What makes something wine from a legal perspective? The difference between intoxicants, narcotics, and vinegar, and so on, along with the rulings for each of them, and the criteria that define each and distinguish it from the others, and so forth, until the jurist has a clear understanding of what constitutes wine (or an intoxicant, or a narcotic, or vinegar) in the legal concept. It is said: wine (intoxicant) is defined legally as: that which obscures the intellect without the senses(212) with intoxication and joy, or that which produces foam and has a strong intoxicating effect(213), and the quantity that causes intoxication is: "that which intoxicates a portion or a measure" (which is a vessel that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) used to wash in, and linguistic sources state that a measure is equivalent to three sa's (plural of sa), and one sa is approximately 3.1 liters, thus the capacity of the measure is 9.3 liters). "Its little" (a handful of it) "is forbidden." Thus, the jurist proceeds in the stage of understanding the obligation in the research through a detailed deductive study of all the evidence related to the obligation that he sees applicable to the reality (i.e., the issue being researched), from the Book and the Sunnah, gathering this detailed evidence in one place, and conducting an analytical deductive study of it, subjecting it to rigorous examination, studying its abrogating and abrogated aspects, its general and specific aspects, its unrestricted and restricted aspects, and the implications of its terms, exerting utmost effort in that study, until the jurist identifies clear criteria that define the essence of this obligation, its definition, characteristics, and its distinction from others precisely. In our example: what are the criteria for the legal concept of wine? What are the criteria for an intoxicant, a narcotic, or for instance, what is divorce, what are its terms, what is the role of intention in it, and what are the circumstances that lead to it and the impediments that prevent its occurrence? What is anger, its characteristics, and the degree that makes it an impediment to divorce? And so forth. These criteria are necessary for the jurist to understand each obligation with precision, enabling him to apply that obligation to reality according to the defined criteria that distinguish it accurately.
Thirdly: The third stage is applying the obligation to reality. The jurist states: by analyzing the compounds in that specific herb and their effects on the intellect, we found that they fall under the legal reality of wine, as the criteria for the concept of wine apply to the criteria of its reality that we have researched thoroughly. Therefore, it takes the ruling of wine, which is the prohibition of both its little and its much, or for example, that it is an intoxicant, where its little is permissible and its much is prohibited. Thus, the jurist has applied the ruling of the obligation to reality.
Thus, the jurist must engage in specific research in order to derive the legal ruling regarding that herb, for some of it is purely rational, and some of it is purely legal.

(212) In this, it differs from narcotics: a narcotic, in language, is that which causes numbness in the body and heaviness and incapacity in the limbs. An intoxicant is anything that induces lethargy and numbness in the extremities. Umm Salamah (may Allah be pleased with her) said: "The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) prohibited every intoxicant and narcotic." An intoxicant has a legal limit, while a narcotic has a discretionary punishment. An intoxicant is impure and its little is prohibited, and it has a legal limit, while a narcotic does not prohibit its little and is not impure. Thus, as you see, it is necessary to understand reality to accurately determine the legal rulings.
