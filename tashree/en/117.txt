The Malikis, who expand the concept of public interest (maslaha), believe that such ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) is permissible because it achieves a legally recognized interest and there is no explicit prohibition against it.
In contrast, the Shafi'is may require explicit evidence or categorize the matter under "blocking the means" (sadd al-dhara'i) or "removing harm" to ensure the protection of the Qur'an. What Abu Bakr and Uthman did was to remove harm; when the killing of the Qur'an's memorizers increased, and there was fear for the Qur'an's preservation if the killing of the reciters, i.e., the memorizers of the Qur'an, continued, they saw the need to compile the Qur'an. Each of them did so based on the Sunnah, specifically the hadith: "There should be neither harming nor reciprocating harm," and not because they saw it as a public interest.
Secondly: The effect of differing principles on legal rulings
The difference in outcomes in subsidiary issues:
Example: The requirement of a guardian in marriage:
The Hanafis: do not require a guardian based on their understanding of the hadith "The widow has more right over herself."
The Shafi'is: require a guardian based on the hadith "There is no marriage except with a guardian."
The difference in rulings on new occurrences:
Example: Commercial insurance:
The Shafi'is: consider it prohibited because it involves uncertainty (gharar).
The Malikis: may permit it based on the principle of "public interest" (maslaha mursala).
The variation in the order of sources:
Example: The Hanafis prioritize analogy (qiyas) over single reports (khabar al-ahad), which affects the ruling when they conflict.
Thirdly: The most important foundational opinions related to the issue of disagreement
Acceptance of multiple legal opinions:
Imam al-Shafi'i: believes that disagreement is a mercy, provided it is based on sound ijtihad.
Ibn Taymiyyah: emphasizes the importance of disagreement as part of the diversity of the ummah.
The authority of analogy:
The majority: see analogy as a primary source for deriving rulings.
The Zahiris: reject it, believing that the texts are sufficient.
The role of public interest:
The Malikis and Hanbalis: accept public interest (maslaha mursala) as an independent source.
The Shafi'is: reject it without textual evidence.
The preference between evidences:
Foundational scholars like al-Ghazali and al-Razi established rules for preferring evidences when they conflict, such as prioritizing the definitive (qat'i) over the speculative (dhanni), or the general that has not been specified over the specific.
