The existence of these values poses a danger to the idea of pluralism, imposing certain views on society that infringe upon the individual's right to think, and renders legislation rigid, whereas its nature, in their view, is relative and changeable. This opens the door wide to contradicting justice and emptying legislation of any societal goals and objectives it seeks to achieve.
Stability, as change is a sign of the corruption of the system and the previous legislation's inability to solve problems correctly. Continuous change indicates humanity's inability to legislate, and change means acknowledging that the previous system did not achieve justice or the desired values at the societal level. It is impossible to revert to all cases that were addressed by the previous regulation to restore rights to them, thus leaving society in a whirlpool of unachieved justice and societal values, which weakens people's trust in laws and legislation, diminishes their respect for them, and reveals the system's inability to solve problems, leading to a lack of reassurance!
Islam has activated a set of rulings to integrate with solutions to problems and produce legislative objectives. For example, when it ruled the prohibition of theft and the cutting off of the thief's hand, the apparent nature of this legislation alone, if applied in isolation from other rulings, may seem difficult to reconcile with Islam being a mercy to humanity. However, upon closer examination, we find that Islam has clarified the reasons for lawful ownership and encouraged it, such as through work (it permitted trade and prohibited usury, regulated contracts and their terms, and addressed related issues such as debts and deferred sales, etc.), inheritance, gifts, maintenance, and rulings related to providing housing, clothing, medical care, and education for people. It has made it obligatory for the government to ensure people's basic needs and prohibited the state from oppressing individuals and seizing their properties, etc. Thus, the totality of the rulings is organized within a framework that produces mercy and justice. The cutting off of the thief's hand is a consequence of his transgression after he has deviated from a comprehensive system of rulings that ensured the thief's needs out of mercy for him (a corrective punishment for the sin) and for society (a deterrent punishment for the sin, protecting people's wealth and security so that no one dares to steal). It is also merciful in being appropriate to the sin, and so on. Therefore, achieving benefit and averting harm requires a higher level of thinking than merely addressing a problem and providing a solution; it requires thinking within the framework of comprehensive societal laws that address the problem at its root, ensuring the individual's needs in all circumstances, and aligning with the security of society and its systems. This necessitated the integration of a set of societal systems, which is not evident in human systems, as they tend to be limited to laws that deal only with the problem itself, without paying attention to its impact on the overall systems.
