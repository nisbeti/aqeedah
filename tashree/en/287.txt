The Eighth Column: Islamic Creed as an Intellectual Leadership in the Struggles of Life:
﴾Is he who walks prone on his face more guided, or he who walks upright on a straight path?﴿ [al-Mulk 22].
﴾And Allah has struck a parable of two men; one of them is dumb(427) and unable to do anything, and he is a burden on his master; wherever he directs him, he brings no good. Is he equal to one who commands justice while he is on a straight path?﴿ [an-Nahl 76].
Allah, Exalted is He, has endowed the words of this noble book with various forms of eloquence, types of rhetoric, and kinds of grandeur, along with the arts of expression, the subtleties of language, the beauty of arrangement and composition, the marvel of narration and the uniqueness of style, the sweetness of expression, the excellence of rhetoric, the splendor of its brilliance, and the clarity of its logic, the correctness of the Sharia and the methodology, the precision of the system, the truthfulness of belief, the strength of argument, the comprehensiveness of commands and prohibitions, the establishment of truth, the enforcement of justice, the perfection of moral virtues, which astonishes the minds of the wise, silences the tongues of orators, and nullifies the eloquence of the eloquent. It was natural for the truth to be embodied and manifested in its guidance as a powerful, self-sustaining force that profoundly influences the souls of those who believe in it, inspiring the believers to pursue noble matters and to refrain from trivialities. At the same time, its guidance has the ability to shape the capacities of the souls that believe in it and to mold them in a way that resonates with that inherent power of truth, allowing them to soar and ascend, elevating their ambitions to the heights of excellence, preparing them to undertake extraordinary deeds and great sacrifices. Thus, they are always striving for perfection and excellence in their actions. When Allah casts that pure, untainted truth against falsehood, which is at the peak of its arrogance and puffed-up nature, it shatters it, and it becomes vanquished.
The verses of the Qur'an were revealed in the most beautiful and eloquent forms of expression, characterized by clarity, strength, and elevation, pulsating with life, filled with images that convey the concepts of comprehensive, balanced, positive, realistic, divine Islamic doctrinal perception - in terms of its source, divine - in terms of its purpose and direction, based on monotheism and revelation, in a comprehensive, correct, goal-oriented, balanced intellectual framework that answers the major questions, explaining existence through its greatest components: the universe, humanity, and life; a total explanation that shapes a person's concepts about life, meaning it shapes their perspective on life, and formulates their defining concepts regarding human relationships with the great cosmic truths(428), and their relationships with humanity, the nature of connections with these truths, and the related concepts of divinity, servitude, accountability and punishment, trials and tribulations, reliance and interaction with the laws and causes, in a correct, elevated, and sound manner, understanding the role of humanity in existence, the purpose of their existence, and their relationship with the Creator. Consequently, this defines for them a way of life and the system that achieves this way, which makes the Muslim confident in the soundness of their perception, ensuring that they walk upright on a straight path! Unlike one who walks prone on their face, wherever they are directed, they bring no good!

(427) Al-Azhari said: There is a difference between the dumb (abkam) and the mute (akhras) in the speech of the Arabs; the mute is one who was created without the ability to speak, while the dumb is one whose tongue can speak, but he does not comprehend the response and does not articulate speech well. Ibn Ashour said: "The dumb is described as being dumb (abkam) with an open 'b' and 'k', which means being mute by nature from birth, such that he neither understands nor can be understood. It is added to his description that he is incapacitated and unable to do anything, ... which is the inability to perceive, to act, and the impossibility of benefiting from him in all his states." Thus, all these attributes are combined in him.
