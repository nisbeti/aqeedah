As for the Sharia (Islamic law) itself, the sources of legislation are the Book (Qur'an) and the Sunnah (traditions of the Prophet) in terms of texts, which are limited. However, their meanings apply to new occurrences until the Day of Resurrection, due to the nature of the language in which they were revealed and its ability to convey many meanings with few words.
There is an important point, which is understanding the meaning of the action and the ruling related to that action. It goes without saying that certain simple actions are associated with rulings, such as the act of drinking alcohol or fasting. There are also actions that consist of a set of specific actions, like prayer with all its movements and pillars, and for example: pilgrimage, which is a collection of integrated rituals that must be derived from the Qur'an and the Sunnah ("Take your rites from me"). Thus, it is not just the circumambulation that constitutes the pilgrimage, but the rulings that are spread throughout what the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) did from rituals, which are collectively referred to as the pilgrimage. However, for instance, when we examine the actions that the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) undertook to establish the Islamic state, and let us take, for example, the Messenger's (peace and blessings be upon him) journey to Ta'if, is the action merely the journey to Ta'if? Should one who establishes the state set out for Ta'if? What is the action that serves as a model for emulation?
Allah, Exalted is He, says: ﴾So if they nurse for you, then pay them their wages﴿. From this verse, a legal ruling is derived, which is that a divorced woman is entitled to a nursing fee. A legal ruling is also derived that any worker is entitled to their wage if they perform their work. This ruling applies to many issues, including that a government employee, a factory worker, a farmer in the field, and others like them are each entitled to their wage if they complete their work, because they are specific workers. Likewise, the carpenter who made the cabinet, the tailor who sewed the garment, and others like them are each entitled to their wage if they perform their work, because they are general workers.
Since Islam came with broad outlines, meaning general concepts, and left it to the human mind to derive from these general meanings the legal rulings for the ever-evolving and diverse issues that arise daily, then, when we find in the biography (sira) that the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) went to Ta'if or addressed the tribes, we ask ourselves: what is the framework under which these actions fall? We find that the texts accompanying the events clarify the intent behind the action, (for example: he went seeking protection, or to convey the message, or seeking support, or seeking to establish a state; these texts that accompanied the event clarify the nature of its purpose so that we can categorize it under a specific title, or derive from it specific rulings), and the conditions surrounding it, (for example, he stipulated to the Banu Shayban that they must protect Islam from all aspects in order for their support to be accepted), by studying all of this, you find that the writers of the sira and the compilers of hadith categorized this type of action under the title: "seeking support."
the intellect continues its journey in deriving the meanings surrounding this action, finding for example that the Quraysh refrained from accepting the call and harmed the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him), and that he seeks a supportive power and authority; Allah, Exalted is He, said to him: ﴾And say, "My Lord, cause me to enter a sound entrance and to exit a sound exit, and grant me from Yourself a supporting authority"﴿ [al-Isra 80]. Allah, Exalted is He, commanded His Prophet to ask Him for a supportive authority to establish his call and implement His Sharia. Thus, the intellect proceeds in thinking to derive the nature of this action and the rulings related to it that were brought by revelation. For instance, revelation did not permit conditional support (such as "if we have the kingdom after you"), and so on. This is how we understand: the actions that are worthy of emulation, or that clarify the general, or that constitute legislation that must be followed. Some actions, therefore, are simple and relate to a specific behavior, while some actions are complex, consisting of a combination of actions, conditions, and circumstances that struggle.
