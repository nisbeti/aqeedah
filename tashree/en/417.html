And the authority of a disbeliever over a Muslim is permissible ... etc., all under the pretext of changing interests. This approach, in reality, strips the Sharia of its binding nature and transforms it into a "historical advisory opinion" in the eyes of its proponents, which is subject to acceptance and rejection by modern human reason. It is a disguised secularism cloaked in the garb of Islam. Scholars have warned that deviating from the textual reference under the banner of interest poses a danger akin to erasing part of the religion. For human reason, no matter how advanced, cannot encompass all dimensions of true interest, as al-Shatibi said: "The minds of the obligated may fall short of understanding the interests of God's rulings in detail."

The impossibility of achieving the objectives of the Sharia while disregarding its texts: The reality is that the methodology of these individuals leads them to mistakenly believe that they are more concerned with the objectives and justice than the text itself! However, the facts have disproven them: when the Sharia was suspended and replaced by man-made laws under the pretext of progress, the alleged justice was not achieved; rather, oppression and corruption spread. An example of this is that the systems of man-made punishments adopted by some Muslims instead of the prescribed limits led to an increase in crime rather than a decrease; the permissibility of usury brought about severe economic crises; and the Western family system – which some were enamored with – ended in the prevalence of adultery and the loss of lineage. Thus, it became clear that the true interest lies in applying the Sharia, not in suspending it, and that "the law of Allah knows the interests of His servants better than their limited minds." Any attempt to make desires a criterion for legislation will ultimately lead to failure and corruption. One of the most telling examples of this is the failure of the democratic experiment in many of our countries; where some thought that merely importing Western mechanisms would bring about good, they were surprised that the electoral process produced conflict, partisanship, and financial and political corruption without achieving the true popular will. This is because it is based on a flawed ideological foundation (sovereignty belongs to the people, not to Allah), which inevitably leads to disorder.

Summary of the critique: The methodology of the Sharia of rational interest is, in reality, a prioritization of desires and a lifting of the sanctity of revelation from leading the nation. We have termed it "liberal Islam" in our book: "From the Sharia of Allah to the Sharia of Interest, the Roots of Liberal Islam" because it subjects the text to foreign philosophical views. Our duty is to clarify the fallacies of this methodology, and Imam Malik was right when he said: "Nothing will reform the latter part of this nation except what reform its first part"; what rectified the early Islam – namely, the arbitration of revelation – is the only thing that can ensure our well-being, not imported theories even if they are cloaked in the garments of objectives.
