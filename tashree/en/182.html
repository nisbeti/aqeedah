Eighth: Choosing the Correct Angle for Organizing Interests

We notice that the construction of human legislation on the realization of interests, which will often be material interests, or whose measures are merely material and utilitarian, at the same time that human legislators cannot definitively grasp these interests, makes the laws susceptible to reversal from one extreme to another, and keeps them perpetually within the realm of potential error, without even a presumptive indication that one of the multiple opinions that may serve as a solution to the problem is correct, which hinders the realization of any value from these laws and legislations. The interest may be realized by an abrogated law, and this may become clear after a while, or it may be temporarily realized by the current legislation, then other circumstances arise that reveal it to be unfulfilled, necessitating its change or amendment, or the solution may lie in something that did not occur to the legislator. Therefore, political parties in the West seek to change legislations and policies every election period due to the negative impacts they reveal on society. Thus, it was imperative to resort to a source of legislation that eliminates the realm of uncertainty in determining interests.

One manifestation of this is that: the granting or denial of freedom, or material benefit, was not made the basis for legislation:

The angle from which legislation is viewed is of utmost importance. Western civilization has exaggerated in sanctifying individual freedom, making this freedom a central axis for its legislations, thus establishing legislation based on the determination of freedom or its absence. The angle from which the West has approached its legislations, whether it is the realization of freedom and enabling the individual to do what fulfills his freedom, or the realization of material utilitarian interests, is an unregulated angle upon which contradictions of rulings can easily be built. There is no clearer evidence of this than the differences in legislations and laws among Western countries themselves regarding similar issues, while the view of man as a human being, a comprehensive and general perspective for addressing all the problems that arise for him since the Quran was revealed until the Day of Judgment, ensures the soundness of governance regardless of time and place, because the essence of man does not change, which gives rise to these problems that need solutions.

However, Islam, while legislating for people what addresses their problems, has made the subject of research the actions of man. It does not view the subject of legislation and organization from the angle of granting the individual freedom when he performs his actions or denying it to him, but rather looks at it on the basis that these are actions emanating from man; what is their ruling?

"As for freedoms, they are also erroneous and impractical. As for their being erroneous, man is created with instincts and organic needs, and satisfying them is the interest he seeks [through the organization of satisfaction], and harming this satisfaction is the corruption he seeks to avoid. Bringing about interest and repelling corruption is what forms a relationship between him and other human beings, and this relationship needs organization, because leaving it without a system leads to chaos and disorder. Therefore, organizing this relationship means constraining it, and its freedom means unleashing it, and unleashing it leads to chaos among human beings. Thus, the idea of freedoms is fundamentally a mistaken idea, because in its precise meaning, it leaves people's relationships without organization: they bring about their interests and repel corruptions as they wish, and this is extremely dangerous for man... As for society, the law based on Western law considers society to be composed of individuals, and it aims to organize the relationships of individuals, proceeding on the capitalist principle.
