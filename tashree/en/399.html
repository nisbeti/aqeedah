If the matter were merely individuals isolated from their ideas and beliefs, and the impact of those ideas on organizing their livelihoods and relationships, then Muhammad ibn Abdullah (peace and blessings be upon him) and his noble companions would not have distinguished themselves from the pre-Islamic society of Mecca. How could they, when they carried a long and extensive call to replace the values of that jahili (pre-Islamic) society with new values!

People are people in Medina and in New York, except that what distinguishes this community from that one are matters that go beyond the individual aspect. There is no doubt that defining a community as a group of individuals is a flawed definition that does not hold up!

In reality, what brought people together to form a community are a set of reasons that led them to agree on a certain way of life. This means that specific reasons caused them to establish lasting relationships among themselves. These relationships arise from the need of the people in that community to safeguard their interests,

Societies and states are established according to the historical social laws - which Islam has commanded us to consider and study - that take into account human incapacity to manage all affairs alone. Thus, one needs a doctor, a carpenter, a blacksmith, a builder, a teacher, and an engineer. Society arises from the existence of interests among people that drive them to establish lasting relationships with one another. The existence of these relationships entails rights and disputes that require organization, care for interests in a way that brings them about, and prevention of evils in a way that averts them, according to an agreed-upon system, and the fulfillment of vital energy in a manner harmonious with the principles agreed upon by the community. "The exchange of goods and services, marriage and neighborliness, leasing and security can only occur through an agreement among these people. Just as their coexistence and the possibility that some may not adhere to what they have agreed upon, and the potential for aggression from another group necessitate the establishment of a defensive system for them, and a system that obliges or punishes those who attempt to deviate from what the community has agreed upon. Thus, the formation of society begins with the need for people to gather and the desires and needs within them that must be satisfied, followed by an agreement on a specific method to achieve that. Then, each individual relinquishes a portion of their authority to place it in the hands of a power that obliges everyone to adhere to what they have agreed upon and preserves the unity and security of the community"(548). Each individual also relinquishes something of their mental and psychological characteristics (their personal thoughts and convictions that may conflict with the beliefs of the community to which they belong) to interact with the community and to acquire new characteristics as a result of living in that society. To facilitate this exchange of benefits, a set of economic, social, judicial, and punitive systems, among others, is established to regulate the ideas and values that prevail in society. Additionally, a set of customs, values, and emotions permeates the community, coloring their lives in a specific manner.

Thus, we clearly see that a collective thought (urf 'amm), and collective feelings have emerged from which a system organizes individuals, resulting in a distinctive character for the community, which led to a certain way of living, and distinctive public customs emerged from it, so the community had independent intrinsic characteristics and was not merely the personalities of its individuals(549).
