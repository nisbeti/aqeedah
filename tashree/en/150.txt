This makes a person not only in need of a coercive force to deter him from adhering to the law but also makes him submit to the authority of Allah in private and public. These are six examples that outline the Islamic approach to addressing problems: First, Islam addresses issues with a fundamental solution related to the essence of humanity. Second, it considers extraordinary circumstances in which a Muslim may be compelled to consume what is prohibited. Third, the Islamic system is concerned with specific matters that arise from its worldview of life and what falls under the concept of civilization. As for the aspect related to modernity, it has left it to humans to innovate as they wish. Fourth, it does not make reality the source of its thought but rather a subject for contemplation in order to change it with correct concepts, not to meet it halfway. Fifth, it achieves servitude to Allah alone, the Exalted, for He is the sole ruler. Sixth, it has established a mechanism for its application and implementation that ensures its self-execution in the absence of authority, but it has also set deterrent penalties(259) for those who deviate from it to ensure its proper application.

(259) The purposes of punishment in Islamic legislation do not stop at the specific deterrence of the offender, represented in reprimanding and reforming him, and the general deterrence of others, represented in deterring any individual from the community who might be tempted to commit a crime. Rather, it goes beyond these two purposes in that it serves as an expiation for the punished and a purification that removes the accountability for the sin of the crime he committed. This is something unique to the Sharia, unlike other positive legislations, as there is no way for these legislations to establish alternative punishments to the Sharia penalties that can achieve the aforementioned purpose. This is because the Sharia penalties are the law of Allah, Exalted is He, while positive laws are of human origin. Moreover, we find that the Sharia penalties ensure the achievement of justice between the offender and the victim, especially in cases of retribution, where it is based on the principle of "the punishment is of the same kind as the act." There is no doubt that this leads to healing the heart of the victim from the offender, which in itself creates a new motive for crime: revenge and retaliation born from the grudge that the offender holds against the victim.
Furthermore, Islamic criminal legislation adopts the principle of individualized punishment, differentiating in punishment in terms of type and degree according to the severity of the crime committed and the danger posed by the criminal. This ensures that the punishment achieves its general purposes (general deterrence) and specific purposes (reprimanding and reforming).
In most positive legislations, imprisonment is the general punishment that encompasses most criminals, and it has not achieved general or specific deterrence in the most advanced and civilized countries. In fact, the crime rate in these countries is continuously increasing, according to official statistics. In contrast, Islamic law, which applies alongside imprisonment, includes other more deterrent punishments such as stoning for the married adulterer, amputation for theft and highway robbery, and flogging for adultery, slander, and drinking alcohol. Since the corruption in these crimes is significant and constant, regardless of time and place, it necessitates that the punishments for them be painful to achieve deterrence, restraint, and discipline for the wicked and the corrupt.
Since the corruption in these crimes varies according to the right that has been violated from the five fundamental objectives of the religion (religion, life, intellect, lineage, and property), it also necessitates that the punishment be varied and appropriate for each crime. The scholar Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyya (may Allah have mercy on him) states in I'lam al-Muwaqqi'in: 2 / 115, "When the ranks of offenses vary, it is necessary for the ranks of punishments to vary. It is known that if people were left to their own minds to determine that and to arrange each punishment according to what suits it from the offense (in type, description, and degree), their opinions would diverge in every direction, and their paths would branch out in every way. Due to the great differences and the severity of the matter, the Most Merciful of the merciful and the Wisest of the judges relieved them of that burden, removed its difficulty from them, and, with His wisdom, knowledge, and mercy, determined it (in type and degree), arranging for each offense what suits it from the punishment and what is appropriate for it from the retribution." The merits of Islamic criminal policy, Dr. Muhammad Wafiq Zain al-Abidin, Al-Bayan magazine, issue 288.
