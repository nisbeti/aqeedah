You also find famous wealthy individuals at the peak of glory committing suicide due to the emptiness of life in their view. Pride in achievement, fame, wealth, luxury, and affluence are not real guarantees of achieving lasting happiness. How could they be, when their ability to achieve true happiness is linked to the existence of conviction in the validity of behavioral references and the presence of correct concepts about life? These alone are the guarantee that happiness will not turn into misery when the glow of fame fades, or wealth decreases, or luxury turns into hardship, leaving a person bewildered as to how to explain it all. If one does not find in their concepts and intellectual standards valid references that make their happiness sustainable, or when their affluence turns into a "deadly routine," they are quickly on the path to the abyss of depression and anxiety, and their satisfaction with themselves or their community shatters!

"What is more important than satisfying instincts and organic needs is how that satisfaction is achieved (i.e., its organization), not the satisfaction itself. The issue is not how much people eat, what they wear, or how much they own, but rather what they eat, how they earn what they buy to eat, and how they acquire possessions. Does the system that organizes their economic lives make their ownership pass through a system of usurious debts that burden them with heavy debts paid by parents and children throughout their lives? A system carefully designed to increase the rich’s wealth and the poor’s poverty?

The issue is not whether people live or do not live, but rather how they live, and whether their satisfaction of instincts and organic needs overshadows other aspects. Is there a balance in satisfaction represented by a regulatory system that achieves this balance? Because imbalance will inevitably lead to disturbance, confusion, and anxiety, that is, to misery, not happiness. Thus, it is judged whether they are truly happy or if their happiness is illusory or temporary, based on erroneous standards."(559)
