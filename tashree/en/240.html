He himself has acknowledged that the legal ruling derived from detailed evidence causes hardship, contradicting the text of the Qur'an that states Allah has not made for us in religion any hardship. Thus, he falls into that which he seeks to avoid.

The Lord of Glory, Exalted is He, says: ﴾Allah intends for you ease and does not intend for you hardship﴿, Al-Baqarah 185, and He said ﴾Allah does not burden a soul beyond that it can bear﴿ ﴾and He has not placed upon you in the religion any difficulty﴿, Al-Hajj 78, and He said ﴾And We did not send down to you the Qur'an that you would be distressed﴿, meaning that you should not exhaust yourself with worship and subject it to severe hardship, as al-Shanqiti said in Adwa al-Bayan. Al-Zamakhshari said: meaning He does not burden it except with what it can bear and is easy for it without exceeding its capacity and effort. This is an indication of His justice and mercy, as He says ﴾Allah intends for you ease and does not intend for you hardship﴿, for it was within the ability and capacity of a person to pray more than five times, to fast more than a month, and to perform more than one pilgrimage. End of quote.

From here, it is a mistake for one to think that the meaning of His saying: ﴾Allah does not burden a soul beyond that it can bear﴿(378), implies that it is up to the obligated person to determine whether this obligation is within their capacity or not, allowing them to shift to something else under the pretext that it is beyond their capacity. Rather, the verse is an argument against him, for the Lord of the worlds says: ﴾Allah does not burden a soul beyond that it can bear﴿, meaning Allah has not imposed an obligation on any soul except that this obligation is within its capacity. Therefore, He followed it with the statement: ﴾For it is what it has earned, and against it is what it has gained﴿. The obligation is within its capacity; if it follows it, it earns good, otherwise, the sin of not adhering to the obligation is counted against it. And we know that the Lord of the worlds, Exalted is He, when He knew that some people were unable to fulfill certain obligations under specific circumstances, He alleviated their burden and permitted them. He said: ﴾And whoever is ill or on a journey, then an equal number of other days﴿. So, whoever is not ill or on a journey is capable of fasting, and it is not permissible for him to claim at any time that the obligation is beyond his capacity, nor is there an excuse for him. And whoever is facilitated with permission to break his fast acknowledges that the original obligation is burdensome for him, contradicting the statement of the Lord of the worlds: ﴾Allah does not burden a soul beyond that it can bear﴿, thus saying to Allah: rather, this is an obligation that You have imposed upon me that I cannot bear, and he contradicts Allah's ruling. What could be more heinous and detestable than this?

Indeed, Muslims in this age have been tested by those who describe adherence to the legal ruling as rigidity, or Hanbali, or extremism, and fundamentalism. This is a lack of respect for Allah, for adherence to His law is all good, and breaking away from this adherence is loss and misguidance. Just as Muslims have been afflicted by those who lean on these verses—ignorantly of their implications—and on the saying of the Messenger ﷺ: from Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) who said, "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ was never given a choice between two matters except that he chose the easier of the two, as long as it was not a sin; but if it was a sin, he was the farthest of people from it," narrated by al-Bukhari. Al-Bukhari also narrated from Anas that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Facilitate and do not make things difficult, and give good tidings and do not repel," and in another narration in al-Bukhari and Ahmad: "And be calm and do not repel." Al-Nasa'i narrated in the chapter: advising with reminders!
