The greatest influence on behavior is the concepts about life.
We have previously pointed out that one of the goals of the philosophy of law (or jurisprudence) is to establish legislation that is suitable for all laws (not for a specific country's law) at all times (not in a specific era). This is a necessity for achieving legal stability and legal security, which in turn achieves the public good and justice. However, those who wish to strip Islamic legislation of its sanctity and make it a product of a specific time and place do not understand the nature of legislation and view it from an incorrect perspective, rather than from the correct angle that, when applied, makes it suitable for all times and places. This prevents society from swinging from one extreme to another due to the fluctuations and changes in laws; what was punishable yesterday becomes desirable today, and then becomes prohibited again tomorrow!
It goes without saying that Islam has provided specific remedies for exceptional circumstances, such as necessities, excuses, and conditions, like the rulings of necessity and the rulings of prayer and fasting for the healthy and their rulings for the sick, disabled, and excused, and so on. It is also worth mentioning that any new reality that arises in human life will have a legal ruling derived from its detailed sources, such as the rulings on cannabis and banking transactions.
