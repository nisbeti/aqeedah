Faith, Piety, and Action
﴾Those who believe in the unseen and establish prayer and spend out of what We have provided for them (3)﴿, and in the revelation: ﴾And We were not absent﴿ [al-A'raf: 7], and He said: ﴾Those who fear their Lord in the unseen﴿ [al-Anbiya: 49]. He, Exalted is He, is unseen to the eyes, not visible in this world, yet not absent in terms of observation and reasoning; they believe that they have a Lord who is capable of rewarding for deeds, thus they fear Him in their innermost selves and in their private moments when they are away from people. Notice the direct connection between His saying: ﴾A guidance for the righteous (2) who believe in the unseen and establish prayer and spend out of what We have provided for them (3)﴿, for the description of the righteous is coupled with the description of those who believe in the unseen. The pious, as we have mentioned, feel the watchfulness of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, at every moment, and Allah is not absent, except that our eyes and senses do not perceive Him, due to the perfection of His attributes, blessed and exalted is He. It is natural for minds to engage and strive to contemplate and attain the faith that makes them realize that this unseen entity is always present, observing every heartbeat, every movement of the soul, and every action of the body. Thus, a person should bring to mind piety, vigilance, and fear, so that these become the driving force and the nourishing energy that revives the spirit in any action they undertake, whether it be prayer, zakat, spending, or otherwise. Without these, actions may deteriorate to become lifeless corpses.
He connects faith and action, linking piety and action, to perfect the work in the best possible manner, so that it is performed in the most complete way befitting the one who does it, seeking His pleasure, expressing gratitude for His blessings, and fearing His punishment, because He is present, observing them at every moment. These are motivators for the excellence of action. When one prays to Allah, the requirements include: humility, submission, meekness, and yielding before Allah, and the presence of Allah's greatness, awe, and majesty in prayer. The greatness of Allah and His majesty fills their heart, and they aspire for a good encounter with their Lord in that prayer first, to attain His reward and succeed in what He has, hoping for His mercy and fearing His punishment; and they establish what He has commanded in the best possible manner.
Thus, the required humility is that of one standing before Allah, but due to human nature, when a person stands before Allah, the Exalted, praying with humility, their prayer is not free from the whispers of the soul, the temptations of the devil, and the worries and problems of life. They may forget at certain moments in prayer that they are standing before Allah, the Exalted, so their humility does not reach its highest level of perfection, even though it is supposed to be filled with submission, humility, and the gathering of awe and fear. This is what is required of them to reach the highest levels of perfection, as they are ﴾Those who will meet their Lord﴿ in their prayer, conversing with Him. The matter, then, requires the presence of the greatest possible amount of awe, fear, and humility, and the Lord is the Bestower of blessings, thus it also requires elevating humility to a level that befits gratitude to Allah for His blessings.
Narrated by Abu al-Yasar, the companion of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said, "Among you are those who pray the complete prayer, and among you are those who pray half, a third, and a quarter, until he reached a tenth." Suraij said in his narration, "until he reached a tenth." Ahmad. Musnad al-Makkiyyin.
All of this calls upon the one praying to be mindful of the status of piety and what it entails of presence and vigilance, and the excellence of action. This is what makes the prayer bear good and blessed fruit, from which a person tastes the sweetness of faith and breathes in the breezes of piety, thus entering among the successful and honored servants of Allah. As He, Exalted is He, says: ﴾Indeed, the believers have succeeded, those who are humble in their prayer﴿ [23:1-2]. Therefore, the prayer that is not accompanied by the remembrance of Allah, nor enveloped by humility and fear, nor shaded by tranquility of the soul and peace of heart, is a prayer with little fruit and minimal effect.
