The law, as an ideological tool, does not believe in fixed values, and the school of sociological jurisprudence adopts a pragmatic practical perspective, taking into account moral values as far as they serve social cohesion.
All of this results from ignorance of the complex reality of man: he is not merely a body, nor a separate soul, but an integrated entity of mind, body, spirit, and self, with mentality and psychology, inclinations and desires, and vital energy, whose condition cannot be rectified unless each aspect is given what it needs in terms of education, purification, and disciplined fulfillment. Achieving happiness in both this world and the Hereafter together. This understanding cannot be attained by reason alone; rather, it must come from the Creator of man Himself, who said: ﴾Does He not know who created, while He is the Subtle, the Acquainted?﴿ [Al-Mulk 14].
From here, Islamic law came with a unique balanced perspective on man:
It combined spiritual, material, moral, and human rights.
It considered his need for permissible enjoyment, but regulated it through marriage, family, and chastity.
It opened doors for work and striving, but regulated it through zakat (almsgiving) and mutual support.
It directed him towards worship and humility, but did not abolish his social or economic life.
This goal is based on a noble relationship of love between man and his Creator, which is not founded solely on fear and compliance, but on awareness of God's bounty, mercy, and generosity, and what that requires in terms of fulfilling the duty of love and drawing closer to Him, as stated in the Qur'an: ﴾Say, "If you love Allah, then follow me, so Allah will love you."﴿ [Al Imran 31].
This reciprocal relationship in love is not merely feelings; rather, it is based on embodying the attributes that Allah loves – such as benevolence, piety, patience, generosity, and mercy – qualities that the Sharia instills and nurtures through obligation, trials, righteous deeds, and continuous purification of the self. Allah breathed into man of His spirit and endowed him with the capacity to know Him and draw near to Him, and this closeness can only be achieved through striving against the self and purifying it to embody some of the attributes of divine perfection, such as mercy and benevolence, so that divine affection grows in the heart of the believer, and this becomes the pinnacle of happiness in this world and the key to eternal bliss in the Hereafter.
Thus, it becomes clear that divine legislation alone – not positive laws – is what takes into account this complex reality of man, directs his instincts correctly, achieves balance between his material and spiritual needs, and leads him to the ultimate purpose of his existence: servitude to Allah, attaining His love, and achieving tranquility in this world, and success in the Hereafter.
With this precise balance, divine legislation fulfills the great purpose of human existence: the worship of Allah, the cultivation of the earth, and achieving balanced happiness in this world and the Hereafter, a balance that no positive philosophy has succeeded in attaining, because it simply ignored man and his purpose. Therefore, legislations cannot be subjected to the scrutiny of minds to comprehend their rulings and reasons and their realization of what those minds perceive as interests and desires; if they do not grasp this, they will turn to human legislations, thinking that they achieve those interests, desires, and benefits, which is never correct. Rather, legislations must be derived from revelation in worship, love, satisfaction, submission, commitment, application, and surrender!
