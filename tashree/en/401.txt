Based on this, one can categorize all systems and relationships that govern society. Another example: the state in the West collects taxes from people's money to provide them with the necessary services in return. Therefore, people concede some of their financial rights (even if it amounts to nearly half of their income in the form of taxes) and agree to this concession, thus achieving mutual benefit, and laws are codified to regulate these relationships, with severe penalties for those who do not pay taxes!
Moreover, there is no doubt that the community will rejoice and celebrate with the groom because he has satisfied his desires and achieved his wish with the idea that the group has agreed upon, namely the idea of marriage. This group that forms the community will become angry and resentful towards anyone who violates it through adultery or other means, and may even resort to killing him or her, or punishing them, or inflicting penalties upon them. In some other communities, they may even rejoice for them! The same applies to other ideas; those who fulfill their needs through purchase are regarded favorably, while those who attempt to seize through anger, theft, or fraud are viewed unfavorably. Thus, all needs are governed by this principle(551).
Accordingly, we have seen the impact of ideas that arise to achieve interests, which establish certain relationships that people agree upon in terms of their feelings towards them, whether of satisfaction or anger, joy or happiness, and the codification of laws that regulate the course of those relationships. Therefore, relationships arise out of necessity, and without necessity, there are no relationships. However, these interests are clarified in terms of whether they are beneficial or harmful by the human understanding of interest. Since concepts are the meanings of ideas, it follows that ideas are what define the interest and thus create the relationship.
Therefore, for a relationship to exist among people, there must be (or at least among the stronger group in society capable of imposing its vision) a unity of ideas, feelings, and systems regarding it. If there is no unity of these three matters among them, there is no relationship. Human behavior and relationships with others are governed by their concepts, which are a blend of thought and feeling. Likewise, the legislation that the authority uses to manage the affairs of society largely governs community relationships, and thus significantly affects the way of life within it, even influencing the personalities of its individuals!
Consequently, society is "a group of people (individuals) among whom relationships exist, linking them through ideas, feelings, and systems." This society is classified according to this system of ideas, feelings, and systems; if it is Islamic, for example, it is Islamic, and if it is liberal, it is liberal... and this is the essence of civilization. Civilization and society, in the words of "Ralph Linton," "are connected through the individuals who make up the society, and their behavior expresses the type of civilization they embody"(552). In reality, there is a strong relationship between the way of life and society or civilization, as interests and a person's perspective on them are influenced by their worldview in life, that is, by their beliefs. If their perspective on life changes, and consequently their view of interests changes, relationships in society will change accordingly, which clearly indicates that the foundation of change is the alteration of concepts and convictions within society.
