Its second aspect: Responsibilities, duties, and rights were not based on changing and fluctuating needs:

Those commands and prohibitions, as well as those responsibilities, duties, and rights, were not based on changing, moving, fluctuating, and variable needs according to circumstances, conditions, time, place, environment, and custom. Rather, the conditions, circumstances, incidents, customs, and interests were regulated by legislations that govern them. (That is, when addressing the incidents and problems arising from or existing in reality, that reality was not made a source for jurisprudence and thought to derive solutions from it, but rather a subject for thought and change to regulate it with its concepts and rulings to derive solutions for it.) Those solutions, whose fundamental characteristic is stability, do not change, thus ensuring the continuous achievement of justice and guaranteeing the realization of its goals and objectives correctly, while maintaining its coherence within the legislative system.

An example of this is when society resorts to solving the housing problem through usurious loans, or solving the problem of accidents and disasters through insurance. In these cases, Islam does not adapt to these new solutions by changing its rulings, nor does it take reality as a source for thought, saying: People need housing and the cost of building it is beyond their reach, and thus there is no solution except to abandon the prohibition of usury and legislate banks and usurious loans! Islam does not do that, because this way of thinking is a product of the capitalist system, where the state relinquishes its guardianship role and transforms into a revenue-collecting state, not intervening in fulfilling individuals' basic needs. Its system is based on concentrating wealth in the hands of a few, rather than distributing wealth fairly across society, where the wealthy monopolize natural resources and their extraction, as well as the resources necessary for building houses, such as cement and iron, causing their prices to multiply. The capitalist system, based on unbacked paper currency, leads the economy into inflation, causing prices to rise as the value of currency decreases. Thus, the vast majority of society will turn into laborers for the owners of companies and capital, unable to afford the price of housing, and the cost of building a house will rise due to the nature of the capitalist system, forcing individuals to resort to banks. Meanwhile, in Islam, minerals that do not deplete, such as oil, gold, salt, iron, cement, and similar natural resources, are not placed under private ownership; rather, they are public property for Muslims. Proper management of their extraction and investment is sufficient to secure the basic needs of Muslims and a large part of their luxury needs by the state, and a monetary system based on gold and silver does not lead to inflation.
