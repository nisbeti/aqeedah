The science of usul al-fiqh (principles of jurisprudence) addresses these evidences in terms of establishing their authority and their status in deriving legal rulings, which will yield the desired fruits, namely the practical legal rulings.

Secondly: The legal ruling (hukm shar'i) and what relates to it is the desired fruit, and it includes: the meaning of the ruling(146), the categories of the ruling(147), the objectives of the legal rulings (maqasid al-sharia), and the pillars of the ruling, which are: the ruler (who has the authority to issue rulings on actions and things, that is, who has the right to legislate: is it the law or humans?), the ruled upon(148) ("who the ruling is issued against, that is, who is obligated to implement this ruling"(149)), and the subject of the ruling(150). The ruling is a statement from the legislator concerning the action, and it is not a description of the action itself, nor a description of it as good or bad, and it does not pertain to entities, nor is there any role for reason in it, and there is no ruling before the arrival of the law, and some rulings have manifestations that clarify the reality of the cause, the reason, the condition, the subject, and the sign(151),

(146) The reality of ruling in itself is clarified to be a statement from the legislator, and it is not a description of the action, nor is it good or bad, nor does reason have any role in it, nor is there a ruling before the arrival of the law (al-Mustasfa by al-Ghazali 1/40)

(147) The legal ruling (hukm shar'i) is divided into two sections: the first section: the address of the Legislator (al-Shari') concerning the clarification of the rulings on human actions through obligation and choice, which is known as the address of obligation (khitab al-taklif). By obligation (bi-l-iqtida) it means: by request, whether it is an absolute request (the absolute request for action is obligatory, and the absolute request for abstention is prohibited) or non-absolute (the non-absolute request for action is recommended, and the non-absolute request for abstention is disliked). And by choice (bi-l-takhyir) it means: permissibility. The second section: the address of the Legislator which clarifies what the rulings on human actions necessitate in terms of matters that the realization or completion of the ruling depends upon, which is known as the address of status (khitab al-wad'). It has five categories: 1. cause (sabab), 2. condition (shart), 3. impediment (mani'), 4. validity, nullity, and corruption (sihha wa batil wa fasad), 5. obligations and concessions (al-'azaim wa al-rukhas). (Taysir al-wusul ila al-usul, Atta Abu al-Rashta 1/33).

(148) The term "the ruled" (al-muhkam 'alayh) refers to the servant whose actions are subject to the address of the legislator, and the most prominent related cases here are: First: The address of the legislator regarding Islam - both in belief and rulings - is directed to all people, both Muslim and non-Muslim, and they will be held accountable for that. Second: The performance of any action by a non-Muslim is not accepted if Islam is a condition for its validity, such as prayer, zakat, fasting, and pilgrimage, while actions that do not require Islam as a condition for validity, such as witnessing a will while traveling, are accepted from him. Third: A Muslim applies the rulings of the Sharia (Islamic law) in obedience to the command of Allah, Exalted is He, and the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him), and the rulings of the Sharia are applied to non-Muslims in the Islamic state by virtue of the dhimmi contract. Fourth: The obligation is lifted from the ruled regarding the direct performance of the action in the following cases: a) the non-pubescent, b) the non-sane, c) the one who is deeply asleep. "The pen has been lifted from three: the child until he reaches maturity, the sleeper until he awakens, and the insane until he regains sanity." (Abu Dawood 4398, Ahmad: 6/100, al-Nasa'i, and Ibn Majah through Aisha, and its chain is authentic, and Abu Dawood, al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i, and al-Hakim have reported it through various chains from Ali). Fifth: Accountability is lifted from the obligated person for the following excuses: a) the one whose will is taken away by coercion that leads to killing or something equivalent, b) the one who is completely forgetful and does not remember what is required of him, c) if the action falls within the realm of error - unintentional - that is, without his choice. "Mistakes, forgetfulness, and what they are coerced into are lifted from my community." Its narrators are trustworthy, but there is a break in the chain, and it was reported by al-Tabarani, al-Daraqutni, and al-Hakim: 2/216 with a weak chain, and it was authenticated by Ibn Hibban: 16/202.

As for anything else, the servants are obligated according to the relevant legal rulings. See Taysir al-Wusul ila al-Usool by the scholar Ata Abu al-Rashtah, volume one, pages 58-59.

(150) The term "the ruled upon (al-muhkum fih)" refers to the "action" of the servant to which the address of the Legislator pertains; it relates to actions and not to entities, and it is not a description of the actions in themselves (al-Mustasfa 1/40). The most prominent related cases here are: First: That Allah, Exalted is He, does not impose upon us or hold us accountable for any action before clarifying it to us. Second: That Allah, Exalted is He, does not accept any action from us unless it is in compliance with His command, Blessed and Exalted, and the command of His Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him). Third: That Allah, Blessed and Exalted, does not impose upon us except what is within our capacity; He does not require us to perform actions that we cannot bear. Fourth: That among these actions are those that are rights of Allah, Blessed and Exalted, and the consequences of such actions, like the prescribed punishments (hudud), do not allow for intercession, nor does the servant have the authority to waive this right. Among these actions are those that are rights of the servant, and the consequences of such actions, like retribution (qisas) or blood money (diya), allow the servant to pardon according to the rulings of the Sharia. Fifth: The address of the Legislator concerning actions can be either direct, which is called the address of obligation (khitab al-taklif), or indirect, meaning it does not pertain to the action itself but to specific conditions of the action, which is called the address of status (khitab al-wad'). (See: Taysir al-Wusul ila al-Usool by the scholar Ata Abu al-Rashta, Volume One, pp. 55-56.)
