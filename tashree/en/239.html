He has not placed upon you in the religion any difficulty; the nature of legislation is ease, not hardship:

All praise is due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon the Messenger of Allah, and upon his family and companions, and those who follow them.

Imam al-Bukhari (may Allah have mercy on him) narrated from Urwah who said: "Abdullah ibn Amr came to us and I heard him say: I heard the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) say: 'Indeed, Allah does not take away knowledge after He has given it to you by force, but He takes it away by the death of the scholars. Then there will remain a group of ignorant people who will ask for legal opinions and they will give their opinions based on their own views, thus leading others astray and being led astray themselves.'"

It is not hidden from one who has caught a whiff of jurisprudence, in addition to those who are among the jurists (mujtahidin), that Islam has a specific and clear method for deriving practical legal rulings from its detailed evidence. The jurist must first observe, when deriving rulings and adopting them, the understanding of reality and the jurisprudence within it, which is the verification of the relevant criteria (tahqiq al-manat). He must understand the essence of the incident or event or the matter for which he wants to clarify the legal ruling. Then, he must comprehend the obligation in addressing this reality based on the legal evidence, which is understanding the ruling of Allah that He has decreed regarding this reality. Then he applies one to the other, meaning that the ruling that has come from the Sharia has had its evidence identified and its rationale understood, but does it apply to this specific matter or not? Thus, examining the applicability of the known ruling, whose evidence and rationale are established, to an individual case is the verification of the relevant criteria. The basis of the ruling is the non-transmissible aspect of the legal ruling, which refers to the reality to which the legal ruling is applied. For example, if I say: "wine is forbidden," then the legal ruling is the prohibition of wine. The verification of whether a specific drink is wine or not, in order to determine whether it is forbidden or not, is the verification of the relevant criteria. For instance, you look at the reality of the beverage to see if it causes intoxication so that the ruling of wine, that is, its prohibition, can be applied to it. In other words, one arrives at knowing the ruling of Allah in this reality through understanding and jurisprudence regarding it.

And after he has grasped it, he moves on to understanding the transmitted texts (naqlayat), that is, to understanding the legal texts or the legal texts related to the subject from which the ruling is to be derived for that incident, event, or matter, or to understand the legal ruling that is intended to be applied to that incident, event, or matter.

And the issue of facilitation (taysir) or choosing between legal rulings related to the same subject does not enter into this methodology, neither closely nor remotely. For example, if a jurist (mujtahid) concludes, after studying the issue of the nullification of ablution (wudu) from touching a woman, that it nullifies it, and another jurist concludes, after studying the issue with its evidence, that it does not nullify it, it is not permissible for either of them to abandon the ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) they have reached under the pretext of seeking the easier option or alleviating hardship. This is because it would be the ruling of Allah concerning him and concerning the one who follows him. It is not permissible for him, while he is engaged in ijtihad, to allow ease or facilitation to influence the derived ruling, for in doing so, he would not be seeking the ruling of Allah on the matter but rather the ruling of his desires or the desires of the people, and this is absolutely forbidden.

No doubt, religion is ease, and it has not come to impose hardship on Muslims, and this is inherent in the religion itself, that is, in the nature of its rulings. For example, prayer is easy; it is five times a day, not fifty, and pilgrimage is once in a lifetime. The expiation for someone who breaks their fast in Ramadan is contingent upon their ability: freeing a neck, or fasting two consecutive months, or feeding sixty needy people. All of this is from the mercy of Allah and His facilitation for His servants. However, if a jurist were to see that people find prayer burdensome and issued a fatwa limiting it to three prayers under the pretext of lifting hardship or facilitating matters, or if he issued a fatwa allowing a person to shift from fasting two consecutive months for expiation to feeding the needy while being able to fast, under the guise of ease, he would have distorted the religion and taken it upon himself to legislate without Allah, and at the same time...
