The governance we have experienced through inheritance, military force, or the importation of democracy has not brought about true freedom or complete dignity; because ultimately, it has not adhered to the Law of Allah, which encompasses justice and mercy.

Conclusion of the Chapter

From this analytical dialectical presentation, we conclude with the following detailed results:

The Sharia (Islamic law) did not leave politics to abstract reasoning: Rather, it established detailed systems and rulings for governance and the state, just as it did for other aspects of religion. Therefore, the claim of a "legislative vacuum" in matters of governance is unfounded; rather, Islam came with a complete state and system of governance, and any alleged vacuum arises from a deficiency in understanding the texts or following their objectives.

Sovereignty belongs to the Sharia, not to the people or reason: Legislation is an exclusive right of Allah, Exalted is He; no one can legislate a ruling without His permission. The role of reason in politics is to understand the texts of revelation and apply them to contemporary issues, not to invent "laws" independently under the pretext of public interest (maslaha). Any system that bases the legitimacy of laws on human will (whether a king, party, or parliament) independent of Allah's ruling is a false and oppressive system, no matter how well it is formulated.

The obligation to establish Islamic authority (the caliphate): Establishing the comprehensive caliphate is not merely a historical option or a worldly means; rather, it is a religious obligation necessitated by the implementation of most Islamic rulings. The definitive legal evidence (from the Qur'an, Sunnah, and consensus) has indicated the necessity of appointing a single imam and the prohibition of Muslims being divided into warring states and parties. The fall of the caliphate has led to the loss of rulings, the suspension of penalties, and the invasion of enemies into the lands of Muslims, and there can be no true revival of the Ummah without resuming this great obligation.

Sharia politics achieves justice and prevents corruption: When the reference for governance is the Qur'an and Sunnah, comprehensive justice is achieved, and interests are regulated; because the Sharia weighs benefits and harms with the scale of divine wisdom, not human whims. Historical experience has proven that the era of the Ummah's prosperity is linked to its rulers' adherence to the Sharia, and that periods of oppression and decline have accompanied the distancing from the Sharia and the recourse to imported laws or personal whims. In contemporary reality, whenever Allah's ruling is applied in even a small aspect, we see its blessings, and whenever it is replaced by the rule of ignorance, misery ensues (such as the rise in crime when penalties were suspended, and the spread of usury that destroyed wealth… etc.).

The space for reason and creativity is preserved under the Sharia: Contrary to malicious propaganda, adherence to the Sharia does not mean rigidity and stagnation. The Sharia has left a wide space for intellectual reasoning in means, as we have explained. The Muslim state is required to develop administrative systems, enhance the economy, and invest in modern sciences – all of which is permissible and required as long as it is within the framework of the objectives of the Sharia and beneficial to the Ummah. The Sharia did not specify the types of weapons in the army, the forms of currency, or the programming languages used in data management! Rather, it commanded us to prepare and organize and gave us freedom in matters other than the prohibited. Thus, Sharia politics combines the stability of goals with the flexibility of methods, making it adaptable to development and compatible with every era while adhering to the constants.
