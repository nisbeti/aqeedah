In this regard mentioned above about conviction, every conviction is a concept or a measure, but not every concept or measure is a conviction, because the concept and measure that have not taken root in the souls and in society do not reach the level of conviction even if they are believed in. That is, they do not reach a degree of firmness, concentration, and stability such that they can be termed convictions. This, of course, does not mean that people are not convinced of them in the linguistic sense of conviction, as they believe in them... rather, they do not fulfill the description of convictions in the technical sense even though they have become a concept and a measure.

In summary: If the idea is secondary and belief in it is established, it is a concept. If it is an idea upon which other secondary ideas are built and belief in it is established, this concept becomes a measure. And if the concept and measure take root in the souls and in society and the nation, they become convictions(379)."
