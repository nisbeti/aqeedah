Raghib al-Isfahani said: "Submission (taw') is compliance, and its opposite is aversion (karh). Allah, Exalted is He, said, ﴾Come willingly or unwillingly﴿, and ﴾To Him has submitted whoever is in the heavens and the earth, willingly or unwillingly﴿. Submission is similar, but it is most often used in the context of obeying commands and adhering to what has been prescribed; Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾Obedience and a good word﴿, meaning: obey. And he has submitted to Him, submits (yata'aw), and he obeys him, he obeys (yuti'u)"(200). Submission is compliance and following easily, and its opposite is aversion, as it is accompanied by hardship and the refusal of the self. Since obedience necessitates compliance with the command, it requires: first, a return to Allah and the Messenger to know the ruling in it, and second, the execution of that command, meaning proceeding with it and acting according to it, which we call following. Thus, obedience is also: following, and it can only be to a being regarding what issues from it in terms of command and prohibition. Obedience necessitates two entities: a commander and an obedient one, and it necessitates the existence of a command whose obedience is sought. Obedience is borrowed for acting upon the command of the commander, as the sign of obedience is compliance, and compliance is to the command and prohibition, that is, to the message.

And His saying, Exalted is He, ﴾But if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger﴿ also means: refer it, meaning seek His ruling regarding the command and prohibition of Allah and His Messenger, and understand those commands and prohibitions and act accordingly, and obey Allah and His Messenger in them. The close relationship between following and obedience cannot be severed, so why is there repetition, and what is its benefit?

"Indeed, His saying, Exalted is He, ﴾But if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger﴿ indicates matters whose rulings are mentioned in the Book and the Sunnah, and it is derived and based on His saying, ﴾Obey Allah and obey the Messenger﴿. It indicates matters that are built upon what is mentioned therein due to their connection with what has been mentioned by a common causal link between them. This is because His saying, ﴾But if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger﴿ becomes a repetition without benefit if it is a reiteration of the command to ﴾Obey Allah and obey the Messenger﴿. Thus, the reference when disputing is to what is not in the Book and the Sunnah, which is consensus (ijma') and analogy (qiyas)..." meaning either your disagreement is about something whose ruling is explicitly stated in the Book or the Sunnah or consensus, in which case direct obedience is required because it is a direct command issued by Allah, Exalted is He, or it is about something not explicitly stated in these three, in which case we need a connection that links it directly to Allah and His Messenger, which necessitates obedience. This is what is done through ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning), and from it is analogy, which connects two matters due to a common cause between them. The jurist establishes the ruling of the original cause on the branch that shares the same cause, thus the branch takes the ruling of the original as a legal analogy, making the ruling of the branch a legal ruling linked to the Qur'an and the Sunnah, necessitating obedience to Allah and the Messenger. That is, the matters that are causally related require deduction to link them to the direct texts to give them the ruling of obligatory obedience.

Thus, the phrase ﴾But if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger﴿ is not merely a repetition of what has preceded in the phrase ﴾O you who have believed, obey Allah and obey the Messenger﴿. The benefit that comes with the command to refer when disputing, which it adds to the command of obedience from the outset, is to give the derived legal ruling through analogy the status of being a legal ruling that is obligatory to obey and follow, just like the ruling of the original that is issued directly in the Book or the Sunnah. Otherwise, the command to refer when disputing would be a repetition without benefit.

"It is necessary that the intended meaning is to refer the ruling to the rulings explicitly stated in similar occurrences, and that is analogy, thus it is established that the verse indicates the command of analogy... This verse indicates that Allah, Exalted is He, has divided occurrences into two categories: some of which have their ruling explicitly stated, and some of which do not. Then He commanded in the first category obedience and compliance, and in the second category, He commanded to refer to Allah and the Messenger."
