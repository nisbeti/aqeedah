Proving that practical legal rulings (ahkam shar'iyya) may be based on conjecture:

First: The Qur'an has outlined for us a clear and evident methodology regarding the nature of the system that will organize our lives, transactions, and conduct. The foundational principles of these legal rulings have been established with certainty, meaning it has been definitively established that Allah, Exalted is He, has commanded us and prohibited us.

And it is certainly established that Allah has commanded us not to speak in matters of belief except with knowledge, certainty, and conviction.

And He, Exalted is He, also commanded us to follow the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) in what he conveyed from his Lord. Whatever news reached us that is speculative in its authenticity or its implication, we must follow it based on the preponderance of evidence that it is indeed the ruling of Allah. This is the path that distinguishes between beliefs, which are based solely on certainty, and practical legal rulings, which are based on the preponderance of evidence, some of which are based on knowledge established by definitive proof, such as the prohibition of usury and the obligation of prayer. I say, these rulings must be followed according to what is most likely to be the ruling of Allah in them, and this is the ruling of Allah that is established with certainty in this matter.

It has been firmly established that the Qur'an contains ambiguous verses (mutashabihat), and that its rulings include both definitive (qat'i) and speculative (dhanni) indications. These are obligatory commands, whether they are definitive or speculative, equally so. In fact, the Noble Qur'an has made the speculative a specification for the definitive. The sanctity of life and the prohibition of killing it are definitive, as is the sanctity of wealth, and a judge may rule their permissibility based on the testimony of individuals, which is a speculative testimony without dispute(175).

Secondly: The Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) sent twelve messengers to twelve kings inviting them to Islam, and each messenger was one in the region to which he was sent. If conveying the message were not obligatory to follow based on a single report, the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) would not have sufficed with one messenger. A single report is speculative; however, in this particular case, there is almost a consensus on its speculative nature if we apply the ruling to this incident and weigh it according to the standards of the scholars. This is because it is a single report informing those to whom it was sent about Islam, meaning it is not mutawatir (consecutively transmitted) in wording, nor mutawatir in meaning (with respect to those it was sent to). The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) would not establish proof against anyone regarding matters of belief—having been prohibited by Allah, Exalted is He, from doing so—based on speculative evidence. For them, this messenger is unknown in terms of justice, and the subject of his report is unknown, and thus the report as a whole and the narrator do not provide anything but speculation to those to whom it was sent. This serves as evidence that this proof and this issue are not in matters of belief, due to the prohibition of using speculative evidence in them, as previously mentioned. It was also evidence that speculative proof is sufficient in legal rulings; here, it is a matter related to conveying the message, not establishing proof regarding belief.

Imam al-Shafi'i (may Allah have mercy on him) said regarding a similar matter: "He has sought in them what he sought in his leaders, that they should be known, so he sent to the region where he is known. If the one sent to was unaware of the messenger, it would be upon him to seek knowledge that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) sent in order to clarify his doubt regarding the news of the messenger, and it would be upon the messenger to wait until he clarifies it for the one sent to."(176)

Thirdly: The Sharia considers the testimony of singular reports (akhbar al-ahad), which are speculative reports in establishing claims in court. Imam Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani said: "A singular report, if it is authentic or good, is considered a proof in all legal rulings and must be acted upon, whether the rulings pertain to acts of worship, transactions, or punishments.
