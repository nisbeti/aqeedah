Uthman (may Allah be pleased with him) when he ordered the burning of the copies of the Qur'an that differed from the dialect of Quraysh and prohibited their circulation to prevent the harm resulting from division and disagreement among Muslims because of that, as well as Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) obliging the senior Companions to remain in Medina and preventing them from leaving to settle in the provinces to avoid the harm that would result from the capital of the state being devoid of those qualified for ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning), fatwa (legal opinion), and matters of governance, who are needed when calamities arise, and to seek their opinion in managing the state. However, this requires verifying that the action leads to what is prohibited based on strong presumption, for not everything that may lead to corruption is prohibited merely based on the possibility.
