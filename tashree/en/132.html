I say what I understand from the term kلالة (kalaala) in the verse. Kلالة in the Arabic language refers to three: to one who leaves neither a child nor a parent, to one who is neither a child nor a parent of the deceased, and to relatives from other than the line of child and parent. So which of these meanings applies to kلالة in the verse? Abu Bakr understood one meaning from these three meanings from the saying of Allah, Exalted is He: ﴾And if a man leaves a kalalah or a woman﴿, so kلالة is the subject of the verb "was," meaning if a man is a kلالة, he inherits. He may have also understood this from the second verse, which is: ﴾Say, Allah gives you Judgment concerning kalalah if a man perishes and he has no child﴿, and from the hadith narrated regarding the reason for the revelation of the verse: ﴾Say, Allah gives you Judgment﴿, for it is narrated that the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) visited Jabir ibn Abdullah when he was ill. He said: "I am a kلالة, so what should I do with my wealth?" Then the verse ﴾If a man perishes﴿ was revealed. Narrated by Muslim. Thus, the opinion expressed by Abu Bakr is an ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) and not a personal opinion.

And from that is the ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) of Abu Bakr, who gave inheritance to the mother of the mother over the mother of the father. Some of the Ansar said, "A woman has inherited from a deceased person, and if she were the deceased, she would not have inherited from her." And she left a woman, and if she were the deceased, she would inherit all that she left behind, so it returned to sharing between them. And from that is his ruling on equalizing the stipends, as Umar said, "Do not treat one who has left his home and wealth to migrate to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) like one who entered Islam under compulsion." Abu Bakr said, "They only embraced Islam for the sake of Allah, and their reward is with Allah, and this world is merely a provision." And from that is Umar's statement, "I will judge regarding the grandfather - the father of the father - based on my opinion, and I will express my opinion regarding him," meaning according to my understanding that I derived from the texts.(233)

And Malik ibn Anas said: "Allah revealed His Book and left a place for the Sunnah of His Prophet, and His Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) established the Sunnahs, and left a place for opinion and analogy."

In a decisive and eloquent statement regarding the issue, Ibn Hazm (may Allah have mercy on him) said—negating that what is meant by opinion here is for the jurist to bring forth the opinion of his mind to become a ruling in the matter; rather, what is meant is his exertion (ijtihad) in the texts by applying analogy (qiyas) in those rulings—:

"And also, if [the hadith of Mu'adh] were authentic, it would not be free from one of two possibilities: either it is specific to Mu'adh alone, in which case they must not follow anyone's opinion except Mu'adh's, which they do not claim. Or it is applicable to Mu'adh and others; if that is the case, then everyone who exercises their opinion has done what they are commanded to do. Since this is the case, everyone who does what they are commanded is correct, and none of them is more entitled to correctness than another. Thus, the truth in this matter lies in contradictions, which is contrary to their claim and contrary to reason, rather this is a clear impossibility. At that point, no one has the right to support their opinion with evidence, because their opponent has also exercised their opinion, and there is nothing in the hadith they rely upon other than the exercise of opinion, and nothing more. Therefore, it is not permissible for them to add any preference that was not mentioned in the hadith. Furthermore, no one is more entitled than another in this regard, so each one of us can exercise their opinion; those who follow are not more entitled than others. It is a clear impossibility that what the ignorant believe about the hadith of Mu'adh—if it were authentic—would allow Mu'adh to permit or prohibit based on his opinion, to establish obligations based on his opinion, and to nullify them based on his opinion. This is something no Muslim would believe, and there is nothing in the Sharia other than what we have mentioned, absolutely."

If I were to add to what Ibn Hazm (may Allah have mercy on him) said, I would say: Which of these opinions that have been attributed the quality of legitimacy will represent the judgment of Allah that He will punish those who oppose it? ﴾So whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it﴿ [Az-Zalzalah 7-8].
