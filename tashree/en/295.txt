And to achieve this, the legislator has relied on linking the laws to the pleasure of Allah, Exalted is He, as they are His commands and prohibitions, and on the concept of making the lawful and unlawful a measure for actions. He has surrounded them with the framework of accountability in this world and the Hereafter, in addition to reinforcing this with concepts such as piety (taqwa) and benevolence (ihsan). Furthermore, the wise legislator has enhanced the inclination of individuals, communities, and the state towards the application and implementation of the rulings by relying on the concept of responsibilities, which are built on the foundation of commitment, proper application, obligation, and accountability, as well as capability, through the aspect of facilitation and alleviation of hardship, and on the concept of guardianship (wilaya) among believers, and the rights that stem from it, and the realization of solidarity, integration, and balance in society. This is alongside the concept of rights, the rights of Allah (referred to as: public rights), and the rights of individuals(437), and the shared right: which is where both rights converge, through a balance between individual rights and the public interest, and public rights, a matter that has led to their belief in the justice of the perspective towards them, and their fairness; there is no favoritism, no loss of rights, and no oppression of the weak by the strong. Among the duties (roles) (for example, the role of women as mothers, wives, homemakers, and their role in public life), and obligations (responsibility for compensation for harmful actions, endowments, and maintenance), and regulations for resolving disputes (contentions), and trusts, for the selection of the ruler is a trust, and fulfilling trusts to their rightful owners is a realization of rights, and the resolution of disputes, and the establishment of justice, and the alleviation of oppression, and security and safety. Among the pastoral characteristics of the Sharia, it is not for taxation, and it works on health care, education, spreading the call, and preserving the principles upon which the state was founded, along with its socio-economic philosophy, with all its foundations and components aimed at building society according to a system that guarantees the fulfillment of basic needs.
Muslims have accepted only that the system they follow should be derived from revelation, meaning that its detailed rulings should be extracted from the general evidence. Therefore, its creed and the system derived from it represent a perspective on life, in relation to worldly life, and this political perspective is based on and measured by: the principle of lawful and unlawful, not utilitarianism, and its method: adherence to the legal ruling. A Muslim takes this principle as the basis for his actions and relationships, "and thus it is imperative for a Muslim to recognize his connection to Allah when performing actions, so he directs his actions according to Allah's commands and prohibitions"(438). The principles and ideas upon which sound governance is based are not mere words to be read, nor theoretical ideas to be spoken, but rather a specific perspective on life, and a specific way of living, so it must be more precious in the hearts of the nation than the lives of its children, and souls must be sacrificed in defense of it and in protection of it. There are no principles and ideas that prepare the nation to sacrifice lives, souls, children, parents, self, and wealth for it like the Book and the Sunnah, and therefore the Book and the Sunnah must be the foundation upon which governance is established.

(437) The rights of individuals are those rights that pertain to individuals and do not involve the public system except in organizing their resolution and realizing the rights raised in court. The rights of the individual refer to those related to his religion, self (blood, honor, and dignity), mind, lineage, and wealth, protecting the personal interests of the individual without the collective, whether the right is public, such as preserving health, children, and wealth (the sanctity of others' property), achieving security, suppressing crime, repelling aggression, and enjoying the public facilities of the state; or whether the right is private, such as the owner's right to his property, the seller's right to receive the price, and the buyer's right to possess the sold item, the right of a person to compensation for his damaged property, the return of usurped property, blood money, the wife's right to maintenance from her husband, the mother's right to custody of her child, the father's right to guardianship over his children, the individual's right to work, and the right of preemption. The right of ownership, the right of usufruct, the right of specialization, and the right of attachment for the fulfillment of a right (pledge). (Islamic Jurisprudence and Its Evidence: Al-Zuhaili. With significant modifications).
(438) The System of Islam, Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani, section: Intellectual Leadership.
