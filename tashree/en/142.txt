The angle through which Islam addressed human actions and behavior:
Thus, problems and needs arise for the individual due to the presence of vital energy within him, manifested in instincts and organic needs, driving him to perform actions. These problems and needs require treatment to clarify the position that must be taken regarding them, in what way they are satisfied, and what ruling regulates behavior? Needs, interests, and problems arise for the individual as a result of his interaction with others in society, which requires organization, and interests arise for the state in which he lives that need to be legislated.
Professor Mustafa al-Zarqa (may Allah have mercy on him) classified relationships in the following manner: First: the rulings related to the worship of Allah, such as prayer, fasting, and others, called: acts of worship. Second: the rulings related to the family, such as marriage, divorce, lineage, maintenance, wills, and inheritance, which are termed in legal terminology: personal status. Third: the rulings related to people's economic activities and their dealings with each other regarding money and rights, their transactions, and the resolution of their disputes through the judiciary, called: transactions. From these two categories (the second and third) is composed what is called in modern legal terminology: civil law. Fourth: the rules related to the authority of the ruler over the subjects, and the rights and duties that correspond between them, which jurists call: sultanic rulings, and this is also referred to as: legitimate politics. This fourth category includes two distinct types of rights in modern legal terminology, namely: administrative law and constitutional law. Fifth: the rulings and rules related to the punishment of criminals and the regulation of internal order among people, called: penalties. Sixth: the rules that organize the relationship of the Islamic state with other states, which constitute the system of peace and war, referred to in legal terminology as: international rights. Seventh: the rulings related to ethics, modesty, virtues, and the merits and demerits of behavior, called: etiquette. From the above, it is clear that Islamic law came to organize the affairs of religion and the world, and this is the meaning of the common expression in this era: Islam is a religion and a state. All these categories of rulings are derived from the texts of the Qur'an, which is the constitution of Islam (the Book), and from the hadith of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) that explain and clarify it (the Sunnah), and these are the two primary sources."(245)
