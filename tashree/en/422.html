The early Muslims divided the state into major provinces according to need, and now the modern state can adopt smaller divisions to improve administration, all of which falls within the bounds of Sharia. The only prohibition is to create entities that contest the authority of the state or become independent from it.

Consultation (shura) and political participation: Consultation is generally obligatory for the ruler (especially in critical public matters); this is a legal ruling supported by the actions of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) and the companions after him. Moreover, holding rulers accountable and enjoining good and forbidding evil in public affairs is a collective obligation (fard kifaya) upon the community. To achieve this, Sharia has established the right of the community to appoint a council or have representatives who listen to and convey its voice. The means: the form and mechanism of the consultative council are matters left to the ijtihad (independent reasoning) of Muslims according to what best ensures true representation. In the early days of Islam, the people of Badr, the emigrants (muhajirun), and the helpers (ansar) in the mosque were the reference for consultation. Today, fair general elections can be held to select representatives from scholars and experts from each region. This method is not democracy in the sense of absolute popular legislation, but rather an Islamic organization for selecting those in charge of decision-making and expanding the base of consultation and accountability. The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said to those who came to pledge allegiance to him at al-Aqaba: “Bring forth your leaders,” meaning bring forth those who represent you to give the pledge, for the pledge represents the opinion that those who give it can make prevail in society and its relations, so that it is not thought of as a matter of democracy. The details of election laws, campaigning, and districts are to be established in a way that achieves the principle of consultation without contradiction.

Financial and economic policy: Sharia has come with a comprehensive economic system: it has preserved ownership in its various forms (individual, state, public), prohibited illicit sources of income such as usury (riba) and gambling (maysir), mandated zakat and spending, and laid down detailed rulings for sales and contracts to ensure justice. It has also imposed on the state the responsibility of providing for the basic needs of the subjects and not collecting taxes except by right ... etc. All of these are legal rulings that regulate economic life. However, there remains a large space for reason in managing the economy and achieving development according to "economic science": such as establishing agricultural and industrial policies to enhance production, for Islam did not specify for Muslims what to plant or manufacture, but rather told them in general to eat from the good things and cultivate the earth. Likewise, trade and market organization, and financial policies (such as budgeting and resource allocation), all of these decisions are rational based on economic expertise, provided they do not conflict with legal rulings (for example: not financing what is prohibited, or preventing a mandatory right).

These practical examples confirm what has been previously stated: Sharia has granted us strict principles in governance and politics, but it has also provided us with various means to achieve these principles. It is the duty of Muslims today to gain knowledge in these details to establish the foundation of a sound Islamic political system: one that adheres to the definitive texts and strives in the speculative matters to achieve the interests of the servants.

This does not mean that implementation is easy without challenges; rather, it is subject to the level of awareness of the community, the sincerity of those in charge, and the efficiency of institutional ijtihad. However, there is certainty that following the prophetic methodology in politics is the only path to true revival, and that every other system that has been tried has only increased the community's division and weakness despite the allure of its slogans.
