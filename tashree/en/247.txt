If we conduct a comparison between human systems and legislations in what is called the legal state, and Islamic legislation through examining the following aspects, and then studying the main differences between them:
Do legislations reproduce society from the perspective of law, such that the state system is built upon the viewpoints of legislators, legal scholars, and controllers who shape societal relations according to their visions, interests, and ever-changing legal theories? Or do legislations and the state system governed by the government reproduce law from the perspective of society, such that legislation and systems represent what society accepts in terms of concepts, standards, and convictions? In other words, is the actual authority of the nation granted to the state, or is it imposed by the state upon the nation?
The ability of the system (laws, regulations, and legislations) to create, achieve, shape, or influence:
In convictions and concepts that guide a person in the struggles of life, there is a reflection on individuals' behavior and on society and the state through relationships;
Finding specific values (measures and higher societal objectives) that are codified and linked to people's lives transforms into societal norms that represent them, elevating humanity when applied, such as justice, fairness, security, the preservation of life, and others?
The extent to which legislation and laws correspond to the vital interests of society, and their ability to create harmony among the components of society, is significant. The validity of the law itself depends on its balance among the different components of society and its preservation of the vital interests within it. When the law no longer aligns with the vital interests of society, it becomes, as Najib Bouderbala says, "legitimate to abandon its implementation and application."
