Contradiction and error, and the intellect, if it exits this circle, requires tools to fortify it to remove from its judgment the attribute of fabrication or error. It is limited by its need for prior information that explains reality, so it cannot generate ideas or judgments except in the presence of that prior information.
It is constrained by its need for a standard to judge the correctness of ideas and the truth of their results, and their degree of strength, which prevents it from fallacies in facts, and margins that clarify to it the correct influences that should affect its judgment (such as prior information about the reality to be explained), and others that it must exclude when issuing a judgment, so that they do not affect its integrity (such as previous opinions it holds that may dominate the judgment and corrupt it, and such as sentiment, and the time and place in the example of legislation), and limits beyond which it may fall into the prohibited, resulting in knowledge that may not necessarily be correct, such as inquiry into what cannot be perceived by the senses or its effects: (metaphysics). Therefore, it needs to adhere to a correct method of thinking about the supernatural to reach correct conclusions(482).
Upon examining with an enlightened and scrutinizing perspective, we find that when the intellect searches for the evidence that fills the corners of the universe, it enters the circle in which it can judge the truth and correctness and definiteness of its results, and it has prior information, and axioms and fundamentals to refer to that elevate its research to the level of certainty and definiteness, and truth in conformity with the reality being investigated, or keeps it within the realm of conjecture, or judges it to be false. Therefore, it is able to issue judgments in matters of belief, such as investigating the existence of the Creator and the correctness of the connection between the Creator and the universe and humanity, whether the connection is one of organization, creation, or the connection of commands and prohibitions through the messengers.
Whereas when it enters the circle related to issuing judgments on actions, interests, consequences, good and evil, beauty and ugliness, right and wrong, praise and blame, reward and punishment, justice and injustice, it possesses some tools (such as understanding the nature of reality), but it lacks some other necessary ones for this inquiry (such as the future consequences of this reality, whether this reality will be good or bad for it, whether it contains its interest or harm? And does the interest lie in material benefit, for example? Or in moral, humanitarian, or spiritual value? And so on, these are keys that the intellect cannot unlock). Therefore, it must refrain from issuing judgments and legislations due to its lack of those characteristics and information necessary to carry out the intellectual process that results in judgment, legislation, and codification. Thus, when a person allows himself to legislate, he is not assigning his intellect the task of legislation, but rather giving it to his whims that drive him to delve into matters where the intellect cannot utter a word, and to his inclinations that suggest to him that the interest is whatever aligns with them. When we expect the intellect to legislate, because it is one of the greatest tools and most wondrous creatures created by Allah, we are like someone who expects a perfectly beautiful and precise car to fly in the air, without possessing the tools required for the process of flying.
In order to assess the intellect's ability to legislate, it is necessary to conduct a study that demonstrates that the intellect (or the legislator) possesses certain characteristics first so that it can legislate and issue judgments.
