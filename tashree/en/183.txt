The liberal perspective, which considers society to be composed of individuals, overlooks the fact that society is not merely made up of individuals, as we will clarify - if Allah wills - in the chapter: "Organizing the Public Interest (maslaha) to Regulate Permanent Relationships." Since individuals engage in actions primarily to attain a benefit for themselves or to avert harm from themselves, relationships are likewise based on the pursuit of benefit or the prevention of harm. Therefore, it is essential for a person to have a system that organizes human relationships in a way that ensures the attainment of benefit and the prevention of harm; these are the foundations upon which all systems and laws must be established(287). [According to what the Sharia has defined as benefit and harm, benefit is not merely what a person desires or what is advantageous to them, nor is harm simply what a person dislikes or what is detrimental to them, especially since a person does not live in isolation within society. What Qais sees as a benefit, Amr may see as harm, and there must be a relationship between Qais and Amr. Thus, there must be an authority that defines benefit and harm for both of them, which, as has been clarified, is the Sharia.]
But the Sharia (law) is broader than to be confined within the framework of organizing the attainment of benefits and the prevention of harms; rather, it has broader objectives and purposes that require contemplation and reflection. Among these, for example:
Essential necessary interests that a person cannot do without for their survival, and they have societal dimensions, such as food and drink, which require societal organization like agriculture, trade, distribution, and food security. The need for housing requires societal organization such as construction, rental, ownership, legal relations, borrowing, and repayment. The need related to health requires societal organization through medicine, hospitals, and medical service providers. The need for security requires societal organization through police, judiciary, and public order. The need for marriage and procreation to preserve the human species requires societal organization through family systems, lineage, marriage, guardianship, and so on.
Then come the needs that facilitate living and alleviate hardship and difficulty, including work and earning a livelihood to meet needs, which require societal organization related to market relations, contracts, wages, and the like. There is also the need for transportation for ease of movement, and its societal organization is represented in transportation means and infrastructure. The need for education is essential for skill development and understanding, and its societal organization is through schools, teachers, and universities. Additionally, there is the need for social welfare to support the needy, and its societal organization is through systems of zakat, charity, mutual support, expiations, and others.
Then come the enhancement interests (masalih tahsiniyya) that make human life more humane, dignified, prosperous, and beautiful. Among them is beautiful clothing, for the purpose of covering and for public taste, and covering the private parts. Their societal organization is represented by the craft of tailoring and production, as well as custom. Among them are manners and taste for good interaction, organized through the language of communication, politeness, appreciation, and similar aspects.
However, when we establish a methodological understanding in dealing with the Sharia, we must start from foundational methodological premises that respect the authority of the text, the limits of reason, and the role of the human being as a responsible agent (mukallaf) and not as a legislator!
