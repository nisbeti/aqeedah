Secondly: Rules and Evidence, General and Detailed Definitions:
Indeed, Islamic legislation (nass) applies to all of human actions that are renewed and varied, regardless of their diversity and differences, as it came in the form of words that have specific meanings, and it used clarification to convey the intended meaning, and it established them in the form of rules and evidence and also general and detailed definitions. Here is the elaboration:
The term (text) is divided into three sections(262):
First: the division of the term based on the meaning(263) alone, which includes the meanings of correspondence, implication, and entailment. This grants the text a tremendous capacity for application, or gives the jurist the ability to derive many rulings from the text due to its inclusion of these meanings. At the same time, it regulates the process of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) with controls and rules for understanding these meanings, so that the jurist does not stray outside this framework(264).
Secondly: The division of the word in terms of the signifier and the signified includes the distinct, the singular, the shared, the transferred, the literal, and the metaphorical,
This gives the text the immense ability to encompass many rulings indicated by the usages of the language.
Thirdly: The division of the word in terms of its meaning alone, and under this category falls the general and specific word,
The referent may be a word or a meaning, and in this research, the intended referent is the meaning. Thus, the division of the word into general and specific is a division of the meaning of the word.

(263) The indications of words are of three types: the indication of conformity (dilalat al-mutatabiqah), the indication of inclusion (dilalat al-tadamun), and the indication of commitment (dilalat al-iltizam). The indication of conformity and the indication of inclusion relate to the forms and structural compositions of sentences, while the indication of commitment pertains to the discourse and its implications, including its underlying reasons, the implied meaning of the discourse, or the tone of the discourse, which is the concept of agreement, or the evidence of the discourse, which is the concept of disagreement. The indication of commitment has five categories: the first is the indication of necessity, the second is the indication of the word, the third is the indication and hint, the fourth is understanding the unspoken from the spoken based on the context of the speech and its intended meaning, and the fifth is the concept, which is called the evidence of the discourse. The indication of conformity is the indication of the word on the complete meaning for which the word is designated, such as the indication of "human" on its meaning, which is the speaking animal. The indication of inclusion is the indication of the word on part of the meaning for which the word is designated, such as the indication of "human" on the animal or the speaking one. The indication of commitment is the indication of the word on the necessary meaning for which the word is designated.
Refer to: The Argumentation with Probable Evidence in Creed by Professor Fathi Salim, Chapter: The Indications and Their Types.
(264) For example: among the types of the indication of entailment (dilalat al-iqtida) is that which is not indicated by the wording and is not explicitly stated, but is a necessity of the wording. This is either in terms of the impossibility of the speaker being truthful except through it, or in terms of the impossibility of the existence of the stated (malfuz) legally except through it, or in terms of the impossibility of its establishment rationally except through it,
As for what necessitates the truthfulness of the speaker, it is like his saying (peace and blessings be upon him): "There is no fasting for one who does not intend to fast from the night." This is because it negates fasting. Fasting is not negated in its form, but rather its meaning: there is no valid or complete fasting, so it is the ruling of fasting that is negated, not the fasting itself. The ruling is not explicitly stated, but it is necessary to achieve the truthfulness of the statement. An example of this is his saying: "There is no action except with intention." And his saying (peace and blessings be upon him): "Mistakes and forgetfulness have been lifted from my nation..." And his saying (peace and blessings be upon him): "There is no prayer for the neighbor of the mosque except in the mosque."
As for what necessitates the establishment of the explicit statement (manṭūq) in Sharia, it is like the saying of someone: "Free your slave on my behalf." This implies ownership, and this is a consequence of the wording rather than the explicit statement itself.
As for what is established by implication for the understanding of the explicit meaning rationally, it is like His saying, Exalted is He: ﴾Prohibited to you are your mothers﴿, because mothers refer to entities, and rulings do not pertain to entities, nor can their relation be understood except in terms of actions, thus the word implies an action, which is intercourse. Likewise, His saying, Exalted is He: ﴾Prohibited to you are carrion and blood﴿, and His saying: ﴾Permitted to you are the beasts of grazing livestock﴿, and similar to it is ﴾And ask the town﴿, meaning the people of the town. And His saying: ﴾So whoever among you is ill or on a journey, then an equal number of other days﴿, meaning: so he should break his fast, and an equal number of other days. This is what is called the indication of implication. Therefore, the jurist (mujtahid) can exercise his intellect in understanding the text to extract from it the rulings indicated by those words.
