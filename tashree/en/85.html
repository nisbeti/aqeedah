The minimum number for a group is three, so it is valid for a group emerging from any sect to have fewer than three members. If the group gains knowledge of the religion and has something to warn their people with when they return from the expedition of jihad, the legal ruling is that the sect must heed the warning of the group. This means that the statement of the group (which can be fewer than three) is authoritative, which is what is required in analogy to fatwa and testimony."(180) The Companions also unanimously agreed to act upon the reports of the singular narrators, even though they are of uncertain authenticity(181). This verse confirms the validity of their practice, for the group that was commanded to warn is made up of individuals, and it is established that their report, which they used to warn their people, is also of uncertain authenticity regarding their people. The legislator commanded that their warnings be acted upon and considered it (i.e., their report) part of the religion, that is, part of revelation(182). ﴾So that they may gain understanding in religion﴿ according to the text of the verse, meaning to warn their people with what they have understood from the religion. This is sufficient to refute the suspicion that what is uncertain from the Sunnah is excluded from the realm of revelation. The report narrated from the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) is of uncertain authenticity, yet it is part of the religion. Those who narrate it from singular sources (when its authenticity is verified by following the rules of the sciences of hadith, and this distinction logically necessitates the belief that the report is revelation and Sunnah and is considered one of the detailed evidences (amarat) for the legal rulings) resemble the case of those whom Allah commanded in the Qur'an to be cautious based on their reports of what they have understood from the religion, even though the authenticity of the report regarding their people is uncertain as long as they are singular informants.

Seventh: Allah, Exalted is He, made the substance of the legal rulings (ahkam shar'iyya) from commands and prohibitions which:

He established the proof against the creatures with His legislation, ﴾And Allah would not lead a people astray after He has guided them until He makes clear to them what they should avoid. Indeed, Allah is Knowing of all things﴿.

And He will hold them accountable for the weight of a mustard seed of their deeds based on their commitment to it or their negligence regarding that commitment, ﴾So whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it﴿ [Az-Zalzalah 7-8].

And it is arranged that faith is merely a claim if one does not refer to it, ﴾But no, by your Lord, they will not believe until they make you judge in that which they dispute among themselves, and then find no discomfort in themselves from what you have judged and submit in [full, willing] submission﴿, and "what" is a form of generality, ﴾O you who have believed, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you. And if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger if you should believe in Allah and the Last Day. That is the best [way] and best in result﴿ [59 An-Nisa], and if the Sharia has not brought forth a ruling and a Muslim has taken a ruling that the Sharia has not brought, then he has judged by other than the Sharia, which is not permissible.

And the religion is described as complete, and the blessing as perfect, due to its inclusion within it, and the obligation is established upon following it.

Thus, what has been established as part of his Sunnah is included in revelation, as indicated by His saying ﴾Follow what has been revealed to you﴿, which serves as a comprehensive criterion in the definition, and the position of ﴾And do not follow﴿ serves as a prohibitive criterion in the definition. The command to follow the unlettered Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) is evidence of its inclusion in the definition, meaning it is evidence that his words, actions, commands, and prohibitions are from revelation, that is, from divine revelation. Therefore, when we combine this understanding with what we established earlier regarding the acceptance of warnings from individuals despite the uncertainty in their authenticity concerning their people, and the necessity of acting upon their warnings and adjudicating based on their testimonies, it is conclusively established that what they transmitted to us as Sunnah is indeed a transmission of revelation.
