Allah, Exalted is He, says: ﴾O you who have believed, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and do not invalidate your deeds﴿, ﴾Follow what has been revealed to you from your Lord and do not follow other than Him allies. Little do you remember﴿ [al-A'raf 3]. Allah, Exalted is He, has commanded to refer to Allah and the Messenger when disputing any matter: ﴾O you who have believed, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you. And if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger if you should believe in Allah and the Last Day. That is best and more suitable for final determination﴿ [an-Nisa 59], ﴾And whatever you disagree over - its ruling is to Allah﴿ [ash-Shura 10]. Adhering to the legal ruling is obligatory, whether its evidence is from the Qur'an or the Sunnah, and whether the Sunnah is a saying, action, or approval; adherence to it is mandatory, as Allah says: ﴾But no, by your Lord, they will not believe until they make you, [O Muhammad], judge concerning that over which they dispute among themselves and then find within themselves no discomfort from what you have judged and submit in [full, willing] submission﴿ [an-Nisa 65]. The word ﴾what﴿ in the phrase ﴾concerning that over which they dispute﴿ is a form of generality, encompassing all matters that arise among the believers. Referring to the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) after his death is referring to his Sunnah, and Allah says: ﴾And it is not for a believing man or a believing woman, when Allah and His Messenger have decreed a matter, that they should have any choice about their affair. And whoever disobeys Allah and His Messenger has certainly strayed into clear error﴿ [al-Ahzab 36]. The implication of Allah's statement here ﴾And it is not﴿, and the inclusion of ﴾was﴿ in the negation is a stronger indication of the negation of choice because the verb ﴾was﴿ indicates existence, meaning that its negation entails the negation of existence in its entirety(187). The word ﴾matter﴿ is the object of the verb ﴾decreed﴿, and the phrase ﴾when Allah and His Messenger have decreed a matter﴿ is a conditional clause, not a negated clause. Therefore, the word ﴾matter﴿ appears as an indefinite noun in the context of the condition, and thus it is general. Note that the negation is not for the clause ﴾when Allah and His Messenger have decreed a matter﴿, but the negation occurs in the phrase ﴾And it is not for a believing man or a believing woman﴿, which negates the choice for the believers on the condition that Allah and His Messenger decree a matter. It is well-known in legal theory that an indefinite noun, whether in the context of a condition or negation, is general, whether it is directly affected by the negation, as in "no one is standing," or is affected by its action, as in "no one stood," and whether the negator is "what," "not," or others(188). "For this reason, it is emphasized in opposition to that, as He said: ﴾And whoever disobeys Allah and His Messenger has certainly strayed into clear error﴿"(189). This is also an indication of the obligation to act upon the command, whether in speech or action, and Allah says: ﴾And whatever the Messenger has given you - take; and what he has forbidden you - refrain from. And fear Allah; indeed, Allah is severe in penalty﴿ [al-Hashr 7]. It is definitively established that Allah, Exalted is He, will hold accountable for an atom's weight of good or evil deeds, and that He, Blessed and Exalted, has not left people without commands or prohibitions regarding any matter related to their lives. Therefore, it is necessary for people to strive to understand the address of the legislator and to act upon it. Thus, the original principle for a Muslim is to understand the ruling of Allah through the address of the legislator, as the address is directed directly from the legislator to everyone, and it is not directed only to the jurists or scholars, but rather to all those who are legally accountable. It has thus become an obligation for the accountable individuals to understand this address so that they can act upon it, as it is impossible to act upon the address without understanding it. Therefore, deriving the ruling of Allah has become an obligation for all accountable individuals, meaning that ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) has become obligatory for all those who are legally accountable. Hence, it is the original principle for the accountable individual to derive the ruling of Allah for himself from the address of the legislator, as he is addressed by this address, which is the ruling of Allah.
