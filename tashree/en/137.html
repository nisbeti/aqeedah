Instincts and Organic Needs and Their Relation to Behavior:

The optimal way to legislate for humans, in order to regulate their behavior and relationships, is through understanding the motivations behind their behavior and relationships, and the influences that affect them. Since human behavior in life is based on their concepts, their behavior is necessarily linked to their concepts in an inseparable manner. Behavior consists of the actions that a person undertakes to satisfy their instincts or organic needs, and behavior is necessarily linked to inclinations in an organic manner. However, a person must guide their inclinations with their concepts, meaning they should regulate their behavior by determining the type of inclination towards the motivation: whether to embrace it or to turn away from it. Therefore, understanding instincts and organic needs is essential in order to comprehend the perspective from which the legislator approached the treatment of human problems!

Allah, Exalted is He, created humans and instilled in each individual certain characteristics that cannot be completely erased from this living being—resulting from the presence of the secret of life within them—that drive them to lean towards performing certain actions or refraining from others. Some of these needs must be satisfied (organic needs, such as sleep, food, and breathing), while others, if not satisfied, leave a person in a state of anxiety (such as instincts, which manifest as fear and sexual inclination). What is part of the essence of a human being and cannot be erased or suppressed is described as a characteristic, represented by instincts and organic needs. What can be erased or suppressed is referred to as a manifestation of this vital energy (such as the manifestation of fear or courage from the instinct of self-preservation).

Considering these characteristics and manifestations, we see that they can be divided into two categories based on their relationship to satisfaction and the stimulus that provokes them. Organic needs are stimulated from within when a person is hungry for sleep or food and drink, and so forth, which, if not fulfilled, could lead to death; thus, their satisfaction is inevitable, and their stimulation comes from within.

As for instincts, they are provoked by an external stimulus, and while their overall satisfaction is necessary, it means that a person must satisfy one manifestation of them at the expense of another manifestation (for example, fear and courage are manifestations of the instinct of self-preservation, so one may satisfy courage at the expense of fear, for instance). Instincts exist in the form of manifestations, which can be grouped into three categories: the instinct for species preservation, the instinct for survival, and the instinct for sanctification or religiosity. The satisfaction of these manifestations (fear, courage, inclination towards the opposite sex, maternal affection, etc.) is not inevitable, and failing to satisfy any of them does not lead to death; rather, some of these manifestations can be completely suppressed.

These three groups are:

1- The instinct for survival, which includes manifestations of fear, courage, love of possession, curiosity, love of homeland, love of kin, love of sovereignty, love of control, and others, all of which relate to the instinct for survival because these manifestations lead to actions that serve the survival of the individual.

2- The second type of these manifestations, such as sexual inclination, motherhood, fatherhood, love of children, compassion for others, and the inclination to help those in need, relates to the instinct for species preservation, as these manifestations lead to actions that serve the survival of the human species as a species, not as individuals.
