The Messenger ... and His saying, Exalted is He, ﴾If you dispute over something, refer it to Allah﴿ is explicit in that it is not permissible to resort to analogy (qiyas) except in the absence of the foundational texts, as the word "if" according to many people indicates a condition(201). This verse, then, indicates the obligation of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning), that is, referring to the Book, the Sunnah, consensus (ijma), and analogy to ensure the implementation of Allah's command and the command of His Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) in all matters of dispute.

"And He, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And the divorced women should wait for three menstrual periods﴿; and the term "menstrual period" (quru) linguistically refers to both menstruation and purity. According to the indication of necessity, which is what reason necessitates for the correctness of the utterance: the command to wait is a command for ijtihad regarding the meaning of quru; because the truth is one and does not multiply, and because the term is semantically ambiguous in a contradictory sense, it cannot be acted upon before determining its intended meaning, and determining its intended meaning can only be achieved through ijtihad. Thus, the ambiguous verses of the Qur'an in the realm of jurisprudence are evidence of the obligation of ijtihad according to the indication of necessity." "And He, Exalted is He, said: ﴾Today I have perfected for you your religion﴿, and He, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And We have sent down to you the Book as a clarification for all things﴿. Therefore, the Sharia is complete and comprehensive, and the observable reality is that many contemporary issues and new developments have no specific ruling. According to the indication of necessity, which is what reason necessitates for the necessity of the speaker's truthfulness, it is required to engage in ijtihad to derive the ruling for these contemporary issues and new developments from the Qur'an and the Sunnah; because the Sharia is complete and comprehensive(202), meaning that the realization of the completeness of the religion and the existence of clarification in the Book for everything depends on the existence of ijtihad and its ability to derive rulings for contemporary issues.

"It has been established that ijtihad is obligatory through multiple hadiths. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: 'If a judge makes an effort and is correct, he will have two rewards, and if he makes an effort and is wrong, he will have one reward.' This is agreed upon from the narration of Amr ibn al-As. And he (peace and blessings be upon him) said: 'A man who judges for the people out of ignorance is in the Fire.' This was narrated by the compilers of the Sunan, al-Hakim, and al-Tabarani, and its chain is authentic, emphasizing that the judge must be knowledgeable about what he judges. It is reported that the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) said to Ibn Mas'ud: 'Judge by the Book and the Sunnah when you find them; if you do not find the ruling in them, then exercise your opinion.' This was mentioned by al-Amidi in al-Ahkam and al-Razi in al-Mahsool. It is reported from him (peace and blessings be upon him) that he said to Mu'adh and Abu Musa al-Ash'ari when he sent them to Yemen: 'By what will you judge?' They said: 'If we do not find the ruling in the Book and the Sunnah, we will compare the matter to other matters, and whatever is closer to the truth, we will act upon it.' This was mentioned by al-Amidi in al-Ahkam and Abu al-Husayn in al-Mu'tamad. This analogy from them is an ijtihad to derive the ruling, [and not their personal opinion in any case, as we elaborated in the section: (Opinion and Jurisprudence)], and the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) approved of them for it. It is reported from him (peace and blessings be upon him) that he said to Mu'adh when he sent him as a governor to Yemen: 'How will you judge if a case arises for you?' He said: 'I will judge by the Book of Allah.' He said: 'And if there is nothing in the Book of Allah?' He said: 'Then by the Sunnah of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him).' He said: 'And if there is nothing in the Sunnah of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him)?' He said: 'I will exercise my opinion and will not fail.' He said: 'Then he struck my chest and said: Praise be to Allah who has guided the Messenger of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) to that which pleases His Messenger.' This was narrated by Ahmad, al-Tirmidhi, al-Darimi, and Abu Dawood. Al-Hafiz Ibn Kathir al-Basri authenticated it and said it is a good, well-known hadith relied upon by the Imams of Islam.

This is explicit in the approval of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) of Mu'adh's ijtihad. Moreover, knowledge of rulings is related and connected to ijtihad, such that it is impossible to comprehend and know the rulings without it, thus ijtihad has become obligatory; because the legal principle states: 'What is not completed without it is obligatory.'
