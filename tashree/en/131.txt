Opinion and Jurisprudence:
The school of Abu Hanifa is well-known as the School of Opinion (ra'y), due to their extensive derivation of issues based on opinion (ijtihad), within the guidelines set by Abu Hanifa's methodology, which trace back to the chief of the scholars of Iraq, the Companion Abdullah ibn Mas'ud (may Allah be pleased with him), and to some of the judgments of Ali ibn Abi Talib (may Allah be pleased with him)(229).
Imam al-Bazdawi says: "Our companions are the pioneers in this field, meaning the scholars of the Hanafi school, and they are the people of hadith and meanings. As for the meanings, the scholars have acknowledged them, so they called them the people of opinion, and opinion is a term for jurisprudence"(230).
However, many people—even some scholars—believe that the meaning of opinion is "the rational opinion of the mujtahid" when he does not find "a text" in the Book and the Sunnah, so he exerts his opinion, meaning the judgment of his intellect! They are mistaken in this, and the details are as follows:
Ahmad (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated from al-Harith ibn Amr, the nephew of al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba, from some people of Homs who were companions of Mu'adh ibn Jabal, that when the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) wanted to send Mu'adh to Yemen, he said: "How will you judge when a matter comes to you?" He said: "I will judge by the Book of Allah." He said: "And if you do not find in the Book of Allah?" He said: "Then by the Sunnah of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him)." He said: "And if you do not find in the Sunnah of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) nor in the Book of Allah?" He said: "I will exert my opinion and not hold back." The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) struck his chest and said: "Praise be to Allah who has guided the Messenger of His Messenger to what pleases the Messenger of Allah"(231). It has also been narrated from him (peace and blessings be upon him) that he said to Mu'adh and Abu Musa al-Ash'ari when he sent them to Yemen: "By what will you judge?" They said: "If we do not find the ruling in the Book and the Sunnah, we will compare the matter to other matters, and whatever is closer to the truth, we will act upon it." This comparison from them is an ijtihad in deriving the ruling, not a judgment of the intellect, and the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) approved of them. We have provided a detailed study(232) of this hadith, both in its text and chain of narration, in our book: Evidence of Belief, so refer to it.
Al-Daraqutni and then al-Bayhaqi narrated in their "Sunan" the letter of Umar to Abu Musa, in which he said: "Understand what is stirring in your heart, which has not reached you in the Book and the Sunnah. Know the similarities and examples, then compare matters accordingly, and act upon what is most beloved to Allah and most similar to the truth in your view." Al-Bayhaqi said: "And ijtihad is analogy (qiyas), and he narrated from a group of the Companions that they exercised ijtihad and made analogies. He narrated from Sufyan who said: Abdullah ibn Abi Yazeed told me, I heard Ibn Abbas say: When he was asked about something, if it was in the Book of Allah, he would rule by it, and if he did not find it, and it was in the Sunnah of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), he would rule by it. If he did not find it, and it was from Abu Bakr or Umar, he would rule by it. If he did not find it, he would exert his opinion." The chain of narration is authentic.
He narrated the hadith of Ibn Mas'ud regarding the story of Barwa' bint Washiq, in which he said: "I will give my opinion, and if it is correct, it is from Allah, and if it is wrong, it is from me." And the hadith of Abu Bakr regarding "al-Kalala": "I will give my opinion, and if it is correct, it is from Allah, and if it is wrong, it is from me and from the devil, and Allah is free from it; al-Kalala is what is besides the child and the parent."
