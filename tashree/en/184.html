However, this is not always the case; that is, the goals of legislation are not limited to interests in the human rational sense. Rather, there are aspects that the intellect cannot grasp their cause, nor the result that follows from their application within the framework of interests and objectives. So where is the interest in raising the finger during the testimony (tashahhud)? And what is the relationship between the objectives and the fact that the number of rak'ahs for the Asr prayer is four? Or in the rulings regarding prostration for forgetfulness (sujud al-sahu), and why is ablution (wudu) required after an event (hadath) and not after sweating and becoming dirty? Why is a woman not allowed to marry her aunt or maternal aunt? And why is the waiting period (iddah) for a widow four months and ten days? Or that for one who cannot find (the means) fasting three days during Hajj and seven when you return? And so on! The farthest from the system of interests and objectives is that the intellect itself is completely incapable of determining legislative interests by itself, unless it finds a "suitable" (i.e., a textual description that has a significant implication for the ruling, which can be analogized) at that point we can derive from it goals, wisdom, and objectives, and place it within the framework of necessary, need-based, and enhancement interests!

Not everything that Allah, Exalted is He, legislates is rationally justified; rather, the majority of rulings come devoid of explicit justification, lacking a significant context that the intellect can contemplate and derive from, or derive its wisdom and objectives.

Moreover, the purpose of legislation is also not limited to interests in the human rational sense; rather, it includes aspects that the intellect cannot grasp their cause, ﴾Fighting has been enjoined upon you while it is hateful to you﴿, ﴾And perhaps you hate a thing and it is good for you﴿. Thus, the intellect cannot determine legislative interests by itself, unless it finds a "suitable" (i.e., a textual description that has a significant implication for the ruling, which can be analogized).

As for the legislative goals and objectives, which in reality are more sublime and important than limiting them to mere interests, they include a category that consists of functional human organizational goals, related to satisfaction and the organization of relationships, and include:

All of these are life organizations, but they should not be understood as the "motivation for legislation," but rather as results that follow unless there is an explicit or suitable legal justification.

And there are worship-related obligations; the Sharia subjects the human being to the command of Allah, not to his desires, whims, and perceived benefits. There is no choice when Allah and His Messenger decree a matter, and the ruling is not contingent upon human approval and apparent interests, nor for the benefit that returns.

And there are goals related to the framework of purification and spiritual education; the Qur'an often mentions the mission of the Messenger in that he purifies them and teaches them the Book and wisdom.
