The necessary answer: All of this remains for us and upon us until the Day of Resurrection. It is affirmed that all the laws of Islam are complete, and the blessing of that upon us is perfect. This is a necessary and conclusive proof that everything the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said regarding religion, and in clarifying what we are obligated to do, is preserved and cannot be mixed with anything else that is not from him"(125) ended.
Indeed, a person is obligated, and the meaning of obligation (taklif) is to impose on the obligated person what the Sharia requires to be done or left, or the equivalence of doing and leaving, according to the conditions of obligation. ﴾So be steadfast as you have been commanded, and whoever has repented with you, and do not transgress. Indeed, He is All-Seer of what you do﴿ [Hud 112]. There is no ruling before the arrival of the Sharia; Al-Shawkani says in Irshad al-Fuhul (p. 6): "There is no disagreement that the ruler is the Sharia, and this is after the Prophethood and the call has reached." He also said in Irshad al-Fuhul, p. 14: "The intellect does not determine good or evil in the sense of legal obligation; rather, it is through it that a person knows what he must or must not seek in the Sharia." Al-Ghazali says in Al-Mustasfa (p. 40): "What is ruled upon is the action of the obligated person, and there is no ruling before the arrival of the Sharia, and the intellect is a means of knowing the ruling, not a ruler." Al-Ghazali also states in Al-Mustasfa, vol. 1, p. 61: "The legal ruling is the address of the Sharia related to the actions of the obligated, and the intellect has no role in its establishment." And Al-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said in Al-Majmu' Sharh al-Muhadhdhab, in the Book of Purification: "And the books of the school state that the origin of things before the arrival of the Sharia is permissibility or prohibition, or there is no ruling before the arrival of the Sharia? And there are three well-known opinions, the correct one among them according to the scholars is that there is no ruling before the arrival of the Sharia, and a person cannot be judged in anything he does with prohibition or hardship, and we do not call it permissible because the ruling of prohibition and permissibility are from the rulings of the Sharia, so how can this be claimed before the Sharia? Our school and the school of all the people of the Sunnah is that rulings are not established except by the Sharia, and that the intellect does not establish anything." Al-Shatibi, may Allah have mercy on him, said in Al-Muwafaqat 4/200: "The origin of actions before the arrival of the laws is the nullification of obligation, for there is no ruling upon him before knowing the ruling, as the condition of obligation according to the usulists is knowledge of what one is obligated to."(126). He said: "Rational proofs, when used in this science – that is, the principles of jurisprudence – are only used in conjunction with textual proofs, and the basis is only the textual proofs, for they are the origin... Thus, the primary reliance is on the legal proofs." Al-Muwafaqat, vol. 3, p. 53. Imam Al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam said in Qawa'id al-Ahkam fi Masalih al-Anam, vol. 1, p. 16: "Rational improvement and disapproval have no role in legal rulings, for the ruler is Allah, not the intellect. If the intellect had independence in improvement and prohibition, there would have been no need for the sending of messengers." This is a decisive statement and a compelling proof. Al-Amidi says in Al-Ihkam fi Usul al-Ahkam, vol. 1, p. 105: "The intellect does not impose anything on Allah nor prohibits anything upon Him, and the ruler in the Sharia is the Sharia, not the intellect." (126) Imam Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani, may Allah have mercy on him, in Al-Shakhsiyya al-Islamiyya, part three: (Usul al-Fiqh) believes that the origin of human actions after the Prophethood of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) is not permissibility, but rather adherence to what the Sharia has brought, because a person is obligated and accountable, and cannot be excused for neglecting adherence under the pretext of the absence of harm or corruption. He supports this with numerous verses that require returning to the Sharia: ﴾So if you disagree about anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger﴿, and ﴾And whatever you differ about, its ruling is with Allah﴿, as well as the hadith: "Every matter not in accordance with our command is rejected," which indicates that the origin is to seek the legal ruling, not to assume rationally. He also rejects the claim that the origin of actions is "to refrain from ruling," because refraining means suspending the Sharia, and the Quran and Sunnah command asking and searching for the ruling: ﴾So ask the people of knowledge if you do not know﴿, "Should they not ask when they do not know...". Thus, there must be a legal proof for the ruling, and it is not permissible to issue a ruling from the intellect without a text or proof. As for material matters, the origin in them is permissibility, because the texts have come generally permitting them all, such as His saying: ﴾He has subjected to you what is in the earth﴿, ﴾Eat from what is in the earth that is lawful and good﴿, ﴾And walk in its slopes and eat from His provision﴿. These generalities indicate the permissibility of everything unless a text is revealed prohibiting it, and thus, prohibition can only occur with an explicit text that specifies this permissibility, as in the prohibition of carrion, blood, and wine, which are exceptions to the origin of permissibility. In summary: there is no ruling before the arrival of the Sharia, and any ruling issued by the intellect without a legal proof is rejected.
