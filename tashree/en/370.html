Influential factors that are probable and necessary affecting behavior:

Our research, then, is in the ruling, the ruler, and the intended purpose of issuing the ruling, which is to determine a person's position towards an action: whether to do it, leave it, or be given the choice between doing it and leaving it. It also involves determining their position towards the things related to their actions: whether to take them, leave them, or be given the choice between taking and leaving; and this determination of their position depends on their perspective on the matter, whether it is good, bad, or neither good nor bad. We have also examined the validity of rulings and legislations for humans wherever they may be, and the potential influences that affect these rulings.

The research requires us to study these ideas (concepts) that have directed and regulated human behavior, which were based on a person's outlook on life (their principles or ideology) that has given the person a certain character and a specific intellectual foundation by which they regulate their behavior. What are the factors that directly influence these ideas alongside that outlook on life? Or let us ask which factors, if they influence the concepts that regulate human behavior (their concepts), necessitate that these concepts change or be modified, and thus change behavior? Consequently, we delve into matters that are thought to have an impact on behavior, such as place, environment, time, customs and traditions, knowledge, and technological, industrial, and urban advancement, to see their relationship with the change in rulings accordingly.

There are real or presumed influences and objectives that affect behavior; it is essential to know which of them should be considered and which do not affect behavior. Time and place, customs and traditions, norms, empirical science, modern technologies, urbanization, and the environment are all influences with varying effects on behavior. But the question is: Is it permissible to change behavior based on the influence coming from customs, for example? And is the correct action that the viewpoint derived from belief, and the system derived from it, is what regulates behavior and corrects the deviation of ideas resulting from customs and traditions (for example), being influential without being influenced by them, or the opposite?

As for place, plains, plateaus, mountains, and hills do not affect the ideas based on which satisfaction is achieved, because the classification of the required action as good or bad, as beautiful or ugly, and the arrangement of reward or punishment for it does not stem from the place, but from the mind or from the law. The effect of place is limited (as the nature of a rural person may differ from that of an urban one, and the vastness of nature may clarify vision and unleash the mind for thinking, but in the end, it either makes the determination of good and evil, beauty and ugliness, reward and punishment belong to the mind or to the law. Moreover, the rural or urban nature is not fixed; rather, it reflects the temperament of the person themselves: are they calm or irritable? Are they hateful or loving? All of this is easily changeable, and concepts are what change it and regulate behavior, and above all, it is not a criterion for right and wrong, for good or evil).

As for time, a person is a person, driven by their organic needs for sleep and food; thus, time has no effect on the principles upon which they build their behavior. (The wisdom that one acquires with the passage of age comes from exposure to beliefs, ideas, and experiences; it is not an effect of time itself, as a man may grow old without being wise and does not resemble his brother who has thought, read, and learned). Rather, what drives their behavior is their thoughts about things and their thoughts about life, which determine for them what is right and what is wrong.

We have elaborated on the impact of technological and civilizational advancement, which is usually associated with time, and it is thought that because of it, behavior and concepts must change in the section: "First: Addressing human problems as a human being," alongside many other sections, which we hope to review.
