How does a Muslim choose the school of thought (madhhab) to follow, or the scholar to consult?
We mentioned a moment ago that a lay follower (muqallid) should ask trustworthy scholars about a jurist (alim) who meets the conditions for issuing fatwas (legal opinions) for him to follow. As for choosing a school of thought to adhere to, it may be that the Muslim was born in an environment and town that follows a specific madhhab, learning its rulings in childhood and in the mosque from the followers of that madhhab, and it is applied in the courts of that country, growing up with the rulings that are practically implemented around him in prayer, fasting, and zakat, for example. Alternatively, he may choose the madhhab that the ruler (i.e., the legitimate leader who has taken an oath of allegiance to govern by the Book and the Sunnah) adheres to, as it will align with the rulings applied in the judiciary and followed by the state, and because the command of the imam is binding and resolves disputes. He may also learn knowledge that enables him to select among the madhhabs based on an understanding of their principles and rulings, or he may consider the apparent virtues of the imams and choose what his heart feels at ease with among them to follow their madhhab. If he becomes well-grounded in jurisprudence (fiqh) and principles (usul) and finds that another madhhab is superior to the one he initially chose, and he is convinced of its principles and strength, he may change his madhhab and follow the other madhhab in the detailed manner mentioned earlier (either as a general following of the madhhab or in specific issues). And Allah, the Exalted, knows best.
