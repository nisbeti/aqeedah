على فهم المطلق والمقيد، ففي كفارة القتل الخطأ، قال تعالى ﴾وَمَنْ قَتَلَ مُؤْمِنًا خَطَأً فَتَحْرِيرُ رَقَبَةٍ مُؤْمِنَةٍ﴿, while in the expiation for zihar, Allah says: ﴾فَتَحْرِيرُ رَقَبَةٍ ...﴿ without restriction. Ibn Abbas restricted the absolute by the restricted, and they mandated the freeing of a believing slave in all cases based on the methodology of reconciling texts, as Ibn Abbas saw that the two verses discuss the same subject (clearly and comprehensively), which is the expiations. He restricted the absolute slave in the expiation for zihar by the restricted one in the expiation for murder, and he required that the slave be a believer in all expiations, meaning he viewed the ruling as one. Hence, the foundational principle is: "Restricting the absolute by the restricted when the cause and ruling are the same."
Ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him) left the absolute unrestricted in a different subject, which is reconciling the two verses on the prohibition of usury in Surah Al-Baqarah and the prohibition of compounded usury in Surah Al-Imran. Ibn Abbas did not restrict the usury from Surah Al-Baqarah by the compounded usury from Surah Al-Imran, because he saw that the restricted in Al-Imran came to describe a common situation and not for legislative restriction. Thus, he left the absolute unrestricted, as the legislative context of the absolute and the restricted here are different, so he did not see the necessity to link them and kept the absolute unrestricted. From this comes the foundational principle: "Leaving the absolute unrestricted when the context or cause differs" (refer to the book Al-Luma in Usul al-Fiqh and Al-Tabsirah by Al-Shirazi).
As for the understanding of the Companions regarding the general and specific, an example is Abu Bakr's (may Allah be pleased with him) opinion on fighting those who withhold zakat, when he disagreed with Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) in understanding the hadith of the Messenger ﷺ "I was commanded to fight the people until they say there is no god but Allah ... and establish prayer and give zakat," narrated by Al-Bukhari. Abu Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) cited the generality in the text as a requirement for fighting, while Umar initially excluded those who withheld zakat considering them Muslims. (Note: those who withhold zakat are not those who deny its obligation, and the term "apostates" was broadly used for those whom the Companions fought after the death of the Messenger ﷺ, and it included those who withheld zakat without denying its obligation, but rather interpreted that it should only be given to the Messenger ﷺ). However, Abu Bakr favored reconciling the texts: that zakat is a part of Islam that cannot be separated, so Umar later accepted his opinion.
Thus, we find that the principles of jurisprudence were rooted in the Book and the Sunnah, using the Book and the Sunnah for the absolute and the restricted, the general and the specific, the abrogating and the abrogated, analogy, and others, and that the Companions practiced these principles instinctively without the need for formalization, as the texts were recent, and the sources of legislation were defined and understood.
The rules of analogy, preference, and understanding were practiced in an unwritten form but were clear, as seen in the letter of Umar ibn al-Khattab to Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, which included analogical and practical guidelines for deriving rulings. In Umar's letter to Abu Musa, it was stated: "Understanding, understanding what troubles your mind that is not in some Book or Sunnah, then know the forms (similarities in another narration) and examples and analogize matters, then relate that to what is most similar to the truth," as mentioned by Al-Shirazi in the ranks of jurists.
However, with the expansion of the Islamic state during the era of the Tabi'in, complex issues began to arise that had not existed before, and the situation changed after the corruption of the Arabic language, the spread of lies, and the fabricated texts attributed to the Prophet ﷺ, along with the introduction of some customs of other peoples through conquests and their mixing with customs based on Islam. It became necessary to establish foundational rules and develop sound methodologies that lead to purifying the hadith from foreign elements, to know the abrogating and the abrogated, to regulate the understanding of the language and its rules, and to regulate the principles of jurisprudence to deal with different types of evidence and to control the process of deduction.
