3- The third type of these manifestations includes the inclination to worship Allah, the inclination to respect heroes, the feeling of deficiency, incapacity, and need, the love of appearance and fame, and others. These return to the instinct of religiosity and sanctification, as these manifestations drive a person to seek a capable, perfect Creator, whose existence does not depend on anything, while creatures depend on Him for their existence(242), or to the sanctification and respect for what compensates for human deficiency through the respect of heroes and the outstanding, or the pursuit of fame and status.

The three instincts are present in humans and cannot be eradicated, nor can one human take them away from another. The manifestations of these instincts vary in existence and non-existence, strength and weakness from one person to another. However, some manifestations of a single instinct can be suppressed or erased, and one can replace another; for instance, the love of a wife can replace the love of a mother, the love of sovereignty can replace the love of possession, and the sanctification of humans and idols can replace the worship of Allah. However, it is impossible to erase the entire instinct or extirpate it from humans, as the instinct is part of the essence of humanity, while the instinctual manifestation is not part of the essence of humanity.

All of these do not change in humans as humans across time and place. A person in China needs food and sleep and becomes anxious if the instinct of procreation is not satisfied, whether through marriage, illicit relationships, or maternal affection. This does not differ from a person who lived in China or America a thousand years ago or will come into the world a thousand years later.

When the manifestation of possession is satisfied by selling a certain commodity or by establishing a company that produces a certain commodity intended for sale to obtain money, this selling process is an exchange of the commodity between two parties based on a contract they agree upon. The buyer obtains the commodity by paying the price, whether he sold sheep a thousand years ago or is selling computers or electric cars today, the selling process remains the same. It fundamentally relates to satisfying instincts (the manifestation of possession from the instinct of the love of survival, or to obtain money to satisfy hunger, for example, or to buy furniture and carpets to satisfy the instinct of sanctification through gaining self-respect or respect from others, or the feeling of happiness, or comfort, which is an organic need, etc., based on analyzing the motives of each in the transaction; someone who bought a sheep will buy it to satisfy an organic need). Thus, every action can be traced back to a motive from an instinct or an organic need, according to the goals and objectives that the action ultimately leads to.

The observable reality is that if a person abstains from food for a certain period, he will perish; this has been the case and will continue to be so until the Day of Judgment, as he needs energy derived from food, medicine, injections, or whatever you wish. However, he cannot do without the energy that helps him work and strive in this worldly life. Hence, time has no effect on changing the ideas that govern his behavior, as long as the motive for the behavior has not changed, and no impact of time is observed in it.

"The instinct: is an innate characteristic present in humans for the purpose of preserving their existence, for the purpose of preserving their species, and for guiding them to the existence of the Creator. This instinct is not directly sensed; rather, the mind perceives its existence by recognizing its manifestations.

As for how a person perceives the belonging of a manifestation to an instinct, he studies the reality of the manifestation. If the manifestation is an inclination or a reluctance that results in an action serving the preservation of human existence itself, then this manifestation belongs to the instinct of survival, such as fear.
