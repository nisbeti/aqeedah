It is forbidden for us, in a state of necessity, if a person fears destruction if he does not eat what is prohibited. However, if he is overwhelmingly certain of destruction and is on the verge of it, it is obligatory for him to eat from the prohibited, acting in accordance with the legal principle "the means to the forbidden is forbidden." And the able person should pray standing, and if he becomes ill, he should pray according to his ability! Thus, Islam has closed every gap and addressed every situation accompanying a person in his journey through life, whether he is on the brink of destruction lost in the desert or nearing it while at home or during the time of the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) or in the future; the ruling does not change.

The results of applying legal rulings: the jurist, when deriving legal rulings, is not required by law to consider all the results, but after applying the legal ruling in reality, one can look at the outcome of applying these rulings in practice, because the correct Sharia produces outcomes in reality that are correct and acceptable and lead to the desired results, provided that it is adhered to and applied well by the individual, society, and state. For example, one of the results of zakat is the elimination of poverty; therefore, we have seen the success of Islam in unifying peoples in the crucible of Islam without coercion.

Islam's view of non-Muslims is one of mercy and invitation, not one of hostility. Islam does not colonize peoples, nor does it plunder the resources of their lands; rather, it brings them justice, fairness, mercy, and protection, and does not compel them to change their beliefs.

It leads to achieving specific objectives and goals, and this is what we have discussed in some detail in later chapters, including the necessary criteria before answering the question: who has the right to legislate and the subsequent chapters.
