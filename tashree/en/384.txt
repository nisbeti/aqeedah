He thinks of public interest (maslaha) and benefit in matters, then it becomes clear to him that his estimation was wrong after a while, and he tries again, sometimes succeeding and sometimes failing. There is no real ability to ascertain public interest and benefit with certainty, and thus the legislator must inevitably fail to achieve true public interest and benefit. We will elaborate on this point in detail in the next chapter, God willing.
Since minds vary in strength and weakness, and since a person continually fluctuates in this world in exploration and understanding, as he progresses in this journey of life, truths that were previously hidden from him become revealed. The mind is undoubtedly incapable of definitively asserting that this is a public interest or that what he has been told is a public interest is indeed so!
Islamic legislation did not make the realization of public interest a goal or an end to be achieved, because the basis of Islamic legislation differs from that of Western positive legislation. Islam issues rulings on the actions of individuals, providing remedies for their actions, thus the rulings are characterized by stability and the correctness of remedies in every time and place. Therefore, since humans are incapable of perceiving true public interest, they may think that a certain matter contains public interest, only to soon realize that it is actually harmful, or vice versa. This fragile perspective is only valid if the source of the judgment regarding whether a matter contains public interest comes from the Wise and All-Knowing, Exalted is He. Thus, public interest is subordinate to the legal ruling, not the other way around; wherever there is Sharia, there is public interest. We will shed more light on this shortly. As for the Western legislator, he has considered public interest, and we have argued with them that they cannot truly grasp public interest or benefit.
Therefore, the claim that "the philosophy of democracy is fundamentally based on faith in human nature and trust in individuals' natural readiness to believe in truths and abide by their rulings whenever they are presented to them in a sound and convincing manner"(533) is invalid, because what is convincing today may be rejected tomorrow, and what is an accepted truth today may be denounced by future generations. "This is how the world has been since its inception, and this is how nations navigate the tumultuous waters of this arduous life since the dawn of civilization; they do not embrace a system until they grow weary of it and become discontented with it, nor do they anchor their ship on a shore they feel secure upon until they find it distasteful after having exhausted its purposes"(534). This is because reason cannot serve as a ruler under any circumstances, as it is not qualified for that and cannot be qualified for it. This is due to its being limited and deficient, needing others for its existence; it is a created being, not a Creator.(535).
