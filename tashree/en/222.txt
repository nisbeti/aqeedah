We must not overlook, in this context, the famous saying of the English historian Gibbon: "The Muhammadan law encompasses all people in its rulings, from the greatest king to the least of the lowly; it is a law crafted with the most wise and knowledgeable legal framework that has no equal in the two worlds"(339).
In the year 1932, researchers at the International Conference on Comparative Law held in The Hague concluded that: "Islamic law is one of the sources of comparative law... Thus, the sources of comparative law are four: French laws, German laws, English laws, and Islamic law"(340).
Christopher Weeramantry, former Vice President of the International Court of Justice and Professor of Law at Monash University, stated in his book A Just Measure: Islamic Law from an International Perspective: "Although the Islamic legal system is one of the most efficient systems in the world, law students in the West know very little about it." Weeramantry also demonstrated in his book The Islamic Influence on European Law and the Philosophy of Law and Human Reason, "that the most famous Western thinkers and legal philosophers such as John Locke, Jean-Jacques Rousseau, Montesquieu, and others were influenced by the thought and philosophy of Islamic law. He states: 'It is certainly unjust and unfair for Europeans to have a great philosophical heritage in isolation from what surrounds it, especially during its formative period, while they stand on the borders of a developed cultural heritage that was one of the most stimulating cultures that the world has ever known.' He adds: 'Islamic law represents a comprehensive book that encompasses all human activities from the cradle to the grave... This extension covers the entire chain of life, and the measure used to develop life is complete justice, and the objective measure for achieving all the various aspects of life is found encompassed in divine legislation; because it is legislation established for millions of people to provide them with the highest constitution for conduct over long centuries"(341).
In July 1951, researchers at the Islamic Jurisprudence Week Conference, organized by the Eastern Law Division at the International Comparative Law Association under the chairmanship of Professor Miu, Professor of Islamic Legislation at the University of Paris, concluded that: "The principles of Islamic jurisprudence have undeniable legislative legal value, and the differences among the jurisprudential schools within this great legal corpus contain a wealth of concepts and information and legal foundations that are a source of admiration, enabling Islamic jurisprudence to respond to all the demands of modern life and reconcile its needs." The conference participants expressed their desire to repeat the activities of the Islamic Jurisprudence Week Conference annually, to follow up on its work, and to activate its decisions and recommendations after its conclusion. They issued their recommendation to form a committee to compile a "Dictionary of Islamic Jurisprudence" to facilitate reference to jurisprudential writings so that Islamic legal information can be reviewed according to modern methods.
