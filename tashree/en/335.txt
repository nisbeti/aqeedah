Fluctuating or unregulated legislation creates chaos in values, increases conflicts, and undermines individuals' trust in justice and in societal values themselves.
The collapse of justice and the exacerbation of oppression:
When usury (riba) is legislated, the balance of wealth distribution is disrupted; the rich become wealthy without work, while the poor are plunged into debts from which there is no escape. Additionally, usurious institutions establish the phenomenon of the "debt trap," which leads to the bankruptcy of small and medium enterprises.
Practical examples of the corruption of positive legislation:
Usury in major projects: When an investor resorts to banks to cover 80% or more of the project's costs, they commit to exorbitant interest rates (which may reach 15%), raising the total cost of the project to unrealistic levels, forcing them later to raise the prices of their products, thus placing the final burden on the consumer.
The crisis worsens when loans are financed from depositors' funds without their knowledge, or from foreign investors, linking the domestic economy to external policies.
If the project falters, a spiral of borrowing begins to pay off the interest, capital erodes, companies collapse, and bankruptcy is declared, leading to increased unemployment and rising inflation.
This process is the same as what occurred in dozens of countries during the major recession crises (2008 being a prime example), where billions of dollars of savers' and investors' money were lost.
Imagine a Canadian mother whose door is suddenly knocked on, and her four-year-old daughter is taken from her because—according to the result of a hair test (under the title "Motherisk hair test") conducted at a prestigious children's hospital—she is deemed to be using drugs or alcohol, and thus considered "unfit for motherhood." The mother screams, cries, denies, pleads, but the law was strict, and the result was "scientific"! Years pass… and the child is raised in a strange home, while the mother is deprived of even seeing or contacting her. But the greatest shock did not come until more than a decade later, when it was later revealed that the test was incorrect and that the scientific standards it relied upon were flawed and inaccurate, and not suitable as a basis for such decisions! This led to disputes among authorities over the custody of more than 35,000 individuals between 2005 and 2015, based on the results of this test—which was later deemed inaccurate and not based on correct scientific standards—around 50 children were taken from their families and given to other families, while thousands of families were unjustly deprived of their children. By late 2017, it was established that these "scientific" tests were unreliable, leading to the annulment of all their previous results.
Thus, due to a law based on inadequate scientific standards, which made it, in fact, a random test thought to be correct and scientific, families were torn apart, the reputations of innocents were tarnished, hearts and memories were shattered, and there was no way to restore what was lost. It is not just a personal tragedy, but a tragedy of a failed law that relied on incomplete and inadequate sciences. The greatest disaster is that all of this would not have occurred had it not been mistakenly legislated in the name of "public interest (maslaha)" without verification, scientific confirmation, or guarantees of legislative outcomes.
