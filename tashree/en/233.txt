Chapter Four
The Nature of Islamic Legislation
After discussing some of the characteristics of the miraculous and impressive Islamic legislation, we will outline some of the features of the nature of Islamic legislation that clearly appear within it, by the will of Allah, the Exalted:
The legal ruling (hukm shar'i) is the address of the legislator concerning the actions of the servants; Islam is political by nature:
The scholars of usul al-fiqh (principles of jurisprudence) defined the legal ruling as the address of the legislator concerning the actions of the servants by obligation, or choice, or status... It was said "the address of the legislator" and not "the address of Allah" to include the Sunnah and the consensus of the Companions, as it indicates the address, so that it is not assumed that it refers only to the Qur'an; because the Sunnah is revelation, thus it is the address of the legislator; and the consensus of the Companions reveals evidence from the Sunnah, so it is the address of the legislator. It was said to be related to the actions of the servants, and not to the obligated ones, to include the rulings related to children and the insane, such as zakat on their wealth. The meaning of it being related to obligation is that it is related to demand; because the meaning of the word obligation is demand. And this demand is divided into a demand for action and a demand for abstention. The demand for action, if it is decisive, is the obligation or the required act, and if it is not decisive, it is the recommended act or the Sunnah or the supererogatory act. The demand for abstention, if it is decisive, is prohibition or restriction, and if it is not decisive, it is dislike. As for choice, it is permissibility. The address of status, or the address related to the actions of the servants by status, is making something a cause or a barrier or something similar, such as the setting of the sun being a cause for the existence of prayer, which is the cause of prayer.(371)
It is mentioned in the Tafsir al-Bahr al-Muhit by Abu Hayyan al-Andalusi: “﴾Indeed, the religion with Allah is Islam﴿ and the linguists have said: the religion is the source, and the religion with a kasra (the vowel) is the noun, and the religion is politics, and the one who practices it is the ruler. And the religion is the state. Al-Nadr ibn Shamil said: I asked a Bedouin about something, and he said: If you had met me with a religion other than this, I would have informed you.” End quote.
Thus, it is natural for us to understand that religion (legislation) is the regulation of the conditions of the obligated ones by commands and prohibitions, so every "condition" or every "reality or incident" or every "dispute" or every "emergency" has a legal ruling to regulate the "action" or the rulings and rights, or the conduct that will relate to those realities and emergencies. It is also natural for us to link politics with the care of conditions and affairs, as this is the true reality and the correct meaning of politics. Therefore, if you understand that the legal ruling is related to specific actions in certain conditions, you will realize the pastoral nature of Islam, that is, its political nature in caring for affairs. The legal ruling is the address of the legislator concerning the actions of the servants, and these actions are indeed conducted in a specific manner in certain conditions. Thus, the legislation came to regulate this conduct with commands and prohibitions, which we call "the legal ruling," imposing this conduct or prohibiting that, and so forth. In this way, it governs the people with these commands and prohibitions, and it even clarifies for them the method of implementing these commands and prohibitions. Thus, there was a blending of religion and politics, and Islam, by its nature, is political.
