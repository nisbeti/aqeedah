Since al-Shafi'i has established that the foundation of this religion is the Book and the Sunnah, he did not permit independent juristic reasoning (ijtihad) based on personal opinion except through derivation or building upon them only, and he considered any opinion that does not rely on either of them as not being part of the Sharia. Al-Shafi'i does not base his understanding of the Sharia on personal understanding, but rather on objective and material understanding. For example, a judge rules based on evidence against the defendant or based on their confession, and does not rule based on their own conjecture regarding the matter. Therefore, he rejects the juristic preference (istihsan) that is seen as hidden analogy (qiyas khafi), which is based on personal understanding of the matter, and he leaves aside the building on the text that is based on clear analogy (qiyas jali).
As for what they call the juristic preference of the Sunnah, the juristic preference of consensus (ijma), and the juristic preference of necessity (darura), al-Shafi'i accepts all of them because they are applications of the Sunnah, consensus, and legal necessity, and not merely juristic preference without text.
As for public interest (maslaha), al-Shafi'i only considers what resembles recognized interests that are based on established principles in the Sharia. As for the conditions and restrictions placed on public interest, such as that it must align with the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), be acceptable and reasonable, and alleviate hardship, such public interest has no value for al-Shafi'i.
Regarding means (dhara'i), al-Shafi'i only considers those that are based on text or what is indicated by text through consensus or legal analogy. From this, we do not observe any existence or effect of what is called the change of rulings with the change of times and places in the view of Imam al-Shafi'i (may Allah be pleased with him).
Professor Mahmoud Abdul Karim Hassan said: "Imam al-Shafi'i states in the Risala: 'Indeed, juristic preference is mere enjoyment,' and then he says: 'The scholar should not speak except from the perspective of knowledge, and the perspective of knowledge is the necessary report through analogy based on evidence of correctness, so that the scholar is always following a report, and the seeker of knowledge through analogy.' Then he said: 'If he speaks without a necessary report or analogy, he is closer to sin than the one who speaks while being ignorant...' He said: 'And Allah has not permitted anyone after the Messenger of Allah to speak except from the perspective of knowledge that has preceded him, and the perspective of knowledge after that is the Book, the Sunnah, consensus, and the reports, and what I have described of analogy upon them'(296). Therefore, al-Ghazali said the same as al-Shafi'i. He said: 'Indeed, whoever seeks public interest has legislated just as whoever prefers has legislated'”(297).
In the book Ahkam al-Qur'an, al-Shafi'i said: "The ruling of Allah, then the ruling of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), then the ruling of the Muslims is evidence that it is not permissible for one who is qualified to be a judge or a mufti to rule or to give fatwa except from the perspective of a necessary report, and that is the Book, then the Sunnah, or what the people of knowledge have said that they do not differ in, or analogy based on some of this. And it is not permissible for him to rule or give fatwa based on juristic preference if juristic preference is not obligatory and not in any of these meanings. And he mentioned in what he argued the saying of Allah, the Exalted: ﴾Does man think that he will be left alone?﴿ [He said] that the people of knowledge regarding the Qur'an have not differed in what I have learned that ﴾alone﴿ refers to one who is neither commanded nor prohibited. And whoever gives a fatwa or rules based on what he has not been commanded, he has chosen for himself to be among the meanings of being left alone, and Allah, the Exalted, has informed him that he has not been left alone and he thinks that he can say whatever he wants, and he claims what the Qur'an has revealed contradicts it. Allah (Exalted is He) said to His Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him): ﴾Follow what has been revealed to you from your Lord﴿, and He, the Exalted, said: ﴾And judge between them by what Allah has revealed, and do not follow their desires, and beware lest they divert you from some of what Allah has revealed to you﴿. Then a group came to him and asked him about the companions of the cave and others: he said, 'I will inform you tomorrow,' meaning 'I will ask Gabriel (peace be upon him)'”.
