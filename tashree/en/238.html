He said: "Do not do that; get up and sleep, and fast and break your fast, for your body has a right over you, and your eyes have a right over you, and your guest has a right over you, and your wife has a right over you." In addition to placing the individual in charge of taking care of his own affairs, he is also obligated to take care of his family's affairs and the community around him, according to the nature of that responsibility and his role within it. This is further supplemented by the responsibility of the state to take care of the affairs of its subjects and to govern them according to the rulings of the Sharia, which guarantees people the sufficiency of their basic needs, such as food, shelter, and clothing, if they are unable to provide for them. The state must work to provide at least the minimum necessities; marriage and what is needed to fulfill its distant interests, and the guarantee of the two natural rights of healthcare and education, enabling individuals to achieve well-being for themselves and those they support(376). The state's care for these rights and affairs takes on a legislative and legal obligation rather than a spiritual one. A Muslim was brought to the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) who had killed a Jew, and he was killed for it. He said: "We are more deserving of fulfilling our covenant." The people of the covenant have the same care for their affairs and the guarantee of their livelihood as Muslims do. From Abu Wail from Abu Musa or one of them, with his chain of narration, that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "Feed the hungry, visit the sick, and free the captive." Abu Ubaid said: "Likewise, the people of the covenant are defended against those who oppose them, and their captives are freed. If they are rescued, they return to their covenant and are free, and there are hadiths regarding this"(377).
