Fourth: Sovereign and civilizational values:

Achieving the legal foundations of the state and its legitimacy and principles of governance, and the relationships between the state and the subjects; sovereignty belongs to the Sharia, and authority belongs to the nation.

Highlighting the civilizational identity of the nation, such that the legislation produces a nation with culture and civilization.

The legislation should encompass the systems of society and the state, such as economy, governance, social matters, penalties, judiciary, and others, and regulate individual affairs.

Axis One: The necessity of legislation to regulate behavior and social relations:

First: The nature of man necessitates the existence of legislation:

Man is a social being who does not live in isolation, and his interactions with others involve conflicts of interests, and disparities in power and ambitions.

Without legislation, a person resorts to his whims, justifying his actions according to his immediate interests, leading to the prevalence of chaos.

Selfishness, greed, power, and cunning may drive a person to exploit others if not restrained by a higher authority.

Second: Regulating social relations is essential for the continuity of society.

Every human relationship (marriage, sale, partnership, management, governance, politics...) requires regulations that govern it, protect the rights of the parties, and establish the framework for resolving their disputes.

Without legislative regulations, the logic of dominance and power prevails, the weak are weakened, and justice is disrupted.

The absence of regulations leads to the spread of injustice, fraud, betrayal, and the loss of rights.

Third: The catastrophic consequences of the absence of legislation or its corruption:

Disintegration of the family: Without provisions regulating marriage, divorce, and inheritance, rights are lost, and the family unit, which is the cornerstone of society, is threatened.

Social injustice: The lack of regulations for the distribution of wealth and financial dealings creates a class that monopolizes wealth, and a class whose suffering intensifies.

Moral disintegration, where illicit relationships and births outside the family framework become widespread.

Loss of security: The absence of deterrent penal legislation leads to the proliferation of crime, turning society into a jungle.

Failure of the state: If the ruling authority is not regulated by binding contracts and provisions, it turns into tyranny or authoritarian disintegration, and its institutions fail.

Fourth: Legislation cannot be left to the whims and desires of the people.
