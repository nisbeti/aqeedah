The Islamic creed addressed the common person, the philosopher, the scholar, and the merchant, both male and female, regardless of their diverse backgrounds and intellectual foundations, with a simple, convincing, and impactful discourse. It was based on instilling dynamic doctrinal concepts that interact with human life, so each of these individuals found evidence that convinced them and helped them fulfill their responsibilities. None of them encountered any ambiguity that warranted clarification, as the linguistic intellectual instinct served as a foundation for a comprehensive and convincing understanding of the evidence, allowing the rational and understanding person to know where their intellect stands and where to advance. Therefore, none of them pondered the meaning of attributes, nor the exaltation (istawa), nor the Throne (Arsh) or the Footstool (Kursi), nor did it even occur to any of them to ask whether the Qur'an was created or uncreated. No one among them explored God's will and power and their relationship with time and realization, and even the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) did not discuss this, nor is there any record of him having uttered a single word on the matter!
The Islamic creed addresses human existence in its own unique manner, characterized by vitality, engaging with all its aspects, energies, and avenues of knowledge, and it does not address thought alone in the human being. As for philosophy, it has a different approach, as it attempts to confine truth within expression, and since the type of truths it tackles cannot be limited to what is articulated, in addition to the fact that fundamental aspects of these truths are, by their nature, greater than the realm in which human "thought" operates, such as the truths of lordship, divinity, and the unseen, philosophy inevitably leads to complexity, confusion, and dryness, and the exploration of metaphysics through a flawed analogy of the unseen to the witnessed, whenever it attempts to address matters of creed. Thus, philosophy has had little to no role in public human life, and it has not propelled humanity forward in any way comparable to what the creed has done, which has advanced humanity through the wilderness of time and the darkness of the path. Therefore, the creed must be presented in the style of the creed, as attempting to present it in the style of philosophy kills it, extinguishes its radiance and inspiration, and restricts it to one aspect of the many facets of human existence. Hence, complexity, dryness, deficiency, and deviation appear in all the discussions that attempt to present the creed in this strange manner contrary to its nature, and in this mold that constricts it.
