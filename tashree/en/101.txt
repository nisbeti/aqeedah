Ijtihad and Taqlid are regulated operational procedures:
The intent of the legislator is reached through one of two organized and regulated processes, which are either ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) or taqlid (following a qualified authority). Ijtihad is a highly systematic process, where the definition of ijtihad in usul al-fiqh (principles of jurisprudence) is: “Exerting effort to attain a preponderance of belief regarding some of the legal rulings in a manner that the mujtahid (one who exercises ijtihad) feels incapable of going beyond it”(205).
Ijtihad is performed by studying a set of evidence related to the issue from verses and hadiths in a detailed and deductive manner, based on specific principles that govern the process of ijtihad and regulate the way to deal with the evidence in terms of understanding, considering, and weighing them against each other. From the totality of that evidence, an induction is obtained that provides a preponderance of belief for the mujtahid regarding the issue, and the ruling is not taken from a single piece of evidence severed from the other related evidence. This is what compensates for the subject of belief by the gathering of numerous corroborating pieces of evidence regarding the issue, raising the ruling to a level of preponderance of belief close to certainty.
Thus, ijtihad is a regulated process of deduction, and the word deduction in the language comes from the root نَبَطَ (nabaṭa), نَبْطًا (nabaṭan), and نُبُوطًا (nubuṭan). To nabaṭ something means to reveal it after it was hidden or to extract it. To nabaṭ a well means to extract its water, and a person whose نَبَطُهُ (nabaṭuh) is not known means that the depth and extent of his knowledge are unknown. And when I say I استنبطت الحكم (istinbaṭtu al-hukm), I mean I extracted it through ijtihad, and I أنبَطْتُهُ إنْبَاطًا (anbaṭtuhu inbaṭan): it is similar to that, and its origin is from استنبط الحافر الماء (istinbaṭa al-ḥāfir al-mā’a). And to أنبَطَهُ إنْبَاطًا (anbaṭahu inbaṭan) means to extract it through his work and reach what he digs for and desires. Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And if they had referred it to the Messenger and to those in authority among them, they would have known it.﴿ [al-Nisa 83]: “They would have known it” means they would extract its management or knowledge, as stated in the dictionaries. Al-Zajjaj said: The meaning of يستنبطونه (yastanbiṭūnahu) in the language is they extract it, and its origin is from النبَط (nabaṭ), which is the water that comes from the well when it is first dug; and the نبط (nabaṭ) of horses is the white under their armpits and bellies, and a نَبْطاء (nabaṭā’) sheep is a white-sided sheep. From this collection of meanings, we deduce that deduction is a process of contemplation and arduous thinking in which the mujtahid employs his mind on the texts to understand them and extract the legal ruling from them, as he confines the numerous legal texts related to the specific issue before him and studies them; extracting what is hidden from meanings from their depths scattered in the implications of the words used in the detailed evidence to reveal their clarity and brightness, until the deducer reaches his goal by being overwhelmingly convinced that the legal ruling on the issue is such and such.
