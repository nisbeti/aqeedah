And the roads are among the public places, and it is permissible for a passerby to walk on the right side of the road or on its left side, and he is allowed to choose this road or that one. This practice was occurring in front of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) and he approved it, and there are many hadiths regarding the etiquette of the road. If the imam sees that he needs to organize the flow of traffic by prohibiting some permissible actions and mandating others, he is allowed to do so, because the Sharia has commanded obedience to the ruler as long as he does not command disobedience. Thus, the Sharia has clarified that the command of the ruler is to be obeyed in organizing the permissible actions, and it has clarified that walking on the roads is among the permissible actions. Therefore, this example has been addressed by the Sharia, and its ruling has been clearly stated, so it is not correct for anyone to say that the Sharia has not addressed it.

As for their statement regarding matters without textual evidence, know that we are commanded to follow the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) alone. Allah, Exalted is He, says: ﴾Say, "If you love Allah, then follow me; Allah will love you"﴿, and the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) does not say anything except by revelation ﴾And he does not speak from [his own] inclination. It is not but a revelation revealed﴿ ﴾Say, "I only warn you by revelation"﴿. Thus, we are commanded to follow what has come by revelation, and its implication is that we are prohibited from following anything other than what has come by revelation, that is, from following anyone other than the Messenger. The derived ruling that they claim has no textual evidence, and for which they have established a ruling without a legal proof, is a means to introduce something outside the path of revelation into the religion, which is invalid. This is sufficient to nullify what has been derived based on this understanding from being considered as part of the Sharia.

And the absence of evidence from the Sharia regarding a matter indicates a deficiency in the Sharia, and it means that the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) did not clarify to the people what was revealed to them, which contradicts His saying: ﴾A clarification for all things﴿, and contradicts the completion of the religion, and His saying: ﴾The right path has become distinct from the wrong﴿. Thus, the absence of a ruling means that the right path has not been made clear, and that Allah's proof over His creation is not established in certain matters, which contradicts His saying: ﴾So that whoever is to perish may perish by clear proof, and whoever is to live may live by clear proof﴿. This is sufficient to refute them, because the ruling that is intended to be taken is a ruling of the Sharia, not a ruling of reason. Therefore, for it to be considered from the Sharia, there must be evidence indicating it, and it must originate from revelation. The legal ruling is the address of the legislator concerning the actions of the servants, and it is not the address of reason, desires, or interests. What has not come through the legislator is not considered a legal ruling. ﴾Say, "I only warn you by revelation"﴿ is indeed a restrictive formulation. And Allah, Exalted is He, says: ﴾Follow what has been revealed to you from your Lord, and do not follow other than Him allies. Little do you remember﴿ [Al-A'raf 3]. The position of His saying ﴾Follow what has been revealed to you﴿ is that of a comprehensive limit, and the position of ﴾And do not follow﴿ is that of a prohibitive limit, which prohibits following the opinions of men and human legislations due to the explicit prohibition against following them. Therefore, any legislation must have a connection that indicates its issuance from revelation. And He, Exalted is He, says: ﴾The judgment is only for Allah. He has commanded that you not worship except Him. That is the correct religion, but most of the people do not know﴿. And Allah, Exalted is He, addressed His Prophet by saying: ﴾Indeed, We have sent down to you the Book in truth to judge between the people by what Allah has shown you﴿ and by saying: ﴾And We have sent down to you the Book in truth, confirming what was before it of the Scripture and a guardian over it. So judge between them by what Allah has revealed﴿. These verses restrict judgment to what Allah has revealed. If we know that what Allah has revealed is sufficient and comprehensive, we understand that there remains no room for judgment by anything other than what Allah has revealed, nor for taking anything other than what Allah has revealed as a judge over the actions of the servants.
