The seventh pillar: "I have only been sent to perfect noble character": Surrounding the rulings with the fence of life systems to yield their fruits:

Islam is the religion of Allah from Adam (peace be upon him) until today, ﴾Indeed, the religion with Allah is Islam﴿, and since humans began on earth with our master Adam (peace be upon him) until today, Islam is what brings them back to the path of righteousness and establishes for them the correct scales to regulate their thoughts and actions. The source of correct values is the Islamic religion throughout the ages, and whenever people deviated from the path of truth, remnants and fragments of it remained for them, as was the case with the disbelievers of Mecca before Islam, who had moral values among themselves and among other Arabs.

However, these scattered and unregulated moral values did not transform into a part of a comprehensive and correct life system. Therefore, we found in those same societies that excelled in honoring guests that they enslaved people and practiced shameful acts without seeing any harm in them. The scattered moral values that were not gathered in a coherent life system had an individual impact (like Hatim al-Ta'i) or were aimed at "showing off"(425) and prestige, not for the sake of the moral value itself. They would feed the hungry to be called generous, not for the sake of the eyes of the poor. Islam made a qualitative leap in morals, so feeding the hungry became an obligation to alleviate the hunger of the poor; otherwise, people would be absolved from Allah's responsibility if they knew that a neighbor was hungry. They engaged in noble character to please Allah and out of fear of His wrath. Thus, morality was placed within the fence of Allah's pleasure, within the fence of fearing His punishment, and within the fence of social solidarity. It was made a value accompanying actions when performed, such as honesty and trustworthiness. "And He encouraged the good among them and forbade the bad: He encouraged honesty, trustworthiness, a cheerful face, modesty, kindness to parents, maintaining family ties, relieving distress, and that one loves for his brother what he loves for himself, and considered all of that and similar things as encouragement to follow Allah's commands. He forbade their opposites, such as lying, betrayal, envy, immorality, and the like, and considered that and similar things as prohibitions of what Allah has forbidden." Morality is a part of this Sharia, and a section of Allah's commands and prohibitions, which must be realized in the Muslim himself for his actions to be in accordance with Islam, and for his adherence to Allah's commands to be complete(426). What a qualitative leap Islam made in morals, for after they were mostly for reputation and fame, they became compassion for the poor and pleasing to Allah, social solidarity, and even loyalty among believers to one another.

Imagine that Imam Ali, who possessed only a little food in his house, encounters a poor person, an orphan, and a captive, and feeds them out of love, from his heart, ﴾And they give food, despite their desire for it, to the poor, the orphan, and the captive. Indeed, we feed you only for the sake of Allah; we do not want from you any reward or thanks﴿ [al-Insan 8-9]. It is usually observed in human nature that if you smile at your neighbor and he does not return the smile, you will become displeased or angry with him, and perhaps next time you will not smile at him. But Islam teaches us to perform virtuous deeds and noble character for the sake of Allah, seeking neither reward nor thanks. Thus, Islam perfected noble character, refined it, and placed it within a comprehensive and integrated system, a system of life, making its goal the pleasure of Allah. The virtues of character will not be established except under the system of Islam. When the Crusaders occupied Jerusalem, they slaughtered tens of thousands of the peaceful inhabitants.
