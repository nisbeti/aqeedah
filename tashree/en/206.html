The completion of the interests of the Muslims and the greatest objectives of the religion"(303), said the scholar Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him)(304): "It must be known that the governance of the affairs of the people is one of the greatest obligations of the religion; indeed, there is no establishment of the religion or the worldly life except through it." And Dr. Diya al-Din al-Rayyis(305) said: "The caliphate is the most important religious position and concerns all Muslims. The Islamic Sharia has stipulated that the establishment of the caliphate is a fundamental obligation of the religion; rather, it is the greatest obligation because the execution of all other obligations depends on it." End of quote.

If we keep in mind that the meaning of caliphate (khilafa) is: the implementation of legal rulings in practice, and the safeguarding of that implementation, since the Sharia has surrounded every aspect of human life, the community, and the state with legal rulings, and has made their application in life one of the greatest objectives of the religion, for which the Book was revealed and the Messenger was sent, it follows that the legislator has granted the authority to implement these legal rulings to the community, and has commanded it to pledge allegiance to a caliph who will establish these rulings; thus, the caliphate is established. How then can the caliphate not be one of the greatest objectives of the religion?

And that its function is to care for the interests and affairs of the ummah (community) according to the rulings of Islam, to convey the Islamic message, and to safeguard the ummah.

The essence of religion is a belief that we hold, and commands and prohibitions that stem from this belief govern our lives. Allah, Exalted is He, has revealed commands and prohibitions so that people may act justly, and that their actions, measures, convictions, and judgments align with the divine system. People have not been left to their own devices in any matter of their lives, ﴾Does man think that he will be left neglected?﴿ 36 Al-Qiyama, and "neglected" refers to one who is neither commanded nor prohibited. Rather, Allah has decreed a ruling in every matter; people cannot act justly, judge with truth, or do what they are supposed to do except by adhering to that command from Allah. Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And for every nation there is a messenger. So when their messenger comes, it will be judged between them in justice, and they will not be wronged.﴿ 47 Yunus, ﴾Indeed, We sent Our messengers with clear signs and sent down with them the Scripture and the balance that the people may maintain [justice].﴿ 25 Al-Hadid. And His saying, ﴾Indeed,﴿: the "lam" is in response to an omitted oath, and the "lam" in His saying ﴾that the people may maintain [justice]﴿ is for the purpose of explanation. Thus, one of the most important overarching objectives of sending clear signs with the messengers is that people may act justly according to the rulings that were revealed with these messengers and prophets.

Thus, religion was revealed to be a system of life that people must govern their lives by its rulings as an obligation(306). ﴾The people were one community, then Allah sent the prophets as bringers of good tidings and warners, and He sent down with them the Book in truth to judge between the people concerning that in which they differed﴿. The "lam" in the phrase ﴾to judge between the people﴿ is a lam of purpose, so one of the most important overarching objectives of sending the prophets, along with the good tidings and warnings, is for the Book to judge between the people to rectify their livelihoods and for truth and justice to prevail in every aspect of their lives! Consequently, the application of these rulings in the reality of life is indeed the greatest objective of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), the purpose of sending the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him), and the purpose of revealing the Book!
