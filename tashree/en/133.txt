In other words, if every person of opinion were to issue a fatwa based on their opinion and it were obligatory to follow it or act upon it, both for themselves and for others who imitate them, on the grounds that it is a legal opinion for which those who oppose it would be punished, then on what basis would the Lord of the Worlds punish on the Day of Judgment? And how would He punish this person of opinion who considers their statement to be the opinion of religion in every matter? This would be more than free from any sin, as their religion is what they issue fatwas about!
Then how would this reasoning align with what has been previously demonstrated regarding the impossibility of the Sharia being devoid of commands and prohibitions that leave a person aimless, or of the evidence that has been established for creation and servants, for which they will be held accountable for the weight of an atom of their deeds?
Thus, the meaning of "I exert my opinion" cannot be, in any case, my rational judgment on the matter, but rather it means my legal exertion (ijtihad).
So the questioner asks, and it is right for them to ask, what does it mean: "If you do not find"? It means I searched in the Book of Allah and did not find, in the Sunnah and did not find, so how is there ijtihad?
The answer is that ijtihad involves analogy (qiyas), where one analogizes a matter to another for a common reason. That is, the hadith—despite being weak—has not exhausted all avenues of ijtihad in its context, so we say that ijtihad by opinion has not excluded all of them, but has exhausted the Book and the Sunnah, and what remains is the consensus of the Companions and analogy or what the mujtahid considers to be legal evidence. Thus, it is permissible to say: "My opinion," because the concept of opinion here is legal ijtihad. However, the subject of the discussion is judgment, meaning ruling in a case by researching the application of the legal ruling to a reality described by two disputants. Therefore, opinion here is in the context of judgment, and this is clear, and Allah knows best.
"And there has been a prohibition against taking opinions that come from people. In the hadith: 'Indeed, Allah does not take away knowledge after He has given it to you by force, but He takes it away with the death of scholars, and there will remain people who are ignorant, who will be asked and will give fatwas based on their opinions, leading themselves astray and leading others astray.' This was narrated by al-Bukhari from Abdullah ibn Amr, meaning they give fatwas based on their opinions which are from themselves [since they are ignorant and do not possess the means of correct ijtihad to derive the legal ruling]. The derived opinion is not considered an opinion from the one who derives it, but rather it is a legal ruling. As for what is considered an opinion, it is the opinion that comes from the individual, and for this reason, the Messenger referred to it as innovation (bid'ah). In the authentic hadith, the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: 'The best speech is the Book of Allah, and the best guidance is the guidance of Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him), and the worst of affairs are those that are newly invented, and every innovation is misguidance.' This was narrated by Muslim through the chain of Jabir ibn Abdullah, and the newly invented matters are innovations, which are everything that contradicts the Book and the Sunnah and the consensus in rulings, whether in action or speech"(234).
The basis of all this has been previously mentioned in detail, which is that we have been commanded to follow revelation, and that warning is confined to revelation, and that the religion has been completed, and Allah has not left the creation with an excuse after the messengers, meaning the proof has been established for people in every matter they need in their livelihood until the Day of Judgment, before the noble Messenger (peace be upon him) departed to meet his Lord, and that Allah has not left people aimless nor in any matter, and thus, the judgment is solely for Allah, and for a ruling to be called legal, it must be taken from the Sharia, and reason is not a judge, and so forth, which we have elaborated on in detail, so refer to it. Thus, any deviation from this precise general line constitutes placing evidence in inappropriate contexts, and whoever deems it good has legislated, and whoever speaks of public interest (maslaha) has legislated, meaning they have transgressed the right of Allah, Exalted is He, in legislation...(235) And all praise is due to Allah, Lord of the Worlds.
