If a matter were devoid of any legal ruling that Allah will hold creation accountable for on the Day of Resurrection, people would be in a state of futility, meaning devoid of command and prohibition. Allah has informed them that He did not leave them in futility, thus it necessitated that the affirmation of the Qur'an and its lack of contradiction must encompass a ruling for every matter. Most rulings are based on evidence that is of speculative indication (the Qur'an and Sunnah; most texts of the Qur'an and Sunnah are of speculative indication, and they are the material and foundation of jurisprudence, from which most practical rulings are derived), or of speculative authenticity (Sunnah), and it is through this that the proof is established upon the creatures, and based on it they will be held accountable. This necessitates the obligation to obey the ruling of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) in matters that arise among people, and "what" is a general form.
From here, abandoning the derivation based on a speculative report places us before issues that lack evidence; either there is no evidence for the matter from the Sharia – they claim – and thus they seek for it a purely rational ruling. How can such a ruling be called a legal ruling? How does this understanding align with establishing proof upon the people? ﴾Messengers bringing good news and warning, so that there will be no excuse for the people against Allah after the messengers. And Allah is Exalted in Might and Wise﴿ [4:165], and His saying ﴾Does man think that he will be left neglected?﴿ [75:36]. How will the performance of this act result in reward from Allah or punishment from Him? Will Allah punish someone for opposing the opinion of a certain person? If it becomes clear that the proof is established by the text of the Book and that Allah has not left anyone in any matter of worship in a state of neglect, and it is established that reason is not a legislator since the ruling belongs solely to Allah, it becomes evident by definitive evidence that each ruling has its proof. However, since it has been established that most of them lack definitive evidence, it is conclusively established that they may be established by speculative evidence, confirming that practical rulings differ from beliefs and may be built upon speculation. All of this is definitive evidence for building legal rulings on speculation.
Eighth: The proof for prohibition is His saying ﴾And do not follow that of which you have no knowledge﴿ [17:36], and a solitary report does not necessitate knowledge, so it should not be followed. And His saying ﴾Indeed, suspicion does not avail against the truth at all﴿ [53:28], and His saying ﴾And do not say about Allah that which you do not know﴿ [7:33]. ... The response is that this is specific to the principles of religions and the fundamentals of definitive acts of worship, whereas the occurrence of action based on a solitary report is indicated by His saying ﴾O you who have believed, if there comes to you a disobedient one with a report, investigate, lest you harm a people out of ignorance and become, over what you have done, regretful﴿ [49:6]. Thus, Allah made the requirement for investigation the fact that he is disobedient; therefore, in the absence of disobedience, action is required, which is the objective. The informant who brings the report may be one or more, and the legislator has permitted acting upon his report and news. And due to His saying ﴾And it is not for the believers to go forth all at once. So why not from every group among them go forth to obtain understanding in the religion and warn their people when they have returned to them that perhaps they might be cautious﴿ [9:122], Allah has mandated caution and abstention from what they have warned and feared them about, whether the warners are few or many – regarding the group that has emerged from the sect, even though the sect can apply to three. The one who emerges will be fewer than them; thus, if caution is required when they speak, their words are proof, which is the objective, by analogy to fatwas and testimony.)
