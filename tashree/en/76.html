(152) The implications of words on meanings are limited to three types: implication of correspondence, implication of inclusion, and implication of commitment. The implication of correspondence and implication of inclusion relate to the forms and syntactic structures of sentences, while the implication of commitment relates to the speech and its indication and inclusion of reasons, and to the content of the speech or the tone of the speech, which is the concept of agreement, or the evidence of speech, which is the concept of contradiction. The implication of commitment has five types: the first is the implication of requirement, the second is the indication of the word, the third is the hint and suggestion, the fourth is understanding the non-explicit from the explicit by the implication of the context of the speech and its intended meaning, and the fifth is the concept, which is called the evidence of speech. The implication of correspondence is the implication of the word on the complete meaning assigned to the word, such as the implication of "human" on its meaning, which is the speaking animal. The implication of inclusion is the implication of the word on part of the meaning assigned to the word, such as the implication of "human" on "animal" or "speaking." The implication of commitment is the implication of the word on the necessary meaning assigned to the word. Refer to: (The Inference from the Probable in Belief by Professor Fathi Salim, Chapter: Implications and Their Types).

(155) The absolute mujtahid is one who exerts effort in legal rulings and strives in the method of deriving legal rulings, whether he has a specific method as is the case in some schools, or does not have a specific method, but naturally follows a certain way of understanding to derive rulings, as is the case with the mujtahids in the era of the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) (Islamic Personality, vol. 1, by Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani, Chapter: Ijtihad and Taqlid).

(156) As for the school mujtahid, he is one who follows a mujtahid from among the mujtahids in the method of ijtihad, but he exerts effort in rulings and does not follow the Imam of his school. This school mujtahid has no conditions other than knowing the rulings of the school and its evidence, and he can follow the rulings of the school and oppose them with an opinion of his own within the same school. (Islamic Personality, vol. 1, by Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani, Chapter: Ijtihad and Taqlid).

(157) This does not have specific conditions or a specific method; rather, it is permissible for anyone who has knowledge of some legal knowledge and some linguistic knowledge that enables him to understand the legal texts to exert effort in a single issue. It is permissible for him to follow the opinions of the mujtahids in a single issue and their evidence and the reasoning, leading him to a specific understanding of the legal ruling that he believes is the legal ruling, whether it agrees with the opinion of the mujtahids or contradicts them. It is permissible for him to follow the legal evidence in a single issue and understand from it what he believes is the legal ruling, whether this issue has been previously discussed by the mujtahids or not. It suffices for the issue mujtahid to be knowledgeable about what pertains to that issue and what is necessary in it, and it does not harm him in that to be ignorant of what is unrelated to it, whether it pertains to legal or foundational issues or otherwise. (Islamic Personality, vol. 1, by Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani, Chapter: Ijtihad and Taqlid).

(158) The one seeking fatwa is not a follower, because the follower is one who takes the legal ruling and acts upon it, whereas the one seeking fatwa is one who learns the legal ruling from a person who knows this ruling, whether that person is a mujtahid or not, and whether the seeker learns it to act upon it or merely for knowledge. (Islamic Personality, vol. 1, by Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani, Chapter: Ijtihad and Taqlid).
