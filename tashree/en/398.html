Appendix 1: Organizing the Public Interest for Regulating Permanent Relationships as the Foundation of Society and the State:

On the other hand, the individual needs of a person that satisfy their instincts and organic needs exceed the limits of individuality, leading to societal problems that require the organization of relationships, as we have previously mentioned. These also relate to communal interests and relationships that arise to satisfy the vital energy of individuals. However, their organization goes beyond mere individual behavior to include regulations for their behavior in this process of satisfaction while living in a society. Thus, in their satisfaction, they are subject to the controls, laws, and systems imposed upon them by society, or they may adhere to some of society's dictates while avoiding what contradicts their personal beliefs as much as possible(545).

It is well known that society is a group of people bound together by continuous relationships, through which that society exists and distinguishes itself. Or, in the words of "Toynbee," "Human society is in itself a system of relationships among human beings"(546). This system that connects individuals to form a society is essentially the sum of the ideas and feelings that these people carry, along with the legislations and laws that govern their affairs.

Many Muslims hold the view that society is a collection of individuals; if the individual is reformed, the society will be reformed. Consequently, their approach to establishing a state and reforming society is summarized in the idea that one should reform oneself, then reform one's family and kin, then reform one's neighbors, and so forth, until society is reformed and change occurs. Thus, "Establish the state of Islam within yourself, and it will be established on earth," based on the belief that if each of us establishes Islam within ourselves and our neighbors do the same, there will be nothing left of the state "except the announcement"! So simply(547)!

Undoubtedly, society is a moral personality of a group of people, not merely a collection of individuals. The passengers of a ship may number in the thousands, but they are not referred to as a society, as there are no enduring bonds of relationships among them, nor do they share interests that they cooperate to achieve. Moreover, what distinguishes the Islamic society from the pre-Islamic society, or distinguishes the Egyptian society from the American society, for example, is that each of these societies is based on certain values, specific relationships that prevail within them, a lifestyle that characterizes them, and systems that organize their lives. If it were merely a matter of individuals isolated from their values, relationships, lifestyles, and systems, no society would be distinguished from another!
