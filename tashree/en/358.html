While the angles that secular liberalism has imposed on problems focus on achieving human freedom of choice, enabling material benefit to the fullest extent, and examining issues as economic or social in nature, detached from their relationship with humanity and society, and from the perspective that truth is relative and that change is the law of life, this has led to their legislations and laws flipping from one extreme to another.

It regulates the relationship of every real or presumed influence on actions and things, so that issues that do not actually affect reality, such as place, time, environment, technological, industrial, and urban advancement, are not included in the matter of legislation and law-making, or those that influence behavior, but legislation addresses that influence with rulings that regulate it, so it does not become captive to erroneous rulings, such as customs, traditions, and norms.

The subject of benefits and harms is not viewed from a purely material perspective, so that it aligns with whims and inclinations, and the limited human estimation of what is beneficial or harmful, but rather it presents to humanity a comprehensive system of life that serves as a way of living, ensuring the proper organization and integration of economic, social, and other relationships, and defining individual and social responsibilities, as well as the responsibilities of the state in a comprehensive and integrated system that brings happiness and tranquility to individuals and achieves stability for society and elevation and honor for the nation.

We notice that building human legislations on achieving benefits, which will often be material benefits, or whose measures are purely utilitarian, at the same time that human legislation cannot definitively grasp benefits, makes laws susceptible to flipping from one opposite to another, and keeps them perpetually within the realm of potential error, without any presumption, even if slight, that any of the multiple opinions that could serve as a solution to the problem might be correct, which hinders the realization of any value from these laws and legislations. The benefit may be realized through a repealed law, and this may become evident after a while, or it may be temporarily realized through the current legislation, then other circumstances arise that reveal it to be unachieved, necessitating its change or amendment, or the solution may lie in something that did not occur to the legislator. Therefore, we see political parties in the West striving to change legislations and policies every election period due to the negative effects they reveal on society. Hence, it was inevitable to resort to a source of legislation that eliminates the realm of uncertainty in determining benefits.

Moreover, the values that are achieved through actions are not always material values; they may also be spiritual, human, or ethical values. Therefore, we find a coldness in the West towards actions that realize other values beyond mere material benefit. If a group of them sees an oppressed person being attacked in the street, you find that those who rush to help him are few, and if they do, it is for the values of Christianity or humanity that are centered in them, not for the values resulting from the secular Western society itself.

Thus, we see that the perspective on benefits should combine the material aspect with the spiritual, ethical, and human aspects, so that legislations are linked to the values they realize, or are part of a system of legislations aimed at creating societal systems that achieve greater benefits, such as the system of zakat, which contributes to the stability and solidarity of society even if it is wealth taken from the rich, or at least there should be a presumption that makes the benefit realized in the opinion because its source is All-Knowing, Wise, and Expert.
