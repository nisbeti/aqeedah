The text of al-Amidi: "The issues that depend on the same principles are considered one unit, while the branches that differ in their evidence are separated."
He distinguishes between "issues" that rely on the same principles (such as analogy), and "branches" that may vary in their evidence. For example: "Zakat" is one issue that includes the zakat on wealth, zakat al-fitr, and zakat on crops, because it depends on the same legal objective (achieving social solidarity). However, the rulings of inheritance are a different issue because their purpose is the distribution of financial rights after death.
As for the scholar Ibn Abd al-Shakur in "Muslim al-Thubut," he adds that an issue can be considered independent if the principles or evidence used to derive the ruling differ. Ibn Abd al-Shakur's text states: "The multiplicity of principles used may render the issues independent, even if they are interconnected in terms of subject." The point of evidence: it highlights that a single issue may transform into multiple issues if the legal evidence used is different. For example: the rulings of prayer are discussed based on one set of principles (such as analogy), thus they are considered one issue. Meanwhile, the rulings of sale may involve different principles (such as preference or public interest), which makes them independent from other contract issues.
As for Imam al-Shatibi in "al-Muwafaqat," he sees that an "issue" is every legal matter derived from detailed evidence and built upon the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia). He defines it as: "Every legal ruling derived from a legal evidence to achieve an objective of the Sharia." For example: the rulings of fasting are considered one issue because they aim to achieve the objective of piety and self-discipline, and they are derived from legal evidence related to this objective.
As for Imam Ibn Taymiyyah in "Majmu al-Fatawa," he defines an issue as: "The legal matter for which the ruling of Allah, Exalted is He, is sought, whether it is an act of worship or a transaction." He points out that issues are determined based on their subject, and that each independent subject is considered a separate issue. An example of this is: the rulings of marriage are considered an independent issue from the rulings of sale, because each pertains to a different subject and is derived from its own specific evidence.
As for al-Juwayni in "Al-Burhan fi Usul al-Fiqh," he defines an issue as: "Every legal ruling derived from a detailed evidence, and built upon a foundational principle." He emphasizes that issues are derived from detailed evidence and are based on specific foundational rules, which means that each issue is linked to a foundational rule that determines how its ruling is derived. For example: the rulings of purification are considered one issue because they are based on a foundational rule related to purification, which is that the default state of a person is purity until proven otherwise. The evidence is the hadith of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him): "If any of you feels something in his stomach and is unsure whether he has passed gas or not, he should not leave the mosque until he hears a sound or smells something." Narrated by Muslim, this indicates that doubt about an event does not invalidate purity, and the rule: purity is a condition for the validity of acts of worship, and the rule: certainty is not removed by doubt. The rulings of purification are derived as an issue from detailed evidence related to this subject, meaning they are built upon these foundational rules. Under this issue falls: ablution, as it aims to remove minor impurity, and ghusl, which removes major impurity, and tayammum. Al-Juwayni sees that all rulings of purification are interconnected because they serve one objective (achieving the required legal purity for performing acts of worship). This interconnection makes them one issue derived from detailed evidence related to the foundational rule "the default in acts of worship is purity."
