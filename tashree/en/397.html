The Angle of Reward and Punishment:

From the perspective of praise for an action or blame for it, in this world, and from the perspective of reward and punishment for it in the Hereafter.

Moreover, if a person were left to judge actions and things based on praise and blame, the judgment would differ according to individuals and times, as it is not within a person's capacity to render a fixed judgment on them. For this reason, it is God who judges them, not man; it is the Sharia (Islamic law) that judges, not reason, as reason has no role in this judgment from this angle. The observable reality is that a person may judge certain things to be good today, and then judge them tomorrow to be bad, and may have judged certain things to be bad yesterday, only to judge the same things today as good. Thus, the judgment on the same thing varies, and it does not remain a fixed judgment, leading to errors in judgment; therefore, it is not permissible to assign the judgment of praise and blame to reason, nor to man.

It is also not permissible to assign the issuance of judgment regarding praise and blame to a person's innate inclinations, as these inclinations issue judgments of praise on what aligns with them and blame on what contradicts them. What aligns with them may be something that is condemned, such as adultery, sodomy, and enslaving people, while what contradicts them may be something that is praised, such as fighting enemies, enduring hardships, and speaking the truth in situations where severe harm is realized. Thus, making judgment dependent on inclinations and desires means making them a measure of praise and blame, which is certainly a faulty measure; therefore, assigning judgment to them is a pure error, as it leads to incorrect judgments that contradict reality, in addition to the fact that the judgment of praise and blame becomes based on whims and desires, rather than on what it ought to be. For this reason, innate inclinations should not issue their judgments of praise and blame. And since it is not permissible for reason to issue its judgment of praise and blame, nor for innate inclinations to issue their judgment of praise and blame; it is not permissible to allow man to issue judgments of praise and blame. Thus, the one who issues the judgment of praise and blame is God, not man; it is the Sharia, not reason.

Accordingly, legislation and the enactment of laws must be for Allah, the Exalted, not for man or reason! This is established by the evidence of the Sharia, and it is confirmed by rational argument.
