Unlike relative legislations, divine law is based on human nature, emanating from the Creator of mankind, suitable for all times and places, and unifying humanity under a single ethical system.
Detail: The human mind does not grow in a vacuum; rather, it is shaped and nourished by the cultural and social environment in which it lives, and is influenced by customs, traditions, prevailing concepts, and perceptions of good and evil, beauty and ugliness in its society. Therefore, the mind, while producing its legislations, carries within it this environmental and customary bias, which makes its rulings linked to a narrow local context that cannot be generalized to all humanity or considered a universal reference.
One who grows up in a materialistic society that sanctifies utility will legislate laws that elevate profit even at the expense of ethics. One who grows up in a culturally deviant society may legislate laws that permit incestuous marriages or assault on the family in the name of freedom. What one society sees as "a greater benefit" may be viewed by another society as "a pure corruption," transforming law from a means of justice into a source of conflict between nations and peoples, as is currently observed in disputes over laws regarding abortion, homosexuality, or the death penalty.
This confirms that a mind influenced by its environment cannot produce legislation that is comprehensive for humanity, as it is bound to its context. Meanwhile, Islamic law has come with legislations issued by the Creator of all humans, directed at all people, and based on the human nature shared by humanity, as He, Exalted is He, said: ﴾The nature of Allah upon which He has created mankind﴿ [Ar-Rum 30], thus its rulings consider the human being as a human, not as a child of a specific environment or subject to a temporary local culture.
From this, divine law possesses the capacity for application in all times and places, as it addresses the human being in his essence, not in his color, gender, or geographical location, and unifies humanity under a just ethical and legislative system that preserves the dignity of all.
Limitations of the human mind's understanding of reality and its complexities:
Summary:
Human reality is complex and multidimensional.
Reality is not formed solely from material data, but from psychological, spiritual, social, economic, and political factors that are difficult for the mind to grasp all at once.
The mind's inability to comprehensively grasp reality and its interactions.
The mind cannot perceive all elements of reality or accurately link causes to outcomes, which weakens its ability to enact precise and just legislations.
Mental legislations suffer from a lack of balance between values and interests.
The mind fails to achieve a balance between individual and collective needs, between rights and duties, and between justice and facilitation, leading to deficient or conflicting legislations.
Relying on experimentation in legislation leads to catastrophic errors.
