The science of usul al-fiqh (principles of jurisprudence):
Usul al-fiqh is defined as the knowledge of the rules through which one can derive legal rulings from detailed evidence, and these rules themselves are also referred to as usul al-fiqh. Thus, the study of usul al-fiqh is a study of the rules(136), and of the evidence (i.e., the general evidence), meaning it is a study of rulings, the sources of rulings, and how to derive rulings from these sources; thus, usul al-fiqh are the rules upon which the attainment of proficiency in practical rulings from detailed evidence is based(137).
"In other words, the subject of usul al-fiqh pertains to the auditory evidence and how to derive legal rulings from it; consequently, it does not discuss beliefs, but rather examines legal rulings from the perspective of the foundations upon which they are built, not from the perspective of the issues contained within the ruling"(138).
"Indeed, the science of usul al-fiqh has received exceptional attention from the jurists of Islam, both the early and later scholars, due to its significant impact on regulating ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) and deriving legal rulings to address the issues of Muslims and applying rulings to new developments"(139).
The foundations of religion and the principles of rulings are based on certainty by necessity. "The sources of legislation must be definitive, and the sources of legislation in Islam are the places where Allah, Exalted is He, has deposited His commands and prohibitions. Therefore, the study of sources is among the most important studies in usul al-fiqh after the study of the ruler, and the sources of legislation are among the general principles"(140). Thus, the general legal evidences that are the foundations of legal rulings (such as the Qur'an, the Sunnah, consensus, and analogy) must be based on certainty; they are like the foundations of religion, that is, like beliefs, in that they are definitive and not speculative. All principles of Sharia, whether they are the foundations of religion or the foundations of rulings, which are the legal evidences, must be definitive and cannot be speculative; Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And do not follow that of which you have no knowledge. Indeed, the hearing, the sight, and the heart - about all those [one] will be questioned﴿ [al-Isra 36], and He, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And most of them follow not except assumption. Indeed, assumption avails not against the truth at all﴿ [Yunus 36](141).

(136) These are general legal principles that encompass general rulings, not specific rulings, such as: "What is necessary for the obligation to be fulfilled is itself obligatory," "The application of both evidences is preferable to neglecting one of them," "The consideration is for the generality of the wording, not for the specificity of the cause," "The cause revolves with the effect in existence and non-existence," or linguistic rules: such as "A word cannot be shifted from its literal meaning to a metaphorical one except by an indication," or jurisprudential rules that are not from the principles, which are rules that encompass specific rulings that share a certain characteristic, and do not include more than one type of closely related rulings, such as: "Repel the punishments with doubts," "The original state is the presumption of innocence," "The burden of proof is on the one who claims, and the oath is on the one who denies," "The command of the ruler resolves disputes." (Sharh Usul al-Fiqh, Thabit al-Khawaja, pp. 11-12)
