The characteristics and components of Islamic civilization are a miracle! How did Muslims replace it with something else!

The main objective of this book is to study these characteristics in depth, to identify its components and examples, and how to establish them in reality. Therefore, we say, and with Allah's success:

If we reflect on the components of Islamic civilization and its characteristics that distinguish it from other civilizations, we will find that it is characterized by the elevation of its conception of the universe, humanity, and life, and then in embodying this conception within a comprehensive, high, and purposeful doctrinal methodology that explains the universe and life, defines the relationships of humans with the major cosmic truths(86), and their relationships with humans correctly, clarifying to them their purpose of existence, and showing humans their position, responsibilities, and role in life, linking this to what precedes worldly life and what follows it.

Islam has encapsulated that comprehensive doctrinal methodology in a "self-sufficient, independent framework, according to its holistic nature, which addresses human existence as a whole, with all its components and energies, and does not address 'human thought' alone with a cold discourse cast in the mold of mental logic"(87). It thus combined faith and good deeds! It became a vibrant belief, not a dry, cold philosophy,

It was vital concepts that move souls, instilling in their surroundings tranquility, reassurance, influence, and being influenced, contemplation, reflection, and the drive to carry its message, along with absolute confidence in reaching and possessing the absolute truth, and acting accordingly!

Islam also combined in importance that doctrinal conception with the system represented in the way of life. Islam has established for humans responsibilities, duties, and social relationships, along with a philosophy that is fully founded on and emanates from that doctrinal conception, relating to oneself, family, relatives, community, state, and even all of humanity, and the environment, achieving justice and fairness and balancing individual rights with public interest and the common good, distinguished by "extreme precision in building rulings, to the extent that the researcher studying issues of Islamic jurisprudence and the opinions of jurists and their theories feels as if he is before a sensitive scale weighing diamonds, revealing the differences between similarities no matter how subtle and obscure"(88).

Islamic legislation has expanded and encompassed human problems; Islam has addressed problems fundamentally related to the human race, not merely related to Zayd or Ubayd, and it has taken into account the aspect of the treatment of actions, (while the treatment in Western civilization has started from the perspective of achieving freedom and material benefit), and Islamic rulings have been applied to realities. Islamic legislation is characterized by "richness in legal theories in organizing rights and obligations and the interests of society in a manner that encompasses all branches of law known to this day, starting from the relationship of a person with his family from marriage rulings to inheritance and everything in between, and ending with the rulings of international law regulating the relationship of the Islamic state with other nations and states in peace and war.
