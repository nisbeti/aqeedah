They prohibit what Allah and His Messenger have prohibited﴿ [At-Tawbah 29], and if what the Messenger prohibited was indeed the same as what Allah prohibited, without any additional prohibition from his Sunnah, it would be a redundant repetition, and far be it from the Qur'an. Moreover, if what the Messenger prohibited was an ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) of his own that could be mistaken, then why are we bound by it? And if historically it pertains to its context and does not relate to us, what is the value of the general Qur'anic command itself that addresses all people, ﴾O mankind, indeed I am the Messenger of Allah to you all﴿, meaning with a message, and that is from the message, and where is it stated that the rest of humanity is excluded from the generality of this message?
In other verses, opposing his ﷺ command and turning away is considered disbelief, ﴾Say, "Obey Allah and the Messenger; but if they turn away, indeed Allah does not like the disbelievers"﴿. The address of the legislator, which includes legislation in the Qur'an, has also been conveyed in the Sunnah, and it is: the same meanings contained in the words and structures with which the revelation descended, both in wording and meaning (i.e., the Qur'an), or the meanings with which the revelation descended and were expressed by the Messenger ﷺ in his own words or through his actions or silence (i.e., the Sunnah). When he expressed them ﷺ, he did not speak except by revelation that was revealed, and he was under scrutiny (as we elaborated in the section: evidence of the infallibility of the prophets is definitive), to achieve utmost precision in conveying Allah's intent. He was infallible and spoke only the truth; these are guarantees that the words of the Sunnah were formulated under the care of revelation to convey the meanings that were revealed to him, so no word escapes him except with the most precise expression of the meanings that were revealed to him ﷺ. The legal ruling in the technical definition is the address of the legislator concerning the actions of the servants by obligation, choice, or status. It is said: the address of the legislator, and not the address of Allah; to include the Sunnah and the consensus of the companions in terms of being indicative of the address, so that it is not mistakenly assumed that it refers only to the Qur'an; because the Sunnah is revelation from Allah, Exalted is He, thus it is the address of the legislator. The right to legislate differs from the address of the legislator, and the Messenger ﷺ is the conveyer of Allah's legislation disseminated in the Qur'an and clarified by the Sunnah. The consensus of the companions reveals evidence from the Sunnah, so it is the address of the legislator, and consensus is merely the negation of error regarding what consensus has been reached, and a revelation of evidence connected to the revelation conveyed to us in the form of consensus, not a production (not an origination) of it, meaning: its concept is not "to confer the right to legislate" to those with whom consensus has been reached after the cessation of revelation.
The Messenger ﷺ says: "Every matter not in accordance with our command is rejected"(455), this indicates that the origin is to follow the Sharia and adhere to it. Allah, Exalted is He, is the one who legislates for His servants, and He is the one who clarifies for them - Exalted is He - the truth from falsehood, correctness from error, good from evil, benefit from harm, and faith from disbelief; there is no ruling except for Him, Exalted is He, meaning that the right to legislate is confined to Allah, Exalted is He, revealed through revelation in the Qur'an and Sunnah, and there is no one to overturn His ruling, nor is it permissible for us to seek any legislation other than His ruling in any matter.
Thus, the right to legislate is solely and exclusively for Allah; ﴾Indeed, the judgment is only for Allah﴿ ﴾6:57﴿, ﴾12:40﴿, ﴾12:67﴿, and the legislator is Allah alone, and the legislation that Allah has permitted has been revealed through the Qur'an and Sunnah, and they are equal in containing legislation and the obligation to act upon both. What the Messenger ﷺ prohibited is like what Allah prohibited; he prohibited it with the right given to him by Allah to formulate the meanings that were revealed to him in his noble words ﷺ, legislating in accordance with revelation, and the address of the legislator means the address of Allah (and the address is the same meanings contained in the words and structures with which the revelation descended, both in wording and meaning (i.e., the Qur'an) or descended.
