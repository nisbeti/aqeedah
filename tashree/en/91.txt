Emergence of Thinking Methodologies and Principles from the Qur'an and Sunnah:
The methodologies of thought among Muslims did not arise as a mere echo of the thinking methodologies of previous nations, nor as a mere replication of them. Rather, these methodologies emerged due to the Muslims' commitment to the integrity of their beliefs, the preservation of the texts of their religion, its jurisprudence, its hadith, its language, and all that is related to it.
In fact, the thinking methodologies of other nations were not in the minds of Muslim thinkers as they confidently took their steps in establishing their sciences, researching them based on a disciplined foundation of thought, deduction, reasoning, and documentation. Thus, a scientific and cultural movement arose in which Muslims challenged themselves, manifesting in various aspects, each based on methodologies of thought, research, and foundational principles. Perhaps this began at the dawn of Islam with what the Qur'an commanded regarding the use of reason and contemplation, considering it an obligation, and rejecting imitation in belief, deeming it forbidden.
The nature of Islam was also political and pastoral, with its rulings descending upon realities (fiqh) to regulate them with the commands and prohibitions of the Sharia. It was thus necessary for Muslims from the dawn of Islam to derive these rulings from their original sources, according to a disciplined methodology of deduction, which began with memorization and care. Then, in the era of documentation, the process of establishing, regulating, and codifying the principles of all these sciences commenced. The Noble Qur'an commanded the rational examination of the evidence and proofs indicating the foundation of belief, directing humanity's attention to the importance of precise sensory observation for sound thinking, urging the possessor of intellect to think and reflect, and distinguishing between truth and falsehood, certainty and conjecture, lies and disbelief, and faith. The verses indicated that the method of aligning thought with reality is to establish a conclusive rational or textual proof for it. It is obligatory for every Muslim to reach a stage of certainty, knowledge, or faith based on the existence of conclusive evidence. Moreover, Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And do not follow that of which you have no knowledge. Indeed, the hearing, the sight, and the heart - about all those [one] will be questioned﴿ (Al-Isra [36]). This means do not follow what you do not know, and do not say, "I heard" when you did not hear, and "I saw" when you did not see, and "I knew" when you did not know. Indeed, the hearing, the sight, and the heart - all of those will be questioned. And if a disobedient person brings you news, verify it, as Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾O you who have believed, if there comes to you a disobedient one with a report, investigate, lest you harm a people out of ignorance and become, over what you have done, regretful﴿ (Al-Hujurat [6]). And if you do not know, then ask those who know, as Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And We did not send before you except men to whom We revealed. So ask the people of the message if you do not know﴿ (Al-Anbiya [7]). Allah, Exalted is He, also said: ﴾And We did not send before you except men to whom We revealed. So ask the people of the message if you do not know﴿ (An-Nahl [43]).
These are the foundations and principles of a Qur'anic methodology that establishes the use of reason and thought, building belief on evidence, following based on knowledge, and prohibiting following without proof in belief. It clarifies the tools of perception and the attainment of knowledge: the intellect (which is referred to as the heart) (190), as perception transfers to it through hearing and sight, and reasoning, meaning the alignment of judgment with reality, thus attaining knowledge and faith, and proceeding in life with insight. These are the foundations of the rational method of thinking, which is the methodology from which the early Muslims emerged, establishing evidence and proofs, and employing the rational method in thought.
