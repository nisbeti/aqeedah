The Qur'an clarifies the danger of making desires a legislator, warning against the judgment of anyone other than Allah in the most significant matters: the lawful and the unlawful, the truth and the falsehood.
The danger of human legislation when it succumbs to whims
results in laws that serve specific groups and destroy values under flashy slogans, which highlights the urgent need for a divine legislative reference.
Detail: The human intellect is not a pure entity free from subjective influences and psychological and social impulses, but rather it is an entity that is affected by psychological and social influences, where desires intermingle with interests, personal inclinations with societal culture, and political affiliation with intellectual upbringing. This structural subjugation to desires and personal interests, changing agendas, emotions, and sometimes to selfishness, instincts, and passions, along with other tendencies, renders the human intellect unqualified to establish just and comprehensive legislation free from bias.
The absence of desire and inclination is impossible in a group just as it is impossible for an individual. As for its impossibility for the individual, it is because the individual cannot rid themselves of their feelings and inclinations, as they are a part of them. The gathering of individuals does not remove desire and inclination from them; rather, it makes desires and inclinations more pronounced, which necessitates a process of reconciling the multiple desires. From this, it follows that legislation based on "the will of the public" or collective will cannot be free from desire and inclination, and thus the first and most important pillar for achieving the purpose of legislation (justice) is completely absent.
A person may believe that they are ruling by their intellect, while this "intellect" is actually governed by what has been ingrained in it from acquired interests, or by a certain ideological background, or by a nationalist or class bias, or even by momentary utilitarian considerations, or it may respond to the interests of a powerful class that has brought them to power to achieve gains at the expense of society. Therefore, we see that the legislations produced by human minds are often a reflection of the interests of the dominant elite or influential groups, rather than an expression of the public interest or objective justice.
This deviation is not always intentional; it may arise from the unconscious, where the legislator is influenced by a prevailing intellectual trend, media pressures, or a dominant public mood, without realizing the impact of this on the value system upon which his legislation is based. Laws are formed that reflect a purely materialistic vision - as in capitalist countries - prioritizing economic benefit even at the expense of family, virtue, or the environment, or adopting a specific nationalist or classist inclination, or enacting legislation in the name of "personal freedom" that unleashes sexual desires or deviant behavioral patterns, due to the corruption of the intellectual foundation adopted by the legislator. Later, those societies find themselves unable to confront the consequences of the moral collapse and social disintegration that arise from such legislation, resulting in the prioritization of those considerations in their laws at the expense of absolute justice,
The Noble Qur'an has warned against this legislative deviation arising from desires, saying: ﴾So judge between them by what Allah has revealed and do not follow their desires﴿, indicating that following desires in the realm of legislation leads to the judgment of others than Allah in one of the most significant matters, which is the matter of what is lawful and unlawful, right and wrong.
