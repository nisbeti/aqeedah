He is patient in the face of sin, for some rulings are legislated to shape the self, refine it, and purify it, not merely to achieve benefit. Fasting is a means to attain piety, and modesty, restraining anger, and night prayers all elevate the soul, purify it, and bring it closer to Allah.
And there are objectives within the framework of testing, trials, and tribulations, so that Allah may distinguish the strivers among you and the patient ones, and to test the sincerity of your faith. Indeed, trials and difficult obligations are tests of true servitude. Otherwise, how do we understand that a Muslim in previous centuries would undertake the pilgrimage from the farthest reaches of Asia to the Sacred House, traversing perilous paths that took months of arduous travel? The objectives are for purification, cleansing, and sacrifice to please Allah, Exalted is He.
Moreover, there is the framework of the unseen and the Hereafter, and working for it, where you find the fruits of many deeds have no relation to worldly life. For example, the shrouding of the deceased, praying for him, and giving charity on his behalf.
Then there is the framework of identity and distinction, for the Sharia (Islamic law) builds a nation with specific characteristics, prohibiting imitation of the disbelievers and imposing differentiation in dress and worship.
To other such frameworks that regulate the legal rulings, their types, objectives, and the motives for their legislation, therefore: building an understanding of the Sharia must be based on integrative analysis, not merely reducing it to "achieving interests." A human being is not just a body, and the Sharia is not merely a set of utilitarian laws; rather, it is a divine system that educates, tests, purifies, and guides. Any attempt to frame the Sharia outside of these frameworks, confining it to the realm of interests and benefits, may lead to emptying it of its message and transforming it into a worldly law!
The issue of interests and harms has not been viewed from a purely materialistic perspective:
The issue of interests and harms has not been viewed from a purely materialistic perspective, such that it aligns with whims and inclinations, and the limited human estimation of what constitutes benefit or harm. Rather, it has provided humanity with a comprehensive system of life that serves as a way of living, ensuring the proper organization and integration of economic, social, and other relationships. It has defined individual and social responsibilities, and the responsibilities of the state within a comprehensive system that brings happiness and tranquility to individuals, achieves stability for society, and elevates and honors the nation.
The validity of choosing values that are realized through actions:
Moreover, the values that are realized through actions are not always material values; they may also be spiritual, human, or ethical values. Therefore, we find a certain coldness in the West towards actions that embody other values beyond mere material benefit. If a group of them sees an oppressed person being wronged in the street, you find that those who rush to help him are few, and if they do, it is for the Christian or humanitarian values they hold, not for the values arising from the secular Western society itself.
Thus, we see that the perspective on interests should combine the material aspect with the spiritual, ethical, and human aspects, so that legislations are linked to the values they realize, or are part of a system of legislations aimed at creating societal systems that achieve greater benefits, such as the system of zakat, which contributes to the stability and solidarity of society, even if it is wealth taken from the rich, or at least there should be a criterion that makes the interest realized in opinion because its source is knowledgeable, wise, and expert.
