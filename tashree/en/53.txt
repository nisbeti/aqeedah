Islam has taken great care to ensure that this system and this doctrinal conception are embodied in a form of societal norms (a'raf). Deviating from the values, ethics, standards, concepts, and convictions established by the legislator has become a crime addressed by the law on several levels, commensurate with its magnitude and impact. This includes communal responsibilities manifested in advising, guiding, and educating, enjoining good and forbidding evil, accountability, denial, and change (by hand, tongue, or heart), as well as the clear roles of individuals, political parties, and the state in this regard, to the extent of compelling the truth, preventing the company of wrongdoers, and resorting to the sword and the use of force to change the wrong. This extends to the responsibilities of the state regarding punishments, among other frameworks that preserve the core values, standards, and convictions derived from the creed, which form the organizing framework of the Sharia and its rulings, establishing a unique way of life, a system that rectifies and brings happiness to humanity, and a set of norms whose opposite is a condemned and rejected wrong!
The deep-rooted misunderstanding and dominance of those values in the nation have been coupled with its decline and division, undermining the structure of that stronghold, until it became—when it turned away from Islamic legislation and distanced itself from it—prey to the arrows of its enemies, placing the nation in the path of colonial winds and dependency. Nevertheless, the strength of the Islamic creed and the accuracy and precision of Islamic law have had an impact that has allowed the nation and this civilization to withstand earthquakes, calamities, and Crusades and Mongol campaigns, as well as attempts to annihilate it time and again, over long centuries and extended periods that fluctuated between hardship and ease, and the good and bad human application of this divine system. This nation cannot rise again to reclaim its lofty position unless it is keen to nurture that civilization and its components so that it remains lush and green, thereby refining its concepts—once again—into precise formulations, improving the understanding of those values and their role in revival, purifying them from the impurities that clung to them during times of decline, linking every thought within it to revelation, and cleansing it of the concepts and values introduced from other civilizations. The better the Islamic nation achieves this, the more capable it will be of rising and ascending the heights of excellence!
