An integrated system of harmonious and cohesive rulings:

Just as the individual human being elevates himself through the thoughts he possesses about the universe, humanity, and life, and his behavior is guided accordingly, satisfying his instincts and organic needs based on his concepts, so too does society and the state distinguish themselves and rise by the nobility of the foundational thought upon which they are built, which forms the basis for the civilization that shapes a way of life and a system for the state, society, and life;

"And the ruling being discussed here is the legal ruling (hukm shar'i) upon which reward and punishment are based. It is not the ruling that a person finds pleasing, whether by his intellect or his desires, nor is it the ruling that is presumed to bring about benefit as perceived by the individual. It is well known that the intellect is incapable of comprehensively grasping human needs and is even less capable of providing solutions for them. It is also known that a person's intellect may perceive good as evil and evil as good. A person may find pleasing what others find repugnant, and his estimation of benefit lacks complete understanding of the realities of all people's issues, as well as knowledge of the outcomes and consequences of people's actions and behaviors"(255).

(256) Refer to the book Islamic Personality, Volume Three: (Usul al-Fiqh) by Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani (may Allah have mercy on him), Chapter: Bringing about benefits and averting harms are not a reason for Sharia as a whole nor a reason for any specific ruling; it contains important and interesting details.

(257) Note our statement: organizing the bringing about of benefits, because the angle from which we will look is not the bringing about of benefit by any means or legislation, that is, the reason for legislation is not the bringing about of benefit, but rather the organization of relationships in a system that will achieve the fulfillment of human needs regardless of delving into the details: what is the benefit or interest anticipated from the legislation, and what is the harm, because it is a speculative and relative inquiry that sometimes applies and often does not, it is a precarious angle if taken as a criterion for the validity of legislations, so the obligated person capable of lending people with usury at that time may ask: Did the prohibition of usury bring me benefit? If he is deprived of material gain, he will view the legislation as having not brought him benefit, because it prevented him from a means to increase his wealth, thus Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And whatever you give in usury to increase in the wealth of people will not increase with Allah, but whatever you give in zakah seeking the countenance of Allah, those are the multipliers﴿, so increasing your wealth through usury is not the benefit, nor is it true growth, but increasing it through zakah is what truly increases it, but you do not know, and the borrower asks the same question in his mind that his interest may not be fulfilled due to the lack of financing except through usury, or he may rejoice in the prohibition because it has protected him from entering the cycle of usury, and thus the solution and legislation enter into a dilemma due to the disparity in people's perspectives and their estimation of benefit!

(258) Indeed, bringing about benefits and averting harms are not reasons for legal rulings in general, as there is no text indicating that bringing about benefits and averting harms is a reason for legal rulings, nor is there any text indicating that it is a reason for a specific ruling, thus it cannot be a legal reason. Especially since the true benefits and harms are unknown to humans; one may think something is beneficial while it is harmful and vice versa, and thus the judgment on estimating benefits rationally varies, which makes the benefit in itself subordinate to the legal ruling and does not make the legal ruling subordinate to it. Our statement does not mean that the Sharia came to harm creation, but the true benefit and righteousness, which is the essence of Sharia, is not what minds estimate as benefit, but rather the system that will address the need that some have estimated as benefit and others as harm, to let it speak its word in organization, that is, by leaving the estimation of benefit to Allah and establishing the correct legal remedy, and wherever He legislates, there is righteousness and benefit. Meanwhile, you see the Sharia alerting us to places of goodness and righteousness for us, especially those we overlook, such as His saying: ﴾Go forth, light and heavy, and strive with your wealth and your lives in the cause of Allah; that is better for you if you only knew﴿. However, at the same time, it legislates rulings of benefit that are not inherent to the ruling itself; for example, where do you find the benefit in raising the finger during the Tashahhud in prayer, or where is the benefit in His command to the Children of Israel to kill themselves? ﴾And when Moses said to his people, "O my people, you have wronged yourselves by taking the calf, so repent to your Creator and kill yourselves; that is best for you with your Creator"﴿. Thus, the true benefit lies in executing the commands of Allah and is not necessarily in the act that is required to be performed.
