Ninth: Activating a set of integrated rulings to solve problems and produce legislative objectives:

Islam has activated a set of rulings to integrate with solutions to problems and to produce the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia). For example, when it ruled the prohibition of theft and the amputation of the thief's hand, the apparent nature of this legislation alone, if applied in isolation from other rulings, may seem difficult to reconcile with the notion of Islam as a mercy to humanity. However, upon closer examination, we find that Islam has clarified the reasons for lawful ownership and encouraged it, such as through work (it permitted trade and prohibited usury, regulated contracts and their terms, and addressed issues arising from debts, deferred sales, and similar matters), inheritance, gifts, and maintenance, as well as rulings related to providing housing, clothing, medical care, and education for people. It has also mandated that the government ensure people's basic needs and prohibited the state from oppressing individuals and seizing their properties, etc. Thus, the totality of the rulings is organized within a framework that produces mercy and justice. The amputation of the thief's hand is a consequence of his transgression after he has deviated from a comprehensive system of rulings that ensured his needs, as an act of mercy towards him: (a corrective punishment for the sin), and towards society: (a deterrent punishment for the sin, protecting people's wealth and security so that no one dares to steal), and mercy in that it is proportionate to the sin, and so on. Therefore, bringing about benefit and averting harm requires a higher level of thinking than merely addressing a problem and providing a solution; it necessitates thinking within the framework of integrated societal laws that address the problem at its root, ensuring the individual's needs in all circumstances, and aligning with the security and systems of society. This requires the integration of a set of societal systems, which is not evident in human systems; rather, we find them limited to laws that deal only with the problem itself, without paying attention to its impact on the overall systems.

"The legislative methodology encompasses all branches of human life in all its forms and represents an integrated unit that cannot be fragmented; it is not permissible to take some of it and leave others. Taking part of it without the rest distorts this Sharia and misrepresents its reality"(291). From here, the partial acceptance of Islam and belief in some of it while neglecting others creates a disruption in society and in the system governing people's lives, preventing the realization of absolute justice. It may then become difficult to limit individuals by the crimes they have committed.

Islam is a complete, comprehensive, and integrated system whose full application leads to the resolution of human and societal problems. The caliphate is essential to complete the links of this system and to ensure that they support one another for the betterment of individuals, societies, and humanity!
