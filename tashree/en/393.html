From here, what has been said about "suitability" in terms of bringing benefit and repelling harm indicates that a person has labeled actions that harm or are disliked by him as evil, and has labeled actions that benefit or are liked by him as good. An action is not deemed good or evil based solely on dislike and love or benefit and harm. "The ruling being discussed here is the legal ruling that is based on reward and punishment. It is not the ruling that a person finds pleasing, whether through his intellect or his desires, nor is it the ruling that is presumed to bring about benefit as perceived by a person. It is well known that the intellect is incapable of comprehensively understanding human needs and is even less capable of providing solutions for them. It is also known that a person's intellect may perceive something good as evil, and something evil as good. As for what has been said about it being suitable for the actions of rational individuals in customs, we have previously discussed the differences in environments and customs among people, and the differences in intellects and the dominance of desires, which undermines the argument of taking the actions of rational individuals as a basis and evidence for actions.

As for making the Sharia (Islamic law) a proof for what the intellect indicates, it necessitates making the intellect a judge in matters of good and evil, which we have shown to be invalid. And making the intellect a proof for what the Sharia indicates necessitates making the intellect a proof for the legal ruling, while the legal ruling is based on textual evidence, not on intellect. The role of the intellect is to understand the legal ruling, not to serve as evidence for it. From here, good and evil are only legal (shar'i) and not rational (aqli), not only from the perspective of the third consideration, but also from the first and second considerations!
