This is one of the characteristics of Islamic legislation; the penalties in Islamic law are directed against anything that touches upon virtuous morals without depending on the consent of the victim or the absence of harm from the crime; because the aim of protecting morals surpasses the aim of protecting the victim in itself, considering that the first aim relates to the common interests and the public order of society. The mutual consent between the perpetrator and the victim does not make the corrupt sound nor does it permit what Allah has prohibited; thus, the Sharia punishes drinking alcohol, apostasy, immorality, adultery, debauchery, and sodomy regardless of the consent of both parties to the crime, and the ruler has no authority to pardon these crimes, because leniency regarding them leads to the disintegration of morals, the corruption of society, and the disruption of its security and order."(332)
