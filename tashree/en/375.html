The same can be said about empirical science, as its scope is limited to researching the properties of materials and their interactions under natural or unnatural conditions, and its method for reaching results is sensory experimentation, which is conducted by isolating the material from its environment and from the many phenomena that interact with it due to their mixing, and in nature, there are no isolated phenomena. Consequently, the properties and characteristics of this reality will inevitably be influenced by the other surrounding phenomena, which will compel the researcher to attempt to isolate that reality from its surroundings for study, because studying it with all the surrounding factors is extremely complex. Thus, the researcher selects from the phenomena what he believes is most closely related to the influence on the properties of the material and its interactions and focuses on it, neglecting others, and reconstructs the reality in the laboratory by applying the specific phenomena he has chosen to the material to study its behavior. This type of study is far from human behavior! Although it is a method used in what is called "psychology" and "sociology" in studying human behavior, these two sciences have faced prominent scientific criticisms, the most important of which are:

The replicability problem: Psychology faces challenges in replicating study results, as many published results cannot be replicated with the same outcomes in subsequent studies. This raises questions about the reliability of some research.

Publication bias: There is a preference for publishing studies that yield positive or exciting results over those that yield negative or expected results, leading to a distorted picture of scientific data.

Over-reliance on non-diverse samples: Many psychological studies rely on samples of university students in Western countries, which limits the generalization of results to the general population or different cultures.

Bias in data interpretation: Researchers can sometimes interpret data in ways that align with their expectations or theories, which can lead to biased conclusions.

Ethical challenges: Psychologists must face ethical challenges, especially when dealing with sensitive subjects or conducting experiments that may pose risks to participants.

Experimental design issues: Some psychological studies may suffer from issues in experimental design, such as selecting inappropriate variables or not using suitable control groups.

Reliance on outdated theories: Some traditional psychological theories remain influential even though they may not be supported by modern scientific evidence.

Challenges associated with new technologies: With the emergence of new technologies such as artificial intelligence and big data analytics, new challenges arise regarding how to integrate these technologies into psychological research in an accurate and ethical manner.

Measurement challenges: Measuring psychological variables (such as emotions, thoughts, behaviors) can sometimes be difficult and imprecise.

Cultural and social influences: There is a challenge in understanding how cultural and social factors affect behavior and psychological processes, and how results can be generalized across different cultures.
