Ten ways in which Islam addresses human problems with solutions that do not change over time and place:

We will here examine the issues that have made Islam comprehensive and complete, capable of providing treatments and solutions for human problems until the Day of Judgment, a remedy that does not change over time or place. How have the texts of the Qur'an and the Sunnah, "limited in number," been able to address human problems that are "unlimited in number and diversity"? This leads one to grasp some features of the miracle of Islamic legislation!

These issues (ways) are: First: addressing human problems considering the individual as a human being, and Islam has taken into account that the essence of a human is constant, in terms of intellect, psychology, inclinations, and behavior, possessing a vital energy that needs to be fulfilled, and this vital energy is part of the human essence that cannot change; rather, the human needs to organize the method of fulfillment and regulate it, and such regulation is characterized by stability rather than change. Second: the application of the text to all new and varied actions by using terms that have specific connotations, and presenting them in the form of rulings, evidence, and both general and detailed definitions. Third: the nature of the treatments has been characterized by stability, comprehensiveness, breadth, and renewal, along with rigidity and lack of development; as a result of the emphasis on stability in treatments, there was a guarantee of achieving justice and the prevalence of societal values. Fourth: the legislator has established specific (signs) criteria that can be applied to things regardless of their diversity, to serve as measures for the jurist to be able to issue rulings related to what has emerged from discoveries of things. Fifth: it has made the application of those solutions to those conditions and incidents according to the methodology of achieving the relevant criteria, and according to disciplined operational procedures for the process of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) based on well-founded methodologies for inference and deduction; if followed correctly, it would encompass new incidents that arise and continue to revolve within the same balanced systems of society without deviating from them, and its solutions for similar incidents remain firmly established throughout the ages and the passage of time. Sixth: it did not address problems as economic or social issues, but rather as problems related to this human being. Seventh: the legislator adopted a unique approach in solving human problems before they occur: Islamic legislation distributed duties, rights, and responsibilities among the individual, family, community, and state according to ability and capacity,

And those commands and prohibitions, as well as those responsibilities, duties, and rights, were not based on changing, fluctuating, and variable needs according to circumstances, conditions, time, place, environment, and customs. Rather, the conditions, circumstances, incidents, customs, and public interests (maslaha) were regulated by legislations that govern them,

Eighth: The perspective from which legislation is viewed is of utmost importance. If the perspective from which the West has approached their legislation is the realization of freedom and empowering individuals to do what fulfills their freedom, or if it is the realization of material utilitarian interests, then it is an unregulated perspective upon which contradictions in rulings can easily be built. On the other hand, the view of the human being as a human being, a comprehensive and general perspective for addressing all the problems that arise for him since the Qur'an was revealed until the Day of Resurrection, ensures the soundness of governance regardless of time and place, because the essence of human beings does not change, which gives rise to these problems that require solutions.

He did not consider the topic of benefits (masalih) and harms (mafasid) from a purely materialistic perspective, such that it aligns with desires and inclinations, and the limited human estimation of what constitutes benefit or harm. Rather, he provided humanity with a comprehensive system of life that serves as a way of living for them, ensuring the proper organization and integration of economic, social, and other relationships, and defined their individual responsibilities.
