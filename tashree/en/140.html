Concepts regulate social relationships and guide behavior to satisfy instincts and organic needs:

A person in this life acts towards the things that Allah, Exalted is He, has made subservient to him in the universe, in order to satisfy his instincts and organic needs. The behavior of a person is driven by a need or an instinct that compels him to fulfill it. When he turns to Allah, Exalted is He, to relieve his distress, he satisfies the instinct of faith and sanctity. When he eats, he does so to satisfy the hunger of the stomach, and when he is full and eats more, he is merely responding to his desire for possession stemming from the appearance of ownership, in the instinct of survival. When he marries, he fulfills the instinct of procreation, and when the fornicator commits adultery, he also satisfies the instinct of procreation in a wrongful manner, judged according to Islamic belief. And thus,

Every action in this worldly life is driven by an impulse that stems from the need to satisfy a manifestation of an instinct or a bodily need that requires fulfillment; thus, behavior is the actions of a person that he performs to satisfy his instincts or bodily needs.

As for the concepts about things, they are that food, sleep, drink, and breathing, and so on, satisfy the organic needs of the body, and that a woman satisfies a man's instinct, and that fear is a manifestation of the instinct for survival among living beings, and that a person tends to save a drowning individual as a fulfillment of the instinct for species preservation, and so forth. All of these ideas are almost the same among humans, and living beings also share similar needs (i.e., organic needs and instincts) with humans. No rational person would claim that a person is distinguished from another because they love vegetables or that one person is superior to another because they drink a lot of water. However, a person who restricts their thinking about life to such concepts and whose behavior in life, that is, their actions stemming from their concepts about things, is based solely on those understandings of the reality of those things, without connecting them to other ideas about life, is undoubtedly a lowly person. Decline in behavior results from a narrow perspective on individual, familial, or national satisfaction; the broader the perspective on humanity, the more elevated and progressive it becomes!

Therefore, understanding the reality of a thing (its essence, truth, characteristics, and attributes) is referred to the intellect, and it is achieved through observation or by subjecting things to experimentation. The result of these experiments is conjectural, approaching the essence, but it does not reach certainty(244). This arises from the process of linking reality with prior information in the brain, and the production of thought that explains what this reality is! However, the thought resulting from the essence of things is conjectural and does not encompass it definitively, because its material consists of the truths of things and their attributes, and the experiment and measurement in them have a ratio, however small, of error. In contrast, the thought that examines their existence in relation to non-existence is definitive. This thought about things is low and is not suitable as a foundational reference (standard) for establishing legislation that permits and prohibits.

Human beings are distinguished from animals in their satisfaction of instincts and organic needs through reason. While animals are concerned with satisfying their hunger in any way possible, humans are distinguished by their ability to reason. When faced with hunger, for example, they consider the things that can satisfy them, reflecting on these items to arrive at concepts about them: do they satisfy or not? What is their taste? What benefits do I hope to gain from eating them?
