The rules apply to many particulars. For example: the obligation to obey the ruler can only be fulfilled with the presence of a ruler deserving of a legitimate pledge of allegiance, so establishing him is obligatory. The general ruling that constitutes a universal principle is the attribution of a ruling to a term from the terms of universality, and thus it is called universal(271). Therefore, every ruling that falls under the meaning of this term is a particular of this general ruling, not an individual of its individuals; such as the principle "the means to the unlawful is unlawful," and the principle "what cannot be fulfilled except by it is obligatory," and similar ones. In these two principles, the legal ruling, which is (the prohibition), is not attributed to a general term, like the term: sale, for example, but rather to a universal term, which is "the means to the unlawful." And the legal ruling, which is (the obligation), is not attributed to a general term, like the description of carrion, for example, but rather to a universal term, which is "what cannot be fulfilled except by it," and thus it is universal. For example, by examining the evidence of contracts, the jurists have reached a universal principle, which is: "leasing is a contract for benefit in exchange." And legal definitions, "For example, the definition of a legal ruling (hukm shar'i) as 'the address of the Legislator concerning the actions of the servants,' and the definition of a contract (aqd) as 'the connection of an offer (ijab) with acceptance (qabul) in a lawful manner that establishes its effect in its place,' each of them is a universal ruling, because in the definition of the legal ruling, it has been stated about the defined term with a universal word, which is the phrase 'the address of the Legislator'; it is valid for many to share in its concept, as it applies to the address of the Legislator whether the address from which it was derived pertains to obligation (demand for action, and demand for abstention), or choice, or whether it pertains to status, such as the definition of a cause (sabab), and the definition of a condition (shart), and similar matters. Therefore, the definition of the legal ruling as the address of the Legislator ... etc. is a universal definition. In the definition of a contract as 'the connection of an offer with acceptance in a lawful manner that establishes its effect in its place,' it has been stated about the defined term with a universal word, which is the phrase 'the connection of an offer with acceptance'; it is valid for many to share in its concept as it applies to sale, marriage, lease, and partnership, and the definition of lease applies to the leasing of a specific worker, a common worker, leasing a house, leasing a car, leasing land, and like the definition of sale, (it applies to the sale of cars or livestock) and similar matters, so all of them are universal definitions."(272).

(271) We take the general principle (the means to the unlawful is unlawful). If we try to examine the terms of the principle in terms of generality and specificity, we find that the term "means" is a general term and the term "unlawful" is also a general term. Thus, this confirms that the inquiry is not related to the individual term, but rather to the phrase (the means to the unlawful). This phrase contains a general meaning, (which is all means to the unlawful), and its ruling is prohibition. Here, we have understood the intended meaning of the general term (a collection of terms, not an individual term), from which a general meaning is derived, which is (all means to the unlawful), and we have ruled it as unlawful. Under this general meaning, by deriving from it and not by analogy, all unlawful means are included. Here lies the generality, and because it is general, it applies to all branches of jurisprudence.

Another example: The principle that "what is necessary for the completion of an obligation is itself an obligation" contains a universal meaning, which is, "everything that is necessary for the completion of an obligation is an obligation." That is, we have ruled it to be obligatory; thus, all prerequisites of the obligation are obligatory according to this principle, whether they are a cause or a condition, and likewise all universal principles ... ... ,

"Here we did not say the obligation or the means, otherwise they would be a general term - considering that 'the' is not for specification - rather we said 'what the obligation cannot be fulfilled except by it,' meaning we used a compound term, but it includes details, and I have called them details and not individuals because the term is compound."
