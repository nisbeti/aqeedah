And the concept of legislation, who has the right to legislate, that is, who is the ruler, is one of the first and most important discussions in the principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh), as it is one of the most significant topics related to rulings, and the first and most necessary to clarify, knowing who is responsible for issuing the ruling, that is, who is the ruler; because knowing him is essential for understanding the ruling and its type. The ruler here is not meant to be the one in authority who executes everything by virtue of his power, but rather the ruler is the one who has the authority to issue rulings on actions and on things; because what exists among tangible entities does not escape being actions of human beings or things that are not actions of human beings; and since human beings, as they live in this universe, are the subject of inquiry, and since the issuance of rulings is for their sake and pertains to them, it is necessary to rule on human actions and on the things related to them. So who alone has the right to issue a ruling on that? Is it Allah, or is it the human being himself? In other words, is it the Sharia (Islamic law), or is it reason? Because what informs us that this is the ruling of Allah is the Sharia, and what enables a person to judge is reason. So who judges, is it the Sharia, or is it reason? Or it could be that reason is present, and the Sharia is a proof for it, or the Sharia, and reason is a proof for it. (474)
To facilitate the research, we will divide it into: the research on things, such as apples, onions, pork, and wine, what is the ruling on each of them? The legal principle is that the default ruling for things is permissibility(475).
This is different from the discussion on eating pork, and different from the discussion on stealing apples, pressing wine, and sitting with its drinker. All of these latter actions are related to things, so they have their rulings, and this is a discussion on the rulings of actions. Onions are permissible, and eating them before going to the mosque is disliked, apples are permissible, stealing them is forbidden, and buying stolen apples is forbidden!
There is also a discussion on general actions, such as absolute hearing, absolute seeing, absolute walking, and absolute sitting, as well as a discussion on innate actions (af'al jibiliyyah), meaning the actions that a person is naturally inclined to, such as a man gesturing while speaking, or the nature of his walking being that he walks quickly. All of these fall under the discussion of general evidence, and their ruling in Islamic law is permissibility(476).

(475) As for things, which are the objects of actions, their original ruling is permissibility unless there is evidence for prohibition. The default ruling for a thing is that it is permissible, and it is not prohibited unless there is a legal text indicating its prohibition; this is because the legal texts have permitted all things, and these texts are general, encompassing everything. Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾Did you not see that Allah has made subservient to you whatever is in the earth?﴿ [al-Hajj 65]. The meaning of Allah's subjugation of all that is in the earth to humanity is His permissibility of everything in the earth. And He said: ﴾O mankind, eat from whatever is in the earth that is lawful and good.﴿ [al-Baqarah 168], and He said: ﴾O children of Adam, take your adornment at every place of worship and eat and drink.﴿ [al-A'raf 31], and He said: ﴾It is He who made the earth subservient to you, so walk in its slopes.﴿ [al-Mulk 15]. Thus, all the verses that have come regarding the permissibility of things are general, and their generality indicates the permissibility of all things, so the permissibility of all things has come through the general address of the legislator. The evidence for their permissibility is the legal texts that have come permitting everything. If something is prohibited, there must be a specific text for this generality, indicating the exception of this thing from the general permissibility; and from here, the original ruling for things is permissibility. Therefore, we find that when the Sharia prohibited certain things, it specified these things explicitly, as an exception to the general text, as Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾Prohibited to you are dead animals, blood, and the flesh of swine.﴿ [al-Ma'idah 3], ﴾And why should you not eat from that upon which the name of Allah has been mentioned, while He has explained to you what He has forbidden to you, except that to which you are compelled?﴿ [al-An'am 119] (Islamic Personality, Volume Three: (Usul al-Fiqh) by Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani, Chapter: No ruling before the arrival of the Sharia.)
(476) ﴾And He is the One who created for you hearing and sight and hearts; little are you grateful﴿ ﴾Al-Mu'minun 78﴿, ﴾Say, "Travel through the earth and observe"﴿ ﴾Al-Ankabut 20﴿, ﴾He is the One who made the earth subservient to you, so walk in its spacious paths and eat of His provision, and to Him is the resurrection﴿ ﴾Al-Mulk 15﴿, ﴾Did We not make for him two eyes? And a tongue and two lips?﴿ ﴾Al-Balad 8-9﴿, from these verses, the principle is derived that "the default ruling for all actions is permissibility due to the general evidence permitting them," so long as the legislator has not specified anything to exclude it from permissibility, it remains permissible. A person may look with his eyes wherever he wishes, except for what the legislator has prohibited him from looking at, and he may hear what he wishes except for what the legislator has prohibited him from hearing, and so on. These actions were performed by people before the revelation came down, and they engaged in many other actions unless there was evidence indicating them specifically, neither prohibition nor command, rather it was silent about them, and what the revelation is silent about and people do is permissible. This is supported by what Thalabah reported from the Messenger ﷺ that he said: "Indeed, Allah has prescribed obligations, so do not neglect them, and He has set limits, so do not transgress them, and He has prohibited certain things, so do not violate them, and He has remained silent about certain things out of mercy for you, not forgetfulness, so do not seek them out." A sound hadith, which al-Nawawi graded as good in his Forty Hadith, and al-Hafiz Abu Bakr ibn al-Sam'ani also graded it as good in his "Amali." Abu Bakr ibn al-Sam'ani said: "This hadith is a major foundation of the principles and branches of religion." He reported that some said: "There is not a single hadith of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ that encompasses, by itself, the principles and branches of religion more than the hadith of Abu Thalabah." End quote. "Jami' al-Ulum wal-Hikam" (2/70). It was narrated by al-Daraqutni [(in his Sunan) 4/184], al-Dhahabi said: "It is suspended and interrupted; Makhluf did not meet Abu Thalabah" ... al-Muhadhdhab [8/3976]. Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani said: "Its narrators are trustworthy, but it is interrupted" ... al-Matalib al-Aliyah [3/271]. It was also narrated by al-Tabarani in "Musnad al-Shamiyeen" (4/338, under number 3492), and al-Hakim in "Al-Mustadrak" (4/115, under number 7114), and by al-Bayhaqi in "Al-Sunan al-Kubra" (10/12) and others.
And what Abu al-Darda reported from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) that he said: "What Allah has made lawful in His Book is lawful, and what He has prohibited is prohibited, and what He has remained silent about is excused, so accept from Allah His excusal, for Allah would not forget anything." And he recited ﴾And your Lord was not forgetful﴿ [Maryam 64]. It was narrated by al-Daraqutni in his Sunan (2/137), al-Hakim in al-Mustadrak (2/406) (10/12), and al-Tabarani in the Musnad of the People of Sham (3/209) through various chains from Asim ibn Rija' ibn Haywah from his father from Abu al-Darda from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) with this wording. This chain is interrupted, for the interruption is evident between Rija' ibn Haywah and Abu al-Darda. Ibn Hajar said in the biography of Rija' ibn Haywah in "Tahdhib al-Tahdhib" (3/229): "His narration from Abu al-Darda is mursal." End quote. (See: al-Tabsirah by Yusuf al-Sabatin, pp. 16-17), and the verses suffice in the principle of the permissibility of general actions, and Allah knows best.
