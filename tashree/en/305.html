ج- And the relationships with other beings such as animals, plants, and the environment(449)

As for the relationships of man with his Lord and with himself, they generally do not lead to the occurrence of relationships that result in problems in society, except for matters like determining the times for fasting, holidays, pilgrimage, zakat, and jihad, where the ruler has an important role. Unlike transactions and punishments, these are relationships among individuals in society that lead to problems and disputes among people, necessitating resolution by the governing authority in the state. The basis of these transactions is systems related to social matters (the relationship between men and women), economics (distribution of wealth, ownership, and its management), education, foreign policy (relations with other countries), and governance (the presidency of the state, his aides in governance, governors, judges, the army, the administrative apparatus, and the advisory council). Governance, therefore, includes: taking care of the affairs of the subjects, managing their interests, resolving disputes among them, and executing judgments upon them in matters that require obligation. All of this occurs according to practical legal rulings that apply to real situations, preventing (the ruling linguistically means prevention) the transgression of legal rulings for others, to reform people through the law! (The ruling linguistically means prevention for reform) meaning that the legal ruling becomes a binding judgment to be followed, so the ruler adjudicates by it, the scholar derives it, the judge resolves disputes based on it, and the state implements it with the authority it derives from the nation, and with the power it has to enforce it when necessary, thus ruling by the legal judgment and obliging adherence to it wherever obligation is granted. Therefore, governance and judiciary, although their meanings overlap linguistically, their reality extends beyond merely resolving disputes to a broader meaning, surpassing it to take care of affairs according to legal rulings that apply to real situations, and the judiciary is specifically designated for resolving disputes, while the ruler is given the authority of the nation to implement judgments and manage its affairs accordingly!

Accordingly, the meaning of the ruling mentioned in the verse: ﴾And judge between them by what Allah has revealed, and do not follow their desires, and beware lest they entice you away from some of what Allah has revealed to you﴿ [5:49]: encompasses every matter in which a legal ruling has been revealed in the Qur'an and Sunnah that they must refer to in transactions, punishments, zakat, jihad, etc.! The meaning of arbitration in the verse ﴾But no, by your Lord, they will not believe until they make you judge in that which they dispute among themselves, and then find within themselves no discomfort from what you have judged and submit in [full, willing] submission﴿ [4:65]: refers to adjudication and beyond, according to what has arisen among the subjects of the state, and the word "what" is a term of generality, meaning it encompasses everything that arises among people from issues and calamities. The meaning of judgment in the verse ﴾That is the judgment of Allah; He judges between you, and Allah is Knowing and Wise﴿ [60:10]: refers to the command of Allah and His Sharia, and the meaning of judgment in the verse ﴾And He is Allah; there is no deity except Him. To Him belongs praise in this world and the Hereafter. And to Him belongs the judgment, and to Him you will be returned﴿ [28:70]: refers to adjudication, which is the determination of benefit or harm for others, "where the 'lam' in the phrase ﴾And to Him belongs the judgment﴿ is the lam of ownership, and its precedence indicates exclusivity, and the omission of the object of the judgment is indicated by the phrase ﴾in this world and the Hereafter﴿, meaning He has the judgment in both realms. Exclusivity is used in its literal and figurative meanings because the judgment in this world is established for others in a figurative sense, while the judgment in the Hereafter is confined to Allah"(450).

In the section: The Nature of Islamic Legislation, we linked the legal ruling with politics, and we clarified the political nature of Islam, and it is appropriate to connect what we said there with the term "command," to create a natural blend between governance, politics, and command.
