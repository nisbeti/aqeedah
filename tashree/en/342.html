Islamic law (Sharia) is derived from the All-Knowing, the Wise, and it has come with eternal principles and supreme objectives that make it suitable for all times and places, without the need for amendment or abrogation.

Detail: The intellect is not a legislator, as its ability to create legislations and issue rulings on actions, interests, consequences, good and evil, beauty and ugliness, reward and punishment, justice and injustice depends on tools and information and capabilities that are not available to it, such as knowledge of the future consequences of an action or reality, whether it will be good for it or bad for it.

As for why the intellect cannot issue rulings (legislations), it is undoubtedly limited and constrained; it is limited in that its thinking is confined to a specific circle, so it can only think of what is perceived by the senses or what has an evident connection to its effects, otherwise it becomes entangled in contradiction and error. If the intellect goes beyond this circle, it requires tools to protect it from attributing to its judgment the quality of conjecture or error.

It is limited by its need for prior information that explains reality, so it cannot create ideas or judgments except in the presence of that prior information. It is constrained by its need for a standard to judge the validity of ideas, the truth of their results, and their degree of strength, preventing it from falling into fallacies regarding facts.

Upon examining the evidence that fills the universe, we find that when the intellect searches for it, it engages in the circle where it can judge the truth and validity of its results, and it possesses prior information, axioms, and fundamentals to which it refers to elevate its inquiries to the level of certainty and decisiveness, and truth in conformity with the reality being investigated, or keep them in the realm of conjecture, or judge them as false. Therefore, it can issue judgments in matters of belief, such as investigating the existence of the Creator and the validity of the connection between the Creator, the universe, and humanity, whether in terms of organization, creation, or the commands and prohibitions through the messengers.

Whereas when it enters the circle related to issuing judgments on actions, interests, consequences, good and evil, beauty and ugliness, reward and punishment, justice and injustice, it possesses some tools (such as a partial understanding of the nature of reality), but it lacks some other necessary tools for this inquiry (such as the future consequences of this reality, whether this reality will be good or bad for it, whether it contains its interest or harm? And since the interests resulting from performing certain actions may lead to a conflict in the types of interests that will be realized or that may be nullified or pushed away due to their conflict with other interests? Does the interest lie in material benefit, for example? Or in moral, human, or spiritual value? And how does one weigh between them when they conflict? And so on, these are locks that the intellect cannot unlock). Therefore, it must refrain from issuing judgments and legislations due to its lack of those characteristics and information necessary to carry out the intellectual process that results in judgment, legislation, and codification. Thus, when a person allows themselves to legislate, they are not assigning their intellect the task of legislation, but rather they are giving it to their whims that drive them to delve into matters that the intellect cannot pronounce a word about, and to their inclinations that suggest to them that the interest is whatever aligns with it. When we expect the intellect to legislate, because it is one of the greatest tools and most wondrous creatures that Allah, Exalted is He, has created, we are like someone who expects a car of utmost perfection, beauty, and precision to fly in the air, without possessing the tools required for the process of flying.
