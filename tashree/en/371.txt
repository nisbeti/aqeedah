What is the effect of the environment on behavior? By "environment," we mean the surrounding context shaped by prevailing ideas, treatments, and perspectives (customs, traditions, and norms), not just homes and shops. The presence of a person in an environment built on certain concepts, such as liberation and utilitarianism, leads to behavior based on this utilitarianism. However, it may not take long before one recognizes the corruption of such influence and discards those ideas. Similarly, a person born in a Muslim environment derives their concepts from their surroundings, while a Westerner derives their utilitarian concepts from their society, and acts accordingly. This is a tangible observation. For example, people in Egypt are characterized by specific behavioral norms that they have inherited, which have no equivalent in the Levant. Thus, the behavior of those living here is influenced by those living there!
Is the influence resulting from customs, traditions, and the environment a guiding force for correct behavior, or should the arrows of legislation and thinkers be directed towards critiquing those ideas, perhaps even preventing their interference in behavior due to their uncertain source of soundness? There is no doubt that ideas arising from customs, traditions, and norms, like any ideas, must be weighed against the scales of Sharia, and must emanate from Sharia to be accepted. Many customs prevail in societies that are rooted in Sharia, passed down through generations. As for those that do not originate from Sharia, they are akin to other pre-Islamic ideas and must be discarded and changed. This type of thinking arises from the intellect, and we have conclusively proven, beyond any doubt, the inability of the intellect to legislate. This refutation applies to all ideas whose source is not Sharia.
However, the codification of laws that bring happiness to humanity and elevate them, and the establishment of legislation that correctly resolves their life problems, steering their efforts away from corruption on earth, makes such ideas stemming from the environment the last resort. Many of these ideas are influenced by customs and traditions that are often outdated and governed by ignorance and backwardness, inherited by children from their ancestors, without considering their suitability for solving their problems or their effectiveness in their worldly pursuits. Moreover, these ideas are products of minds that contradict each other in determining what is good and what is corrupt, in defining the true interest of humanity, and in achieving justice and upholding truth. Submitting to human legislation does not yield the correct solutions for them. For instance, someone raised in an environment that permits same-sex marriage and considers it lawful, whose thoughts are influenced by that environment—meaning their judgment is affected by their existence in that environment—will not have sound legislation influenced by this judgment merely because they are a product of their environment. Instead, it will be judged as abhorrent and rejected. Therefore, the effect of the environment does not guarantee the validity and goodness of the judgment, which makes legislation based on it deeply distant from being suitable for humanity in this world, prompting thinkers to target these ideas as the primary focus of their reform efforts.
Conversely, being mired in the filth of this corrupt reality severely hinders human advancement and revival, as the corrupt reality alters fundamental revolutionary ideas about the universe, humanity, and life. There is no place in this collection of ideas for the inherited customs and traditions of ancestors that children find themselves in.
It is indeed true that the environment, customs, and traditions have an impact on behavior. However, the legislation intended for the betterment of humanity must not be influenced by the environment, neither closely nor remotely. This is what we aim to explore in this research, noting that it is not the place that influences but rather the ideas present in that environment. This is akin to the creed that a person adopts as a measure.
