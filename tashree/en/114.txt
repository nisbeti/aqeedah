Finally, if the issue being researched is independent, he may follow the principles he has accepted as mentioned in the previous details. However, if the issue is part of a larger matter, such as the issue of prayer, he must strive in all matters of prayer to have his opinion accepted in all those rulings. If he limits his research to a specific aspect of prayer, such as the discussion of the nullifiers of ablution, then his research is not considered valid in terms of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) and taqlid (following a jurist), and it is not permissible for him to follow his own ijtihad, just as it is not permissible for a follower to imitate him in that specific aspect. This is because such imitation would lead to the necessity of following other jurists in the remaining issues of prayer, in which he has not exercised ijtihad, which would result in a conflict of principles, and this is not permissible in the realm of ijtihad and taqlid.
