Fatal scientific errors have underpinned laws and rulings for decades in the most advanced Western countries, leading to catastrophic results!

Among these are, for example, scientific errors in the use of evidence in crimes such as DNA testing or fingerprinting. An example of this is what was reported on the BBC English website: "Fatal errors in the results of relying on forensic tests that led to wrongful convictions, such as reliance on fingerprints, hair, DNA, and other evidence." For instance, in a case where Kerry Robinson was accused of a crime based on DNA testing, two agents from the Georgia Bureau of Investigation stated that Kerry could not be excluded from being implicated in the crime. The same evidence was presented to seventeen other analysts who reached different conclusions: only one of them arrived at the same result, while four stated that the results were insufficient, and twelve declared that he could be excluded from being a criminal(525). In Canada, a serious issue also arose regarding the use of hair samples to verify whether parents were using drugs or were addicted to alcohol, thus questioning their suitability to care for their children. This test was conducted on more than 35,000 individuals by 2010, resulting in fifty children being taken from their parents and placed with other families for care. The parents were prohibited from contacting their children, only to find out many years later that the test results were incorrect and not suitable for the purpose for which they were intended. This truth was uncovered in late 2017. This testing was conducted at a children's hospital in Toronto, with the aim of protecting children from the dangers of parental addiction! (526) It led to the destruction of families, wrongful accusations against some parents who were innocent, tarnished reputations, shattered mental health, and children being moved to unfamiliar families.

The examples similar to the errors of forensic medicine are too numerous to compile in a single book, and these are examples of reliance on highly advanced scientific research that has led to such disasters. So how can one rely on the perspective of a lawyer or a judge when they legislate and seek to ascertain the interest or suitability of legislation for society? There is no doubt that the results will be even more catastrophic, and the errors in judgment will become apparent dozens of times, changing again and again, while society becomes their field of experimentation! So where is justice and equality? And where is the true realization of people's interests?
