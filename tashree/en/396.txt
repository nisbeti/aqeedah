Humanity is neither called good nor evil; it is only called killing. The designation of being good or evil comes from an external description. Therefore, killing an aggressor is good, while killing a citizen, a treaty-bound individual, or a person under protection is evil. The first killer is rewarded, while the second killer is punished, even though they both committed the same act without distinction. Rather, good and evil arise from the factors that drive a person to perform the act and the objective they aim to achieve by doing so. The factors that motivated the person to act and the goal they seek are what determine the description of the act as good or evil, whether the person likes it or dislikes it, and whether they derive benefit or harm from it.
Thus, the description of an action as good or evil pertains to the law (sharia) and not to humans. This is affirmed by the saying of Allah, Exalted is He: ﴾So whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it﴿ (al-Zalzalah). On the Day of Judgment, the Almighty will hold people accountable for an atom's weight of their deeds, describing them as good or evil, and He is the One who initially determined their goodness from their evil. He will hold them accountable for following what He commanded and refraining from what He prohibited. Therefore, the description and judgment of good and evil belong to the law (sharia) and not to reason.
