The ruler — that is, the imam, or the caliph — is entrusted with the responsibilities of caring for the affairs and interests of the ummah (community), and it is made clear that he is responsible for them, responsible for establishing them, and responsible for his subjects and what he has done in caring for them. Here, "responsible" means the responsibility of care, and its meaning is that Allah, Exalted is He, will question him about the duties of caring for the affairs of the ummah and what he has done in that regard. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) made every overseer of the rights of others a guardian over them. The term is derived from care and consideration. And Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾Men are the protectors and maintainers of women, with what Allah has preferred some of them over others and with what they spend of their wealth.﴿ [al-Nisa 34], meaning they are responsible for fulfilling their needs and caring for their affairs, and they will be held accountable for that. Moreover, the Islamic ummah bears the responsibility of bearing witness against other nations; the role of the ummah is to be a witness over the nations by establishing the proof against them through a good understanding and application of Islam within itself, and by conveying it to the nations, and compelling the nations to submit to the system of Islam, not to submit to its creed by force, but rather to compel the nations to submit to the system of Islam. This is the meaning of its guardianship over the thoughts of the nations! ﴾And thus We have made you a middle nation, that you may be witnesses over the people and the Messenger may be a witness over you.﴿ [al-Baqarah 143], meaning that Islam has mandated responsibility for the family, the neighbor, society, and humanity, from the head of state to the shepherd who tends his sheep on the slopes of the mountains and in the valleys. This responsibility expands and contracts according to the party addressed by the obligation. If the addressee is an individual who can perform the required action alone, such as performing prayer, fasting, pilgrimage, reading the Qur'an, or providing for one's parents, then the responsibility is individual, as it falls within the limits of his abilities and authorities. However, if the addressee of the obligation is a group such that the required matter cannot be accomplished except by them or by someone acting on their behalf, such as applying the rulings of Islam in the affairs of life, or the responsibility of conveying the Islamic message to non-Muslims, or jihad in the way of Allah, or holding rulers accountable, or establishing the caliphate, or enjoining good and forbidding wrong, then the responsibility is collective.(373)
It is noteworthy that when Islam assigns a responsibility to a Muslim, it considers providing him with the means to fulfill that responsibility. For example, when it assigned a man the responsibility of caring for the affairs of women for whom he is a guardian, it assisted him by granting him, in certain cases, double the share of inheritance compared to that of a woman, so that he can fulfill his duty of providing for them financially. Furthermore, the obligations in general take into account the physical and psychological capacities of both men and women, leading to some differences in obligations due to those distinctions. When the ruler is entrusted with the responsibility of caring for the affairs of the subjects, he is aided by appointing assistants and placing governors over the provinces to help him in properly managing the affairs of the subjects in a vast state. Nevertheless, he still bears the responsibility of overseeing the proper care of these subjects by those he appoints and ensuring their proper selection.
Dr. Abdul Rahman Habankah al-Maydani says: "Conditions for establishing responsibility: The first condition is that the employer must be capable of bearing responsibility: The legislator has defined the capacity to bear religious responsibility that entails punishment in the Hereafter as being based on reason and maturity."
The second condition: that the action is a voluntary action: meaning it originates from the will of the doer. Whenever this condition is not met, responsibility for it is nullified, such as in cases of tremors or the movements of a sleeper.
The third condition: The intention must be present in the action, the intention and purpose, for what results from it, whether good or evil. If the doer has an intention or aim other than that, then the true responsibility before Allah will be according to his intention and aim, not according to the outward appearance.
