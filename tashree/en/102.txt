Ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) is based on the science of usul al-fiqh (principles of jurisprudence), which consists of measures, rules, a method of thinking, and a research approach, aimed at ensuring that its outcomes—namely, the issues of fiqh (Islamic jurisprudence)—are based on a correct method of inquiry that protects against error, or are confined within a framework according to precise criteria derived from the principles of jurisprudence, allowing the researcher to arrive at a conclusion that is acceptable according to Islamic law. It is said that he has exerted effort (ijtihad), and has reached a preponderance of evidence that the ruling of Sharia on the matter is such-and-such, and the result of his ijtihad is acceptable according to Islamic law.

(207) The disruption in understanding the speaker's intent arises from five possibilities: polysemy, transfer, metaphor, ellipsis, and specification. If the possibility of polysemy and transfer is negated, then the term is designated for a single meaning. If the possibility of metaphor and ellipsis (i.e., estimation) is negated, then the intended meaning of the term is what it was originally designated for. If the possibility of specification is negated, then the intended meaning of the term encompasses all that it was designated for, leaving no disruption in understanding, thus the intended meaning from the auditory evidence is understood. This is in relation to the preponderance of evidence because it is sufficient for deriving the legal ruling, meaning that if these five possibilities are negated, nothing remains to disrupt the conjecture, and thus the legal ruling is understood. However, to avoid disruption in certainty, which is necessary for beliefs, it is not sufficient to merely negate these five possibilities; that is, it is not enough to argue with auditory evidence regarding beliefs to achieve certainty by merely negating these possibilities, but other conditions must accompany them. For auditory evidence does not yield certainty except after ten conditions, which are these five plus the negation of abrogation, precedence and postponement, changes in grammatical case, inflection, and logical opposition. If these ten are negated, nothing remains to disrupt certainty, and thus the auditory evidence yields certainty and can be used to argue for beliefs, and by extension for legal rulings, so its indication then becomes certain, in addition to its establishment being certain.
The conflict among the five possibilities: polysemy, transfer, metaphor, ellipsis, and specification, occurs in ten forms, and its criterion is that each one is taken with what follows it. Polysemy opposes the remaining four, which are transfer, metaphor, ellipsis, and specification. Transfer opposes the three remaining, which are metaphor, ellipsis, and specification. Metaphor opposes the two remaining, which are ellipsis and specification. Ellipsis opposes specification. Thus, these are ten forms. (208) How the Arabs express the designated terms: The term in Arabic can either be literal or metaphorical. Most usages in Arabic are metaphorical.
The Arabs used the following sources to express designated terms: First: the literal meaning, with its three categories: linguistic, customary, and legal; Second: the metaphor, to express imaginations and comparisons; Third: the Arabization, to express names of things and proper names; Fourth: derivation, to express meanings.
The literal meaning: is the terms that were designated to indicate what is in the mind of meaning; the literal meaning: is the term used for what it was designated for in the convention of communication. If the designated term is used for the meaning it was originally designated for by the people of the language, namely the pure Arabs, this literal meaning is called linguistic reality, such as: nazr (a little), dhumah (blackness). If the designated terms are used for a meaning other than what they were designated for, meaning they were transferred from their original linguistic meaning to another meaning, then if the transfer was due to custom or convention, it is called customary reality, such as dabbah (creature), which was originally designated in the language for anything that moves on the earth, thus encompassing humans and animals, but the customary meaning for the people of the language has specified it to four-legged beings, abandoning the original meaning. Likewise, al-gha'it (excrement) is originally a linguistic term for a low-lying area of land but became commonly known by convention for the impure excretion. The first, namely dabbah, was designated for a general meaning and then specified by the custom of the people of the language with some of its names, while the second (al-gha'it) was a name designated for one meaning and became well-known for another meaning.
The customary reality has two types: the first is the linguistic customary reality, which is what the pure Arabs have agreed upon; the second is the specific customary reality that is agreed upon by the people of each science, such as their specific terminologies, like the terminology of grammarians on nominative, genitive, and accusative, or when we say that faith is the firm belief that corresponds to reality based on evidence, which is not specific to the pure Arabs. If the reason for the transfer is legal, it is called legal reality, such as prayer, which in its original language means supplication.
Metaphor: The categories of metaphor: and what has been revealed to me (see: The Proverbial Example in the Writer's and Poet's Literature by Izz al-Din ibn al-Athir) through correct observation is that metaphor is divided into two categories: expansion in speech and comparison; and comparison: is establishing a similarity between two or more things and intending their sharing in one or more attributes using one of the tools of comparison, such as: Khalid is like a lion in bravery. Comparison is of two types: complete comparison and ellipsis comparison: complete comparison is when both the compared and the comparison are mentioned, and ellipsis comparison is when only the comparison is mentioned, and it is called a metaphor. This name is designated to differentiate it from complete comparison, otherwise both can be referred to as comparison, and both can also be referred to as metaphor due to their shared meaning. The second type of metaphor is: metaphorical borrowing. It is when you borrow for something what is suitable for it, thus placing the borrowed word from another context. For example, in borrowing body parts for something that is not an animal: the head of the matter, the head of the capital, the face of the fire, the eye of the water, the eyebrow of the sun, the nose of the mountain, the nose of the door, the tongue of the fire, the hand of time, the wing of the road, the liver of the sky, the trunk of the tree. The third type of metaphor is the metonymy: which occurs when the relationship between the meaning used and the original meaning is not based on similarity. An example is: ﴾They put their fingers in their ears﴿, where not every finger is in the ear, thus the whole is mentioned while intending the part. The fourth type is the synecdoche: which is when the necessary meaning of the term is intended while allowing for the possibility of the literal meaning, and it is then called a synecdoche: for example, "the sleeper of the morning," where the necessary meaning intended is that we are talking about the woman who is served in her home, while the literal meaning may be that the woman sleeps and delays her sleep until the morning.
