While rights in the West have turned into burdens that an individual can rid themselves of whenever the eyes of the law are absent or its guardians are negligent, Islamic law has established human rights on the basis that humans are honored due to Allah's (Exalted is He) honoring of them. This honor, and the elevation in their ranks—after prohibiting any reasons for discrimination among people based on color, gender, origin, or lineage—has been linked to the worship of Allah and piety, and to the nobility of thought as one lives life aware of their purpose in it, being a positive reformer, not a corrupter, regulating their behavior by their doctrinal standards. Thus, a person can elevate their rights in accordance with the nobility of their faith and actions. Islamic law has granted the human soul its sanctity, whether Muslim or dhimmi (non-Muslim living under Islamic rule), stemming from the fact that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) imposed punishment on disbelievers just as he did on Muslims. The Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) killed a Jew as punishment for killing a woman, and he was brought a Jewish man and woman who had committed adultery, and he stoned them. The people of the dhimma (protected non-Muslims) have upon us the same protection as Muslims, as the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: “Whoever kills a person under a covenant with Allah and His Messenger has violated the covenant of Allah, and he will not smell the fragrance of Paradise, though its fragrance can be detected from a distance of forty years.” The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) was brought a Muslim who killed a Jew, and he killed him, saying: “We are more deserving of fulfilling our covenant.”(421) Islam has legislated what ensures the preservation of human life and what honors it, as Allah (Exalted is He) said: “And We have certainly honored the children of Adam.” It has prohibited killing and aggression against it, in order to preserve human life, and has established retribution for it, as Allah (Exalted is He) said: “And there is for you in retribution [a saving of] life.” And He said: “Indeed, Allah will punish those who torture people in this world.” It has prohibited everything that leads to harming the human soul, whether Muslim or dhimmi. It has prohibited adultery and slander, in order to protect honor and dignity, establishing the punishment for slander as eighty lashes, and considering the slanderer a liar whose testimony is not accepted. Islam has established the preservation of lineage, encouraging marriage, maintaining genealogies, and promoting the proper upbringing of children, making the family a fundamental building block of civilization. Islam has also established the preservation of the mind, prohibiting everything that could impair its function, such as drinking alcohol, using drugs, and practicing sorcery, and has legislated punishments for those who engage in such acts. Islam has established the preservation of wealth, prohibiting the taking of others' property except with their consent, and has legislated rules to protect wealth and individual ownership, prohibiting theft and establishing the punishment of amputation for the thief, prohibiting the destruction of property, and instituting guardianship over the foolish, insane, and minors to protect their wealth. It has laid down specific rules for public ownership, state ownership, and individual ownership. Islam has established the preservation of security, legislating the punishment for highway robbery against those who cut off roads and assault honor, property, or lives. Allah (Exalted is He) said: “Indeed, the penalty for those who wage war against Allah and His Messenger and strive to spread corruption in the land is none but that they be killed or crucified or that their hands and feet be cut off from opposite sides or that they be exiled from the land.” It has legislated deterrent discretionary punishments for those who disrupt security through sedition, terrifying rumors, and national or sectarian strife, or others. It has commanded the preservation of the state, prohibiting rebellion against it except according to specific rulings, to prevent fighting, division, and weakness, as the state is an entity that implements the laws of Sharia and fights the enemy, rebels, and aggressors.(422)
