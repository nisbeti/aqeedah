The second section of the research: The intellectual aspect: Examining the ability of humans to legislate

Who is the ruler? Who has the right to legislate? Is it for Allah, Exalted is He, or for humans?

We have established a conceptual foundation for this question in the executive summary of our book: "The Black Box of Western Thought: Islam, Democracy, Secularism, and Liberalism Compared to Intellectual Foundations," and in its body, we have provided a statement of the complex intellectual problems that arise from placing legislation in the hands of humans. Here we present the solutions that restore the matter to its correct position. What are the consequences of returning legislation to the Creator instead of humans? And why must we do this? Why is it imperative to take Islamic law (sharia) in its entirety, with its miraculous characteristics, as a methodology for the reform of humanity, the establishment of truth, the implementation of justice, and the proper organization of life?

Any state in the world is based on two fundamental principles: sovereignty and authority(478). Sovereignty refers to the entity that possesses the supreme power in the state (the one with the final say in disputes and discussions on various matters) in terms of having the authority to legislate, either through a mandate from the public wills or through the management of the public will by specific laws and regulations imposed by that entity, and subjecting the actions of the wills to that sovereignty in all that is issued by the sovereign entity in terms of legislation, which reflects on the application of rulings in all relationships, in individual behavior, and even in the relationship with things. This sovereignty has, since the emergence of nations, been either for Allah or for humans; and when sovereignty is for humans, it takes various forms, as it may be concentrated in a person like Pharaoh, who said ﴾I am your most exalted lord﴿, ﴾I do not know of any deity for you other than me﴿, or it may reside in the ruling party, such as the Communist Party in the Soviet Union. Before the French Revolution, it was in the hands of the Church, invoking what is known as divine right, and then after the revolution, it shifted to "the people," represented by the legislative council. In reality, those who legislate are a group of legal scholars and constitutional jurists, and the legislative council votes on their legislation; the people do not legislate as is commonly assumed. Generally, despite the different forms of sovereignty, they all agree on granting the authority to legislate to humans. After Allah sent Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him), sovereignty was confined to Allah alone, and He established His state on this basis, considering any ruling contrary to this foundation as a tyrant that must be disbelieved in. From the above, it becomes clear that the authority to legislate and judge actions has two sources, and there is no third: either for Allah (the sharia), which is what was brought by the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), or for humans (the intellect(479)/ desires) with different names(480).

(478) Authority in Islam belongs to the nation. It is said in the language: "Sallatuhu," meaning he granted him authority and power, and "Sallatuhu 'alayh," meaning he enabled him and governed him in it. "Tasalat 'alayh" means he dominated, enabled, and controlled. Authority is power and coercion, and the meaning of authority is the one who has the right to control and dominate in the state. Judgment, kingship, and authority are synonymous, and it is the power that executes rulings. Authority can only be realized through two matters: the first is the care of interests, meaning the general interests of the people (the system of governance and state systems) through specific rulings, and the second is the power that protects the subjects and executes the rulings, meaning security.

(479) By examining the Holy Qur'an, which commands the use of intellect, thinking, reflection, and contemplation to reach an understanding of the truths of the universe and to infer the correctness of belief, we find that it does not condemn the intellect even once. When some people dared to legislate and permit the forbidden, they followed their desires. Therefore, the dilemma is: to whom does legislation belong, to Allah or to desires? It is not said to whom the right to legislate belongs: to Allah or to the intellect!
