The legitimacy (shar'iyya) is, at the same time, distinguished according to the jurisprudential school, differing in the way of dealing with evidence and some rules of deduction.
Therefore, it is necessary for the mujtahid (jurist) to ensure that his ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) is connected to a well-established science of usul (principles), and it is necessary for the muqallid (follower) to only follow someone whom he trusts has a connection of his ijtihad (or his informing him about the legal ruling) to a well-established science of usul (such as following a jurist of a particular school, or following a scholar whom he knows has complete and well-established principles and adheres to them in his ijtihad in a systematic manner, and who is a mujtahid in all rulings of the specific issue he is asked about and follows him in all rulings of that issue (such as prayer, for example)).
Fourth: In the era of the Companions, and before the corruption of the language, that is, before the end of the second century of the Hijra, the mujtahids did not need to establish specific well-defined rules for understanding the legal texts, due to their natural linguistic ability to understand the texts, and due to their proximity to the time of Islam, and the spread of Islamic life and its deep integration into the customs and relationships of society, regulating the behavior of Muslims, and the spread of jurisprudential schools and the known opinions of jurists among them, which ensured that their ijtihads were based on a broad knowledge of the texts and their meanings, and their applications in practical life, and their relevance to events. However, the situation changed after the corruption of the Arabic language, the spread of lies, and the fabricated texts attributed to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), and the introduction of some customs of other peoples through conquests and their mixing with customs based on Islam. It thus became necessary to establish rules and create sound methodologies that lead to purifying the hadiths from foreign elements, and to know the abrogating and abrogated texts, and to regulate the understanding of the language and its rules, and to regulate the principles of jurisprudence to deal with different types of evidence and to control the process of deduction, to ensure a correct understanding of the legal ruling and its derivation. Indeed, what cannot be fulfilled without it is an obligation, so it is essential to distinguish between the era before the corruption of the language and after it in dealing with the issue of ijtihad and taqlid (following).
It is true that the engagement of jurists in jurisprudence before that era and after it resulted in three types of mujtahids: the absolute mujtahid, the mujtahid of the school, and the mujtahid of the issue. It is also true that the mujtahid of the school, although he follows his school in the method of ijtihad and in the principles upon which his school is based, may arrive at ijtihads that contradict the opinions of the school or may agree with them. He is thus bound by the principles and method of understanding, not merely by following opinions like the muqallids. He is a mujtahid, but it is necessary to regulate the reality of the mujtahid of the issue (after the era of the corruption of the language). However, before that, it is essential to understand what the meaning of the issue is, what its framework is in the principles of jurisprudence, and what effect that has on the commitment of the mujtahid to specific principles:
Imam al-Ghazali in "Al-Mustasfa" sees that the issue encompasses everything related to a single objective and is researched using a single methodology. He defines the issue as: "A subject that brings together what needs to be researched to achieve its legal goal." Al-Ghazali indicates that the issue is not a partial matter, but rather a comprehensive framework in which the subject is viewed as an interconnected unit researched using a single set of principles. An example of this is the topic of "purification," which is considered a single issue that includes ablution, washing, and tayammum, where these rulings are researched using a single methodology such as the principles of the Shafi'i or Maliki schools.
Imam al-Amidi in "Al-Ihkam" believes that the distinction between issues depends on the independence of the cases from each other in terms of objective or evidence. If the objectives of the rulings differ, they are considered different issues. If the evidence from which the rulings are derived varies, they are separated into independent issues.
