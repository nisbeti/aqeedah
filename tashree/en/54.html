The framework in which doctrinal concepts in Islam were cast:

The method of thinking and reasoning among the Companions (Sahaba) and the Successors (Tabi'in) in the first decade after the blessed Hijra was characterized by reliance on linguistic intellectual instinct, and it depended on an influential discourse that combined rational foundations with addressing human nature, prompting individuals to adhere to these rational proofs that resonate with their innate disposition and feelings. This approach established a creed intended to serve as an intellectual leadership for humans in life, shaping the distinctive civilizational identity of the Islamic nation, possessing a specific vision of life based on a set of social values, knowledge, beliefs, arts, ethics, and traditions; that is, on a creed from which legislation emerged that shapes life, manifesting in a way of living, and reflecting in a set of values accepted by society, and standards and convictions governed by the state, documented within a legal framework represented by a constitution and legislation that safeguard, protect, and implement those convictions, standards, and values, until they transform into established societal traditions and customs, which gives the Islamic nation a particular character, and a cognitive, behavioral, and value-based coherence that ensures happiness and tranquility for individuals.

The creed represented that divine concept(104) comprehensive(105) (answering the questions that explain the universe, humanity, and life in a holistic manner, thereby shaping the individual's concepts about life, that is, his perspective on life) that is comprehensive, purposeful, balanced, elevated, and sound regarding existence, defining human relationships with the great cosmic truths(106), and their relationships with humans, in a correct manner, clarifying to him his purpose of existence, and showing him his position, responsibilities, and role in life, linking that to what precedes this worldly life and what follows it, in a way that guarantees influence in the societal relationships stemming from or built upon that concept, "a self-emergence, not contrived"(107), an emergence that produces a behavioral methodology and a system of life, that is, a way of living, and is manifested in adopting it as an intellectual leadership through which the individual navigates the challenges of life, advancing or retreating, according to what it determines for him as right and wrong, good and evil, or truth and falsehood, and responsibilities and duties.

He is the one who placed in this concept the characteristics that meet these evolving needs within this framework." Characteristics of the Islamic concept and its components, Sayyid Qutb.

(105) It is, therefore, complete and whole. It does not accept enhancement or completion, nor does it accept "spare parts" from outside. It is the creation of Allah, so nothing from the creation of others can harmonize with it. Humanity cannot add anything to it, nor can it always adjust it forward. It came to add to the heart and mind, and to life and reality. It came to awaken all of humanity's potentials and capabilities, releasing them to work in complete positivity, while also maintaining control and guidance, yielding its utmost good fruits, safeguarded from dispersion in fields other than its own, from being hindered in revealing its essence, from deviation from its nature and direction, and from corruption by any factors of corruption. It does not need— in all of this— to borrow from outside, nor to have blood other than its own! Nor to have a methodology other than its own. Rather, it necessitates that it be unique in human life, with its concepts, inspirations, methodology, means, and tools. So that human life aligns with the life of the universe—in which it exists—and does not collide with the movement of the universe, causing it damage and destruction!" Characteristics of the Islamic concept and its components, the martyr Sayyid Qutb, may Allah have mercy on him.
