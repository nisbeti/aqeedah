The legislator has provided the text in this manner and left it to the human intellect to struggle, strive, and exert utmost effort to derive rulings on the new and varied issues from these texts. He has made ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) not only permissible but also an obligation of sufficiency (fard kifaya) that no era should be devoid of. If an era is devoid of mujtahids (jurists capable of independent reasoning), then all Muslims have sinned.
However, this breadth in the Sharia (Islamic law) does not mean that it is flexible enough to apply to everything, even if it contradicts it, nor does it mean that it is evolving in such a way that it changes with time. Rather, it means that the texts are broad enough to derive multiple rulings and that the rulings are broad enough to apply to many issues, indicating that there is no legislative vacuum.
Furthermore, this does not imply that this flexibility and evolution allow one to take any ruling they desire from it, as it only provides what is contained within it based on the wording or the meaning implied by the wording, and does not provide what cannot be understood as a meaning in any of the accepted indications. The derivation from the evidence, that is, ijtihad, is based on extracting what is in the text from its explicit or implicit meaning, or from the indication of the text, according to the three known indications (the indication of correspondence, the indication of inclusion, and the indication of commitment), or based on the rationale of the text, meaning the legal cause mentioned therein, and nothing else.
Likewise, this does not mean that these general meanings align with every era and every time. Rather, it means that every era and every time finds solutions to the problems that arise within these broad outlines according to the perspective it holds, and according to what is indicated by the explicit wording of its sentences, or their implicit meanings, or their indications, or their causes, not according to the era and time from one perspective or according to what prevails in the era from treatments and rulings.
Thus, this breadth in the texts for deriving multiple rulings, and the breadth of the rulings to apply to many issues, is what has made Islamic law sufficient to address all the problems of life in every time and place, for every nation and generation, and it is neither flexibility nor evolution.
