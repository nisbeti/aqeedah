Ibn Abbas narrated that the Commander of the Faithful, Umar ibn al-Khattab, once came for pilgrimage, and Safwan ibn Umayyah prepared food for him. The people began to eat, and the servants stood before them serving them. Umar became very angry about this and said: "Why do I see your servants not eating with you? Do you disdain them? There is no people who monopolize their servants except that Allah acts against them." Then he called the servants and ordered them to sit with their masters and to eat with them from the same platters. He himself did not partake of any of Safwan's food due to his intense anger at the mistreatment of the servants.

It is narrated that when Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) left Medina for Jerusalem to negotiate with the patriarch regarding the surrender of the city following its siege by the army of Abu Ubaidah ibn al-Jarrah, he took with him his servant. He did not wish to take from the public treasury (bayt al-mal) except for one camel for their journey, and he divided the stages between himself and the servant. They would take turns riding the single camel, with Umar riding one stage while the servant walked behind him, then the servant would ride the next stage while Umar walked behind him, until they approached Jerusalem. When it was the servant's turn, the servant suggested that Umar ride while he walked behind him, so that they could enter the city in a manner befitting the Caliph of the Muslims. However, Umar insisted that the servant ride and he walk behind him, and they entered Jerusalem in this manner.

Thus: Given that the human dimension of the issue of slavery cannot be delayed, Islam has immediately commanded a change in society's perception of slaves. This was manifested in changing their treatment, their discourse, the burdens placed upon them, and in equating them with free individuals in social and human value. It is noteworthy that society responded immediately to this perspective without any delay, as seen in the matters of brotherhood, leading armies, marriage, and others. The reason for the success of this change and the rapid response of society to it was the "change in societal culture" that Islam worked on, which was based on certain pillars that we will identify shortly, with God's permission.

The second stage: preparation for emancipation:

Slaves are not freed by decree, for the slave is no longer a "thing" or property after this treatment, but has risen to the status of a hired worker, indeed above a hired worker, because you pay the hired worker his wage and he goes on his way, whereas the enslaved person is fed from the best of what you feed your family, and is clothed as you are clothed, and he is your brother; you assist him in the tasks you have assigned to him!

The previous step was, in fact, a spiritual liberation for the enslaved, restoring them to humanity and treating them as noble human beings who do not differ from their masters in terms of origin; rather, it is circumstantial conditions that have limited the external freedom of the enslaved in their direct dealings with society. Aside from this point, the enslaved had all the rights of human beings.

And you must remember that after having lived a life of servitude their entire lives, some slaves were not psychologically prepared to be free and masters of themselves, responsible for their needs and actions. The slave excels at many tasks when commanded by his master, so he has nothing to do but obey and execute. However, he does not excel at anything that falls under his own responsibility, even if it is the simplest of tasks, not because his body is incapable of performing them, nor because his mind—in all circumstances—is incapable of understanding them; but because his soul cannot bear the consequences of them, imagining therein imagined dangers and unsolvable problems, thus fleeing from them to preserve himself from risks!

The system of slavery in the world - before Islam - instilled in the souls of the slaves a sense of inferiority, and they became accustomed to relying on others. It is not appropriate to suddenly throw them into the depths of society to act on their own.
