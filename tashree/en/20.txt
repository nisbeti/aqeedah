One has come to be the foundation to which the other refers, and the standard by which the other’s determinations, concepts, and perceptions are tested, and by which its imbalances and deviations are corrected. There is undoubtedly harmony and agreement between them, but on this basis, not on the basis that they are equal counterparts, with one being entirely equivalent to the other.
Moreover, the intellect that is free from deficiency and desires does not exist in the world of reality; rather, it is an "ideal".
The interpretation of Muhammad Abduh of Juz' 'Am has been clearly influenced by this perspective, as well as the interpretation of his student Rashid Rida and the interpretation of his student Sheikh al-Maghribi of Juz' Tabarak, to the extent that he stated on several occasions the necessity of interpreting the text to align with reason!
It is a dangerous principle, for the use of the word "reason" refers to something unrealistic! As we said, there is your reason, my reason, the reason of so-and-so, and the reason of such-and-such, and there is no absolute reason that is not subject to deficiency, desire, passion, and ignorance that judges the Qur'anic text according to its "determinations." If we were to mandate interpretation (ta'wil) to align the text with these many reasons, we would end up in chaos!"(29)
