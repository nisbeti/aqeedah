And yet, Allah, Exalted is He, has commanded His servants to walk in its expanses, to spread throughout the earth, and to seek sustenance from its sources, meaning that they should work to obtain this sustenance. The way to obtain sustenance is through work and effort, and the attainment of sustenance may depend on effort and work, or it may not. A worker may labor and not receive compensation for their work, and a merchant may trade and incur losses in their business. Allah may provide sustenance without work, such as through inheritance, receiving a gift, or finding a sum of money. Thus, effort is not a cause for obtaining sustenance, but rather one of the conditions. Such faith expands the horizon regarding sustenance and motivates individuals to seek it without being confined to any specific condition. A single glance at the reality of life shows the extent of the impact of the belief that the Provider is Allah, Exalted is He, and that effort is one of the conditions of sustenance, on sustenance. [A practical example: An employee was dismissed from work, which is a decree from Allah. They may act by refraining from seeking work, arguing or believing that hardship is decreed for them and that they must suffer. In reality, they do not know whether it is written for them to suffer or to be happy! It is incorrect for them to mix their "despair" and their assumption that it is written for them to suffer with their actions. This confusion is the hidden predestination, and any thought that considers it written for them to experience this or that must be separated from action. Because they do not know, perhaps their dismissal from their previous job is better for them. When they strive and obtain a new job, they may find it better than the previous one (in their worldly life and the outcome of their affairs), and that their previous job was a constraint preventing them from seeking a better job. Allah freed them from this constraint. Therefore, the correct stance is to act by taking the means and searching for a new job, and obtaining a better new job is also by the decree of Allah, which they did not know. However, their good opinion of Allah, and that whatever comes to them from Allah is all good, and that what befalls them was not meant to miss them, all formed a motivation for positivity, effort, ambition, and tranquility instead of regression, despair, and hopelessness that the hidden predestination and idleness push them towards. Therefore, it is necessary to separate action from the first framework (the framework of hidden predestination) and integrate it with the second framework: that is, tranquility, positivity, and trust in Allah, without considering in their calculations while striving that Allah has decreed for them to be dismissed from work, and hardship, which is written, and its natural result is suffering and a lack of wealth. (Separating from the framework of hidden predestination is what is required.) But it is not possible to separate a Muslim from understanding positive predestination based on trust in Allah and that He is the Provider. This is an Islamic doctrinal framework. In all cases, striving and taking the means should be pursued. Indeed, belief in predestination forms: a positive intellectual leadership that drives the believer to work and to overcome difficulties.]
As for patience as a concept related to belief, it involves entrusting the management of affairs in the universe to the Creator, regarding what falls within the realm of divine decree and predestination. The vicissitudes of time do not alter the good connection of the Muslim with their Lord; they do not despair, nor do the trials and tribulations change them. Rather, they inspire themselves with determination to proceed in this world, knowing that what has befallen them was not meant to miss them. Thus, the Muslim remains, through their patience, a distinguished figure among creation, always rising to the heights of excellence. ﴾And We will surely test you until We make evident the fighters among you and the patient, and We will test your news﴿ [31 Muhammad], ﴾O my son, establish prayer and enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong and be patient over what befalls you. Indeed, that is of the matters requiring determination﴿ [17 Luqman].
And patience as a concept related to actions is manifested in compelling oneself to perform acts of obedience to the utmost of one's ability, and in restraining oneself from sins as far as one can distance oneself from them. Thus, patience is the act of compelling oneself to adhere to the legal rulings in the best possible manner.
