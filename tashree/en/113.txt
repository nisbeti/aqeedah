Finally, Imam al-Sarakhsi in "Usul al-Sarakhsi" defines the issue as: "Any jurisprudential matter in which a legal ruling is sought, and is derived from the evidences of the Sharia." He indicates that issues are determined based on the jurisprudential matters in which a legal ruling is sought, and are derived from the evidences of the Sharia, which means that each jurisprudential matter is considered an independent issue. An example of this is that the rulings of zakat are considered an independent issue from the rulings of prayer, because each pertains to a different jurisprudential matter, and is derived from its own specific legal evidences.
Al-Sarakhsi clarifies that matters related to a principal issue must be integrated within a single methodological framework.
For example: Ablution (wudu) is a condition for the validity of prayer, so its rulings cannot be separated from the framework of prayer unless the research is specialized in purification (taharah). Al-Sarakhsi relies on two criteria to differentiate between issues: the legal objective (ghaya shar'iyya), if the rulings serve the same objective, they fall under one issue; for prayer, its objective is the establishment of worship, while for zakat, it is economic organization and social solidarity. Thus, the objective of prayer differs from the objective of zakat, making them two separate issues. The second criterion is the type of legal evidence (dalil shar'i); if the evidence is different, the issues are considered independent. The evidence for prayer consists of verses and hadiths that address that issue, while the evidence for zakat addresses the details of the shares and expenditures and similar matters. This difference in the details of the evidence makes them different issues.
Thus, we conclude from this that the criteria for defining an issue (mas'ala) in usul al-fiqh (principles of jurisprudence) among the scholars of usul encompass a set of matters within a specific research framework that makes them one issue, and does not treat each subsidiary matter as an independent issue, as if each nullifier of ablution were an independent issue from the other nullifiers, for example,
Therefore, it is incumbent upon the mujtahid (scholar) in a particular issue to follow specific principles in his ijtihad (independent reasoning) for every matter, or to imitate a particular mujtahid (scholar) or a specific school of thought in all aspects of that particular issue. However, within the same issue, it is not permissible for the mujtahid or the muqallid (follower) to mix those principles with other principles.
In the book Islamic Personality, Volume One, by the scholar Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani (may Allah have mercy on him), we find that the concept of "issue" (mas'ala) in ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) is defined as a general framework that encompasses all the subsidiary rulings related to a single topic. Issues are differentiated based on specific principles and rules used by the mujtahid (jurist), and the rulings are derived according to these principles.
