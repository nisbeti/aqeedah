The first column: Transforming the concept of halal and haram into a societal conviction:
We said that a person needs a motivator to adhere to the binding legislations imposed on them, and to the obligations assigned to them by others. Islam began by instilling the concept that halal (permissible) and haram (forbidden) are measures and references for behavior, then by linking the concept to belief, such that it represents Allah's pleasure when adhered to, and His wrath otherwise. It represents the commands and prohibitions of Allah, Exalted is He, which are connected to His divinity, His right to legislate, the obligation of obedience to Him, the humility before His might, submission to His greatness, and the brokenness before His awe, as well as the submission to His command and the avoidance of His prohibition, out of a desire for His pleasure, fear of His anger, love for Him, hope for His reward and grace, and fear of His punishment. Then, there is submission to Him, acknowledging that He knows the secret and what is even more hidden, and that He knows what benefits humanity and what keeps it from corruption on earth, and that His wisdom is profound. Furthermore, the Wise Legislator described His commands and prohibitions with frightening attributes, deterring souls from transgressing them. Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾Except that they fear that they will not be able to maintain the limits of Allah. So if you fear that they will not be able to maintain the limits of Allah, then there is no sin upon them in what she ransoms. Those are the limits of Allah, so do not transgress them. And whoever transgresses the limits of Allah - it is those who are the wrongdoers﴿ [al-Baqarah 229]. Al-Tabari said in his interpretation: "Those are the signs of His rulings, distinguishing what is lawful for you from what is forbidden for you, O people, so do not transgress what He has made lawful for you from the matters He has clarified and detailed for you from the halal to what He has forbidden for you, thus exceeding obedience to Him into disobedience." And Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾Those are the limits of Allah, so do not approach them. Thus Allah makes clear His verses to the people that perhaps they may become righteous﴿ [al-Baqarah 187]. He referred to them as verses: ﴾And do not take the verses of Allah in jest﴿ [al-Baqarah 231]. He established the reward for obedience to Allah within His limits: ﴾Those are the limits of Allah, and whoever obeys Allah and His Messenger, He will admit him to gardens beneath which rivers flow, abiding therein forever, and that is the great success. And whoever disobeys Allah and His Messenger and transgresses His limits - He will admit him to a Fire, abiding therein forever, and he will have a humiliating punishment﴿ [an-Nisa 13-14]. He called it a covenant(396), meaning a binding promise: ﴾And remember the favor of Allah upon you and His covenant which He contracted with you when you said, "We hear and we obey." And fear Allah; indeed, Allah is Knowing of that within the breasts﴿ [al-Ma'idah 7], meaning when you pledged allegiance to the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) to listen and obey, which entails the fulfillment of Allah's promise to them based on their commitment to His covenant. He attributed the covenant to Himself, Exalted is He, to magnify His command: ﴾Those who fulfill the covenant of Allah and do not break the contract﴿ [ar-Ra'd 20]. ﴾And let those beware who oppose the Messenger's order that they be struck by a trial or a painful punishment﴿ [an-Nur 63]. This indicates the obligation of obeying the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) in what he commands, and the command to obey Him, Exalted is He, and to obey His Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him): ﴾O you who have believed, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and do not invalidate your deeds﴿. This indicates that the command to follow the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) implicitly and necessarily means the command to obey. If the action is not carried out, then the obedience is merely a claim without reality. Following and submission - which are signs of obedience - must either be acting in accordance with the statement and affirming it, or following in action and emulating him in the proper manner. He commanded to refer to Allah and His Messenger in matters that arise among people, and to return them to Allah and His Messenger, taking everything that has been brought forth, and the judgment of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) in any matter is like the judgment of Allah, Exalted is He, with no choice in the obligation to adhere to it: ﴾And it is not for a believing man or a believing woman, when Allah and His Messenger have decreed a matter, that they should have any choice in their affair. And whoever disobeys Allah and His Messenger has certainly strayed into clear error﴿(397).

(396) And he described the contract between spouses as a strong covenant, and as a trust from Allah, and the word of Allah, to magnify His command. How could it not be, when it is a limit from the limits of Allah? The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said, "Indeed, you have taken them by the trust of Allah, and you have made lawful their private parts by the word of Allah." It is also referred to in the Qur'an: ﴾And you took from them a solemn covenant﴿ [Surah an-Nisa 21].
(397) The indication of Allah's saying here ﴾And it was not﴿, and the inclusion of ﴾was﴿ in the negation is a stronger indication of the negation of the ruling because the action of ﴾was﴿ indicates existence, thus its negation entails the negation of existence in its entirety. (Al-Tahrir wa al-Tanwir by Ibn Ashour, meaning that the verse necessitates the prohibition of choice in any matter, regardless of what it is, when the decree in that matter occurs!) The word ﴾a matter﴿ came as a direct object for the verb decreed. The phrase ﴾When Allah and His Messenger have decreed a matter﴿ is a conditional clause and not a negated clause, and therefore the word ﴾a matter﴿ is indefinite in the context of the condition, thus it is general. Note that the negation is not for the clause ﴾When Allah and His Messenger have decreed a matter﴿ but the negation is in the phrase ﴾And it was not for a believing man or a believing woman﴿, thus negating the choice for the believers on the condition that Allah and His Messenger decree a matter. It is well-known in usul al-fiqh that an indefinite noun, whether in the context of a condition or in the context of negation, is general, whether it is directly negated as in "no one is standing," or whether it is acted upon as in "no one stood," and whether the negation is "not," or "no," or otherwise. (Islamic Personality, Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani: Volume Three, Ways of Establishing Generality of Terms) "And thus he emphasized the opposite, saying ﴾And whoever disobeys Allah and His Messenger has certainly strayed into clear error﴿" (Tafsir Ibn Kathir). This is also an indication of the obligation to act upon the command, whether in speech or action.
