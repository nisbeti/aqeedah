The revelation has a meaning, and the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) expressed this revelation in words from himself, or through actions of his, or by his silence (i.e., the Sunnah). The address of the Legislator that includes legislation has been mentioned in the Qur'an and in the Sunnah. The legal ruling is the address of the Legislator related to the actions of the servants by obligation, or choice, or status. It is said: the address of the Legislator, and not the address of Allah, to encompass the Sunnah and the consensus of the Companions in terms of being indicative of the address, so that it is not assumed that it refers only to the Qur'an; because the Sunnah is revelation, thus it is the address of the Legislator. The consensus of the Companions reveals a proof from the Sunnah, so it is the address of the Legislator. The right to legislate is different from the address of the Legislator, and the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) is a conveyer of the legislation of Allah, Exalted is He, disseminated in the Qur'an and the Sunnah. The Sunnah is a legal proof, providing conclusive evidence of the prophethood of our Master Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him) and his message, and it is the definitive proof that he does not speak out of desire; it is only revelation that is revealed, ﴾And he does not speak out of desire (3) It is only a revelation that is revealed (4)﴿ and that what he warns of is indeed revelation from Allah, the Exalted: "I only warn you by revelation." Thus, the Sunnah is revelation from Allah, the Exalted. However, revelation pertains to the meanings and implications of the Sunnah, not its words; that is, the reference of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) is the Qur'an and revelation. Therefore, although the structure of the sentences in the Sunnah is formulated by the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), this formulation, in its reference, is also revelation from Allah, in which he articulates his statements in accordance with what ensures the conveyance of the meanings revealed in words accepted from revelation, and does not deviate from the path of the Qur'anic revelation itself. He was granted the comprehensive speech (jawami al-kalam) (peace and blessings be upon him) and reached the pinnacle of eloquence (peace and blessings be upon him). Allah, the Exalted, educated him and perfected his education, so what came out of his noble mouth was the most precise expression of the meanings revealed, accurately conveying the intent of the revelation. There have come verses regarding revelation in general that include the Sunnah along with the Qur'an, such as His saying: "And if I am guided, it is by what my Lord reveals to me," and His saying: "Indeed, We have revealed to you as We revealed to Noah and the Prophets," and His saying: "And follow what is revealed to you from your Lord," and His saying: "I only warn you by revelation," which is a style of restriction and emphasis. The warning has come in the Qur'an and the Sunnah, in speech, action, and silence, as it includes the explanation of the general Qur'an, and the Sunnah is a proof like the Book, equally without any difference between them; due to the conclusive evidence upon it as it is upon the Qur'an. Limiting oneself to the Book is the opinion of those who deviate from Islam. Allah, the Exalted, said: "And whatever the Messenger gives you, take it, and what he forbids you, refrain from it," and He said: "Whoever obeys the Messenger has obeyed Allah," and He said: "Let those who oppose his command beware lest a trial strike them or a painful punishment befall them (63)," and He said: "And it is not for a believer, man or woman, when Allah and His Messenger have decreed a matter, that they should have any choice in their matter," and He said: "But no, by your Lord, they will not believe until they make you judge in that which they dispute among themselves, and then find no discomfort in themselves from what you have judged and submit in [full, willing] submission (65)," and He said: "So if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger." Referring to the Messenger after his death is referring to his Sunnah. And He said: "Say, 'Obey Allah and the Messenger,'" and He said: "Say, 'If you love Allah, then follow me; Allah will love you.'" These texts are definitive in their authenticity and clear in their indication, explicitly stating the obligation to adhere to the Sunnah as one adheres to the Book. Denying the Sunnah is certainly disbelief, and it is obligatory to adhere to the Sunnah just as one adheres to the Qur'an, equally without any difference between them"(456)
