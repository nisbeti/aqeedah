Everyone, and the practice of the Western legal status also depends on the conflict between rights and one another. Women's rights have only been achieved and established through the struggle of women against men, and thus, Sheikh Muhammad al-Ghazali (may Allah have mercy on him) expressed this by saying, "Islam did not address the issue of rights as the West did; the West established rights for people in opposition to one another, turning human relationships into a conflict of interests, and then into competition and strife, while Islam formulated people's rights in the form of duties that fall upon those around them, for which Allah will hold them accountable on the Day of Judgment. The rights of the wife are duties upon the husband, and vice versa, and the rights of the neighbor are duties upon the neighbor. Thus, the system of rights in Islamic civilization transformed into a system of giving, compassion, and mutual consent, not a system of conflicting interests, strife, competition, coercion, and oppression."(419)
Thus, the Islamic framework is based on building the system of Islamic rights on the foundation of brotherhood and compassion, and on the basis that the believers are allies of one another. When you give the poor zakat (almsgiving) or charity, you do not do this out of mere generosity or favor, but rather because they have a right of guardianship over you, and guardianship entails support, assistance, and solidarity. This wealth is a right that they have over you. Therefore, Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And in their wealth is a right for the beggar and the deprived﴿, ﴾And those who have wealth, there is a known right for the beggar and the deprived﴿. These are foundations for preserving relationships of affection, compassion, brotherhood, and mutual support among the believers. From here, we find that the faith-based foundation of rights in Islam, as duties upon the Muslim individually and collectively, has made Muslims compete to fulfill them. In fact, its impact extends clearly to the dhimmis (non-Muslims living under Islamic rule) in the state; they have what we have in terms of fairness, and they are subject to what we are subject to in terms of justice. Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾If you disclose your charities, it is good; but if you conceal them and give them to the poor, it is better for you﴿, and this is general for the poor. The dhimmis have the same care for their affairs and the guarantee of their livelihood as the Muslims do. From Abu Wail, from Abu Musa or one of them, with his chain of narration that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "Feed the hungry, visit the sick, and free the captive." Abu Ubaid said: "And likewise, the dhimmis are defended against those who oppose them, and their captives are freed. If they are rescued, they return to their covenant and promise free, and there are hadiths regarding this."(420) The acceptance of a non-Muslim of the dhimma contract with Muslims, and their living under the protection of the Islamic state, entails their receiving rights of protection, care for their affairs, defense, and securing their basic needs such as housing, clothing, education, healthcare, and other rights, just like any Muslim living in the state.
From Mu'adh (may Allah be pleased with him), he said: I was riding behind the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) on a donkey called 'Ufair. He said: "O Mu'adh, do you know the right of Allah upon His servants, and what is the right of the servants upon Allah?" I said: Allah and His Messenger know best. He said: "The right of Allah upon the servants is that they worship Him and do not associate anything with Him, and the right of the servants upon Allah is that He does not punish anyone who does not associate anything with Him." I said: O Messenger of Allah, should I not give them the good news? He said: "Do not give them the good news, lest they rely on it." This is agreed upon as authentic. The concept of rights has been established as a basis for a mutual relationship between the servants and their Lord, Exalted and Glorified is He. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) adopted the concept of rights as a foundation for living when he was informed that Abdullah ibn Amr was praying all night, fasting all day, and completing the Qur'an every night. He said: "Do not do that; sleep and wake, fast and break your fast, for your body has a right over you, and your eyes have a right over you."
