(504) The objectives of Islamic law (sharia) include the pursuit of benefits (masalih)(505) and the prevention of harms (mafasid), and the proponents of positive laws(506) may add to these the realization of what is called human rights, the right to equality, and other objectives.
When we look at criminal legislation, we will find that its essence is to impose penalties in accordance with the crimes, deterring their commission, and compensating for sins (blood money, compensations, etc.), to deter society from committing crimes, to rectify the sinner's wrongdoing, to compensate the victim in a manner that matches their loss, and to preserve the public right!
When we examine the body of laws regulating societal relations, we must find integration within them, and the societal dimension must be evident. For example, in Islam, there are laws and regulations that lead to the realization of social solidarity, as the laws and rulings encompass the matter from all aspects to establish a societal system based on social solidarity! At the level of the individual, family, neighborhood, community, and state, this is founded on the basis of responsibility in this world and accountability in the Hereafter. This includes the individual's responsibilities towards caring for parents and the elderly, the neighborhood's responsibility to feed the hungry, to care for orphans, to uphold the rights of neighbors, guests, and travelers, and the state's pastoral responsibility to ensure the basic needs of the subjects when they are unable to fulfill them, and so forth. It is supported by the necessary laws to achieve this, such as the system of zakat, expiations, almsgiving, assisting the needy, rescuing the distressed, endowments, wills, loans, and others. Where do you find this in Western legislation? Yes, they have excelled in legislation related to finance, tax systems, and the like, but when it comes to societal legislation that establishes cooperative, supportive, and collaborative communities, their laws have not uttered a word! This is only because the nature of their system is purely capitalist! It is individualistic(507), purely so(508)!

(504) Refer to the book Islamic Personality, Volume 3, Usul al-Fiqh by the scholar Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani (may Allah have mercy on him), Chapter: The pursuit of benefits and the prevention of harms are not a reason for the sharia as a whole nor a reason for any specific ruling; it contains an interesting and important detail.
(505) This is because the pursuit of benefits and the prevention of harms are not a reason for the legal rulings in general, as there is no text indicating that the pursuit of benefits and the prevention of harms is a reason for legal rulings, nor is there any text indicating that it is a reason for a specific ruling, so it cannot be a legal reason. Especially since the benefits and harms are, in reality, unknown to humans; one may think something is beneficial while it is harmful, and vice versa. Consequently, the judgment regarding the estimation of benefits rationally varies, which makes the benefit itself subordinate to the legal ruling, and the legal ruling should not be made subordinate to it. Our statement does not mean that the sharia came to harm creation, but the true benefit and righteousness, which is the essence of the sharia, is not what minds estimate as beneficial, but rather by leaving the estimation of benefit to Allah, and wherever He legislates, there is righteousness and benefit.
(506) It is known that the legislator did not establish the law arbitrarily or randomly, but rather sought a desired goal behind it, with wisdom motivating it, and reasons and objectives driving it. However, the concern of positive law scholars with the objectives of their legislative texts is much less than that of the scholars of Islamic sharia; this is because the motivation that exists among sharia scholars does not exist among legal scholars. The explanation for this is that positive legislation is subject to continuous amendment and change, so it does not have a pressing need for a purposive perspective that extracts from it the intended wisdom and beneficial goals. Rather, these wisdoms and goals themselves change from time to time, so what about the legal details? See: The Objectives of Positive Law in Light of the Objectives of Islamic Sharia, by Dr. Alyan Bouzyan, published in issue 150 of the Contemporary Muslim magazine.
