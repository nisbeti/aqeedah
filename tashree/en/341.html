From here, the danger of human legislation that succumbs to desires becomes apparent, where self-interest is disguised under the guise of "freedom," instincts are clothed in the garb of "rights," and deviation is legitimized through what is called "the right of choice," resulting in human intellect producing biased, unjust legislative systems that serve one group at the expense of another, or entrench destructive values under shiny slogans.

The limitations of the intellect in perceiving consequences and outcomes (temporal deficiency):

Summary:

The limitations of the intellect in understanding the consequences of actions and the outcomes of legislations.

The intellect does not possess the necessary tools to know the future results of actions, nor does it have a comprehensive understanding of the outcomes, which limits its ability to enact sound legislation.

The intellect is governed by prior information and tools that it cannot surpass.

The human intellect does not operate in a vacuum; rather, it requires prior data and experiences, and it is incapable of forming concepts or legislations without them.

The intellect is suitable for doctrinal reasoning, not practical legislation.

The intellect can reason about major doctrinal issues, such as the existence of Allah, as it possesses the necessary tools to issue those judgments; however, it is not qualified to issue legislative rulings related to actions, consequences, and interests due to the inadequacy of its tools for such matters.

The absence of an absolute reference standard in estimating and prioritizing interests.

The intellect is unable to determine which interests should be prioritized in cases of conflict, whether they are material, moral, spiritual, or immediate beneficial interests.

The confusion between reason and desire in the absence of reference.

When the intellect is left to legislate without revelation, its decisions often turn into responses to whims and personal interests, rather than objective rational judgments.

Human legislation is contextually limited and rapidly changing.

Positive laws are often immediate, temporary, and subject to continuous amendment due to their inability to encompass all aspects, which deprives them of stability and permanence.

The cycle of "legal patchwork" in positive systems.

The frequent amendments in positive laws reveal their structural failure and lead to a destabilization of public trust and a loss of legal authority.

The permanence and absolute perfection of divine legislation.
