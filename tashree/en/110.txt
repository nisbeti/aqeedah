The one who is legally responsible (mukallaf) must strive to reach a predominant assumption (zann ghalib) regarding the issue. Imitation (taqlid) does not lead to knowledge or to a predominant assumption, and therefore many scholars have prohibited imitation except for the weak, the incapable, and the compelled, which refers to the layperson (a'ami) who does not possess the tools of independent reasoning (ijtihad). This is the one for whom they have mandated or permitted to seek fatwas and to imitate the one from whom he seeks a fatwa. As for others, the principle is that they should exert effort to derive the ruling, which is preferable; however, it is permissible for them to imitate as we have explained previously. They may either be a following imitator (muqallid mutabi') who possesses some recognized knowledge in legislation but it is not sufficient for him to engage in independent reasoning, and it is permissible for him to imitate, but he must know the evidence of the jurist he follows; or they may be an illiterate imitator (muqallid ummi), or a layperson, who does not possess any recognized knowledge in legislation, and for him, the fatwa of the imam suffices, so he asks him about the ruling, and he tells him: it is forbidden (haram) or obligatory (fard), and so on(222).
Based on this, it is necessary to follow a jurist (mujtahid) and an imitator (muqallid) along a path (manhaj) based on knowledge so that there is no entry point for fabricating claims about Allah, making what He has made lawful (halal) unlawful (haram), or speaking about Allah without knowledge, ﴾Indeed, He only commands you with evil and immorality and to say about Allah what you do not know﴿. ﴾Say, "My Lord has only forbidden immoralities, what is apparent of them and what is concealed, and sin and oppression without right, and that you associate with Allah that for which He has not sent down any authority, and that you say about Allah what you do not know﴿. Following the principles in deriving rulings is the only way to speak about Allah with knowledge - in terms of scientific methodology - and to ensure that its results and outputs are certain knowledge or a predominant assumption, because the Wise Legislator has permitted that a predominant assumption be a valid path and methodology for deriving legal rulings from their evidences, which sometimes manifest those rulings with certainty and at other times with probability.
The existence of a methodology - with its determinants, rules, principles, controls, measures, subdivisions, and its way of dealing with evidences in understanding, weighing, and establishing - and following it is a guarantee against the existence of contradiction, conflict, and inconsistency in the method of independent reasoning and in dealing with texts and evidences between one issue and another, and a means to avoid mistakes. It is a comprehensive methodology in thinking, a correct arrangement of principles and mental processes necessary for uncovering the truth or reaching the desired outcome (which is the legal rulings here), and a foundation for the processes of reasoning, proving, and deriving. No science can be established except on methodologies that suit it; otherwise, it would become a random endeavor. The principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh) protect against fabricating lies about Allah, and they ensure that independent reasoning is based on "knowledge" and not merely speaking about Allah without knowledge. The methodologies of usul al-fiqh are not merely an entry point to understanding legal texts and detailed evidences, nor are they just a means for analogy and derivation from them, but rather the methodology is a comprehensive general law that prevents falling into error, capable of dealing with texts and evidences for systematic study in harmony and capable of deriving rulings from them. The outputs of the science of usul al-fiqh do not necessarily produce certainty; rather, they are in harmony with the nature of the evidences and indications found in the general evidences regarding the issues, which are characterized as certain or probable, consistent with the fact that the evidences include general and specific, absolute and restricted, abrogating and abrogated, capable of conducting processes of weighing between them and resolving conflicts, and also consistent with the nature of the Legislator's permission to follow assumptions in rulings.
