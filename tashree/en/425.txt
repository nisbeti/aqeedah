As for the mechanisms for implementing foreign policy, they are means left to the discretion of the caliph: such as establishing a Ministry of Foreign Affairs, sending ambassadors, and participating in international systems that do not contradict the rulings of Islam. For example, attending international conferences to discuss environmental or trade issues is a permissible means if the ruler deems it beneficial and free from prohibitions, while joining organizations based on principles contrary to Islam (such as the supremacy of international legislation over Sharia sovereignty) is prohibited, no matter how beneficial it may seem.
The economic system and wealth management: The ruling of Sharia prohibits usury (riba), gambling (maysir), monopolies (ihtikar), and fraud (tahfif), and mandates zakat, spending, and regulating public ownership (such as minerals and the like), and does not allow taxes to be imposed except out of necessity and within the limits of Sharia. These are constants. As for detailed economic policies (such as choosing a specific agricultural system, adopting a digital currency, or the types of developmental projects), they are means subject to study and experimentation. The state may adopt a modern economic system, such as establishing Islamic banks or an Islamic financial market, as long as it adheres to the prohibition of usury and gambling. It is permissible to adopt new technologies like blockchain to regulate financial records, as they are tools to achieve the transparency required by Sharia without contradiction. The only prohibition is adopting economic theories that contradict Sharia justice (such as predatory capitalism that leads to gross exploitation, or socialism that denies individual ownership guaranteed by Sharia).
These points outline a clear map: Sharia has drawn the boundaries of the field and provided us with the compass, leaving us room to maneuver within it. The ruling is the mandatory fence represented by the texts and principles of Sharia, and the means are the procedures and tools beneath it from which we choose the most effective and suitable for time and place. Thus, two great matters are achieved for the nation: steadfastness on the unchanging truth, and renewal and flexibility in beneficial methods, whatever they may be.
Understanding this delicate equation is sufficient to liberate the Muslim mind from the artificial dichotomy of transmission and reason. There remains no contradiction; reason serves Sharia and innovates in serving its objectives, while Sharia directs reason and restrains its desires and biases. With this understanding, we place matters in their proper context: Sharia policy is a reason disciplined by revelation, and revelation is realized through reason. We ask Allah to grant us understanding in our religion, and to prepare for this nation a matter of guidance in which the people of His obedience are honored, and the people of His disobedience are humiliated, and in which the Book of Allah and the Sunnah of His Messenger are upheld. Indeed, He is the Guardian of that and capable of it.
