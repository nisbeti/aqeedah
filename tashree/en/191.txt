The detailed explanation establishes the basis of the legal rationale and includes individuals under this general ruling, which determines whether something is harmful or not. This is defined by the legislator through its general or overarching principles that safeguard honor, wealth, and lives. An action that leads to the violation of honor, or the assault on wealth or blood, or similar matters, such as restricting people on the roads, or preventing them from benefiting from resources like river waters and public state facilities, is considered a violation of the legal ruling. Thus, it harms others, and harm here is prohibited. For example, all punishments and legal penalties are harmful to the Muslim, but they are lawful harm that leads to the realization of collective mercy (the stability and security of society) and individual mercy.
Moreover, the understanding of "no harm and no harming" is based on the fact that harmful substances in themselves (like poison) lead to the destruction of the body, damaging some of its parts, or causing functional disorders. The same applies to narcotics; since these things harm the body, they are prohibited, and this is the rationale for their prohibition. People are equal in this regard. However, matters that are originally permissible but may harm certain individuals, such as sugar for diabetic patients, are prohibited if consumed by someone for whom it poses a risk in a quantity that results in harm. This harm is assessed according to the severity of the illness and the consequences of consuming sugar and its quantity!
As for actions, there are actions that are harmful in themselves, and actions that lead to harm. Cutting off a hand is harmful, but Islam has established the penalty of cutting off the hand of a thief under certain conditions. On the other hand, an action that leads to harm is mostly from the permissible actions, but if it is established that it leads to certain harm, it is prohibited. Importing goods is permissible, but if it leads to specific harm to farmers and craftsmen, it is prohibited to the extent that it alleviates the harm from them.
It should also be noted that the Sharia, having come with these broad outlines, has established regulations that encompass all rulings that one might argue are easy for the mind to agree with (even though human legislations differ in their details). It has prohibited the killing of a soul without right, prohibited the assault on wealth and honor, and legislated rulings that address causing harm and injury to others. The Sharia has not left it to the intellect to approve or disapprove according to whims in these matters, which some might think the intellect could independently arrive at their rulings and legislations. As for more complex and difficult issues regarding their relationship with interests, acceptable behavior, and good and evil, it is even more the case that the intellect cannot decisively conclude a correct opinion on them.
It is established(289) that the Sharia has come to prohibit harm and the causing of harm, whether individually or collectively. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: “No harm and no harming,”(290) and among the applications of this is that Allah, Exalted is He, has prohibited the husband from causing harm to his wife when divorcing her by intending to harm her through his taking her back, as He said: ﴾And do not hold them to harm in order to transgress﴿ [al-Baqarah 131]. He also prohibited harm in bequests: ﴾After a bequest which is to be observed or a debt without causing harm﴿ [an-Nisa 12]. It appears that the jurists agree on the prohibition of what is established to lead to something unlawful or harmful. Therefore, the state has the right to intervene to prevent any action that leads to something unlawful or harmful. It has been shown from the evidence in the lives of the Companions that they acknowledged the state's intervention to prevent harm, as the consensus of the Companions was established on accepting what Uthman ibn Affan did.
