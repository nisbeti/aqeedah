Distortion of Islam from Within

On the other hand, the war against Islam has taken a more dangerous turn, a turn towards distorting Islam in terms of jurisprudence (fiqh), principles (usul), and methods of thinking and reasoning, tainting Islam with ideas that aim to bring its rulings and concepts closer to Western ideas. To achieve this, they have established rationalist schools that impose reason in legislation, making it a ruler. As we observe, there is a huge difference between what the early scholars of usul disagreed upon and what is being promoted today as modern jurisprudence, relying on a distorted understanding of the propositions of the ancients, with premeditation and intent, aiming to lead Muslims today to a vague jurisprudence, with no features except a pallor on its face, and no identity since it is a return to the dictates of the West in its campaign aimed at uprooting Islam. Its foundation and support lie in the destruction of every relationship with the text and the liberation from the text, even from its implications, and the escape from it to a world governed only by limited interests perceived by the mind of the mufti or the questioner.

Thus, the slogan of every mufti among them and their first reasoning has become the saying of "public interest (maslaha)," and "objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia)"(76), and "the spirit of the text," and the rational understanding of the intent of the legislator based on the rational understanding of public interest, and "moderation(77)" which is "the opposite of extremism" - according to Western definitions of fundamentalism and moderation - and adherence to the literal text has become a sign of rigidity, extremism, and ignorance. Consequently, this jurisprudence has led them to drown in the swamp of a stagnant reality, and you can imagine what kind of jurisprudence will emerge from such an atmosphere!

However, it is always our duty to remember the obvious principles that the scholars of usul derived from the Qur'an and the Sunnah, to serve as a barrier between us and falling into the traps of the fallacies of "jurists of reality, minorities, moderation, priorities, interests, and balances." We have exerted our utmost effort in defining and demonstrating them throughout this research.

(76) Not as proposed by al-Juwayni, al-Ghazali, and al-Shatibi, but by using the term maqasid to bypass the principles of jurisprudence and deduction.

(77) Islam does not seek common ground with falsehood; rather, it refutes and obliterates it and takes its place, building upon its ruins (Munthir Abdullah). Islam does not stand in the middle between the misguided beliefs of the Jews and the distorted beliefs of the Christians; it is a crime to assume that the beliefs of Islam are in the middle of distorted beliefs. For monotheism in Islam is not in the middle between trinity and the taking of Ezra as a son; Exalted is He above that. There is no ruling for prayer, pilgrimage, enjoining good and forbidding wrong, zakat, hijab, or Islamic laws that you find in the middle between the ruling that is with the Jews and that which is with the Christians! Rather, each has its own law and methodology! The one who clings to his religion and adheres to its rulings is not described as extreme or rigid, nor is the one who escapes from them described as moderate, but rather as a deviant. Extremism in religion is not adhering to the correct understanding or applying the correct understanding in reality; rather, the extremism in religion that religion fought against is the distortion of religion. Extremism in religion led the Christians to believe that Christ is the son of God; Exalted is He above that, and extremism in religion alters the very concepts of religion itself. Therefore, terms like "moderate Islam," and describing Islam in terms of leniency and moderation between excess and deficiency, extremism and rigidity, radicalism, and fundamentalism, are all American merchandise that we reject.
