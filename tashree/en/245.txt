One of the fundamental principles of the systems that will prevail in the life of society and the state is the rulings of the sultan(380) (i.e., the one who has the authority to control and dominate the state, to implement the rulings that achieve balance among the components of society and apply the system that guarantees the vital interests of the community. The sultan is realized only through two matters: the first is the care for interests, meaning the general interests of the people, (the system of governance and the systems of the state) through specific rulings, and the second is the power that protects the subjects and implements the rulings, meaning security and sovereignty(381), (i.e., who has the final say in disputes and discussions on various matters(382), meaning that the ultimate authority in the state and outside it belongs to whom?). We see that the order in terms of what should be realized first is for the sultan first and then for sovereignty second,
Although the sovereignty of the Sharia and making the Sharia the measure for every action and legislation is a matter of great doctrinal significance and importance,
Establishing a balanced and thriving society cannot be achieved merely by imposing laws and regulations, but rather by changing the convictions and concepts among the people, and then elevating these concepts and convictions to reach the seat of power to be applied to them through legislations, laws, and constitutions, meaning that the sovereignty of this system must be established as it represents the general opinion of the nation based on the concepts, standards, and convictions built on its fundamental creed, not as it represents legislations seen by constitutional jurists, legal experts, and influential figures in the state, which are documented as a constitution and legislations enforced by the power and authority of the state and its prestige, and thus societal relations are built upon them as in the secular Western state, where the opinion of the people is only considered within a narrow scope when major changes are desired, or after electoral seasons!
Therefore, we see that the state - which is the executive entity of the set of concepts, standards, and convictions upon which the system of governance is based - this state in Islam, in order to be established in reality, must begin by convincing the people to change their concepts, standards, and convictions about the universe, humanity, and life, and about the nature of the system that will govern them. Among these is the idea that sovereignty belongs to the Sharia, which is one of the fundamental ideas upon which public opinion is to be built and transformed into reality in the life of the individual, society, and state.
Once public opinion advocating acceptance of this system and belief in it, along with a strong desire to implement it in reality, is established, the state will be founded based on it, and documented within a legal framework represented by a constitution and legislations that safeguard, protect, and implement those convictions, standards, and values. (380) The sultan in Islam belongs to the nation. It is said in the language: "Sallatuhu," meaning he was granted authority and power, and "Sallatuhu 'alayh": he enabled him and governed him therein. "Tasalat 'alayh": he controlled, enabled, and dominated. The sultan is power and coercion, and the meaning of sultan is the one who has the authority to control and dominate in the state, for governance, kingship, and sultanate are synonymous, which is the authority that implements rulings. The sultan is realized only through two matters: the first is the care for interests, meaning the general interests of the people, (the system of governance and the systems of the state) through specific rulings, and the second is the power that protects the subjects and implements the rulings, meaning security.
(381) Sovereignty in Islam belongs to the Sharia, and sovereignty means the entity that possesses the supreme authority in the state, exercising and directing it in all relations, and even in matters, and to whom all actions emanating from it are subject.
