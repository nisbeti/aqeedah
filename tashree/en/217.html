Islam has valued the human intellect, so many of its rulings are in accordance with sound reasoning. It has clarified the reasons, causes, and wisdom behind many rulings, enabling the accountable person to commit to the essence of adherence and also to feel reassured about understanding the impact of the ruling on life, thus forming a motivation for commitment, conviction, and understanding. Although a person is not permitted to judge legislation or accept it based on its alignment with their interests, the rulings that may provoke intellectual tendencies in humans, which might clash with their desires, have been made subject to reasoning or explanation of their wisdom. An example of this is the issue of distributing the spoils (fay'), as when Islam explained the intended benefit of distributing the spoils to the poor to reduce the gap between the rich and the poor in society, it became a source of reassurance for the community and commitment, and there was no resentment towards the ruling that deprives some individuals of the spoils while granting them to others. Muslims have felt that the rulings of Islam indeed achieve justice, mercy, societal reform, and the eradication of poverty, and so forth. This forms a firm intellectual and rational conviction that Islam is a solution to problems. The causes clarify the purpose, the reality of legislation, and the rationality of the text. For example, anger affects the integrity of judgment; therefore, a judge is prohibited from ruling while angry, and so on.

Islam also takes into account the innate dispositions in humans that determine their moral references. Generally, humans love good by nature and dislike injustice, killing, and evil. These dispositions form a kind of reference that facilitates the acceptance of legislations that align with these dispositions. However, these dispositions are not the ruler, nor are they the measure that determines right from wrong, but they lead to a kind of reassurance in accepting legislations that are compatible with them. It should be noted that these inclinations are influenced by concepts and are colored by them, and they do not exist in humans in their innate form. This mixture may turn them against their nature; for instance, someone raised in a generous household tends to be generous, and vice versa. People's standards for justice and injustice vary, so innate dispositions are not suitable as a basis for judgment. Purity became a crime among the people of Lot. Thus, rulings organize the innate nature and its satisfactions, and they do not leave the innate nature and feelings unregulated.
