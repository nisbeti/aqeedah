A human being is not merely a body or an intellect, but an integrated being who needs a balanced divine way to satisfy his needs, purify his soul and direct his instincts.

The Islamic Sharia alone grasps the composite makeup of the human being

For it takes into account:

The balance between permissible enjoyment and spiritual purification.

The right to work and wealth, joined with zakat and mutual support.

Worship and humility, without abolishing the vitality of social and economic life.

Achieving happiness through love of Allah and following His law

The goal of the human being is not worldly benefit alone, but nearness to Allah and adopting the attributes He loves, which plants tranquillity in this world and salvation in the Hereafter.

Rejecting the subjection of divine legislation to the judgment of the intellect

Because the intellect, being ignorant of the purposes of existence, may not evaluate divine legislation according to its whims or the interests that appear to it; rather, revelation is taken in devotion and submission.

The detail: one of the most severe points of weakness of the human intellect is its compounded ignorance of the nature of the human being himself and of the purposes of his existence in life; hence it is unable to build a legislative system that achieves his happiness and the fullness of his humanity. The human intellect, however great its intelligence and experience, cannot, independently, that is, from itself, grasp whether the human being was created only to eat, drink and enjoy, or was created for a higher wisdom and a deeper mission that goes beyond his momentary existence in this world; and whether his happiness lies in bodily satisfaction, or in purifying the soul and attaining spiritual perfection by realizing his connection with the Creator. This conceptual confusion about what the human being is and what his greater function is has made man-made systems swing between excess and neglect. Human thought has not been able to answer these great questions in a final, codifiable way, and the difference of philosophical answers has led to the rise of legislative systems that are opposed in their aims and contradictory in their vision of the human being:

Some materialist philosophies, such as capitalism, made the human being a consuming creature whose happiness is measured by the extent of his bodily pleasure and financial gains, so individualism, anxiety and frustration swelled, collective values collapsed, and family ties disintegrated.

Communism, by contrast, came with the opposite: it confiscated private property, killed individual initiative and persecuted every manifestation of religion, so the spirit was suffocated, the economy collapsed, the state became despotic, and the natural disposition (fitra) was pushed aside.

Even the Western legal schools differ in their philosophical method for building law. The positivist school (Legal Positivism) separates law completely from morality, and law for it is what the political or legislative authority decrees, regardless of its moral content. The natural law school (Natural Law Theory) ties law to morality and draws its legitimacy from higher rational and moral principles such as justice, and holds that a law's contradiction of natural moral laws invalidates it.
