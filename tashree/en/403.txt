Thus, the essence lies in the relationships and systems, not in the beliefs of individuals. Based on these relationships, societies are described as being in decline or advancement, progress or backwardness.
Since we have established that the unity of ideas, feelings, and systems among people is what regulates their relationships and governs them, and defines for them the public custom, and the authority that manages it based on the care of this system, the reform of society is contingent upon the reform of the public custom, and its corruption upon its corruption. Therefore, the way to reform society is based on the foundation of the unity of ideas and feelings and establishing a system on the same basis. The extent to which these ideas are sound and truthful determines the soundness of society.
As for the individual, his components are that he is a living being with desires, organic needs, and instincts that must be satisfied, such as food, clothing, sleep, and marriage, and his relationships with his Lord are represented by beliefs and acts of worship, his relationships with himself are represented by food, drink, and morals, and his relationships with others are represented by transactions, which are mostly subject to the system of society that has agreed upon a system of transactions and regulations that it oversees. Thus, the soundness of the individual is linked to the soundness of his belief, the soundness of his worship, and the soundness of the system that organizes his life affairs, which has emerged from or is built upon his belief. The components of the individual are different from those of society.
What makes society strong is not merely the strength of the stones or bricks with which its walls (the individuals) are built, but also the strength of the cement that binds the stones together, the good geometric shape that provides ease of entry and exit, and the height of the wall that prevents it from being scaled by other ideas and systems.
Therefore, the soundness of societies is achieved through the reform of their collective concepts, customs, standards, and convictions, which is a task different from working on the reform of individuals, strengthening their worship, and their connection to Allah, and improving their morals.
A society cannot be reformed or corrupted except through the soundness or corruption of public opinion. If public opinion is corrupted, the character of the society is corrupted, and the corruption of the individual has no effect on the corruption of society unless this individual's corruption transforms into a breach of public opinion and corrupts it.
There is no doubt that the soundness of society facilitates and enhances the process of individual reform, while the corruption of society makes the process of individual reform extremely difficult, like swimming against the current!
