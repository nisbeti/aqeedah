Classification of the Most Important Issues Affecting the Process of Ijtihad as Outlines

The process of ijtihad is influenced by several fundamental issues that can be classified under two main axes: dealing with general evidence and dealing with detailed evidence. Each axis has key issues that affect the methodology of ijtihad and its outcomes.

First: Dealing with General Evidence (The Primary and Supplementary Sources of Legislation)

The sources relied upon in ijtihad:

The Holy Qur'an: There is consensus on it being the primary source, but disagreement arises in understanding its implications, such as general and specific, absolute and restricted.

The Prophetic Sunnah: There is disagreement regarding the authority of solitary reports and how to deal with the Sunnah that may seem to contradict the Qur'an.

Consensus (ijma): It is accepted as a definitive source by the majority, with disagreement regarding its types (consensus of the companions, consensus of the ummah, consensus of the Ahl al-Bayt, consensus of the people of Medina).

Analogy (qiyas): It is accepted by the majority and rejected by the Zahiri school.

Preference (istihsan): It is accepted by the Hanafis and Malikis, while the Shafi'is reject it as it is considered a judgment based on personal inclination.

Public Interest (maslaha mursala): It is widely adopted by the Malikis, while the Shafi'is restrict it by textual evidence.

Blocking the Means (sadd al-dhara'i): It is considered by the Malikis a strong tool for regulating future rulings, while its use is less common in other schools.

Presumption of Continuity (istishab): It is used by the Shafi'is and Hanbalis to affirm original rulings in the absence of new evidence.

Ranks of Evidence:

The arrangement of evidence in cases of conflict, such as prioritizing the Qur'an over the Sunnah, and prioritizing consensus over analogy.

Differences among schools in prioritizing certain evidence, such as the Hanafis prioritizing analogy over solitary reports.

Methods of Dealing with Supplementary Evidence:

The scope of use for each source (such as public interest) and its limits.

The rejection by some schools of sources like analogy or public interest due to the lack of explicit texts supporting them.

Second: Dealing with Detailed Evidence (Methods of Understanding Legal Texts)

Methodology of Understanding Texts:

Adhering to the apparent meaning of the text: such as the methodology of the Zahiris, in contrast to methodologies that rely on interpretation among the Malikis and Shafi'is.

Dealing with ambiguous texts: ijtihad to understand texts with potential meanings (such as the term "qurء").

Understanding texts according to context: the impact of the reasons for revelation on understanding texts among the majority.

Conflict Between Texts:

Rules for reconciling conflicting texts or prioritizing between them.

Dealing with the abrogation of rulings: such as the abrogation of some Qur'anic commands by the Sunnah or vice versa.
