Indeed, Allah is severe in punishment﴿ [Al-Hashr 7], and He, Exalted is He, said: ﴾But no! By your Lord, they will not believe until they make you judge in that which they dispute among themselves, and then find within themselves no discomfort from what you have judged and submit in [full, willing] submission﴿ ﴾An-Nisa 65﴿. Thus, making judgment, accepting the ruling, the absence of discomfort in the soul, and complete submission to His judgment are conditions for the realization or perfection of faith: ﴾And obey Allah and obey the Messenger and beware. And if you turn away, then know that upon Our Messenger is [only] the responsibility for clear notification﴿ ﴾Al-Ma'idah 92﴿, ﴾Whoever obeys the Messenger has obeyed Allah; and whoever turns away - We have not sent you over them as a guardian﴿ ﴾An-Nisa 80﴿, ﴾O you who have believed, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you. And if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger if you should believe in Allah and the Last Day. That is the best [way] and best in result﴿ ﴾An-Nisa 59﴿. He equated absolute obedience not restricted to the command of Allah with absolute obedience not restricted to the command of His Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him), by using the conjunction that added to the command of obedience the meaning of equality in taking the ruling itself, because both forms of obedience are obedience to the legislation of Allah which was revealed through revelation, and it is obedience to the Creator and to the infallible Prophet in what he conveys of legislation, a matter that results in the unity of the source from which humanity receives the Sharia, which is the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him), ﴾And whatever the Messenger has given you - take; and what he has forbidden you - refrain from﴿. Thus, the authority of legislation that must be obeyed in Islam is derived from the fact that this legislation was brought by the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) as the Qur'an and the Sunnah, a principle that Sayyid Qutb described in his work "In the Shade of the Qur'an" as representing the Islamic constitutional theory, which corresponds in the West to the notion that the nation is the source of authority.

Therefore, these are some aspects that Islam has encompassed regarding the concept of halal (permissible) and haram (forbidden), making it a societal conviction, which the community strives to uphold through enjoining good and forbidding wrong in every situation where any command or prohibition is violated. It has also transformed into an individual conviction for the Muslim, to the extent that if he violates any command or prohibition, he faces severe reprimand from his own reproaching soul!

If this is the case, we understand how the commitment of individuals, the community, and the state to the legal rulings is natural, strong, and immediate without delay, accompanied by a complete concern for proper adherence, without the need for police oversight! Before a Muslim undertakes any action, he must inquire about the legal ruling regarding that action and adhere to it without hesitation!
