The Arab, when he refers to his regulation of his affairs as both religion and politics at the same time, does not differentiate between them, and he considers any regulation of any behavior as a policy for him and a religion simultaneously; he thus understands the correct nature of the term politics.
Ibn Allān al-Siddiqi said in Dalil al-Falihin: In "Al-Kashaf" [by al-Zamakhshari] regarding His saying, ﴾Are we indeed to be judged?﴿ (as-Saffat: 53), "or its meaning is to be governed, meaning ruled in the context of religion as in politics."
It is mentioned in Lisan al-Arab among the meanings of religion: "Religion means the Sharia and authority, and from it is His saying, ﴾And fight them until there is no fitna and the religion is all for Allah﴿ [al-Anfal: 39]," etc.
Thus, the term politics in language is related to the governance of affairs and matters based on commands and prohibitions, wherein the one who commands and forbids or the one responsible for overseeing these matters is obeyed in what he commands and forbids. Islam has not left any detail or issue without addressing it with its commands and prohibitions, and it has established regulations for accountability based on the proper application of these commands and prohibitions, in this world and the Hereafter. The ruler is made responsible for certain duties through which he ensures the proper application of these commands and prohibitions, and he oversees the proper application by individuals and parties of what he has commanded them. Islam has made the individual responsible for some of these matters and accountable for their proper application, as well as for the proper application by the ruler of what he is responsible for, and likewise for the parties, and so on.
Al-Fayruz Abadi said in Al-Qamus Al-Muhit: "I governed the subjects politically: I commanded them and forbade them. And al-Zabidi said in Taj al-Arous: (I governed the subjects politically), with a kasra: (I commanded them and forbade them). To govern the matter politically: to carry it out. In Al-Sihah: meaning to be commanded and to be commanded over. And politics: the act of managing something in a way that improves it." etc. Abu Su'ud al-Imadi said in his Tafsir: ﴾Indeed, We sent Our messengers with clear signs and sent down with them the Scripture and the balance that the people may maintain [justice]﴿ [al-Hadid: 25], and it is said that it refers to justice to establish politics and to repel aggression. etc. Here comes the natural blending between governance and politics and between command!
From this is the hadith "The wise one is the one who holds himself accountable." etc. Some interpreters have said that its meaning is to hold oneself accountable, but the more accurate meaning extends to governing oneself so as not to fall into what one will be held accountable for. Do you not see that he (peace and blessings be upon him) explained it with what follows: "The wise one is the one who holds himself accountable and works for what comes after death, while the wicked one follows his desires and wishes upon Allah, the Exalted." Thus, the wicked one follows his desires, while the wise one governs himself according to the commands of Allah and restrains his desires.
Al-Azhari said in Tahdhib al-Lugha: "Abu Ubaid said: His saying: 'He held himself accountable' means he humbled and enslaved it... and religion for Allah from this is merely obedience to Him and worshiping Him. It has been said regarding His saying: 'The wise one is the one who holds himself accountable' that it means to hold it accountable." etc.
Thus, religion and politics are the worship of Allah, the Exalted, by following His commands and refraining from His prohibitions in regulating behavior and relationships!
