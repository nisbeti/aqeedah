It reaches every human being in every time and place, and to achieve societal and individual values, it is not enough for a person to feel instinctively repelled by an action or inclined towards it. (For some minds are inclined towards fornication and drinking alcohol; is their inclination sufficient evidence for the validity or improvement of the act? Where is the measure and where is the balance?!! The balance and the correct measure have been lost, and instinct can be affected by external factors such as culture. The Westerner does not see "by instinct" what the Muslim sees "by instinct," due to the change in balances and judgments resulting from external culture! The influence of instinct varies in minds in strength and weakness, precision in understanding and obscurity! Then, even if we hypothetically concede the validity of instinct as a measure and source for describing actions as good or bad, as they say: knowledge is good, and ignorance is bad, then show us how instinct will judge the ugliness or beauty of learning atheistic philosophy? Or on the ugliness or beauty of a ruling or law related to joint-stock companies?) This is something that the mind cannot encompass due to the multitude of external complications and their intricacies, so it cannot judge the action as good or bad, and thus it is not valid for it to judge the action with praise or blame; it must derive this judgment from another source, which is Allah, Exalted is He. Here, the research is from the perspective of judging the action, not from the perspective of its measure, and here the research is from the perspective of punishments for actions and rewards for them, not from the perspective of undertaking or refraining from them. Therefore, there is a difference between good and evil and between beauty and ugliness, and they are two completely separate inquiries.
Minds vary in strength and weakness, precision in understanding and obscurity, so the mind cannot have the ability to judge all actions in different circumstances and situations due to the absence of unseen factors, or because of a partial view that is not comprehensive, or preferences that may later prove to be corrupt, or from what the mind may not perceive as a fragmented understanding of reality, which can turn the judgment into the opposite of correctness in its consequences. What appeared to it as a benefit or a source of advantage may reveal that evil lies in one of its dark corners, as it has no conclusive knowledge of the outcomes and results of actions. For this reason, human legislations have been known to shift and fluctuate from one extreme to its opposite!
This does not strip the human mind (influenced by religious culture, provided it is not materialistic) of the ability to issue judgments in general and in detail, such as the goodness of justice and truthfulness, and the ugliness of oppression and aggression. However, the mind cannot grasp the goodness or ugliness of a set of actions without necessity or through contemplation and reflection, nor can it assert the consequences of any action, or the conditions that surround it to make it absolutely good and correct in that situation, or its results on the individual, society, and the public interest; it must take that from the Sharia, not from the mind, for there are no intrinsic qualities in the action that make the judgment on it clear and unambiguous.
This, and although justice is good according to the mind, achieving what establishes it is beyond the capacity of the mind to judge. Some may think that justice means equalizing men and women in inheritance or in public and private responsibilities, but this view proves to be deficient and often perpetuates injustice rather than justice, because the system of male and female interaction is more complex than being related to inheritance or work; rather, it pertains to a system of pastoral responsibilities, duties, rights, varying capabilities and preparations, and different conditions, which makes achieving justice in each of these matters something that the mind cannot judge.
As for describing it solely from the perspective of the mind, it is invalid; because the mind is subject to variation, difference, contradiction, following desires, and considering partial or incidental interests that soon prove to be corrupt. The mind's measurements of goodness and ugliness may be affected by the environment in which it lives, and they may vary and differ across ages as they succeed one another. If the measure of ugliness and goodness is left to the mind, then something may be considered ugly by one group of people and good by another; indeed, the same thing may be good in one era and ugly in another.
