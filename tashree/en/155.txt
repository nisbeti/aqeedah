We observe the following:
Actions and relationships fall under similar rules and titles, such as ways to satisfy sexual desire; all methods of satisfaction can be identified, and the ruling for each method can be determined, and this ruling remains fixed until the Day of Judgment. The rulings on marriage, divorce, and all other social system rulings, along with their details, conditions, pillars, limitations, and controls, remain eternally fixed.
A person's need for food is fulfilled by eating from what is on the earth that is edible, so what is forbidden among them is restricted, such as wine, pork, carrion, and others, and the ruling of permissibility is given to the remaining items, ﴾And why should you not eat from that upon which the name of Allah has been mentioned, while He has explained to you what He has forbidden you, except that to which you are compelled? And indeed, many lead astray by their desires without knowledge. Indeed, your Lord is most knowing of the transgressors.﴿ [Al-An'am 119].
It gives rulings on necessity, as Islam considers both normal conditions and exceptional — extraordinary necessity — conditions and provides rulings for each. Therefore, the resolution of a problem arising from a physical need extends to considering the act that satisfies hunger in both normal and exceptional conditions, as well as the rulings of prayer and fasting for the healthy and their rulings for the sick, disabled, and excused, and so on. For example, a traveler is permitted to break his fast, to combine and shorten prayers, and a sick person prays according to his ability, whether standing, sitting, or reclining. In the absence of water, he resorts to tayammum (dry ablution), and the one in necessity may eat from carrion and drink wine to preserve his life that is close to destruction. Thus, Islam has legislated rulings for normal cases (azimah) and has legislated rulings for exceptional cases (rukhsah), and so on.
Since the treatments are linked to human actions and relate to occurrences, each occurrence has a fixed ruling. The reality of adultery is different from the reality of kissing, which is different from the reality of touching and the reality of looking; each has its own ruling and punishment that corresponds to it, as a just recompense. The rulings are characterized by stability and precision in their application to reality. The reality of the solar eclipse prayer is different from the reality of the funeral prayer; each has its own rulings. Hence, wherever there is a reality, there is a ruling. Whenever an eclipse occurs, its prayer is established, and whenever adultery occurs, its punishment is enacted.
It is also noted that some of these actions are merely specific transactions that recur in every time and place. For example, the reasons for acquiring wealth, that is, the reasons for possessing wealth such as work (including hunting, trading, reviving barren land, and working for wages), inheritance, and state gifts to the subjects, and others. The reasons for the growth and increase of wealth are also specific transactions that have fixed rulings, such as the rulings of sale(261), leasing, contracts, gambling, and others. It is observed that transactions are the same, whether working for wages in China or in America.

(261) It is noteworthy that Islam has defined frameworks to safeguard transactions such as sale, for example, regulating the operations related to it through contracts that have three defined pillars: the two parties to the contract, the subject of the contract, and its wording. It has conditions, rulings, and controls that clarify when the contract is void, valid, or corrupted, what is permissible to sell and what is forbidden, and the rulings regarding the inclusion of ignorance in the sold item or price or time (gharar), and conditions of the contract such as the eligibility of the contracting parties, and the subject of the contract being capable of its ruling, etc. (See details in the book: Old and Contemporary Sales by Yusuf al-Sabatin, may Allah have mercy on him). He states, "This is the ruling of sale or this is the ruling of the option of sale or this is the ruling of currency exchange," and he says: ﴾And Allah has permitted trade and forbidden usury.﴿. He also states in what was narrated by al-Bukhari in the chapter (The two sellers have the option as long as they have not separated): "The two parties in a sale have the option over each other as long as they have not separated, except for the option of sale." He states in what was narrated by Muslim: "The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) prohibited silver for silver and gold for gold except in equal amounts, and he commanded us to buy silver with gold however we wished, and to buy gold with silver however we wished. A man asked him, 'Is it hand to hand?' He said, 'This is how I heard.'" Thus, the rulings of sales are completely regulated, just as the rulings of partnerships are astonishingly regulated, to the extent that we will see, as the French historian Gustave Le Bon states in his book "The Civilization of the Arabs," that the most famous French general, Napoleon Bonaparte, upon his return to his homeland France from Egypt in 1801, took with him a legal book from the school of Imam Malik ibn Anas called "Sharh al-Dardir on the Text of Khalil," and it is considered that Maliki jurisprudence was the first Islamic jurisprudence to accompany Europeans. This legal book that Bonaparte took with him, Le Bon states, was the foundation of French law, which was one of the most important reasons for the renaissance of the state, especially in the matter of rulings, contracts, and obligations, thus Islamic jurisprudence, especially Maliki, had a significant impact on French legislation, especially the civil code known as the Napoleonic Code."
