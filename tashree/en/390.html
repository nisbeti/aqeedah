The angles and considerations that must be examined when answering the question: Who has the right to legislate?

The production of ideas and a person's judgment on things and actions comes from understanding their reality: what is it? Then, from the perspective on life that determines a person's stance towards things and actions. Without this perspective on life, a person cannot be proactive; it becomes indifferent to him to eat anything as long as it satisfies his hunger. Stealing food becomes equivalent to eating through hard work, and killing becomes indifferent; there are no values to deter a person or define their behavior!

Therefore, the proactive, civilized person does not suffice with merely recognizing the reality of an apple as something that satisfies hunger, and that it is neither rotten nor unripe, in order to eat it. Rather, this action is preceded by a perspective on life that clarifies to him the reasons for possessing it and what pertains to its ruling; if it is lawful, he eats it!

To determine the stance on an action: whether a person should abandon it, perform it, or choose between action and inaction, and to determine his stance on the things related to his actions, whether to take them or leave them, or to choose, we find that this stance depends on viewing it through the following angles: the reality of the thing or action, goodness and badness, benefit and harm, praise and blame, reward and punishment.

Angle of goodness and badness:

The Muslim theologians of old discussed determining a person's stance on the action that governs behavior based on the angle of goodness and badness, which is based on perfection and beauty, and the angle of benefit and harm, which is based on the doctrinal or moral perspective, that is, imposing human values on the action and thing to describe it as good or bad, and the angle of praise and blame or reward and punishment, which is based on imposing the result of performing the action and the intended goal of doing it upon it.

His view of the thing or action, (what is their reality?), does it show perfection and deficiency in the reality of the action or thing?

From the angle of goodness and badness, that is, through the following considerations:

The first consideration: compatibility with human nature, and with innate inclinations in humans, and with their purposes or their opposition. Goodness is said to be compatibility with nature, and badness is said to be its opposition, as in saying: rescuing a drowning person is good, and accusing an innocent person is bad! What aligns with the purpose and brings benefit or averts harm is good, and what does not align with the purpose is bad, like killing Zaid for his enemies and allies; it is, from the first consideration, good, that is, compatible with the purpose, and from the second consideration, bad, that is, not compatible with their purpose. Or we can say: "What leads to what aligns with a person in acquisition and preservation is good; acquisition brings benefit, and preservation averts harm," and they express this totality with the term "suitable"(540), and it is said to be compatible with the actions of rational individuals in customs"(541).

The second consideration: that goodness is perfection, and badness is deficiency, as in saying: knowledge is good, and ignorance is bad, meaning it is a quality of perfection that necessitates the elevation of its possessor, while badness is a quality of deficiency that necessitates the degradation of its possessor.
