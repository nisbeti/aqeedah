Legislative goals and objectives that affect the governing rule of behavior:
Once we have concluded our study of the influences that genuinely and ostensibly affect behavior, we move on to examine the goals and objectives that can impact human behavior. Among these are: bringing about benefits, averting harms, preventing damage, establishing justice, preventing oppression, and ensuring the realization of fundamental rights such as the preservation of lineage, intellect, religion, wealth, life, human dignity, maintaining security, and safeguarding the state, or achieving servitude to the Creator, or ensuring the fulfillment of needs, or organizing relationships, and similar objectives and goals. All of these serve as influences on the legislative thought that governs behavior, even though Islamic and positive legislations differ in their perspectives and the realization of some of those objectives! We have addressed some aspects of this in the chapter on the objectives and goals of legislation and the enactment of laws, which must be clarified before answering the question: who has the right to legislate? So refer to it.
However, what is important in our research is that Islam has taken into account the realization of specific objectives and goals when enacting certain rulings. Among the nature of these objectives and goals are those that are temporary, such as narrowing the gap between the poor and the rich in society. The rulings legislated for this matter exist with the presence of the motivating cause for that legislation; if the entire society becomes wealthy, the ruling becomes dormant until the gap returns. These rulings are those that the legislator has justified with a reason that exists with the existence of the effect and ceases with its cessation. The second type is permanent, aimed at achieving objectives and goals wherever problems arise that the legislative rulings have organized to address. Islam seeks to establish justice at all times, prevent oppression, and achieve security, and so forth. Thus, Islamic legislation is indeed permanent! It is regulated in the manner of deriving laws through their extraction from evidence according to the rules of usul al-fiqh (principles of jurisprudence), and it is comprehensive, such that it takes some of its parts along with others to achieve the regulated legislative objectives and goals!
