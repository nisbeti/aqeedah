Important rules to frame the process of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) and taqlid (following a jurist):
First: Allah, Exalted is He, has commanded the accountable individuals with an emphatic command to follow what has been revealed by Him ﴾Follow what has been revealed to you from your Lord, and do not follow other than Him as allies. Little do you remember﴿ [al-A'raf 3], and He has restricted warning to revelation ﴾I only follow what is revealed to me, and I am only a clear warner﴿ [al-Ahqaf 9]. Therefore, it is incumbent upon the accountable person—whether a mujtahid (one who exerts effort to derive legal rulings) or a muqallid (one who follows a jurist)—to know the ruling that obliges him to obey Allah and His Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) and to fulfill the obligation.
Second: Since the purpose of ijtihad and taqlid is to reach the legal ruling in order to act upon it, what is required is not the personal opinion of the mujtahid, nor the imitation of his person by the muqallid. "For it is not from legitimate taqlid to take the opinion of a certain person as if it were his own opinion, or to consider it an opinion of a certain scholar. All of this is not legitimate taqlid, but rather it is taking from outside of Islam, which is prohibited by Sharia. It is not permissible for a Muslim to do so because Allah has commanded us to take from the Messenger Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him) and not from anyone else, regardless of who they are. Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And whatever the Messenger has given you, take it; and what he has forbidden you, refrain from it. And fear Allah; indeed, Allah is severe in punishment﴿ [al-Hashr 7]. There has also been a prohibition against taking opinions that come from people. In the hadith: “Indeed, Allah does not take away knowledge by removing it from the hearts of the servants, but He takes away knowledge by the death of scholars, until when there are no scholars left, people take ignorant leaders, and they are asked and they give opinions without knowledge, so they go astray and lead others astray.” Narrated by al-Bukhari. This means they give opinions based on their own reasoning. Therefore, the opinion that the mujtahid derives from the legal evidences is not considered his opinion; rather, it is a legal ruling. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: “Whoever introduces something into this matter of ours that is not part of it, it is to be rejected.” Narrated by al-Bukhari. He also said: “As for what follows, the best speech is the Book of Allah, and the best guidance is the guidance of Muhammad, and the worst of affairs are the newly invented ones, and every innovation is misguidance.” Narrated by Muslim. The newly invented matters are innovations. [Everything that contradicts the legal rulings when a method or manner for performing that legal ruling has been established, and one acts in another way or manner, is an innovation and a rejection.] It is everything that contradicts the Book and the Sunnah and the consensus regarding rulings, whether in speech or action. The legal ruling must be taken only from the legal evidences and not from anything else(221). We have previously demonstrated the necessity of exclusively following revelation.
Moreover, the opinion that the mujtahid derives without exhausting his efforts, or without considering the legal evidences, or without using the correct tools of derivation from the principles of jurisprudence and language, is not considered a legal opinion, and it is not permissible to follow it under any circumstances.
Thus, it is essential that the path and methodology followed by those who engage in ijtihad and taqlid to reach the ruling of the legislator is sound, as this methodology is the only correct path that leads to understanding the intent of the legislator and deducing the ruling from it. If one does not exhaust their efforts in obtaining a preponderance of belief regarding the ruling of the legislator, then the derived ruling is not the ruling of the legislator. If one does not follow established principles in dealing with the evidences and language, then the one deriving does not arrive at the ruling of the legislator.
Third: This also goes back to "the fact that Islam has prohibited us from following other than the path of knowledge, as He said: ﴾And do not follow that of which you have no knowledge. Indeed, the hearing, the sight, and the heart—all of those will be questioned﴿ [al-Isra 36]. From this, it becomes clear that the origin in every matter of the Sharia, whether it is something we are commanded to do or prohibited from doing, is that we reach knowledge of its ruling through one of the paths that lead to knowledge.
