The fourth condition: The action must be within the capacity to perform or refrain from it; there is no responsibility for the action in cases of inability, whether the inability is to act or to refrain. It is self-evident that ability is a condition for establishing responsibility. The texts of Islamic law indicating this condition are numerous, including Allah's saying: ﴾Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity﴿.
The fifth condition: The doer of the action must enjoy his freedom when performing the action, not being coerced into it. The lifting of responsibility in cases of coercion is indicated by what Ibn Abbas narrated from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) that he said: "Indeed, Allah has overlooked for my nation mistakes, forgetfulness, and what they are coerced into." Narrated by Ibn Majah and al-Bayhaqi, and it is an authentic hadith.
When we look at the set of laws regulating societal relations, we must find integration within them, and the societal dimension must be evident. For example, in Islam, you find laws and legislations that lead to achieving social solidarity (takafol al-ijtima'i). The laws and rulings encompass the matter from all its aspects to establish a societal system based on social solidarity! On the level of the individual, family, neighborhood, community, and state, and it is founded on the basis of responsibility in this world and accountability in the Hereafter. Among these are the responsibilities of the individual for caring for parents and the elderly, the neighborhood's responsibility for feeding the hungry, supporting orphans, and upholding the rights of neighbors, guests, and travelers, as well as the state's pastoral responsibility to ensure the basic needs of the subjects when they are unable to fulfill them, and so on, supported by the necessary laws to achieve this, such as the system of zakat, expiations, charity, aiding the needy, rescuing the distressed, endowments (waqf), bequests (wasiyya), loans (ariyya), and others. Where do you find this in Western legislations? Yes, they have excelled in legislations related to finance and tax systems, because their system is one of revenue collection, not one of care. However, when it comes to societal legislations that establish cooperative, supportive, and collaborative communities, their laws have not uttered a word! This is only because the nature of their system is purely capitalist! Individualistic(374), purely so(375)!
