The method of the Islamic system in ensuring its proper application in reality:

The motivation that drives a Western individual to apply laws lies in seeing them achieve justice in his individual capacity (meaning he sees the effect of that legislation on him as an individual), his fear of punishment, and his upbringing to respect the laws. These are the issues relied upon for compliance with human laws. If that individual sees the tax system as unjust and can risk bypassing it, feeling secure from oversight and punishment, many will do so without hesitation, while others will refrain. You also see him revolting against it by supporting parties that oppose it. It is common to see the manipulation of people's emotions and the buying of their electoral votes by promising to alleviate the tax burden from their shoulders. This is a personal incentive that does not take the form of belief or faith in the validity of the remedy and the commitment to its implementation, nor the psychological impact that legal rulings have on the soul (the reproaching soul that chastises its owner for committing sin), nor the fear of God's punishment in the Hereafter. A Muslim considers adherence to legal rulings as an act of worship, fears Allah, and hopes to attain His pleasure by adhering to His rulings. When the matter is public, the community advises, enjoins what is right, forbids what is wrong, holds accountable, and restrains the oppressor, and so forth. The weakest motivation in the Islamic community is the fear of punishment, while that fear in the West is the primary incentive relied upon. The difference is vast, and the gap is wide. In Islam, there is a comprehensive system of motivations that urge a person to act voluntarily and willingly, drawing closer to Allah, in addition to deterring those who do not comply through authority and the punishment of the Hereafter. There is no doubt that you have noticed that Islamic legislation is based on "integrated" systems that are interwoven in any matter related to it, even in ensuring its proper application. There is a series of rulings, regulations, and responsibilities that guarantee the proper and effective application of it, with roles for the individual, the community, and the state!

We will provide an example of how Islam surrounds obligations with a comprehensive system of legislation that guarantees the application of this ruling in reality in a way that bears fruit, with implicit safeguards for its effective performance in fulfilling what it was legislated for, and clarifying how it is established in reality. The legislator does not suffice with commanding that the ruler judges by what Allah has revealed, so that the customs of the community become the customs of Islam, and deviating from the values, morals, standards, concepts, and convictions established by the legislator becomes a crime addressed by the legislator on several levels commensurate with its magnitude and impact, according to a mechanism that fits the nature of the wrongdoing that is being changed or deviated from(385). Responsibilities are distributed at all levels: the individual, the community, and the state, and it elaborates on the rulings regarding deviating from those rulings(386) and on the state, and the rulings for the protection of the state (the criminal legislation specific to the state), including the rulings on the subjects rebelling against the state(387), and the rulings on the ruler deviating from the system of values upon which the state was established(388), especially the deviation.
