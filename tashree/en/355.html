The legislative objectives (maqasid al-sharia) vary in their complexity, and minds differ in studying the effects of the many and diverse factors influencing the issue, and in assessing the preference between them, as well as the immediate or subsequent effects that result from that. There are also other factors whose existence cannot be denied, which necessitate the acknowledgment of the inability of reason to legislate and the need to prevent desires from influencing legislation.

That the legislator should possess knowledge encompassing the nature of humans, and what is suitable for people in their worldly life and the Hereafter, and what rectifies them, and improves their livelihood and actions, and what the creation can bear and what they cannot bear, and what alleviates hardship and difficulty from them, and what is appropriate for every time, place, and condition in a manner that ensures the establishment of truth and the realization of justice at all times.

The legislator must possess comprehensive knowledge of the nature of the reality in which he legislates, as well as the issues, circumstances, and conditions that determine the validity of the judgment regarding goodness and virtue or their absence, or that affect the reality. These issues are undoubtedly complex, and their effects on the desired outcome may conflict, necessitating a preference among them (for example, wine and gambling have benefits for people, but their sin is greater than their benefit), or they may be known only relatively or incompletely to the human mind, and the knowledge surrounding the nature of diverse problems (economic, social, etc.), which requires technical and specialized expertise that is often not available in parliament. If, in such matters, recourse is made to specialists, they lack the authority granted to them to rule, as well as the infallibility from error, and the immunity from being influenced by desires and biases. They also lack the mandate to decide on these issues and to represent their opinion as the general will. Furthermore, their differing perspectives on issues and their solutions (there is no clearer evidence of this than the diversity of economic schools and their confusion in solving economic problems despite the expertise of those involved) present serious obstacles and sensitive influences that are crucial for establishing justice and the validity of rulings, and for a correct understanding of the public interest. These are issues that the mind cannot fully grasp, which necessitates leaving legislation to the Creator.

These dilemmas cannot be resolved by favoring the reasoning of one person over another or a group, or by adopting the reasoning of a particular faction.

A reference for the remaining minds, in light of a reality that affects all minds and desires due to those deficiencies and needs necessary for the validity of the resulting judgment, and dilemmas cannot be solved by presenting statistics on the impact of a certain legislation in society, such as studying the number of divorce cases, for the motivations, circumstances, and psychologies, and the influence of each on those outcomes is extremely intricate and complex. The solution to the dilemma is not found through an experimental scientific study, because the essence of the experiment is to recreate the natural phenomenon within the laboratory, or to expose the material to certain conditions and observe its behavior under those conditions, and then there is observation and conclusion. A human being is not like an atom in their interaction with events, and humans have the ability to design social causal systems and control them, unlike material that can be subjected to experimentation. Furthermore, the measures or motivations necessary for issuing a judgment, or those influencing actions such as good and evil, free will, volition, spiritual values, and the like cannot be addressed by experimentation, as they are metaphysical issues that cannot be quantified or measured.
