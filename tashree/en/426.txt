Appendix 4: Are people in the West happy?
As for the understanding of happiness and tranquillity, people differ in how they understand it and in what establishes it in reality: a poor man may think his happiness lies in becoming rich, and a sick man may see that his happiness is not achieved unless he is fully healed(558),
and the materialist doctrines see happiness in satisfying instincts and organic needs to the greatest degree, and in unleashing freedoms, especially personal freedom. They understand happiness as "pleasure", "ecstasy" or "luxury", which has been reflected in a limitless social chaos, reflected in the spread of problems of violence, disease, family breakdown, drugs and crime, and prisons filled as never before in history, and so on. Possessing power, fame and money, and increasing it by any means, became the goal of goals, which produced a class able to control the destinies of states, and peoples working for it for what keeps them alive. Fewer than five percent of the population came to own more than ninety percent of the wealth, and they owned all the power as well, and the rest was divided among the rest, accompanied by hardship of living (enormous debts on individuals and families) and the toil of work, so that the causes of happiness for them were truly causes of misery!
The focus was on material satisfaction, ecstasy, momentary pleasure, pride in achievements and confidence in the level of luxury, accompanied by neglect of the importance of the spiritual value and the necessity of satisfying it. That led to an incomplete conception of life and a departure from the effect of happiness on the soul, which is represented by tranquillity, psychological comfort, inner peace, sound mind, positivity and contentment with oneself. This is not complete except through conviction of the soundness of the reference for conduct, clarity of the purpose of life, the existence of a true positive meaning for which a person lives, and conviction of the soundness of the thoughts and feelings a person holds and by which he conducts his life, which give him the true ability to deal with life's pressures by interpreting them correctly in a way that leads to contentment and tranquillity. True happiness is not the relative, momentary, fluctuating happiness, or the dynamic, changing kind, which turns into a deadly negativity that destroys a person's contentment with himself or with the circumstances surrounding him when they turn.
The separation that took place in the West between the human being who gained luxury of living, and the concepts, standards and convictions from which the system that directs conduct will emerge, so that it cared for the first (luxury) and cut the umbilical cord between that human being and the second (that his luxury be the product of sound concepts, standards and convictions that direct conduct), is nothing but making an intellectual rift between happiness and the foundation from which it cannot be separated in life, namely a person's possession of sound concepts, standards and convictions that direct conduct.
