Al-Bukhari: "When the believers are freed from the Fire, they will be held at a bridge between Paradise and Hell, where they will settle the injustices that occurred among them in this world until they are purified and refined, then they will be permitted to enter Paradise."
He also linked it to the issue of trust, ﴾O you who have believed, do not betray Allah and the Messenger or betray your trusts while you know [the consequence]﴿ [Al-Anfal 27]. The Muslim views Allah's commands and prohibitions as a contract that Allah, Exalted is He, has entrusted him to fulfill properly. Any deviation from this contract is akin to betraying that trust, and this serves as a strong deterrent against committing sin. The evidence for this is: ﴾And remember the favor of Allah upon you and His covenant by which He bound you when you said, "We hear and we obey." And fear Allah; indeed, Allah is Knowing of that within the breasts﴿ [Al-Ma'idah 7]. This refers to when you pledged allegiance to the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) to listen and obey, which entails fulfilling Allah's promise to them based on their commitment to His covenant. He attributed the covenant to Himself, Exalted is He, to emphasize its importance: ﴾Those who fulfill the covenant of Allah and do not break the contract﴿ [Ar-Ra'd 20]. It is a contract for listening and obeying, and here, failing to uphold it is considered betrayal.
Moreover, public office in the Islamic system is regarded as a trust that has been entrusted to them, which the public servant must fulfill and care for in the best manner, due to the rights it entails and what it represents in terms of trust, confidence, and responsibility. Thus, it is obligatory to ensure proper selection, monitoring, and accountability by the state and the community regarding its proper execution. Al-Bukhari (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated from Al-Hasan that "Ubaidullah bin Ziyad visited Ma'qil bin Yasar during his illness in which he died. Ma'qil said to him: 'I will tell you a hadith that I heard from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him). I heard the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) say: There is no servant whom Allah entrusts with a flock and does not guide them with sincere advice, except that he will not smell the fragrance of Paradise.'" Al-Manawi stated in Fath al-Qadir: This means that he is entrusted with the care of a flock, which refers to those under his care, as he is appointed to manage their interests and is given control over their affairs, being the guardian and trustworthy custodian of what he is responsible for. The Legislator, Exalted is He, has made governance a trust that must be delivered to its rightful owners(400), as He followed His command to deliver trusts to their rightful owners with the instruction that the ruler must govern justly, and that the believers must obey Allah and His Messenger and those in authority among them.
Islam has also emphasized the comprehensiveness of responsibility (accountability) for words, thoughts, and knowledge, ﴾And do not follow that of which you have no knowledge. Indeed, the hearing, the sight, and the heart - about all those [one] will be questioned﴿ [Al-Isra 36]. This means do not follow what you do not know, and do not say, "I heard" when you did not hear, and do not say, "I saw" when you did not see, and do not claim knowledge when you do not know.
Additionally, Islam has linked accountability to the concept of gratitude for blessings and fulfilling their rights: ﴾Then you will surely be asked that Day about pleasure﴿ [At-Takathur 8], in that the competition for more has distracted them from accepting what saves them, with a threat and fear of being held accountable for the blessings they enjoyed in this world without thanking Allah for them. In the hadith: "You will be questioned about the gratitude for that blessing," it serves as a reminder for them to be grateful for every blessing. The questioning of the believers [about the blessings] is a question for arranging reward for gratitude or for holding accountable for the unlawful blessings [for the disbelievers]. (Ibn Ashur).
Thus, it is evident that the system of accountability in Islam leads to self, societal, judicial, and administrative oversight concerning the proper execution of responsibilities.

(400) Al-Bukhari (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated from Abu Huraira (may Allah be pleased with him) who said: "While the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) was in a gathering speaking to the people, a Bedouin came and said, 'When is the Hour?' The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) continued speaking. Some of the people said: 'He heard what was said and disliked it,' and others said: 'Rather, he did not hear.' When he finished his speech, he said: 'Where is the one who asked about the Hour?' The man said: 'Here I am, O Messenger of Allah!' He said: 'When trust is lost, then wait for the Hour.' The man asked: 'How is that loss, O Messenger of Allah?' He said: 'When authority is entrusted to those who do not deserve it, then wait for the Hour.'" The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) linked the loss of trust with the delegation of authority to those who are unworthy, meaning the delegation of governance to those who do not deserve it and do not carry it out properly. Thus, he explained the noble verse: ﴾Indeed, Allah commands you to render trusts to whom they are due and when you judge between the people to judge with justice. Excellent is that which Allah instructs you. Indeed, Allah is ever Hearing and Seeing﴿ [An-Nisa 58]. Ibn Battal said: The meaning of "when authority is entrusted to those who do not deserve it" is that the leaders have been entrusted by Allah with His servants and it is obligatory upon them to advise them, so they should appoint the righteous. If they appoint others who are not righteous, they have betrayed the trust that Allah has entrusted to them. (Fath al-Bari by Ibn Hajar, Book of Raqaiq, Chapter on the Elevation of Trust).
