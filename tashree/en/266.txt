the third column: The Concept of Accountability:
We discussed in some detail the concept of accountability in the section: "The Islamic System's Method in Ensuring Proper Implementation in Reality," when the concept of accountability relates to ensuring the integrity of concepts, standards, values, and convictions in society. Accountability is not limited to holding humans accountable on the Day of Judgment, but extends to the accountability of society, individuals, and the state to one another, and their questioning to evaluate their adherence to the legal rulings (commanding good and forbidding evil) and their implementation.
Islam links accountability to self-monitoring of behavior, manifesting the concept of accountability as the Muslim holding himself accountable before being held accountable for his actions in the Hereafter, and weighing them before they are weighed against him, ﴾O you who have believed, fear Allah and let every soul look to what it has put forth for tomorrow. And fear Allah; indeed, Allah is All-Aware of what you do﴿ [al-Hashr 18]. Sayyid Qutb said in Fi Zilal al-Qur'an: "It is also an expression with meanings and implications broader than its words, and its mere seriousness on the heart opens before it a page of its deeds, indeed a page of its life, and extends its sight across all its lines to contemplate them, and to look at the balance of its account with its details and specifics to see what it has prepared for tomorrow on this page. This contemplation is sufficient to awaken it to its weaknesses, deficiencies, and shortcomings, no matter how much good it has previously done or effort it has exerted. So how if its balance of good is little and its balance of righteousness is meager?! It is a touch that the heart never sleeps after, nor does it cease to look and reflect." End quote. In the hadith: "The wise one is the one who holds himself accountable and acts for what comes after death, while the wicked one is the one who follows his desires and wishes for Allah, the Exalted," narrated by al-Tirmidhi and graded as good. The wicked follows his desires, while the wise governs himself according to Allah's rulings and restrains his desires. This type of accountability represents a principle that drives the Muslim to maintain constant self-monitoring of his actions. If he risks and commits a sin, his reproaching self will chastise him severely, and these concepts will lead him to return to the path of righteousness, making these concepts part of the effectiveness of Islam in implementing the Sharia.
The concept of accountability also manifests in that it is one of the requirements of obligation that Allah, the Exalted, has arranged for the performance of obligations with the promise of reward or the warning of accountability for even the weight of an atom of action. Allah, the Exalted, said: ﴾So whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it﴿ [al-Zalzalah 7-8], ﴾And the record will be placed, and you will see the criminals fearful of what is in it, and they will say, "Woe to us! What is this book that leaves nothing small or large except that it has enumerated it?" And they will find what they did present before them. And your Lord does not do injustice to anyone﴿ [al-Kahf 49], ﴾This is our record that speaks about you in truth. Indeed, we were transcribing what you were doing﴿ [al-Jathiyah 29], ﴾And indeed, over you are keepers, noble and recording; they know whatever you do﴿ [al-Infitar 10-12], ﴾And We will set up the scales of justice on the Day of Resurrection, so no soul will be treated unjustly at all. And if there is the weight of a mustard seed, We will bring it forth. And sufficient are We as accountants﴿ [al-Anbiya 47], whether in private or in public: ﴾To Allah belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is in the earth. And if you disclose what is within yourselves or conceal it, Allah will bring you to account for it. Then He will forgive whom He wills and punish whom He wills. And Allah is over all things competent﴿ [al-Baqarah 284]. In the authentic hadith agreed upon: "There is none among you except that his Lord will speak to him, with no interpreter between him and his Lord. He will look to his right and will see nothing but what he has brought forth, and he will look to his left and will see nothing but what he has brought forth, and he will look in front of him and will see nothing but the Fire facing him. So protect yourselves from the Fire, even if with half a date."
Islam has firmly linked the issue of accountability with the issue of rights and achieving justice, and lifting oppression. Abu Huraira reported that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "You will certainly be made to return the rights to their people on the Day of Resurrection, even if it is a matter of retribution for a hornless sheep from a horned sheep."
