a specific legal text or consensus: The existence of a Qur'anic or prophetic text or consensus that explicitly prohibits an action means that it is harmful according to Islamic law, whether the mind perceives its detailed cause or not. For example, alcohol, usury, and the exposure of private parts are prohibited by text, and thus they are harms that must be avoided absolutely, even if some people perceive immediate benefits in them. Allah, Exalted is He, says: ﴾They ask you about wine and gambling. Say, "In them is great sin and [yet, some] benefit for people. But their sin is greater than their benefit."﴿ [al-Baqarah 219]. Therefore, everything that revelation has prohibited due to its foulness or harm is considered harmful, even if it is beautified by people's desires.

Violation of a right or a legally recognized interest: Any action that leads to harming others or violating one of the five necessities (religion, life, intellect, wealth, honor) is a prohibited harm. The Sharia came to preserve these objectives, prohibiting everything that violates them, such as unlawful killing, theft, adultery, slander, and drinking intoxicants... The principle is stated in the saying of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him): "The blood, wealth, and honor of every Muslim are sacred." The rule is: "Harm must be removed"; thus, everything that constitutes an infringement on the material or moral rights of others must be prevented and eliminated.

The inherent harm of a thing or action: The legal ruling (prohibition of destruction/prohibition of causing harm to oneself or others/prohibition of corruption/removal of harm) is established by general evidence, and then it is applied to individuals based on the realization of the description. If a thing or action is harmful by its nature to all people or the majority of them, it is considered a legal harm and is prohibited. For example, deadly poisons and mind-altering drugs are prohibited in themselves because their harm is unequivocal. Likewise, all substances or actions that have been proven by medicine or conclusive experience to destroy the body or corrupt the intellect or disrupt human nature without any overriding necessity fall under the general texts that prohibit throwing oneself into destruction and everything that involves corruption and spoilage. The rule is: whatever has pure or predominant harm and involves corruption is prohibited. However: why is it not said, "Medicine is the reference for defining harm"? Because this implies two unintended matters: the first is that "harm" is a legislative normative term defined by medicine (which is false). The second is that reason/science creates a legal ruling (which is also false). Therefore, medicine does not define "legal harm"; rather, it provides expert testimony that proves that this thing is "destructive/corrupt/harmful predominantly" in reality, and thus the legal ruling is applied to it. A general Qur'anic principle in the realm of knowledge is: ﴾So ask the people of knowledge if you do not know﴿, which is a foundation for referring to specialists when ignorant of a technical subject, and the practical foundational rule commonly accepted among jurists is: the ruling on a thing is a branch of its conception; and the conception in medical matters can only be established through experience. Thus, the legal principle is: it is prohibited for a Muslim to destroy himself or corrupt his intellect, and it is prohibited to cause harm in the first place (no harm and no harming). Then, every substance/action that has been established through sensory perception and credible experience and the testimony of experts to be destructive or corrupting to the intellect or damaging to the body in a predominant manner falls under that principle as an application, not as a creation; because this is part of realizing the conditions, not from the perspective of legislating a standard of harm.

Relative specific harm in certain circumstances: An action or substance may originally be permissible, but it becomes harmful in the case of a specific person or particular circumstance. In this case, the action is prohibited for that person or in that circumstance specifically. For example, alcohol is originally a permissible substance, but consuming it becomes a prohibited harm for a diabetic patient if it will exacerbate his condition. Likewise, permissible foods or medicines that some individuals are allergic to and that cause them harm are prohibited.
