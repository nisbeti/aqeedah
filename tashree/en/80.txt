The obligation of certainty in the sources of Islamic legislation and its evidence:
In order to consider any thought from the system of Islam that comes in the Book, or the Sunnah, or consensus, or analogy, whether it is a report(165) or an expression(166), as part of the religion, it is necessary to establish a connection with revelation.
As for the detailed evidences (the signs, that is, the partial evidences regarding the practical legal rulings related to the discussed issue, such as verses of the Qur'an or hadiths of the Sunnah) from which we derive the ruling of each issue, the connection with revelation is established through certainty or through the preponderance of evidence based on exhaustive induction of all the evidences related to the issue, in order to derive the most accurate understanding from their totality, through the process of ijtihad, as will be detailed later. This process of exhausting all means, which involves studying all the evidences and signs related to a particular issue in their sources, is carried out by examining their particulars and what they contain of implications to deduce the legal ruling disseminated within them, that is, to prove its existence based on those evidences and signs, and the formulations and meanings of those formulations, which share in providing evidence for that ruling and reinforce the strength of that evidence. This process raises the degree of uncertainty present in individual evidences to a level close to certainty, though it does not reach it. The strength of those evidences combines with one another, filling the gaps of uncertainty present in individual evidences, resulting in a form of knowledge regarding that ruling due to the multitude of narrators involved in transmitting individual signs, and the strengthening of some of them by other stronger chains, and the inclusion of different expressions for the same shared meanings, and the variety of different occurrences in expressions or events that indicate that ruling.
As for the sources of legislation (general evidences(167)) in which we search for the detailed evidences, it is essential that these evidences possess the characteristic of certainty as evidences, meaning that it is established that the Wise Legislator has accepted them as sources that encompass the system He has chosen for human life, so that we can search for the detailed evidences in them for three reasons:
"First: The ruling that a Muslim must follow is a legal ruling, not a rational one; it is God's ruling on the matter, not the ruling established by humans. Therefore, the general evidence from which this ruling is derived must have come through revelation (that is, the Qur'an, Sunnah, consensus, and analogy).
Second: Proving that the general evidence from which the ruling is derived has come through revelation must be a definitive proof, meaning that the evidence that the evidence from which the ruling is derived has come through revelation must be a conclusive evidence, not a presumptive one; because it is from the fundamentals and not from the branches, thus presumption is not sufficient in it. It is from the realm of beliefs and not from the realm of legal rulings. What is required to derive the ruling from it is evidence that has come through revelation, not just any evidence. It must be established that it has come through revelation, and proving that it has come through revelation is from the realm of belief and not from legal rulings. Hence, it is necessary for it to be evidence that has been established that revelation has come through it with definitive proof; because beliefs are only taken with certainty, while legislation is taken with certainty or with the preponderance of evidence."

The schools of thought also differ regarding other evidences, such as juristic preference (istihsan), public interest (maslaha mursala), customary practices (urf), the legislation of those before us, the opinion of a Companion, presumption of continuity (istishab), blocking the means (sadd al-dhara'i), and other types of consensus such as the consensus of the Ummah or the people of Medina. The science of usul al-fiqh addresses these evidences in terms of establishing their authority and status in deriving rulings, which will yield the desired outcomes, namely the practical legal rulings.
