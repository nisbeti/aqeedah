The implications of words:

General and specific: Does one act upon the general before it is specified?

Absolute and restricted: Is the absolute always interpreted in light of the restricted?

Text and apparent meaning: The difference between what explicitly indicates a ruling (text) and what implies it (apparent meaning).

The relationship between commands and prohibitions:

Does a command always imply obligation, or can it imply recommendation, or merely a request with the context determining the type of ruling?

Does a prohibition always imply prohibition, or can it imply dislike? Or is the prohibition merely a request?

The impact of the Arabic language:

The understanding of the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) of the language and its effect on their interpretation of texts.

The differences in linguistic interpretation among the jurisprudential schools.

Thus, we see that these issues lead to differences in legal rulings due to the varying principles followed, and the differing methodologies in dealing with general or detailed evidence, which creates variations among the jurisprudential schools. However, the impact of these differences necessitates a systematic approach to the evidence and legal indications and prevents random handling or mixing between these schools during the derivation of legal rulings. This means that a jurist must adhere to specific, well-defined principles before engaging in ijtihad (independent reasoning), and also that a follower must adhere to a method that ensures that the one they follow is bound by specific, well-defined principles in the particular issues they inquire about. If a follower wishes to imitate a ruling on a matter of prayer, then the follower must ensure that the one who gives them a fatwa (legal opinion) adheres to specific principles in all matters of prayer and does not mix between different schools in their fatwas for that follower. Therefore, the follower must ask the mufti: What is the ruling on such-and-such issue in such-and-such school, rather than just asking for any legal ruling on those matters, so that they are not given one ruling according to the Shafi'i school and another according to the Hanafi school in matters of prayer, for example.
