Chapter Two

Study: How Islam Addresses Human Problems with Solutions that Do Not Change Over Time and Space

Introduction and Presentation:

Among the legal sciences parallel to the science of the objectives of Islamic law (maqasid al-sharia) is the philosophy of law (or jurisprudence), which has taken a leading position in Western legal studies under the title: (Philosophy of law), due to the nature of its subjects that focus on the study of the rules of granting authority(236) and obedience to it, the goals and wisdoms that lie behind legal systems, and uncovering the relationship between law and society. These generally revolve around: protecting "human rights," achieving justice and the common good; legal stability and legal security, and every goal that has foundations upon which it is based and means that lead to its realization. It examines the nature of law, especially in relation to human values, practices, and communities, and ethics – its connection to some ethical elements; according to the natural law theory(237), or conversely: the necessity of its separation from them as established by the positivist school for this separation(238); thus, law does not contain any ethical elements in its content. This philosophy attempts to establish legislation that is suitable for all laws (not for a specific country) at all times (not in a specific era).

We will see that legislators in the West base their legislation on foundations that rely on achieving freedom (for example, as long as adultery or homosexuality is consensual, it becomes permissible because it enables them to exercise their freedom, and they have gone so far as to enact laws permitting "euthanasia" when chosen by the patient), or they rely on achieving material benefit, and these are two perspectives.

(236) The Austrian-born jurist Hans Kelsen (1881-1973) - a fierce opponent of natural law theories - identified the central problem of the philosophy of law as how to interpret the normative power of law - the right to inform people what they must (or must not) do, and not just what they must (or must not) do under the threat of punishment. This means: who has the right to prohibit you from a certain action or compel you to another? Whether he forces you with the penalties he has the right to impose, or he compels you absolutely to adopt his conceptions so that you bind yourself to his laws and legislations always! And how did he acquire that right? And in a mandatory manner, he punishes for violations of it! So when a judge listens to a case and the plaintiff decides, and orders the defendant to pay financial compensation, the judge's authority to do so stems from the rules of the legal system that empower the judge to issue such decisions, taking into account the various procedural and substantive constraints that have been enacted by the legislative body. But what gives these rules their authority? Perhaps it is the constitution; the foundational document of the legal system that establishes a legislative body authorized to enact the procedural and substantive rules that govern court decisions and determine who can exercise the judge's authority and under what circumstances. But what gives the constitution the right to do that? An infinite regression looms (an infinite sequence of causes) if one assumes an additional source to grant authority to the constitution itself. Since law demands authority, the only way to avoid infinite regression is to assume that the authority of the foundational document or constitution is derived from a "basic norm," the essence of which is something like "the constitution must be obeyed." Thus, ultimately, the question remains: why should society obey what has been documented by the legislative body composed of a few individuals (constitutional jurists, lawyers, and some politicians) from a constitution that they deemed appropriate for the state, and then imposed the laws they deemed suitable for that state, which all individuals must obey! Thus, they have granted themselves the authority to impose obedience to the document upon which its obedience and the obedience to what has emerged from it or from that legislative committee is binding and punishable for violations!
