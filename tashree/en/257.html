The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) freed those slaves who were in his possession, and Abu Bakr spent large sums of money purchasing slaves from the noblemen of the disbelievers of Quraysh, to free them and grant them liberty. The public treasury would buy slaves from their owners and free them whenever there was a surplus of funds available. He made one of the eight categories of zakat a means for the emancipation of slaves! It was determined that zakat would be disbursed from the public treasury - which is the general treasury in modern terminology - to assist those slaves who had entered into contracts of emancipation (mukataba) to pay the price of their freedom if they were unable to do so from their own earnings.

Thus, the second phase addressed the psychological aspect of the slaves, by preparing them psychologically to bear the responsibilities of their own lives and to enter the trials of life and society as free individuals, after the first phase had addressed the human aspect!

The third phase: the phase of emancipation:

Islam established practical rulings that necessitate the emancipation of slaves. It enacted rulings that compel emancipation, as it made the emancipation of a slave owned by a close relative who is prohibited (mahram) automatic, whether the owner consented or not, whether he emancipated him or not. Every person who owns a close relative by blood, whether he acquired him through purchase or inheritance, that relative immediately becomes free and is emancipated without the need for his owner to emancipate him. Abu Dawood narrated from al-Hasan from Samurah that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: “Whoever owns a close relative is free.” The punishment of a slave through burning, amputation, mutilation, or severe beating, and even if he slaps him, makes it obligatory for his emancipation. If his master does not emancipate him, the ruler will emancipate him forcibly from his master. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: “Whoever slaps his slave or beats him, his expiation is to emancipate him.” Narrated by Muslim through the chain of Ibn Umar.

Islam provided slaves with means for liberation, such as mukataba, and the emancipation of slaves as expiation for sins, or through the act of kindness of freeing slaves, or by teaching a slave ten Muslims to read and write, and so on.

Mukataba is the granting of freedom to a slave whenever he requests it himself, in exchange for an agreed-upon sum of money between the master and the slave. The emancipation here is obligatory, and the master has no right to refuse or delay it after the agreed amount has been paid. Otherwise, the state (the judge or ruler) intervenes to enforce the emancipation by force, granting freedom to the one who requests it. Thus, after Islamic legislation has prepared the slave psychologically to contemplate bearing the responsibilities of himself, if he finds himself desiring freedom, the system of mukataba guarantees him immediate realization of his freedom.

Islam did not stop at encouraging emancipation and establishing rulings that compel it, but it also established a specific category in the public treasury for the emancipation of slaves, as it made zakat allocated for the emancipation of slaves, making it one of the eight categories. Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾The alms are only for the poor and the needy and those who collect them and for bringing hearts together and for freeing captives and for those in debt and for the cause of Allah and for the traveler﴿ [al-Tawbah 60], thus His saying: “and for freeing captives” refers to the emancipation of slaves. He did not specify a certain amount for this category, and it is permissible for the state to allocate a portion of zakat funds for the emancipation of slaves; in fact, it is permissible for it to allocate all zakat funds for the emancipation of slaves if there is no necessity for another category of zakat. This is because the disbursement of zakat does not have to be made to all eight categories, but it may be specified to one of these categories as deemed appropriate by the caliph of the Muslims.

Thus, a slave may request from the public treasury the price of his mukataba, and he can free himself from slavery immediately whenever he wishes.
