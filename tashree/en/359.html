The validity of legislation and its objectivity means that it does not favor one person over another. Legislation should not stem from achieving deceptive slogans such as social equality (without considering the differences in nature, responsibilities, and abilities), and it must be practically applicable (like social solidarity), rather than imaginary (like building democracy on the opinion of the majority or the general will).

To ensure that behavior leads to the cultivation of the earth through righteous deeds rather than causing corruption therein, it must take into account the humanity of the individual and their intellect, achieving their true interests. This requires that the legislator possesses the ability to understand the true interest and the public interest, along with the influences surrounding them.

The ability to achieve justice and fairness, equality before the law, and the guarantee of rights, while achieving a balance between individual rights and the public interest and the common good, and that the punishment corresponds to the offense, with equality and similarity in punishment.

Legislation must delve into the depths of various problems, what influences them, and what they are affected by, viewing them from a comprehensive perspective based on an understanding of human nature, the reality of its motives, aspirations, and desires, as well as an understanding of human life and the diversity of its needs and fluctuations.
