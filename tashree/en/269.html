The Fourth Column: The Concept of Responsibilities and Duties, a Terrifying Concept!

We have discussed the topic of responsibilities in some detail in the section: "The Pastoral Nature of Islam versus the Extractive Nature of Capitalism," so refer to it. Responsibility in Islam can be expressed with the term: taklif (obligation), and the capacity of the obligated person to undertake it is referred to as "ahliyyah" (eligibility), provided that the conditions for bearing responsibility that we discussed in the section on the pastoral nature of Islam are met. The concept of responsibility intersects with the concept of accountability and is closely related to it, as the word responsibility means obligation and also includes the meaning of being held accountable for the proper fulfillment of this obligation. Thus, the meanings and dimensions previously mentioned in the section on accountability also apply to this section. Since Islamic legislation is based on the concept of: shari'ah ruling, relying on the concept of taklif, and the meaning of taklif is the obligation of the obligated person to do or refrain from what the shari'ah requires, or to treat doing and refraining equally, according to the conditions of taklif, it follows that the implication of taklif is that a person must adhere to the rulings of the shari'ah, to govern all aspects of their life, to shape their life in its form, and to manage the affairs of everything and everyone over whom the shari'ah has granted them authority, or for whom they have been tasked with the responsibility of acting in accordance with that obligation through decisions, commitments, choices, actions, and the management of interests, assessing them according to their significance to fulfill the trust of undertaking that responsibility, whether the responsibility is for oneself; their body and religion, or for their family, relatives, and neighbors, and those over whom they have authority, or who have specific rights upon them, or for society, the state, or humanity. A Muslim will be held accountable for their adherence to the obligation in the manner that fulfills it, in every matter of their affairs, and they will be held accountable for fulfilling rights and performing duties, and they will also be taken to task for any shortcomings, and they will be held accountable for the consequences and repercussions of this responsibility by considering the results, consequences, and rights. This responsibility continues from the moment of obligation until the moment of fulfilling all the consequences of that responsibility, by providing an account of it and being questioned about it! Islam—while making responsibility perpetual, which must be performed in the best manner—teaches us the inclination towards perfection, excellence, positivity, patience, perseverance, seriousness, and dedication as fundamental concepts that entail the proper fulfillment of responsibility. An example of this is the saying of Allah: ﴾Men are the protectors and maintainers of women, with what Allah has given one over the other and with what they spend of their wealth﴿ [al-Nisa 34], meaning they are responsible for fulfilling their needs and managing their affairs. When Islam granted men the duty of qawamah (guardianship) over women (wives, sisters, daughters, or divorced women who have rights upon them), the meaning of qawamah is the utmost care for their needs and striving for their interests, and the duty of caring for them and what that responsibility entails, including labor and effort in the world, and striving for sustenance that men must undertake, and the effort and movement to achieve the duty of material and psychological care or otherwise for women, and the obligation to act rather than remain idle, which requires constant and diligent movement to ensure proper care. It is a responsibility that entails giving hardship and toil to men and comfort to women, and it is a significant and serious responsibility that honors women, allowing them to be free to fulfill their societal, familial, and personal roles with ease, given that there is someone to attend to all their needs. In Sahih Muslim, the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said, "It is sufficient sin for a man to withhold the sustenance from those whom he has authority over."

Islam is keen on shaping and refining Islamic personalities to bear responsibilities and their consequences at all levels, thus they are active, positive, reformist personalities that believe in their role in life and strive to achieve it, bringing about reform, change, and calling to righteousness.
