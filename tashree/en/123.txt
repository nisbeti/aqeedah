The Regulation of the Method of Taqlid (Imitation):
We have previously stated that ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) is a regulated operational procedure that ensures the safety of the mujtahid (jurist) from being influenced by his desires and whims when studying the legal texts to extract the legal ruling from them, and it ensures that he proceeds according to regulated rules of thought. If you follow the methodology of ijtihad and the methodology of taqlid, you will absolve yourself before Allah; otherwise, you will not!
From this, we can say that the various methodologies of usul (principles of jurisprudence) among the mujtahids in the different fiqh (jurisprudence) schools, such as the Hanafis, Shafi'is, Malikis, Hanbalis, and others, are merely organized methodologies for the operational procedures (and methods) that they use to study the various legal evidences related to the issue they are engaging in ijtihad on, according to the fixed principles necessary for the process of deduction, the foundations of derivation, and according to the regulated rules for balancing and preference, the application of evidence, and the regulation of definitions and terms. If you follow the methodology of ijtihad, your opinion is legally acceptable whether you have reached the truth or missed it, but if you engage in ijtihad without adhering to that regulated methodology, the resulting opinion will not be accepted, no matter what.
Taqlid is also a regulated operational procedure that ensures the safety of the muqallid (imitator) in following a recognized ijtihad according to rules of taqlid that prevent him from switching between schools and opinions in pursuit of whims, and it ensures that the opinion he imitates is based on consistency with the principles he relied upon. It is not permissible for the muqallid to imitate two mujtahids who differ in their principles on a particular issue, such as taking rulings related to the issue of prayer from al-Shafi'i and others related to the issue of prayer from Abu Hanifa! He may take from this one certain nullifiers of wudu (ablution) and from the other different nullifiers; this is not permissible. If the issues differ, (for example, if he imitates one mujtahid regarding the rulings of prayer and another mujtahid from a different school regarding the rulings of zakat), it is permissible to take from a non-mujtahid, due to the consensus of the Companions on allowing the muqallid to consult any scholar on an issue, because the rulings of each of these issues (prayer and zakat in our example) do not entail a disruption of other legal rulings related to each of them, (for example, if he imitates one in a nullifier of wudu and the other in the nisab of zakat), whereas in the details of each of these issues (for example, the details of the issue of prayer and its related subsidiary issues), a disruption will occur if he imitates more than one school or imitates scholars belonging to different schools or differing in their principles, such as in the case of wudu, purity, and nullifiers of prayer from the issue of prayer. Switching between schools or imams disrupts some of the rulings when taking from two sources of different principles, "such as prayer and wudu and the pillars of prayer. It is not correct for one who imitates al-Shafi'i to imitate Abu Hanifa in his statement that touching a woman does not nullify wudu and continue to pray according to the school of al-Shafi'i, nor is it correct to imitate someone who says that many movements do not invalidate prayer regardless of how many there are, or that not reciting al-Fatiha is not a pillar of prayer, while continuing to pray imitating someone who says that excessive actions invalidate prayer or that al-Fatiha is a pillar of prayer"(225). It is not permissible to switch between schools and imams in the subsidiary issues of a single matter because they refer to a specific methodology in ijtihad, and to specific regulated principles upon which the processes of ijtihad are based, which differ among the schools and scholars.
