Ijtihad and the Regulation of Its Operational Procedures:
The ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) of the Companions was characterized by simplicity and a direct connection to the legal evidence from the Qur'an and the Sunnah. Their understanding of the legal texts was clear due to their abundant knowledge of the Arabic language by nature, as their language was pure and innate, which allowed them to comprehend the legal texts clearly without the need to establish linguistic or foundational rules. Furthermore, they also had an understanding of the context of revelation, a close proximity to the evidence, and direct hearing from the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him), along with the spread of Islamic life and its deep integration into the customs and relationships of society, regulating the behavior of Muslims, which made their understanding of the context and intent in the texts precise. This ensured that their ijtihads were based on a broad knowledge of the texts and their meanings, their applications in practical life, and their implementation on real-life situations. Thus, the jurists did not need to establish specific regulated rules to understand the legal texts. This situation continued until before the spread of linguistic errors and the corruption of the Arabic tongue, which began to appear gradually after the second century of the Hijra due to the mixing of Arabs with non-Arabs.
The foundational principles of ijtihad were present in the Book and the Sunnah. For example, if we take qiyas (analogy), we find evidence for its legitimacy and use in the Book and the Sunnah. Ibn Abbas reported that a woman said, "O Messenger of Allah, my mother has died, and she had a fast of a vow upon her. Should I fast on her behalf?" He said, "Do you not see that if your mother had a debt and you paid it off for her, would that suffice for her?" She said, "Yes." He said, "Then fast on behalf of your mother." This was narrated by Muslim. Ahmad narrated from Abdullah ibn al-Zubair that a man from Khath'am came to the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) and said, "My father embraced Islam, and he is an old man who cannot ride. The pilgrimage is obligatory upon him. Should I perform Hajj on his behalf?" He said, "Are you his eldest son?" He said, "Yes." He said, "Do you not see that if your father had a debt and you paid it off for him, would that suffice for him?" He said, "Yes." He said, "Then perform Hajj on his behalf." Al-Bukhari narrated, and it was mentioned in the Ihkam by al-Amidi that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) "sent Mu'adh and Abu Musa to Yemen as judges, each one of them in a different area. He said to them, 'By what will you judge?' They said, 'If we do not find the ruling in the Book or the Sunnah, we will analogize the matter with another matter, and whatever is closer to the truth, we will act upon.' He (peace and blessings be upon him) said, 'You have both hit the mark.'" This all pertains to the analogy and comparison between two similar cases, which is why we find the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) exercising ijtihad using qiyas in their reasoning.
It was narrated from Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) that he said regarding the drinker of alcohol: "I see that if he drinks, he becomes delirious, and if he becomes delirious, he commits slander, thus he should receive the punishment for slander." This was mentioned by Ibn Qudamah in al-Mughni, as he analogized the drinker of alcohol to the slanderer. It was narrated from Ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with them) that he said: "Does Zayd ibn Thabit not fear Allah, making the son of the son a son, while he does not make the father of the father a father?" He did not intend by this to name the father, knowing that he is not literally called a father, but he made him like a father in the context of inheritance among siblings, just as the son of the son is like the son in their inheritance. Thus, Ibn Abbas analogized the grandfather to the son of the son in the matter of inheritance among siblings. This was mentioned by al-Sarakhsi in al-Mabsut, al-Shirazi in al-Tabsirah, and many other jurists.(193)
The Companions also provided us with examples of preference between the evidences. For instance, they preferred the narration of Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) over the narration of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri. The narration of Aisha states: "When the two circumcisions meet, ghusl (ritual bath) becomes obligatory" (narrated by Muslim). The narration of Abu Sa'id states: "Water is from water" (narrated by Muslim). The Companions preferred the understanding derived from Aisha's narration because the wives of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) are more knowledgeable about the rulings of ghusl related to him.
