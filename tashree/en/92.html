Let us take, for example, the concept of analogy (qiyas) which was originally one of the foundations of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh) and independent reasoning (ijtihad); it was known and prevalent during the life of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him). The hadith of Mu'adh regarding this, when the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) sent him to Yemen, is well-known and recognized as a fundamental basis for analogy. In fact, the evidence for analogy comes with proofs from the Sunnah(191). Moreover, the Companions discussed the causes (illal) and their conditions, and they reached what resembles consensus on the legitimacy of relying on analogy when its pillars and conditions are met(192). This, along with our swift examination of its generalities, shows us that the methodology of the foundations in Islam has an independent character, is highly precise, addresses the principles of evidence, deduction, and analogy, and that it emerged as a deduction from the Qur'an and the Sunnah and is built upon them.

Ahmad narrated from Abdullah ibn al-Zubair that a man from Khath'am came to the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) and said: "My father embraced Islam, and he is an old man who cannot ride. Is pilgrimage obligatory upon him? Should I perform pilgrimage on his behalf?" He said: "Are you his eldest son?" He said: "Yes." He said: "Do you not see if your father had a debt and you paid it off for him, would that suffice for him?" He said: "Yes." He said: "Then perform pilgrimage on his behalf." Al-Bukhari narrated, and it was mentioned in al-Ihkam by al-Amidi that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "He sent Mu'adh and Abu Musa to Yemen as judges, each one of them in a different area, and he said to them: 'By what will you judge?' They said: 'If we do not find the ruling in the Book or the Sunnah, we will analogize the matter with another matter, and whatever is closer to the truth we will act upon.' He (peace and blessings be upon him) said: 'You have judged correctly.'" This all pertains to the equality and analogy between two similar cases or examples. It was mentioned in Umar's letter to Abu Musa: "Understanding, understanding regarding what troubles your mind that is not in any Book or Sunnah, then recognize the similarities and analogies, and analogize matters, then that which is most similar to the truth." This was mentioned by al-Shirazi in his book on the ranks of jurists. It was narrated that Ali (may Allah be pleased with him) said regarding the drinker of alcohol: "I see that if he drinks, he becomes delirious, and if he becomes delirious, he slanders, thus he should receive the punishment for slander." This was mentioned by Ibn Qudamah in al-Mughni, where he analogized the drinker of alcohol to the slanderer. It was narrated from Ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with both of them) that he said: "Does Zayd ibn Thabit not fear Allah, making the son of the son a son, while not making the father of the father a father?" He did not mean to literally call the father a father, but he likened him to a father in the inheritance rights of siblings, just as the son of the son is like the son in their inheritance rights. Ibn Abbas analogized the grandfather to the son of the son in the inheritance rights of siblings. This was mentioned by al-Sarakhsi in al-Mabsut, al-Shirazi in al-Tabsirah, and many other jurists. It was narrated that Abu Bakr: "Inherited the mother of the mother over the mother of the father, and some of the Ansar said to him: 'You have inherited a woman from a deceased person, if she were the deceased, she would not inherit her, and you have left a woman, if she were the deceased, she would inherit all that she left.' He then returned to sharing between them in the sixth, analogizing the kinship of the deceased in the inheritance of the living from him to the kinship of the deceased in his inheritance from the living if the roles were reversed. Thus, his sharing between the mother of the father and the mother of the mother in the sixth was based on analogy." This was mentioned by al-Ghazali in al-Mustasfa.
