Is life merely the professional path? To be immersed in its details to the extent that you live for it, waiting for the end of the month to receive your salary, pay the bills, and continue the wheel of life - the lifestyle - in a stark routine as if it were a maze from which you cannot escape - except for a few moments before returning to that maze, either reluctantly or willingly? If one of us were to die at this hour, while in this maze unable to escape from it, could he pinpoint the true meaning of his life? The central question in life, without a doubt, is: what is the purpose of life? Why are you in this existence?

The example of life; with its professional path, its wealth, its children, and its sensory and spiritual pleasures, is like a fish living in water, swimming in it, without realizing that it is the ocean surrounding it. It lives, yet it cannot reach the understanding that life for it is the ocean with its wonders and breathtaking beauty, with its balance, diversity, and readiness to embrace it to live within it. Although it is a small, transparent fish, it is capable of living at depths that a human cannot reach without machines capable of withstanding the immense pressures upon them. Yet it does not realize this, and its "life" is limited to preoccupying itself with satisfying its hunger and avoiding its enemies, without appreciating the breathtaking beauty of the ocean and contemplating its preparation - despite its vastness - to be a nurturing environment for it, and its own preparation to be able to live in it, nor reflecting on its role in that great balance. It does not contemplate its connection to existence, nor existence's connection to it, to realize the purpose of its existence, and the goal and meaning that arise from that. Similarly, a human who lives his day like yesterday in that maze, without contemplation, reflection, and awareness!

It is not in the nature of life to be entirely blissful; rather, a person fluctuates from trial to blessing, from comfort to hardship, from happiness to distress. Moreover, the situation may develop for those who are miserable and whose spirits are broken, whose hearts are empty, leading them to a state of unhappiness, psychological strain, anxiety, depression, and frustration; some who have reached this state were at the peak of their glory, giving, fame, and wealth, yet their souls were devoid of the happiness based on tranquility, positivity, inner peace, psychological comfort, and faith, instead of that fleeting happiness based on satisfying instincts and organic needs to the fullest, and on the release of freedoms, especially personal freedom. That fleeting "happiness" is founded on "pleasure" or "ecstasy," or "luxury," and the realization of material values alone; this is a happiness that does not lead to tranquility, as it neglects spiritual value and overlooks the balance in satisfying instincts and organic needs between achieving human, spiritual, material, and moral values. True happiness and tranquility arise from a lifestyle that achieves balance in organizing human life, stemming from a person's realization of a life with a purpose, a "meaning" for which he lives.

The Austrian neurologist and psychoanalyst Viktor Frankl says: "Many people today have the means to live, yet they lack a meaning to live for(560)."

When those who have been burned by the fire of Western civilization pose this question to themselves, and those thin veils are lifted from them, they will surely realize at that moment that they have not lived happily, and that true happiness is not those fleeting joys they have experienced from time to time!
