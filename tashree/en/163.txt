And general rules, and general rulings(273). The word "sale" encompasses all types of sales, as in His saying, ﴾And Allah has made trade lawful and prohibited usury﴿ ﴾ البقرة 275 ﴿. The general ruling of the permissibility of sale applies to all types of sales; it is a general ruling, and the term "sale" here is a general term, being a singular word (not a collection of terms as in a universal ruling or general principle). We have ruled that it is permissible; thus, the general rule is that all sales are permissible, meaning that the ruling of permissibility applies to all instances of the term "sale." The permissibility of all instances of sale is the general rule, and this rule is not universal to be applicable in all areas of jurisprudence, but rather it is specific to the area of sales alone, and thus it is general and not universal. And as in His saying, ﴾Prohibited to you are dead animals﴿ ﴾ المائدة 3 ﴿, it applies to all dead animals; it is a general ruling.
The general legal rule (al-qaa'idah al-kulliyyah al-shar'iyyah) does not allow for exceptions, meaning that some particulars cannot be excluded from the ruling, because this would undermine its generality and make it a majority. This is unlike legal principles (qawa'id fiqhiyyah), where specification is possible, allowing for some of its members to be excluded from the general ruling, such as the prohibition of selling a present (hadr) to a nomad (bad).
Also, the legal evidences contain the (regulated) causes ('illah), analogy (qiyas), and similarity in rulings, so many matters share the same causes and are therefore compared to one another and take the same rulings.
The process of analogy (qiyas) has essential components without which it cannot be completed, namely: the branch (far') that is to be measured, the original (asl) that is to be used as a basis for comparison, the specific legal ruling (hukm shar'i) pertaining to the original, and the common cause (illah) that links the original and the branch. The branch has conditions, and the ruling of the original has regulated conditions, while the cause has eight regulated conditions(274). Thus, the legislator has defined a completely regulated process for the jurist (mujtahid) to derive the ruling that the original has in order to establish a similar ruling for the branch due to their similarity in the cause of the ruling.
These issues have made Islam comprehensive and complete, capable of providing treatments and solutions to human problems until the Day of Judgment,
For example, Allah, Exalted is He, says: ﴾So if they nurse for you, then give them their due compensation﴿. From this verse, a legal ruling is derived, which is that a divorced woman is entitled to a nursing fee. A legal ruling is also derived that any worker is entitled to payment if they perform their work. This ruling applies to many issues, including that a government employee, a factory worker, a farmer in the field, and others like them are each entitled to their wages if they complete their work, because they are specific workers. Likewise, the carpenter who made the cabinet, the tailor who sewed the garment, and others like them are each entitled to their wages if they perform their work, because they are general workers.
Since Islam has come with broad outlines, meaning general concepts, and has left it to the human intellect to derive from these general concepts the legal rulings for the ever-evolving and diverse issues that arise daily, it is from here that the derivation from these general concepts for each of the renewed and varied problems of humanity comes. Therefore, there is no incident that has occurred except that it has a place for a ruling, and no event takes place except that it also has a place for a ruling, and no problem can realistically, not hypothetically, occur except that it likewise has a place for a ruling.
