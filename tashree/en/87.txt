They may be mistaken, and yet we have relied on their testimony in the case, and the judge rules based on that testimony while considering the other evidence related to the case. Therefore, we have permitted reliance on the report of the individual (khabar al-ahad), which is speculative, for adjudicating rights and claims. This is how justice proceeds throughout the world; if we did not accept testimony, justice would be hindered, and the interests of the people and their transactions would be disrupted. Moreover, it would not be possible to implement retribution (qisas) and punishments (hudud), and no wrongdoer in society would be punished, which would lead to countless evils. Therefore, it is essential to study this significant statement from the commentator on the principles of al-Bazdawi, where he said: "Chapter on the classification of the narrator whose report has been made an evidence": "And when it is established that the report of the individual is an evidence, know that every report is not meant to be accepted as true, nor is it to be rejected as false; rather, we must accept the statement of the just, and he may be lying or mistaken. It is not permissible to accept the statement of the immoral, and he may be truthful. Rather, the accepted is what must be acted upon, and the rejected is what we are not obligated to act upon. Then, there are conditions for acceptance, some of which are agreed upon and some disputed. This chapter is to clarify some of its conditions; because its essence is the requirement that the narrator be known for narration, justice, accuracy, and understanding for his report to be accepted unconditionally, whether it agrees with analogy or contradicts it, and understanding is not a condition for some."(185) This is a statement worthy of being written in gold!
