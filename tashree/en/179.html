From foreign banks with usurious interest, the project owners establish it while committing to repay the loans and the interest accrued on them, let’s say for example it is 15%, which will significantly increase the principal amount borrowed, making the amount due 115 million, especially in large debts that project owners may struggle to repay before their projects become productive enough to cover the costs of operation, production, labor, management, improvements, repairs, and marketing, in addition to repaying debts and interest, along with inflation. The borrower enters a cycle of inflated due amounts over time, which have swollen due to the commitment to repay the debts and the interest accrued on them, which sometimes takes a large portion of the principal debt, drowning them in debts they cannot repay the usurious interest on, let alone the principal itself. The danger of these debts to companies is that they are like a black hole from which there is no escape, or what experts refer to as a "debt trap," where companies borrow again to cover budget shortfalls, but soon find that those debts and their interest consume the company’s revenues in the following years, causing a deficit, forcing them to borrow again to cover the deficit or pay the due interest on debts, and thus companies borrow a third, fourth, and fifth time, without being able to escape this vicious cycle, ultimately forcing them to sell their assets to repay the debts, or perhaps declare bankruptcy in the most severe cases(283). The project owners raise the prices of the produced materials to cover these enormous costs, placing the largest burden of repayment on the citizens who used the bank's savings to finance these projects by purchasing goods at a higher price than would have been possible, leading society into a spiral of inflation. If inflation occurs and reaches levels that the economy cannot bear, the state refrains from lending, causing some struggling projects to fail, resulting in unemployment, and consumers are unable to purchase goods, leading to multiple economic problems as a result(284).

From here, one can sense the greatness of Islamic legislation which prohibited usury, thus preventing a massive economic problem from occurring before it arises, as the black hole resulting from it, which multiplied the burden many times over, along with the economic problems arising from the capitalist system, such as inflation, were decisive factors in imposing a multiplied burden on people for purchasing the same goods, and on project owners in repaying their due debts. Therefore, you find a significant percentage of medium and small struggling projects declaring bankruptcy in every economic recession cycle.
