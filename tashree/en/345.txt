Detail: One of the structural shortcomings of the human intellect is its inability to fully encompass the reality for which it claims to legislate. The human and social reality is inherently complex, multidimensional, intertwined in its influences, variable in its manifestations, and linked to biological, psychological, economic, political, intellectual, ethical, material, and moral factors, which makes the attempt to "regulate this reality with purely human legislation" a venture doomed to structural failure.
For while the human intellect can analyze aspects of reality through observation and study, it is incapable of encompassing all its elements and long-term interactions, unable to penetrate the depths of various problems and what influences them and what they influence, and to encompass all of that with knowledge of the human soul and the reality of its motives, aspirations, and yearnings, as well as knowledge of human life and the diversity of its needs and fluctuations, and achieving balance in satisfying human organic needs and instincts, and balance in obtaining individual rights and public interest and the common good, and achieving justice, fairness, facilitation, and alleviation of hardship; these are fundamental principles that must exist in the definitive measure to confer the quality of good or evil or to achieve correctness and beauty or ugliness in behavior(485).
Rousseau expressed this reality brilliantly by saying: "Il faudrait des Dieux pour donner des lois aux hommes," meaning "We need gods to enact laws for people!"
Even in scientific fields that rely on experimentation and observation, such as medicine for example, humans still know very little about their bodies and their systems; how many diseases did doctors think were psychological only to later find out they were organic! And how many medications did they believe were beneficial, only to later reveal their harm after years of use! If a person has not fully comprehended the body that is within them, nor the physiology of the interaction between its organs and systems, how can they be confident in their perceptions about society as a whole? And how can they legislate systems that govern relationships between individuals, or satisfy their instincts, or define the values of good and evil, while they are incapable of grasping the deep network of interaction that connects actions to their outcomes, values to their consequences, and legislation to its effects in the present and future?
