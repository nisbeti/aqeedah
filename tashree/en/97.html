The ones referred to in the verse are the People of the Book, as the discourse also comes in a general form that includes all people of remembrance (dhikr). The Muslims are the people of remembrance because the Qur'an is a remembrance ﴾And We have sent down to you the remembrance that you may make clear to the people what was sent down to them﴿ ﴾16:44﴿. Thus, those knowledgeable about the legal rulings are among the people of remembrance, whether they are scholars of independent reasoning (ijtihad) or scholars of transmission (taqlid). The one who follows (muqallid) only asks about the legal ruling in a particular issue or issues. Accordingly, the verse indicates the permissibility of taqlid.(198) It also indicates the necessity of having scholars who teach the legal rulings so that the followers can refer to them and ask them. Since the existence of new issues and occurrences is a constant matter, it is necessary to have mujtahids who derive their rulings to explain them to the questioners. Knowledge of the rulings that the inquirers ask about—both old and new—cannot be achieved except through understanding the legal texts and comprehending how to apply them to the issues being asked about, meaning it can only be achieved through ijtihad or through knowledge of fiqh, which is the product of ijtihad and its teaching. Thus, the verse indicates the obligation of ijtihad.

And His saying, Exalted is He: ﴾And when Allah took the covenant from those who were given the Scripture, [He said], "You must make it clear to the people and not conceal it." But they threw it away behind their backs and exchanged it for a miserable price. And wretched is that which they purchased﴿ ﴾3:187﴿, is this noble verse specific to the People of the Book, namely the Jews and Christians, or is it general for all people of knowledge? It encompasses all people of knowledge. Ibn Kathir titled his commentary on this verse as follows: (The covenant of Allah with the people of knowledge to clarify it and not conceal it from the creation of Allah).

The subject in which this noble verse was revealed is that Allah took a covenant from those who were given the Scripture through the tongues of their prophets to believe in Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him) and to mention him among the people. However, they concealed and discarded the covenant out of greed for trivial gains. The verse reproaches them and threatens them. At the end of the verse, His saying, Exalted is He: ﴾And wretched is that which they purchased﴿, transitions the verse from specificity to generality. Ibn Kathir says: (And in this is a warning to the scholars not to follow their path lest they suffer what they suffered). The lesson is in the generality of the wording, not in the specificity of the cause, and the subject of the noble verse is the clarification of the Book. There is no doubt that the Qur'an is a Book, so it is evidence of the obligation of clarification. The clarification of the Qur'an by those who were given the Book from among the Muslims to the people necessitates that they interpret it and provide its legal rulings to the people and answer them about all the issues they inquire about with clear explanations. The clear explanation of the legal rulings that people ask about necessitates ijtihad in the legal texts to provide the ruling of Allah regarding them, meaning for ijtihad.(199)

And His saying, Exalted is He: ﴾O you who have believed, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you. And if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger if you should believe in Allah and the Last Day. That is the best [way] and best in result﴿ ﴾4:59﴿. As for His saying, Exalted is He: ﴾Obey Allah and obey the Messenger﴿, it means to judge by the commands and prohibitions of Allah regarding the matters of your lives and relationships, and to obey them in this. Obedience is in a matter that requires obedience, which is the conveying of commands and prohibitions, a matter that requires knowledge of the ruling of a thing and its implementation. There is no following except through submission, and no submission except through obedience. From here, the command to obey includes referring to that command and implementing it. If a calamity befalls a believer, then he must refer its ruling to Allah and the Messenger, judge by them in it, derive its ruling, and act upon it to achieve obedience.
