The epistemological value of conjecture:
Induction is considered one of the most important methods of thinking for acquiring knowledge in epistemology, to the extent that the scientific experimental method has elevated its status, making it a recognized foundation of modern science, its law, and the secret of its greatness. The Islamic epistemology has combined in the process of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) both induction and deduction. Thus, induction in Islamic epistemology is a type of analytical, interpretive, and sometimes explanatory induction, not the formal induction as in Aristotelian logic. Moreover, induction in the epistemology of Muslims does not stop at merely enumerating the evidence related to the subject, nor at the qualitative review of the topics of its terms, as is done in incomplete logical induction. Rather, it is an analytical and inferential induction of all the evidence related to the issue after gathering it and exhausting all efforts in studying it. It aims to analyze the implications of the texts regarding the ruling, extracting their meanings in a progressively precise process that combines analysis, synthesis, and inference, where the mujtahid (jurist) strives to achieve the alignment of thought with the specific reality, focusing on the substance of knowledge embedded in the various evidences, its content, and the meanings it conveys, based on the indications and evidence that point to a specific ruling. It does not concentrate on the form and appearance of knowledge as the Aristotelian deductive (syllogistic) method does, which addresses the aspect of conjecture inherent in some indications used in the process of deduction, as required and suitable for the nature of the research (ijtihad to derive the legal ruling, which does not necessarily need to be definitive in all cases). The convergence of evidence in conveying specific rulings compensates for the performance of some in aspects of the ruling that others among the indications have not conveyed. It compensates for the collective performance of various conjectural indications of common meanings that elevate the epistemological value of the ruling to the level of predominant conjecture that approaches moral consensus and certainty. The degree of confidence in the authenticity of the hadiths and indications attributed to the Sunnah, and their authenticity in relation to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), is strengthened by their corroboration with other hadiths that convey the same meaning or come from multiple chains with the same text and wording or similar meanings.
Since the theory of knowledge for all humanity is based on methods whose foundation is induction and deduction (with differences, as Aristotelian logic is based on incomplete induction, while the scientific experimental method is based on analytical induction, and both result in conjectural conclusions), and since judicial rulings cannot be definitive in most cases, yet judges issue binding rulings, it follows that all humanity recognizes that the construction of knowledge and the issuance of legislation and rulings based on conjecture is inevitable.
Thus, the fact that the indications of jurisprudence and the reports transmitted from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) are of a conjectural nature in terms of authenticity and that they contain conjectural implications does not diminish their importance in Islamic thought; rather, this is the nature of human knowledge, consisting of definitive and mostly conjectural elements.
