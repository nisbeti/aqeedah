It will lead to a chapter on enabling research without coercion, from its existential distance, and its binding rational methodology that requires following evidence and submitting to it, and this is a matter of great importance.
Islamic law guarantees freedom of belief before faith in Islam itself; however, when the argument is conveyed, it obliges adherence to the truth that has been clarified, and it does not permit the use of freedom of belief as a means to undermine or retreat from the truth, because when the truth is known through evidence, it becomes necessary to adhere to it, not to deny it. From this standpoint, the Sharia guarantees non-Muslims in the abode of Islam the freedom to remain on their beliefs, without acknowledging their correctness, and it prohibits coercion to enter Islam, but at the same time, it prevents serious rational inquiry from being trivialized in a way that undermines respect for reason and thought. Such fundamental ideas should be the result of thinking, scrutiny, and proof, and what results from that should not be retracted after verification, thus apostasy from Islam is not accepted.
These rights are inseparable from obligations, and they are not isolated from the system of divine values, but are regulated by the measures of truth, justice, and wisdom, governed by the objectives of the Sharia and its values in achieving the welfare of the individual and society, without making the individual himself a reference in assessing interests and harms, but rather the reference is revelation, and the honor lies in the obligation to follow guidance.
Preserving the values of society, its security, morals, and stability.
Considering the reality of human beings, their nature, and the purposes of their existence.
Dealing with real changes without subjecting values to whims or interests.
It must emanate from a respected, accepted higher authority that imposes adherence to it, not from the whims of the majority or authority without delegation.
From here, this chapter came to clarify that legislation cannot be correct, valid, or binding unless it meets essential conditions in its source and content, the most important of which are:
It must be based on a precise understanding of the reality of human beings, including their needs, instincts, and existential purposes, not on an incomplete conception of them as materialistic or positivist philosophies do.
It must originate from a knowledgeable, wise, and just authority, not from a biased, limited, and fluctuating human mind.
It must be stable in its major objectives, achieving specific societal goals, with its systems harmonizing and integrating in achieving them, without contradicting each other.
It must achieve true higher values, such as justice, preserving dignity, and equal opportunities, rather than serving the interests of the powerful or legitimizing deviations.
The chapter also clarifies that the corruption of legislation or the absence of legislation leads not only to legal disorder but to a comprehensive destruction of society: family disintegration, moral chaos, economic injustice, political paralysis, and psychological and spiritual ruin.
Therefore, it was necessary to clarify the characteristics of correct legislation and expose the shortcomings of existing legislations based solely on human reason without revelation.
