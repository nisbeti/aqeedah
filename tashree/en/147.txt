However, the texts of Islam in the Book and the Sunnah are at the same time related to the specific action of the servant; they are the legal evidence for the ruling of the Sharia on this action, and they are not related to public interest (maslaha) nor do they serve as evidence for public interest. When Allah, Exalted is He, says: ﴾O you who have believed, when you contract a debt for a specified term, write it down﴿, and when He says: ﴾And have witnesses when you conclude a contract﴿, and when He says: ﴾And if you are on a journey and cannot find a scribe, then a security deposit should be given﴿, He is clarifying the ruling on writing down the debt, the ruling on witnessing during a sale, and the ruling on collateral. He did not clarify that these are in the interest or not in the interest, neither explicitly nor implicitly, and the text does not indicate that this ruling is a public interest or not, neither closely nor remotely, nor in any way. So from where can it be said that these are interests indicated by the Sharia so that they are considered a public interest and thus considered legal evidence?
And also, the legal reasons (illal shar'iyya) have come, like the legal texts (nusus shar'iyya), related to the actions of the servant and as evidence of the legal ruling concerning this action, and they did not come to clarify the public interest (maslaha) nor to indicate the sign of the public interest. When Allah, Exalted is He, says: ﴾So that it does not become a circulating commodity among the wealthy among you﴿, and when He says: ﴾So that there is no discomfort upon the believers in the wives of their adopted sons﴿, and when He says: ﴾And those whose hearts are to be reconciled﴿, He is clarifying the reason for distributing wealth to the poor rather than the rich as being to prevent the circulation of wealth among the wealthy, and He clarifies the reason for the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) marrying Zainab (may Allah be pleased with her) as being to demonstrate the permissibility of marrying the wife of an adopted person, and He clarifies the reason for giving to those whose hearts are to be reconciled as being the state's need to win their hearts.
Thus, it does not clarify that this is a public interest (maslaha), but rather specifies a particular thing that is a reason for a specific ruling, without any consideration for public interest or lack thereof, nor any regard for it, whether closely or remotely. So from where can it be said that these reasons were indicated by the Sharia (Islamic law) so that these interests are considered a legal proof? If the texts of the Sharia did not indicate that they came for a public interest, neither in their indication of the ruling nor in their indication of the reason for the ruling, it is not permissible to say that the texts indicated specific interests or interests in general, because nothing of the sort has been found in the legal texts at all.
Thus, it becomes evident that the legal texts (nusus shar'iyya) do not serve as evidence for specific interests (masalih) or for interests in general; therefore, these interests cannot be considered a legal proof (dalil shar'i).
And if this is the case regarding what they say are public interests (masalih) for which there is a text from the Sharia (Islamic law) considering them specifically or in their type, then it is all the more appropriate that the interests that have no text in the Sharia indicating them should not be considered based on the legal evidences.
