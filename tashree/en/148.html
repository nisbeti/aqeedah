How does Islam address human problems with a solution that does not change over time and space, through the derivation of six analytical premises?

We will see this through the derivation of six analytical premises. For example: sex is one of the manifestations of the reproductive instinct, and this manifestation can be satisfied either through marriage, or through fornication, or through homosexuality, or by not satisfying it at all by turning away from it to another manifestation of the reproductive instinct, such as turning away from the wife to the affection of the mother or turning away from sexual satisfaction through castration or medication. These are the possibilities through which this manifestation of the reproductive instinct can be satisfied, and there are never any others. Here, the Sharia (Islamic law) came and clarified that the correct satisfaction can only be through marriage. Marriage is the relationship of a man with a woman who is lawful for him, prohibiting marriage to close relatives, prohibiting the satisfaction of the instinct through homosexuality with the same sex or with animals, and prohibiting satisfaction through fornication. Islam delineates the rulings on all of this, imposing a punishment for fornication whether the fornicator is married or unmarried, and imposing penalties on those who engage in homosexuality, and it prohibits castration. Thus, the legislation addresses the problem from its roots, regardless of whether it pertains to Zayd or 'Ubayd. Consequently, every human being in the world faces urges that drive him to solve the problem of sex; this problem and its treatment do not change with the change of time or place, and it is the only correct solution because it comes from the All-Knowing, the All-Aware. Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾Does He not know who created? And He is the All-Aware, the All-Acquainted﴿ [Al-Mulk 14]!!

Similarly, a person always needs to satisfy the hunger of the stomach. Islam came and permitted all things in the universe, except for certain specific categories that it detailed, including carrion, blood, and the flesh of swine, and so on. It detailed what is prohibited for us comprehensively, ﴾And why should you not eat of that upon which the name of Allah has been mentioned, while He has explained to you what He has forbidden you, except that to which you are compelled? And indeed, many lead astray by their desires without knowledge. Indeed, your Lord is Most Knowing of the transgressors﴿ [al-An'am 119]. Thus, wherever a person goes or travels, pork is forbidden for him, and alcohol is forbidden for him; it does not change or get affected by time or place.

Moreover, Islam has taken into account the abnormal situations that people go through, such as famines and the brink of death. It has permitted what has been prohibited to us in cases of necessity if a person fears death if he does not eat the prohibited. However, if he is overwhelmingly certain of death and is on the verge of it, it is obligatory for him to eat from the prohibited, in accordance with the legal principle that the means to the forbidden is prohibited [and the forbidden here is the destruction of oneself by starvation due to the absence of permissible food]. Thus, Islam has closed every gap and addressed every situation accompanying a person in his journey through life, whether he is on the verge of death while lost in the desert or nearing it while at home, whether in the era of the Companions or in the future; the ruling does not change.

On the economic front, for example, while we see capitalist thinkers not distinguishing between the economic system and the science of economics, economics for them is what studies human needs and the means to satisfy them. They consider the production of goods and services, which are means of satisfying needs, along with the distribution of these goods and services to be one single inquiry; thus, the distribution of goods and services falls under the inquiry of producing these goods and services.

Based on this, they view economics as a single perspective that encompasses economic material and how to acquire it, without separating between them and without distinguishing one from the other; that is, they view the science of economics and the economic system as one and the same without any difference between them.
