Narrated by Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, he said: While we were traveling with the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), a man came riding on his mount. He began to look around to the right and to the left. The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "Whoever has excess riding capacity should share it with one who has no riding capacity, and whoever has excess provisions should share it with one who has no provisions." He mentioned various types of wealth until we saw that none of us had any right to excess(360). (361) Moreover, some branches of the system of social solidarity extended to include non-Muslims among the subjects, ﴾Allah does not forbid you from those who do not fight you in religion and do not expel you from your homes from being righteous toward them and acting justly toward them﴿ [al-Mumtahana 8].

On the family level, both spouses share the responsibility of fulfilling their duties according to their natural disposition and capabilities. "The man is a guardian of his family and is responsible for his dependents, and the woman is a guardian in her husband's house and is responsible for her dependents"(362). Each spouse's rights are preserved: ﴾And for them is a share like that which is upon them in kindness﴿ [al-Baqarah: 228]. The man has the responsibility of providing for his wife even during her waiting period after divorce, and children have the right to care and upbringing, while they have the duty to be dutiful to their parents and to treat them kindly. The wealthy in the family should assist the poor and incapable, so that the foundations of the family are established, strengthened, and remain intact, preventing societal disintegration. One of the established principles in Sharia is that rights and duties are reciprocal; if inheritance is a right for the heir when a person dies wealthy, then spending is obligatory upon him if he becomes incapable! He must provide for those whom he would inherit if they were to die, should that relative become incapable or in need! Furthermore, maintenance is obligatory even with differing religions, among other aspects of solidarity within the family and its rulings(363). The rulings then extend to the poor, incapable, or needy relatives, and this maintenance is a form of kinship ties. If there are no close relatives who can provide for the poor and incapable, the obligation shifts from the immediate family to the extended family, which is the community represented by the state, as the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) used to do, as he would support the incapable, even marrying them off, and as the caliphs after him would do, as they would patrol the neighborhoods and incoming caravans searching for the poor to assist them.

Then, solidarity within the community means that individuals are responsible for advising, enjoining what is right, and forbidding what is wrong to preserve the values of society. Islam has blended individual interest with public interest, so that achieving private benefit complements public benefit, and achieving public benefit encompasses individual interest. The individual in the Muslim community is collectively responsible for maintaining public order and for actions that may harm the community or disrupt some of its interests. Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And the believing men and believing women are allies of one another. They enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong and establish prayer and give zakah and obey Allah and His Messenger. Those are the ones whom Allah will have mercy upon. Indeed, Allah is Exalted in Might and Wise﴿ [al-Tawbah: 71]. The individual is also commanded to excel in his social performance, to be an active and influential presence in the community in which he lives. Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And cooperate in righteousness and piety, but do not cooperate in sin and aggression. And fear Allah; indeed, Allah is severe in punishment﴿ [al-Ma'idah: 2].
