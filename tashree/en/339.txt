Laws. The electoral process itself is tainted by biases, exclusion, and manipulation, and does not produce a representative who embodies the consensus of the nation or even represents its majority; rather, it represents the relative majority that enabled it to obtain the most votes from those who participated in the electoral process, which strips it of the legitimacy of representing the people's opinion. Furthermore, elected representatives lack an objective mechanism to achieve a balance between conflicting interests of the people or to satisfy the collective mind, or to refer back to them for consultation on their opinions regarding the laws they legislate. Instead, they often produce legislation that is imposed in the name of democracy, without stemming from genuine consensus, comprehensive understanding, or representation of what they call the general will.
Accordingly, human reason is inherently incapable, in light of this relativity and subjectivity, of producing stable, just, acceptable, and binding legislation for all members of society, let alone for different societies. This necessitates the existence of a legislative reference that transcends this relativity, possessing an absolute standard, and a source encompassing wisdom and justice, which is the reference of divine revelation.
The subjugation of reason to whims, interests, and biases:
Summary:
The human intellect is inherently non-neutral.
The intellect is influenced by emotions, interests, and intellectual and social affiliations, making it unable to produce legislation that is fair and devoid of bias.
The impossibility of stripping individuals and societies of their whims.
Whim is inherent to the individual, and it is exacerbated in groups, which makes collective legislation (like democracy) necessarily subject to multiple whims.
The dominance of class and elite interests in the making of legislation.
Many laws are issued in favor of influential groups, not to achieve social justice, thus turning reason into a tool for dominance rather than public interest.
The impact of the collective unconscious and intellectual currents on the deviation of legislation.
Deviation does not necessarily have to be intentional; it may occur due to societal, media, or ideological pressure, which undermines the balance and justice of legislation.
The predominance of materialistic or individualistic perspectives over the legislative system.
Laws are sometimes legislated to serve immediate desires or material inclinations such as pleasure or utilitarianism, which disrupts the value system of modesty, family, and the environment.
Transforming whims into a legislative reference in the name of freedom and rights.
Deviations are legitimized through slogans like "personal freedom" and "the right to choose," leading to the legalization of corruption and moral and social dilution.
The Qur'anic warning against following whims in legislation.
