Judgment. There is no incident except that it has a ruling in the Sharia of Allah; the ruling may be an explicit text from the Qur'an or the Sunnah, or it may be implicit, derived through analogy based on a stated reason... etc.
According to this principle, it is not permissible for a Muslim to say that the Sharia has remained silent on a matter and has not clarified its ruling. Yes, the Sharia may have overlooked some matters and not prohibited them out of mercy for the people and to provide them with ease – as mentioned in some hadiths – but this does not mean the existence of a "legally permissible area for the intellect" in the sense of legislative void. Rather, it means that those matters remain under their original ruling (permissibility), and this is also a legal ruling.
Thus, there is no worldly matter – especially those related to the public lives of people – except that the Sharia provides guidance on it: either a direct obligation (obligation/prohibition) or a general permission that delegates authority to the people regarding means (permissibility/delegation). In this way, the role of the intellect is regulated: it operates within the framework of rulings, not as a creator of them from a void. The intellect is permitted to understand reality and its technical details, to apply rulings to incidents and achieve their contexts, as well as to choose appropriate means administratively as long as they are permissible according to the Sharia. However, it is not permitted to establish new legislation that makes the prohibited permissible or prohibits the permissible or neglects an obligation. These are the limits of obligation: the Sharia begins where the relationship of command and prohibition related to the perspective on life begins, and it stops where there is pure material and purely technical experience.
This can be summarized by saying: "Islamic legislation encompasses all aspects of human life and represents an integrated unit that cannot be fragmented; it is not permissible to take some of it and leave some." It is a grave mistake to imagine that the Sharia has focused on acts of worship and personal status while leaving the system of governance or the economy, for example, without guidance. This perception dissects religion into disparate parts and undermines its structure of justice. The historical reality contradicts this: Muslims since the prophetic era established a state that implements the rulings of religion, and the rightly guided caliphs and those after them governed with Sharia-based politics and never claimed that the Sharia was silent on any matter of governance. If Muslims today hear the claim of "political void in the Sharia," it is a result of intellectual defeat in the face of the Western model, not a truth of Islam.
The centrality of the reference of revelation in the Islamic system of governance (the philosophy of the caliphate)
To understand the extent to which the Sharia permeates political aspects, we briefly present the philosophy of Islam regarding the system of caliphate and governance. The caliphate is the political system that Islam brought to carry the trust of governance as revealed by Allah. The caliphate is not merely a historical label; it is a representation of the one who has the Sharia in executing His commands among the people. Imam al-Raghib al-Isfahani says: "The caliphate: is the representation of another." Thus, the caliph represents the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) in applying the religion and establishing justice. Allah has appointed all believers on earth for this purpose: ﴾He it is who made you successors upon the earth﴿ [Fatir 39] to test them in establishing His methodology. Therefore, man is a vicegerent of Allah in that he adheres to His methodology and Sharia in managing the earth.
From this, the essence of the philosophy of Islamic governance is: sovereignty belongs to the Sharia, and authority belongs to the nation on the condition of adherence to the Sharia. The Muslim ruler (caliph or imam) receives his authority through a pledge based on the Book of Allah and the Sunnah of His Messenger; he is bound to "judge by what Allah has revealed" and must not deviate from it. If he strays from that, his legitimacy is nullified.
