Rational preference (istihsan) is invalid according to Islamic law, because the legislator has explicitly stated the prohibition of usury and the obligation of zakat, achieving this within the framework of a complete legislative system aimed at establishing goals and outcomes in society and life that regulate and oversee affairs. Reality proves the correctness of the definition of harm according to the Sharia: for the benefits of usury have led to global financial crises and alarming class gaps – real corruptions that the proponents of "the law of interest" have overlooked.
In the medical and health field: In some secular societies, there is a rational opinion that euthanasia is not harmful, on the grounds that it ends the chronic suffering of the patient. This human definition clashes with the law of Allah, which has categorically prohibited the killing of oneself, even out of compassion: ﴾And do not kill the soul...﴿ [al-Isra 33]. Revelation considers the ending of human life – without right – to be a great harm and a clear sin, regardless of good intentions. Experience has also proven the danger of this isolated rational thinking, as the standards of "mercy killing" have slipped from alleviating pain to killing the mentally weak and the elderly under the pretext of "quality of life"; thus, medicine has shifted from saving lives to taking them in some cases, and the line between mercy and crime has become blurred when the revelation of Allah was set aside.
In the social and moral field: Liberal thought promotes the idea that "there is no harm as long as it is consensual"; for example, their laws permit homosexuality and the personal use of drugs under the guise of individual freedom, as long as it does not cause direct physical harm to others. This narrow definition of harm – limiting it to direct physical injury – ignores moral, spiritual, and societal harm, and overlooks the rights of the child resulting from such relationships and the family that may be destroyed by them, while Islam expands the concept of harm to include psychological and moral aspects; for the Sharia has prohibited harming a person even with a word or an unjust glance, and has forbidden everything that undermines modesty and chastity or weakens the structure of the family and society. When some countries legalized pornography and drugs under the banner of freedom, we witnessed severe harms: family disintegration, the collapse of the family system, the spread of diseases and addiction, and crimes resulting from them. Reality has proven that the "interest" they claim to achieve was the first victim of suspending the commands of the Sharia.
In the legal and punitive field: Voices of "rationality" have emerged considering the Sharia penalties to be brutal "harmful" punishments for the criminal and calling for their suspension. They halted the penalties of retribution, amputation, and flogging in some Muslim countries under the pretext that they harm the offender! But what was the result? Crimes that those penalties were meant to deter have proliferated. On the other hand, the wise revelation clarifies that enforcing the penalty on the criminal – even if it appears painful to him – is a mercy for society and even for the offender himself; for it is an expiation for his sin and a deterrent for others. The Sharia has justified the amputation of the thief's hand – for example – by stating that it comes after ensuring his basic needs and preventing the causes of theft; thus, this amputation serves as a deterrent remedy for those who dare to take the property of others. The cancellation of these penalties under the guise of alleged rationality has unleashed the criminals to wreak havoc; thus, murder, theft, and violations of honor have increased, and it has been proven that suspending the Sharia punishment was the very harm itself.
These examples show that defining harm based on abstract human "interest" deviates significantly from the standard of the Sharia. Abstract reason may turn truth into falsehood and falsehood into truth under the influence of desires, the pressure of prevailing culture, or temporary interests.
