The determinants and characteristics that ensure the validity of legislation, which must be possessed by the legislator concerning the understanding of reality and the characteristics related to the nature of the ruling:
Identifying the correct angle from which to view the reality in question, so that problems are addressed in a manner suitable for all times and places, a treatment characterized by comprehensiveness, breadth, stability, and renewal that keeps pace with the changes of life, and avoids rigidity and lack of development, so that the legislator does not fall into the dilemma of needing change, or the differing rulings due to the variation of times, places, and environments, or the necessity of a complete reversal in ruling from one extreme to another, which would inevitably lead society into intellectual confusion, or would certainly result in the failure to achieve justice.
Islam has defined this angle as a universal perspective, addressing human problems in a manner related to the essence of humanity, as human beings. Since the essence of humanity and its characteristics (its vital energy from instincts and organic needs) remain the same, the human of the atomic age does not differ from the human of a thousand years ago in their characteristics, essence, and needs, the treatments will remain valid for those problems.
And since the treatment is characterized by universality, it is a remedy that does not pertain to one tribe over another, nor to one people over others, and it takes into account the impact of differences in gender, abilities, energies, and talents on responsibilities, duties, and rulings. It has assigned specific rulings to females that suit their femininity, and to males with other rulings that suit their original creation. When there was no impact of differing energies on the ruling, both were addressed with the same discourse with other rulings that had no effect based on gender. Islamic legislation distributed duties, rights, and responsibilities among the individual, family, community, and state, according to ability and capacity, which ensures the participation of the individual in achieving the welfare of the community and its security and preserving its values, as well as the responsibilities of the community and the state in solving the problems of the individual and distributing wealth fairly to bridge the gap between the classes of society (for example: enjoining good and forbidding wrong, social solidarity, and the spoils).
It did not base those commands and prohibitions, nor those responsibilities, duties, and rights on changing, moving, fluctuating, and variable needs according to circumstances, conditions, time, place, environment, and custom, but rather regulated the conditions, circumstances, incidents, customs, and interests with legislations that govern them. (That is, when addressing the incidents and problems arising from or existing in reality, it did not make that reality a source of jurisprudence and thought for deriving solutions from it, but rather a subject for thought and change to regulate it with its concepts and rulings to derive solutions for it.) Those solutions, whose fundamental characteristic is stability, do not change, thus ensuring the continued achievement of justice and the realization of its goals and objectives correctly, and maintaining its coherence within the legislative system. Reality is not taken as a source for thinking; for instance, one does not say: people need housing and the cost of building it is beyond their reach, and thus there is no solution except by abandoning the prohibition of usury and legislating banks and usurious loans. Islam does not do that, because this way of thinking is a product of the capitalist system, in which the state relinquishes its guardianship role and transforms into a tax-collecting state, not intervening in fulfilling the basic needs of individuals. Its system is based on concentrating wealth in the hands of a few, instead of distributing wealth fairly across society, where the wealthy monopolize natural resources and their extraction, and the necessary resources for building houses, such as cement and iron, causing their prices to multiply. The capitalist system, based on a paper currency system not backed by gold, leads the economy into inflation, causing prices to rise as the value of currency decreases.
