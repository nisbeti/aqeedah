Courage, miserliness, and others, and if the appearance results in an action that serves the survival of the human species, then this appearance belongs to the instinct of species, such as affection, compassion, sexual inclination, and rescuing a drowning person, among others.
And if the appearance results in an action that serves a person's feeling of helplessness and need for the Creator, then this appearance belongs to the instinct of religiosity and sanctification, such as fear of the Day of Judgment, the inclination to respect the powerful, and admiration for the order of the universe, among others.
Thus, the appearance is different from the action; the inclination to possess is different from possession, because the inclination to possess is a feeling within a person towards things to bring them close and acquire them, while possession is the act of doing. Like buying a car or stealing money, the appearance does not satisfy the instinct, but rather the action prompted by the appearance is what satisfies the instinct or fulfills part of the satisfaction... The inclination to please Allah is different from worship, because worship satisfies the instinct of religiosity, while mere inclination does not provide satisfaction... The sexual inclination does not satisfy the instinct of species, while the act of a man and a woman together satisfies some of this instinct, and if this act is repeated between them without having children, this action becomes unsatisfactory for the instinct from the perspective of this appearance, because the essence of the action resulting from the appearance is to serve the instinct to which this appearance belongs... Thus, intercourse without procreation does not achieve complete satisfaction, because it does not lead to the continuation of the human species, and therefore does not serve the instinct of species."(243) Ended.
The differences between instincts and their appearances are that the instinct is part of the essence of a human being; it is a characteristic of their nature, while the appearance of the instinct is not a characteristic, and thus the instinct as a whole cannot be eradicated from a person, and it must manifest in some form of its appearances, while an appearance of it can be completely eradicated, favoring one appearance over another.
Here, some may illustrate the stimulation of organic need and instinct from the outside with the example of a satiated person who sees food and salivates and is about to eat, claiming that the organic need was stimulated from the outside, while in reality, this stimulation did not arise from the organic need, but rather from the appearance of the survival instinct manifested in the love of possession.
Notice that a mother is willing to sacrifice herself for her child, which means the survival of the species, not the survival of the individual; she is one of the manifestations of the instinct of species, not survival. Then notice that you may respect someone, and this respect is never because you fear them, because fear manifests as flattery, fleeing, or defense, which contradicts respect, as Professor Muhammad Abu Wael says. The feeling of respect has no relation to the concern for the essence of the person nor to the continuation of the human species, but rather it is a manifestation of a third instinct, which is religiosity.
The appearance that manifests this feeling of natural deficiency and helplessness in a person and their need for the managing Creator, this appearance is sanctification, and sanctification may manifest in its true form through the worship of Allah, or it may appear in a lesser and lower form, that is, through veneration, respect, and admiration.
