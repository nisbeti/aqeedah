Causes of the Differences Among Jurisprudential Schools in the Principles of Ijtihad and Their Impact on Legal Rulings
The differences among jurisprudential schools in the principles of ijtihad are due to several reasons related to the methodology adopted in dealing with legal texts, understanding the Arabic language, sources of legislation, and the contextual interpretation of evidence. Below are the most important of these reasons and their impact on legal rulings:
First: Reasons for the Differences in the Principles of Ijtihad
Differences in the concept of legal texts and their scope:
Impact: Some schools adhere to the apparent meanings of texts (like the Zahiri school), while others (like the Hanafi school) have expanded in interpretation and reasoning. This affects the derivation of rulings in the absence of explicit texts.
Example: In the issue of killing a group for the act of one, the Zahiris do not permit this due to the absence of a text, while the majority permit it based on analogy.
Differences in the consideration of additional sources for legislation:
Sunnah: There is a disagreement regarding the authority of the reports of the singular (akhbar al-ahad); the Malikis emphasize strict conditions for accepting singular reports related to public matters, while the Shafi'is accept them unconditionally.
Analogy (qiyas): The Zahiris do not see analogy as a legitimate source, while most other schools have relied on it.
Preference (istihsan): The Hanafis accepted it for pragmatic considerations, while the Shafi'is rejected it as "a judgment based on personal inclination."
Impact: This leads to a wide variation in rulings, such as: the Hanafis using preference in sales issues, while the Shafi'is rely on direct analogy.
Divergence in the stance on consensus (ijma):
The Malikis broaden the concept of consensus to include "the practice of the people of Medina," while the Shafi'is reject it as an additional reference.
Impact: The disagreement in issues such as the act of taking hold (qabdh) in prayer: the Malikis relied on the practice of the people of Medina and left it, while the Shafi'is made it obligatory based on the Sunnah.
Differences in linguistic standards:
Understanding the implications of words, such as general and specific, unrestricted and restricted.
Impact: This leads to variation in the derivation of rulings. Example: The word "qur" in the saying of Allah: ﴾Three menstrual cycles﴿. The Hanafis interpreted it as "menstruation," while the Shafi'is interpreted it as "purity," leading to a difference in the duration of the waiting period (iddah).
Variation in understanding the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia):
Some schools, like the Malikis, place great importance on public interest (maslaha mursala), while other schools (like the Shafi'is) restrict its consideration to textual evidence.
Impact: In issues such as the compilation of the Noble Qur'an into a single manuscript during the time of Abu Bakr and Uthman (may Allah be pleased with them): this action was not mentioned in any prior legal text, but it was a necessity to achieve a great legal interest: preserving the Qur'an from loss or distortion,
