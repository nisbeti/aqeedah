He says, "Every one of you is a shepherd, and every one of you is responsible for his flock. The imam is a shepherd and responsible for his flock, and a man is a shepherd in his family and is responsible for his flock, and a woman is a shepherdess in her husband's house and is responsible for her flock, and a servant is a shepherd in the wealth of his master and is responsible for his flock." He said, "And I think he said, 'and a man is a shepherd in the wealth of his father and is responsible for his flock, and every one of you is a shepherd and responsible for his flock.'" The hadith has about 45 narrations(352). From these texts, we find that Islam has made it obligatory for its adherents, from the head of state to the shepherd who tends sheep on the slopes of mountains and in the valleys, to have responsibilities that expand and contract according to the entity addressed by the obligation and their capabilities."(353)

"Social solidarity means that individuals in the community are responsible for their group, and that every capable person or authority is a guarantor in their society, providing it with goodness, and that all human forces in the community converge to protect the interests of individuals and avert harm, and then to maintain the protection against harm to the social structure and to establish it on sound foundations... The essence and implication of social solidarity is that every individual in the community feels that they have duties to this community that they must fulfill, and that if they fall short in fulfilling them, it may lead to the collapse of the structure for themselves and others, and that individuals have rights in this community that those in charge must give every right-holder their due without negligence or neglect, and that harm must be averted from the weak and the deficiencies of the incapable must be addressed, and that if this is not done, the bricks of the structure will erode, and it will inevitably collapse after a while."(354)

Perhaps the most eloquent expression encompassing the meaning of social solidarity is what Abu Musa al-Ash'ari (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him): "The believer to the believer is like a building, part of which strengthens the other,"(355) then he interlaced his fingers. And the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) was sitting when a man came asking or seeking a need, and he approached us with his face and said: "Intercede, and you will be rewarded, and let Allah decide through the tongue of His Prophet what He wills,"(356) and from al-Nu'man ibn Bashir, he said: The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "The example of the believers in their mutual love, mercy, and compassion is like that of one body; when one part of it complains, the rest of the body responds with wakefulness and fever."(357) In another narration, "The believers are like one man; if his head complains, the rest of the body responds with fever and wakefulness."(358)

The Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) clarified the community's responsibility for every needy individual within it, in a strong warning to both the individual and the community: "A believer is not the one who eats his fill while his neighbor is hungry beside him, and he knows it."(359)
