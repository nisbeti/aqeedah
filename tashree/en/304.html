The conventional and terminological reality of judgment:

Ataa bin Khalil Abu al-Rashtah said: "It is worth mentioning that the term 'حَكَمَ' (hakama) that the Arabs established, meaning in language or what is called the linguistic reality, is 'قضى' (qada): It is mentioned in Lisan al-Arab: (and judgment (hukum) is knowledge, understanding, and ruling with justice, and it is the source of 'حَكَمَ' (hakama) ... 'قضى' (qada): judgment (hukum) ...) and in al-Qamus al-Muhit: (judgment (hukum), with a dhamma: ruling (qada)) ... and in Mukhtar al-Sihah ('حُكْمُ' (hukum) is ruling (qada) ...)

However, this term 'حكم' (hukum) was used terminologically in the time of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), the Rightly Guided Caliphs, and the Arabs thereafter, meaning kingship and sovereignty, thus it is a terminological usage of 'conventional reality'.

Thus, the term 'حكم' (hukum) is a linguistic reality in judgment, and a specific conventional reality (i.e., a term in kingship and sovereignty)"(446).

Judgment in language: Ibn Faris said: Judgment (qada) is ruling. Allah, Exalted is He, said regarding those who said: ﴾So decree whatever you are to decree﴿ [Ta-Ha 72] meaning make and rule. Therefore, the judge is called a 'قاضي' (qadi), because he rules judgments and enforces them. (447) Al-Jawhari said in al-Sihah: Judgment (qada) is ruling, and its origin is 'قَضايٌ' (qaday), because it is from 'قَضَيْتُ' (qadaytu), and the plural is 'الأقْضِيَة' (al-aqdiya). The term 'قَضِيَّة' (qadiyya) is similar, and the plural is 'القَضايا' (al-qada'iya). 'قضى' (qada), meaning he ruled, and from it is His saying: ﴾And your Lord has decreed that you worship none but Him﴿.(448)

The comprehensiveness of the command of judgment for all relationships, not just judgment alone:

When Allah, Exalted is He, commanded His Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) to judge by what Allah has revealed, what relates to this command from what Allah has revealed includes: the relationships of man with his Creator (beliefs and acts of worship), the relationships of man with himself (morals, etiquette, permissible foods, clothing, and adornment), and the relationships of man with others among humanity (transactions and punishments), and the details of these relationships:

A- Family relationships such as marriage, divorce, maintenance, guardianship, kinship, and inheritance, and the rulings of public and private life, mixing and seclusion (the social system)

B- In private relationships, in contracts and transactions, industry and agriculture, the exchange of material benefits through trade, contracts, institutions, and companies.

C- In public relationships, meaning the relationships between the ruler and the ruled, i.e., constitutional relationships and relationships with public authority, systems of judgment and evidence, rulings of penalties and punishments, and legislation regarding public funds such as land tax, spoils, taxes, and duties, and public ownership and state funds.

D- In international relations, meaning the relationships of groups, nations, and states with one another, in the rulings of treaties, jihad, messengers, and embassies.
