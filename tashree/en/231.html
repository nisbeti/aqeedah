"Like a building whose parts support one another"(364), and the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) illustrated the state of individuals in society regarding their cohesion and mutual support in a wonderfully representative manner when he said: "The example of the believers in their mutual love, mercy, and compassion is like that of a body; when one part of it complains, the rest of the body responds with wakefulness and fever"(365). On the other hand, the community is also responsible for preserving the sanctities of the individual and ensuring their rights, privacy, honor, and dignity. Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾O you who have believed, let not a people ridicule [another] people; perhaps they may be better than them. And do not insult one another and do not call each other by [offensive] nicknames. Wretched is the name of disobedience after [one's] faith. And whoever does not repent - then it is those who are the wrongdoers. O you who have believed, avoid much [suspicion]; indeed, some suspicion is sin. And do not spy or backbite one another. Would one of you like to eat the flesh of his brother while he is dead? You would detest it. And fear Allah; indeed, Allah is Accepting of repentance and Merciful﴿ [al-Hujurat: 11-12]. The Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) depicted this mutual support in a beautiful example when he said: "The example of the one who stands at the limits of Allah and the one who transgresses them is like a group of people who drew lots for a ship. Some of them were in the upper part and some in the lower part. Those in the lower part, when they wanted to get water, would pass by those above them. They said: 'If we were to make a hole in our part and not harm those above us, we would be fine.' If they left them to do what they wanted, they would all perish, but if they took hold of their hands, they would be saved, and they would all be saved"(366). Among the rulings of social solidarity are those related to caring for the elderly, supporting orphans and children, assisting the poor and needy, taking care of the elderly, divorced women, and widows, caring for foundlings, the afflicted, and the distressed, observing the rights of neighbors, the rights of guests and strangers, travelers, and others. To achieve this, Islam has mandated(367) zakat, expiations, charity, assisting the needy, helping the distressed, waqf (endowment), bequests, and loans. The state also has pastoral responsibilities. Al-Bukhari (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated from Abu Huraira (may Allah be pleased with him) that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "I am more deserving of the believers than themselves. Whoever dies while owing a debt and has not left behind a means to repay it, it is upon us to repay it, and whoever leaves wealth, it is for his heirs"(368).

"Reflect on how Islam has made the expiations for sins a form of social cooperation. Whoever breaks their fast in Ramadan must either free a slave, fast for sixty days, or feed sixty needy people. Whoever swears an oath and breaks it must either free a slave, feed ten needy people, or clothe them. Whoever falsely claims that his wife is like his mother in prohibition must fast for two consecutive months, and if he cannot, he must feed sixty needy people. And whoever breaks their fast in Ramadan due to incapacity or illness, with no hope of making it up in the future due to old age or chronic illness, must provide a meal for a poor person for each day! Thus, you find that the expiations for sins are a form of social solidarity, as if the sin committed and the shortcoming in worship is a social transgression. Social transgressions can only be atoned for through community cooperation, which fills the gaps and rectifies the deficiencies. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) considered every act of giving to the poor as an expiation for sins, saying: "Shall I not guide you to the doors of goodness? Fasting is a shield, and charity extinguishes sins just as water extinguishes fire."
