Justice in matters that reveal outcomes involving injustice, and similarly, this applies to the pursuit of benefits (masalih) and the prevention of harms (mafasid). Therefore, it is necessary to prevent desires from influencing legislation; thus, reason must refrain from issuing judgments and legislations due to its lack of the characteristics and information necessary to carry out the intellectual process that results in judgment, legislation, and codification. When a person allows themselves to legislate, they are not assigning their mind the task of legislation; rather, they are assigning it to their desires that drive them to engage in matters about which reason has no authority to speak, and to their inclinations that deceive them into believing that the benefit is whatever aligns with them.

Moreover, the interest (maslaha) may pertain to the individual alone, to bring about his own benefits, and the intended interest may be a public interest. Here, people's perspectives on what achieves these interests and what wards off harms conflict, and desires, immediate views, and trial and error take control, resulting in a devastating outcome for society. "What is the interest that is brought about, and what is the harm that is repelled? Is it what a person loves and hates? Or is it what benefits him and harms him? Or is it good and evil? Or is it beautiful and ugly? Or is it something else? There is no doubt that we need criteria by which we can judge that the system brings about interest and repels harm!"(288)

The answer to this is that an individual in society lives with others among humankind, and there may be conflicts between what they love and what they hate, what benefits them and what harms them. It becomes impossible for them to bring about benefits for all or to ward off harms from all, and it is indeed impossible to reach a consensus on considering certain measures as achieving benefits or warding off harms. In fact, it is fundamentally impossible to take their opinions on every matter related to them, due to the multitude and complexity of relationships and the variety of possible solutions, as well as the differences in their desires, minds, judgments, and environments. Therefore, there must be an authority that is distinct from them, to clarify what is the benefit that should be brought and what is the harm that should be warded off, and on what basis (according to what system) these should be brought or warded off. This is the most important question. Everyone may agree that the need for security, prosperity, housing, and education are benefits that should be brought, but the more critical question is: what is the system that will make the bringing of these benefits a reality of justice, welfare, and security? What is the reflection of that organization on the differing viewpoints regarding those solutions, and on the ability of those solutions—which will inevitably be a choice for one solution among a wide range of possible solutions—to provide the most effective solution to the problem? For example, capitalism has presented the solution of usurious loans to the housing problem. What is the reflection of that solution on people's lives in society? Is it the optimal solution? What problems have arisen from it? And so on.

From here, we say above: the organization of bringing about benefits (masalih) and the organization of repelling harms (mafasid), so the foundation is to look at the "system" to be the basis that will organize the relationships of society. The system defines "good and evil, benefit and harm, advantage and disadvantage" and regulates behavior and relationships with legislations that organize them, not the other way around. It is not the case that a person looks with their opinion and desires at specific benefits and harms, thinking that the benefit is what they like or what brings them immediate material gain, and that the harm is what they dislike or what harms them. This is because these are partial measures that are insufficient to issue the correct judgment on whether behavior, system, legislation, or relationship will truly bring about a benefit or not, or whether it will truly repel a harm or not. These measures are not suitable to be the starting point.
