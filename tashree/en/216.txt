In his Sahih: From Urwah from Aisha that the Quraysh were concerned about the case of the Makhzumite woman who stole, so they said, "Who will speak to the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) about her?" They said, "Who would dare to do so except Usama, the beloved of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him)?" So Usama spoke to him, and the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said, "Do you intercede in a punishment from the punishments of Allah?" Then he stood up and delivered a sermon, saying, "O people! The reason that those before you were destroyed was that when a noble person among them stole, they let him go, but when a weak person stole, they enforced the punishment upon him. By Allah, if Fatimah, the daughter of Muhammad, were to steal, I would cut off her hand."(330).
And punishments are a mercy from Allah for the people, a means of opening the door to repentance, and an expiation for sins. Some people are led to Paradise by chains, and punishments are remedies for the individual and for the preservation of society; they are not intended for their own sake.
Facilitation and the removal of hardship: There is no doubt that religion, by its nature and rulings, is ease, and it did not come to impose hardship on Muslims. This is inherent in the religion itself, that is, in the legal rulings. For example, prayer is easy; it is five times a day, not fifty. Hajj is once in a lifetime, and the expiation for one who engages in sexual relations during Ramadan is contingent upon ability: freeing a slave, or fasting two consecutive months, or feeding sixty needy people. All of this is from the mercy of Allah and His facilitation for His servants. However, if a jurist sees that people find prayer burdensome and issues a fatwa to shorten it to three prayers under the pretext of removing hardship or facilitation, or issues a fatwa allowing a person to shift from fasting two consecutive months to feeding while being able to fast, under the pretext of facilitation, he would have distorted the religion and taken it upon himself to legislate without Allah. At the same time, he would have acknowledged that the legal ruling derived from detailed evidence causes hardship, thus contradicting the text of the Qur'an that Allah did not make for us in religion any hardship. He would fall into what he seeks to avoid. The Lord of Glory, Exalted is He, says: ﴾Allah intends for you ease and does not intend for you hardship﴿ [Al-Baqarah 185], and He said ﴾Allah does not burden a soul except with that within its capacity﴿ [Al-Baqarah 286], ﴾And He has not placed upon you in the religion any difficulty﴿ [Al-Hajj 78], and He said ﴾We did not send down the Qur'an to you that you be distressed﴿ [Ta-Ha 2], "meaning We did not send down the Qur'an for you to exhaust yourself in worship and to subject yourself to severe hardship."
The rulings and legislations are suitable for humans; there is no hardship in them, ﴾And He has not placed upon you in the religion any difficulty﴿, nor is there any obligation beyond capacity, ﴾Allah does not burden a soul except with that within its capacity; it will have what it has gained, and upon it is what it has earned﴿. They take into account human nature and satisfy needs and organic instincts in a balanced manner that neither represses nor overwhelms. They are realistic rulings, not imaginary ones. Humans love children, women, and wealth, and Islam has taken all of this into account.
The Sharia is distinguished by what is not found in other legislations; Islam has established it as measures based on belief, making it a doctrinal deterrent that motivates a person to adhere to it out of fear of Allah, love for Allah, and hope in Allah's mercy. This makes a person not in need of a coercive power to deter him from adhering to the law, but rather makes him submit to the authority of Allah in secret and in public. He does not see escaping from the punishment of the ruler in this world as a gain or profit; he knows that Allah is watching him, and its punishments are both worldly and otherworldly.
Legislation has taken into account the realization of public interest and justice while considering human beings. This has created credibility between the legislator and the obligated person, making the obligated person adhere to the rulings voluntarily, not by force. When Western man lost trust in the church, he turned to another system and abandoned divine legislation for human legislation. Therefore, the Islamic system establishes trust.
