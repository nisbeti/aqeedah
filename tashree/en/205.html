Maqasid al-Sharia al-Kubra

Know that there is not a single verse in the Qur'an in the form of a justification for the legislation of the Sharia, but rather the verses clarify the objectives and goals and the results that arise from sending the messengers, revealing the books, and the wisdom behind legislating the Sharia. This is like His saying: ﴾And We have not sent you except as a mercy to the worlds﴿, and His saying: ﴾And We send down from the Qur'an that which is healing and mercy for the believers﴿, and His saying: ﴾And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me﴿, ﴾And We did not send down to you the Book except to clarify for them that in which they differ and as guidance and mercy for a people who believe﴿, ﴾Indeed, We sent Our messengers with clear signs and sent down with them the Scripture and the balance that the people may maintain [justice]﴿ [25 Al-Hadid], ﴾Mankind was [of] one religion, but they differed. And if it had not been for a word that preceded from your Lord, it would have been concluded between them﴿ [Al-Baqarah 213]. "Since the cause (illah) is linked to its effect in existence and non-existence, the fact that the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) is a mercy, and that the Qur'an is healing and mercy, all indicate that the Sharia came as a mercy for the servants. However, the fact that the Sharia came as a mercy is the result that follows from the Sharia, and not the motivating factor (illah) behind its legislation. That is, Allah, Exalted is He, informed us that His wisdom in legislating the Sharia is that it results in being a mercy for the servants, not that the reason for its legislation is that it is a mercy. Accordingly, the fact that the Sharia is a mercy for the people is the ultimate goal of the legislator that he aims for through the legislation of the Sharia, and not the reason for which it was legislated"(300).

Therefore, these verses that clarify to us the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) and its aims, which are to establish justice and fairness, and for the Book to govern the lives of people, elucidate the purpose of the revelation of the Sharia and its intended goals. We can say that these are the great overarching objectives of the Sharia: establishing justice, preventing oppression, enforcing the Sharia, mercy, worship, guidance, and clarifying the rulings, ...

The scholars have derived eight essential objectives (maqasid) for humanity, which are: the preservation of life, wealth, religion, intellect, and lineage, the preservation of the state, the preservation of security, and the preservation of human dignity. We may also add to these the greater objectives derived from these verses: the establishment of justice, the enforcement of the Sharia, mercy, worship, guidance, and the clarification of rulings.(301)

The caliphate - that is, the application of Sharia through a state - achieves the major objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) of establishing justice, ensuring the rights of the oppressed, and implementing the laws of Allah. Al-Jazairi (may Allah have mercy on him) says, "(The scholars (may Allah have mercy on them) have agreed that leadership is an obligation and that the Muslims must have a leader who establishes the rituals of the religion and ensures justice for the oppressed against the oppressors, and that it is not permissible for there to be two leaders over the Muslims at the same time in the entire world, whether they agree or differ(302).)" (301) Imam al-Juwayni and his student Imam al-Ghazali derived the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) from the Shafi'i school, and Imam al-Shatibi followed them, identifying five necessary objectives: the preservation of life, property, religion, intellect, and lineage. Imam Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani added to these the objective of preserving security, the objective of preserving the state, and the objective of preserving human dignity, deriving them through the severity of punishment for crimes that violate them according to the law. The one who rebels against the state with arms is fought, and the one who breaks the pledge of obedience and is pledged as a second caliph is killed, among other rulings and details that can be reviewed in the book Islamic Personality, Volume Three: (Usul al-Fiqh) by Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani. Thus, the necessary objectives became eight, and upon examining the evidence, we found that establishing justice, enforcing the Sharia, worship, guidance, clarifying rulings, and mercy are also comprehensive objectives for which the Book was revealed and the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) was sent.
