The Islamic conception has always been founded on the reality that humans are entrusted (mukhalaf) with the earth to establish a system of justice that is an extension of the system of justice upon which the dominion of the heavens is based. This is defined by Islamic belief through the nature of the major relationships it engages with, and the nature of the connections and interrelations between these truths: the truth of divinity(108), the truth of servitude to Allah, the truth of lordship(109), and the purpose of their existence in this life. This conception does not overlook for a moment that the connection between the belief system and the governing system of life is an inseparable one. Thus, the social, economic, judicial, and political systems of life emerge organically from this conception, and it builds its solutions based on this foundation. The legal rulings are a revelation that must be followed because legislation is a right of the Legislator. Therefore, the Muslim adheres to the solutions that shape their behavior on the basis that they are a revelation from Allah. This is how the concepts of belief are intertwined with legislation, ensuring a good commitment to them and their proper application in reality, based on the fact that they are rulings from the Legislator to regulate actions. This structure acquires a spiritual strength that makes its implementation a means of drawing closer to Allah, and deviation from it a sin that incurs punishment in this world and the Hereafter. In its initial emergence, it is built upon connections that work in secrecy to ensure proper application, and its violation results in punishment that also emerges from the belief system itself on its other side, forming a deterrent that urges the sinner to return and repent. Thus, the action is surrounded by belief on both sides!

The Islamic belief has never been a rigid philosophy nor theoretical assumptions that address "human thought" alone in a cold manner cast in the mold of intellectual logic(110). Rather, revelation has combined faith and righteous action! It has been a vibrant belief, not a dry and cold philosophy; it consists of vital concepts that move souls, instilling tranquility, reassurance, emotional impact, reflection, contemplation, and the drive to carry its message, along with absolute confidence in reaching absolute truth, and motivating one to perform extraordinary deeds and actions!

(108) Allah is called "God" because creation turns to Him and supplicates in their needs and hardships. There are different opinions regarding the linguistic origin of the word. Some scholars believe it is derived from elevation and loftiness, while others see it as derived from worship and servitude. The name "Allah" is an exaltation of this meaning by adding the definite article to the word "God" to indicate greatness and uniqueness, and the hamza has been omitted for ease of pronunciation due to the frequent use of the word. "Allah" is the one who is truly worthy of worship, deserving of reverence and submission, and He is the refuge of creation in all their needs and hardships.

(109) The term "Lord" in Arabic has two meanings: 1) the master, meaning the one who manages and governs, commands and prohibits, legislates; 2) the owner, meaning the one who possesses an object or thing, granting them the right to manage it. See: Al-Hakimiyya and the Sovereignty of Sharia by Dr. Al-Mas'ari, pp. 28-29. Ibn Faris said: The root letters of "Lord" (rab) indicate foundational principles. The first is the rectification of a thing and overseeing it. Thus, the Lord is the owner, the creator, and the companion. The Lord is the one who rectifies a thing. It is said that so-and-so "raised" his property if he took care of its rectification. This is a water container that is "raised" by the Lord. The term "rab" applies to grapes and others because it nurtures the thing. And a horse is "rab" (nurtured). The Lord is the one who rectifies a thing. Allah, Exalted is He, is the Lord because He rectifies the conditions of His creation. The term "rabbi" refers to one who knows the Lord. I raised the boy, I nurture him. The term "rabiba" refers to the one who nurtures. See: Mu'jam Maqayis al-Lugha. The term "Lord" originally means nurturing, which is the gradual guiding of something to its perfection, encompassing all blessings, and it is described for emphasis, like fasting and justice. It is said to be a description from the one who nurtures, and its root is "rabab," then it was assimilated. It is named for the owner because he preserves and nurtures what he owns, and it is not applied to anyone else, Exalted is He, except with a qualification, such as His saying: ﴾Return to your Lord﴿. Ibn Juzayy said: Its meanings are four: the deity, the master, the owner, and the rectifier, all of which are applicable in ﴾Lord of the worlds﴿, but the most appropriate meaning is the deity due to its specificity to Allah, Exalted is He. See: Tafsir al-Bahr al-Madid by Ibn Ajibah with modifications, and Abu Hayyan al-Andalusi said in Al-Bahr al-Muhit: The Lord is the master, the owner, the stable, the deity, and the rectifier, and some have added the meaning of the companion.
