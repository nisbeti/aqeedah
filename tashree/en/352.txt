Thus, we find that Islam has provided a measure for humans (its source being the overarching idea, namely the creed) by which they can evaluate things, avoiding all the obstacles and gaps previously mentioned, so that they can distinguish between their ugliness and beauty, refraining from the ugly action and engaging in the beautiful one. This measure is solely the Sharia; what the Sharia deems good is good, and what it deems bad is bad. This measure is eternal; thus, what is good does not become bad, nor does what is bad turn into good; rather, what the Sharia has declared good remains good, and what it has declared bad remains bad. The same applies to good and evil. This measure is valid for all people at all times and places, organizing their lives, interests, and livelihoods in the correct manner. Meanwhile, secularism and liberalism have left the organization of life to humans, and we will study the measures they have established or accepted, with God's permission.
Therefore, it is essential for a person to have rulings that regulate his behavior and societal relationships, that is, to organize his life and the life of society and the state. He must have measures by which to evaluate his behavior and relationships, and this measure in Islam is the Sharia, which informs him about good and bad, about good and evil, about what brings reward or punishment, about what pleases Allah and what angers Him, about what leads to his benefit, the benefit of his livelihood, the benefit of his community, and the benefit of the earth, and what leads to the corruption of all that, about the lawful and the unlawful. This measure guides him through the challenges of life with intellectual leadership.
These rulings that regulate human behavior were brought by Islam in the form of obligations to ensure the effectiveness of their application, as adherence to them becomes associated with attaining the pleasure of Allah, while at the same time serving as a basis for holding a person accountable for their proper adherence.
