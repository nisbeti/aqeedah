Thirdly: Among the characteristics of the Sharia (Islamic law) are comprehensiveness, breadth, stability, renewal, lack of flexibility, and lack of evolution:

It is true that Islam came with broad outlines, meaning general concepts, and left it to the human intellect to derive from these general concepts the legal rulings for the ever-renewing and diverse problems that arise daily. However, this does not mean that there is flexibility and evolution, allowing one to take any ruling they desire from it, because it only provides what is indicated by the wording or the meaning that the wording conveys, and does not provide what cannot be understood as a sign in one of the accepted indications. The derivation from the evidence, that is, ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning), is based on extracting what is in the text from its explicit meaning or inferred meaning, or from the indication of the text, according to the three known indications, or from the rational basis of the text, that is, the legal reasoning contained therein, and nothing else.

Likewise, this does not mean that these general concepts keep pace with every era and every time; rather, it means that every era and every time finds solutions to the problems that occur within it according to these broad outlines, based on the perspective within it and according to what is indicated by the explicit wording of its sentences, their inferred meanings, or their indications or rationales, not according to the era and time from one perspective or according to what prevails in the era of treatments and rulings. This breadth in the texts for deriving multiple rulings, and the breadth of the rulings for their applicability to many issues, is what has made Islamic law sufficient for addressing all the problems of life in every time and place, and for every nation and generation; it is neither flexibility nor evolution.

And since the treatments are linked to human actions, and since they relate to occurrences, each occurrence has a fixed ruling. The reality of adultery is different from the reality of kissing, and different from the reality of touching, and different from the reality of looking; each has its own ruling and punishment, thus the rulings are characterized by stability.

"This stability stems from a fixed conception of the components and values, which comes from a fixed source of knowledge and will, a source that sees the entire field and the whole line, so that the curves of the path are not hidden from it, nor can it estimate today in a way that reveals its error and deficiency tomorrow, nor is it influenced by passion or desire that affects its balances and estimations... and we do not need to be cautious against freezing in a rigid mold, while we hold on to this characteristic in Islamic conception - the characteristic of movement within a fixed framework around a fixed axis - so the risk of freezing does not apply to such a conception, nor to the life that moves within its framework. Movement is the norm in it, and the universe does not freeze, nor does it rot, nor does it decay, nor does it stagnate; it is in constant motion and in constant development. However, this development is not an absolute evolution that departs from a fixed origin"(275), but rather it is a development that does not remove man from his essence, his components, his vital energy, his spiritual aspirations, his organic needs, and his instincts to something else that would change his essence, necessitating another system and other treatments. That man is limited in his essence by time and place; he is "a being that occurs in time, and ends after occurring in time, existing only in a place, and does not extend beyond the place, just as he exists only in time and does not extend beyond time"(276). His actions revert to limited motives, and move within a framework of limited behaviors that satisfy these motives, not going beyond them to others. If he is hungry, he must eat, so we regulate the rulings of what is lawful and unlawful in acquiring what he will eat, his benefit from it, and what is permissible from it and what is prohibited, with limited rulings that we do not need to...
