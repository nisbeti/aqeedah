Tenth: Exploiting the Potentials of the Arabic Language, Its Eloquence, and Its Capacities for Expansion, Influence, and Dissemination
It remains to mention the importance of the revelation of legislation in the Arabic language, with its richness, potentials, and inherent characteristics, the most important of which are expansion, influence, and dissemination, which are intertwined with the inherent characteristics of Islam: expansion, influence, and dissemination. In it, there is inflection and grammatical case, metaphor and literal meaning, Arabization, coining, derivation, and so forth, which makes it a bountiful sea. The eloquent Arabs excelled in its use and innovated in it to the extent that it became their craft. It suffices to present one example that highlights the capabilities of this vast language, which Islam has utilized to its fullest to accommodate human issues until the Day of Judgment. Some of our scholars said—when mentioning what the Arabs have in terms of metaphor, representation, inversion, precedence, and postponement, among other linguistic conventions in the Qur'an—"And likewise, no translator can convey it into any of the other tongues as the Gospel was translated from Syriac to Ethiopian and Roman, and the Torah and Psalms and all the Books of Allah, Exalted is He, were translated into Arabic because non-Arabs do not have the same capacity for metaphor as the Arabs do. Do you not see that if you wanted to convey His saying, Exalted is He: ﴾And if you fear treachery from a people, throw back to them on equal terms﴿, you would not be able to find words that convey the meaning that has been embedded in it, to elaborate on its entirety, to connect its parts, and to reveal its hidden aspects? You would say: If there is a truce and covenant between you and a people, and you fear treachery and breach from them, then inform them that you have breached what you had stipulated for them and notify them of war so that you and they are equally aware of the breach.
If Islamic law had come in a language other than Arabic, it would have required thousands of volumes to express its rulings in the detail that the Arabic language encompasses with its capacities for expression. And Allah, the Almighty, spoke the truth when He said: ﴾In a clear Arabic tongue﴿.
This enabled Islam to address human problems until the Day of Resurrection, through deriving many rulings based on those principles, which encompass individuals, and the definitions apply to many occurrences, and the reasons are shared in many incidents, and the delimitations regulate what defines general concepts that can be applied to their parts. Stability is a phenomenon in Islamic law that a person can feel.
