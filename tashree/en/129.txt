When Islam addresses the economy, it distinguishes between the economic system and the science of economics. Thus, it does not restrict human thought in their creativity in the science of economics and ways to improve production, for example.
Moreover, the Sharia allows people to comprehend the details of reality and understand it through reason (refer to the chapter: the three stages of ijtihad and the concept of the regulated understanding of reality).
Indeed, the mujtahid (jurist) must conduct specific research to be able to derive the legal ruling on any incident or issue. Some of this is purely rational (a precise understanding of reality), and some is purely legal (applying the legal ruling to that reality).
The same understanding applies to the judiciary; the judge in disputes must understand reality accurately, which is purely rational research, then applies the legal ruling and judges according to the relevant rulings for that reality.
Furthermore, we know that there are types of truths, some of which the Sharia takes from the terms and definitions established by people and builds upon them for legal rulings. A truth is the words that are established to indicate what is in the mind in terms of meaning, and a truth is the word used for what it was established for in the terminology of communication. If the established word is used for the meaning it was designated for by the people of language, namely the pure Arabs, this truth is called linguistic truth, such as: "nazar" (a little), "dahma" (blackness).
If the established words are used for a meaning other than what they were designated for, meaning they have been transferred from their linguistic meaning to another meaning, then if the transfer is due to custom or terminology, it is called customary truth. For example, "dabbah" (creature) was originally established in the language for anything that crawls on the earth, thus including humans and animals. However, the customary meaning for the people of language has specified it to four-legged beings, abandoning the original meaning. Similarly, "ghayt" (excrement) originally referred to the low-lying area of land but became commonly known for the impure excretion. The first, namely "dabbah," was established for a general meaning and then specified by the customary use of the people of language with some of its names, while the second ("ghayt") is a name established for one meaning and became famous for another meaning.
Customary truth has two types:
The linguistic customary truth, which is what the pure Arabs have become accustomed to,
And the specific customary truth, which is what the people of each science have become accustomed to, such as their specific terminologies, like the terminology of grammarians regarding nominative, genitive, and accusative cases, or our saying that faith is the certain belief that corresponds to reality based on evidence, which is not exclusive to the pure Arabs.
There is also the legal truth, where the Sharia has transferred the commonly accepted term in the language to a legal meaning, such as "salah" (prayer), which originally meant supplication in the language. From here, the Sharia, which established the application of the legal ruling based on a precise understanding of reality, entrusted reason with understanding reality, and then the mujtahid applied the legal ruling to that reality, also utilized the language and the customary and linguistic terms that people have become accustomed to.
