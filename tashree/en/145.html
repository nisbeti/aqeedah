As for the relationships of a person with their Lord and with themselves, they generally do not lead to the emergence of relationships that result in problems in society, except for matters like determining the timings of fasting, festivals, and pilgrimage, as well as zakat, jihad, the organization of endowments, mosques, hospitals, and health systems; for the ruler has an important role in these.

In contrast, transactions and punishments are relationships between individuals in society that lead to problems and disputes among people, which necessitate resolution by the governing authority of the state. The foundation of these transactions consists of systems related to social matters (the relationship between men and women), economics (the distribution of wealth, ownership, and its management), education, foreign policy (relations with other countries), and governance (the leadership of the state, its assistants in governance, governors, judges, the military, the administrative apparatus, and the consultative council). Governance includes: taking care of the affairs of the subjects (internal and external policy and judiciary), managing their interests, resolving disputes among them, and executing rulings upon them in matters that are obligatory, thus binding them to it. All of this occurs according to practical legal rulings that are applied to real situations, and we will clarify shortly - if Allah wills - in detail how Islam addresses these problems.
