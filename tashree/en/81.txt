The verses of the Qur'an are explicit in prohibiting conjecture in the fundamentals, and they condemn those who follow conjecture. They are a clear text that the foundations of the Sharia, whether they are the foundations of religion or the foundations of rulings, must be definitive and cannot be speculative; thus, there is nothing in the principles of jurisprudence that is not absolutely definitive, due to the explicit prohibition against that. In fact, all principles of jurisprudence are definitive. Accordingly, for a legal evidence to be considered a proof, it must be supported by definitive evidence of its validity, and whatever does not have definitive evidence supporting it is not considered a legal proof. The evidences that have definitive proof of their validity are four and nothing else: the Book, the Sunnah, the consensus of the Companions, and analogy (qiyas) that has a cause indicated by a legal text. Anything other than these four is not considered a legal proof, as there is no definitive evidence for it. Thus, the foundations of legal rulings, that is, the legal evidences, are confined to these four, and nothing else is considered valid(168). However, reasoning based on other than these four evidences, which has the semblance of proof, is considered legal reasoning, and the rulings derived from it are considered legal rulings; because it has the semblance of proof. But for those who do not consider them as evidences, it is not a legal ruling for them, although it may be viewed as a legal ruling in their perspective; because there is a semblance of proof."(169).
Thirdly: It is certain that a person's behavior in life is based on their concepts about it, and their perspective on life, while its foundation is belief, is composed of a set of concepts, standards, and convictions within the community. These ideas, which manifest as a collection of concepts, standards, and convictions, are not all beliefs; some are beliefs, while others are legal rulings. Since rulings are derived based on predominant conjecture, it is feared that if it is not confirmed that the origin of the rulings has been revealed, non-Islamic ideas may arise within the community, due to the existence of rulings derived from an origin not revealed. If this proliferates and extends over time, it will affect the community's perspective on life(170), and consequently, it will influence their behavior. Therefore, it is essential to ensure that the evidences from which the ruling intended to be applied by the state or by individuals has been revealed."(171).
"Legal evidences are of two types: one refers to the words of the text and what their literal and implied meanings indicate, and the second refers to the rational basis of the text, that is, it refers to the legal cause. As for the first, it is the Book, the Sunnah, and the consensus of the Companions, which requires understanding and contemplation. As for the second, it is analogy (qiyas), which requires the legal cause indicated by the legal text."(172).
