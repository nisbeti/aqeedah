The comprehensive legal principles that encompass the secrets and wisdom of the Sharia, and to the extent of understanding them, the status of the jurist is elevated and honored, and the brilliance of jurisprudence becomes apparent and recognized(161).
Some of the usul (principles) books that studied the general objectives of the Sharia included those goals and aims that result from applying and adhering to the Sharia, and they serve as a resource for the jurist to refer to in order to sense the consistency of the ruling reached by his ijtihad with the objectives of the Sharia, without making these objectives a criterion in his derivation, as they are not the causes of the Sharia that would affect the derived ruling, but rather they motivate him to exert more effort to strive in the issue in light of the rulings that correspond to them and align with those objectives derived from numerous evidences and rulings.
Thus, al-Qarafi's method in al-Dhakhira and then in al-Furuq gathered a set of comprehensive principles that encompass the secrets and wisdom of the Sharia, and the general evidences of jurisprudence,
and al-Shatibi's method(162) established the general objectives of the Sharia, and the reasons that form the rich juristic capacity of the jurist and his intellect that comprehends the legislative objectives derived from studying the detailed specific rulings during ijtihad.

(159) The term "issue" here refers to any action or set of actions whose validity does not depend on anything else, ... Examples include: ablution (wudu): actions whose validity depends on them because the validity of prayer depends on ablution; therefore, ablution is not considered an issue according to the definition, but it is considered a part of prayer that is necessary for its validity.
Prayer: actions whose validity does not depend on anything else, so it is an issue, and everything that is necessary for its validity, such as the pillars and conditions of validity like purity and facing the qibla, is considered part of it ... Therefore, if a person imitates a jurist in prayer, he must imitate him in all its components, such as ablution, washing from major impurity, performing tayammum, facing the qibla, and the pillars of prayer. (Taysir al-Wusul ila al-Usul by the scholar 'Ata Abu al-Rashtah, part two, chapter: Definition of the issue)
(160) Clear in the Principles of Jurisprudence. Muhammad Hussein Abdullah. Dar al-Bayariq, pp. 18-19. With modifications.
(161) See: Al-Furuq: titled Anwar al-Buroq fi Anwa' al-Furuq, by Shihab al-Din Ahmad ibn Idris al-Qarafi - vol. 1, and al-Qarafi stated that he had written much of it scattered in the chapters of the book "Al-Dhakhira," then he gathered it and added to its summary and explanation, revealing its secrets and wisdom, and he included other principles until their total reached five hundred and forty-eight principles, and he clarified each principle with what corresponds to it from the jurisprudential branches, and he named it: "Anwar al-Buroq fi Anwa' al-Furuq," which is a distinguished book in its field that no one has matched.
(162) Imam al-Juwayni and his student Imam al-Ghazali derived the objectives of the Sharia from the Shafi'i school, and Imam al-Shatibi followed them, identifying five essential objectives: the preservation of life, property, religion, intellect, and lineage. Imam Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani added the objective of preserving security, the objective of preserving the state, and the objective of preserving human dignity, deriving them through the severity of punishment for those who commit crimes that violate them legally. Thus, the one who rebels against the state with arms is fought, and the one who disrupts the unity of obedience and is pledged as a second caliph is killed, among other rulings and details that can be found in the book "Islamic Personality," part three: (Principles of Jurisprudence) by Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani. Thus, the essential objectives became eight, and through examining the evidences, we found that establishing justice, enforcing the Sharia, worship, guidance, clarifying rulings, and mercy are general objectives for which the Book was revealed and the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) was sent, and neglecting them leads to disbelief, tyranny, injustice, or immorality and severe punishments! Thus, the objectives of the Sharia became fourteen.
