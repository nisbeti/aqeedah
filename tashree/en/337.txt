The limitations of human intellect as a source of legislation, and its inherent deficiencies:
We have thus established the necessity of legislation for life, and the need for legislation to possess certain characteristics. Is the intellect capable of producing those legislations that meet these necessary conditions, through which the goals and objectives and solutions that legislation must achieve can be realized?
To begin with, despite the immense capabilities of the intellect in innovation and thought, it is limited by natural boundaries that render it incapable of producing legislation characterized by certain attributes, no matter how much effort is exerted. Perhaps one of the most prominent aspects of the inherent deficiencies of the intellect is as follows:
Relativity and subjectivity:
The most important ideas related to:
The relativity of the intellect and the differences in its perceptions among individuals and communities.
People differ in understanding and assessing interests and harms, which leads to fundamental differences in their perspectives on values and rulings.
The absence of a fixed objective standard in rational legislation.
The human intellect is influenced by the environment, affiliations, and circumstances, and does not possess an absolute standard by which to issue unified or binding rulings.
The differences in intellects in understanding major concepts (such as justice, freedom, and interest).
What one nation sees as justice, another may see as oppression, leading to confusion in fundamental concepts in the absence of a fixed reference.
The inability to know how to practically achieve values.
Justice and interest are values that are agreed upon nominally, but how to achieve them requires a precise system, which is not available to the intellect alone.
The influence of whims and immediate interests in determining benefit and harm.
Without legal controls, a person's view of benefit and harm becomes governed by whim or trial and error, which threatens the stability of society.
The failure of democratic systems to produce legislation that represents the general will.
The democratic process produces laws imposed in the name of the majority, but they do not reflect a true consensus or general representation.
The impossibility of rational agreement on a unified legislative system.
Experience proves that people cannot reach a rational consensus on unified legislation due to fundamental variations in perspectives.
The need for a legislative reference that transcends human intellect.
Due to the relativity, subjectivity, and innate deficiencies of the intellect, the need for revelation as an absolute reference with comprehensive wisdom and justice becomes evident.
