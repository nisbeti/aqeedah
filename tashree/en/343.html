No matter how intelligent and perceptive a person may be, their understanding of the future consequences of an action remains limited, as it is confined by their personal experience and temporal horizon. A person cannot encompass all the factors influencing reality, nor can they accurately predict how proposed laws will interact with human behavior, or with the constantly changing economic, social, and psychological contexts, nor how the laws will interact with those factors that may be complex and intertwined, requiring reconciliation among them to avoid conflict or to prevent the failure to achieve the objectives of legislation.

For this reason, human legislation suffers from short-sightedness; it is often established to address immediate problems, resulting in situational laws that fit a specific moment in time, but soon become inadequate or conflicting when the circumstances change. Hence, amendments and modifications to positive legislation follow one after another, leading to laws being constantly altered and amended, and sometimes conflicting, causing the legal system to lose its stability and effectiveness, and social behavior to become disturbed as a result.

Legal and social studies have indicated that human legislation—as a human product subject to time and change—cannot remain in a single state, nor can it maintain permanent stability; thus, we see countries suffering from an endless cycle of "legal patchwork," where the need to amend laws becomes a continuous state and an indicator of their failure to encompass, which deprives them of stability, prestige, and the ability to provide social security and legal stability.

As for divine legislation, it has been issued by the All-Knowing, the Wise, who encompasses the conditions of His creation in the past, present, and future, and it has been established to address human life as it is, without being tied to a specific time or particular circumstance. Rather, the Sharia has come with comprehensive principles and higher objectives that enable it to adapt to new developments without losing its essence or requiring substantial abrogation or any modification. This is why His saying, Exalted is He: ﴾Today I have perfected for you your religion and completed My favor upon you and have chosen for you Islam as religion﴿ [al-Ma'idah: 3] indicates the completeness and permanence of legislation. The law of God has been complete since its revelation, suitable for all times and places.

The limitations of the mind in terms of place (environment and culture):

Summary:

The mind is influenced by the cultural and social environment.

The mind does not operate in a vacuum; it is shaped under the influence of the norms, customs, and prevailing concepts in its society, which limits its objectivity.

The bias of the local mind makes its legislation relative and non-universal.

Laws based on environmental perceptions may be considered "beneficial" in one context and "harmful" in another, leading to conflicting laws and societal disputes.

Human legislation is biased by its temporary civilizational context.

What a materially or culturally deviant society legislates may be catastrophic if generalized, such as laws that permit deviance or undermine the family in the name of freedom.

Islamic Sharia addresses humans as humans.
