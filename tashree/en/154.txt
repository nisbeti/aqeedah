First: Addressing Human Problems as a Human Being
First: Address human problems as a human being, that is, as a human being, not as Zayd or Ubayd, nor as a member of a specific nationality or race. Since the nature of human beings, their essence, and characteristics (their vital energy from instincts and organic needs) remain the same, the human of the atomic age does not differ from the human of a thousand years ago in their characteristics, essence, and needs. These are fixed, unchanging characteristics; the essence of a human being is based on stability, not change in essence. What arises from that essence in terms of needs and what those needs require in terms of solutions is also stable. The essence of a human being is not what they eat, or what they wear, or where they live, or what they ride, or what they use, but the reality is that, at their core and in truth, a human being remains a human being—mentally and psychologically, in inclinations and behavior—since the time of the father of humanity, Adam, until today. Their innate nature has not changed, their original motivations have not altered, and their basic needs have not been annulled. They are the same human beings who possess vital energy manifested in instincts (in their various forms of fear, courage, sexuality, affection, etc.) and organic needs (such as the need for food, sleep, and breathing). They behave in ways to satisfy their instincts and organic needs, and this vital energy is part of the essence of a human being that cannot change. Rather, a human being needs to organize the way of satisfaction and regulate it, and such regulation is characterized by stability, not change. It cannot be that satisfying the instinct of sexuality through deviance is correct at one time and wrong at another, nor can acquiring wealth through usury or monopoly be good at one time and bad at another. The discovery of cannabis (marijuana) or new types of alcoholic beverages does not make consuming them permissible, as they fall under the category of intoxicants and narcotics. Thus, the legislation that provides a ruling for the actions taken to satisfy their vital energies, and gives a ruling on things regarding permissibility and prohibition, is fixed.
Since this vital energy needs to be satisfied, that is, it drives a human being to perform actions to fulfill it—because work or behavior is what will achieve this satisfaction, or part of it—when the manifestation of the reproductive instinct arises, they perform the act of marriage, for example, to satisfy that manifestation. When they flee from battle, they satisfy the manifestation of fear from the instinct of self-preservation. Likewise, when they remain steadfast in battle, they prioritize the manifestation of courage over the manifestation of fear. When they eat, they satisfy their organic need for food and energy, and when they eat beyond their need, they satisfy the manifestation of possession from the instinct of self-preservation. When they pray, they satisfy the instinct of religiosity and sanctification, and when they seek fame and the admiration of others, they satisfy the instinct of sanctification, and so on.
Thus, needs arise from the motivations for satisfaction that drive behavior, that is, action. They have interests they want to pursue and harms they want to avert in their social relationships, so this behavior and these relationships need organization, and the needs require solutions.
The Legislator has commanded the human being as a human being and has prohibited and given them choices through a discourse that regulates their actions (that is, their behavior and relationships). The demand inferred from the commands and prohibitions, whether it is decisive or non-decisive, or the choice between action and abstention, is the ruling on human actions. The discourse, that is, the commands and prohibitions, indicates the necessity of action or abstention or permissibility, meaning it indicates the type of ruling.
Therefore, the one addressed by the ruling is the human being, inasmuch as they are a human being, but the subject of the discourse is the actions of the human being; and this ruling that they are addressed with is not granting them the freedom to do as they see fit, nor is it restricting that freedom, but rather it is a remedy for every problem they encounter in this life, that is, it is a clarification of the ruling for every action that emanates from the human being as a human being.
