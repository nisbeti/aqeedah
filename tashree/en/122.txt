Changing Ijtihad and Following Another Mujtahid:
"If a mujtahid (jurist) reaches a legal ruling through his ijtihad, it is God's ruling in his case, and it is not permissible for him to abandon his ijtihad and act contrary to it except in the following cases:
The first is: to act according to what the caliph has adopted, even if it contradicts what he has adopted.
The second is: to act according to what unites the word of the Muslims, even if it contradicts what he has adopted. These two apply to both the mujtahid and the follower (muqallid), while the third and fourth apply only to the mujtahid and not to the follower.
The third is: superior knowledge, if it becomes apparent to him that another mujtahid is more capable of connecting the issues or more knowledgeable about the reality or has a better understanding of the evidence, or is more knowledgeable about the textual evidence.
The fourth is: if it becomes apparent to him that the evidence he relied upon in his ijtihad is weak, and that the evidence of another mujtahid is stronger than his evidence, [provided that the principles are the same and not merely looking at the evidence, that is, provided that the established principles in the process of weighing the evidence are followed].
In other cases, it is not permissible for him to act contrary to what he has adopted(224).
