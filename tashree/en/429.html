This does not mean that people do not experience moments of joy and happiness in their lives, as they enjoy beautiful sights and pleasant outings. However, the meaning is that true happiness arises from the balance in organizing a person's life, and the tranquility that comes from a way of living, and from the correct interpretation of the meaning of life. Indeed, the opposite of happiness is misery; thus, a system that does not do justice to society will render most of its members miserable, even if they have temporary moments of joy!

In a program on CBC Radio Canada, the philosopher Lee McIntyre was hosted, who had a unique experience, as he set up a table at a subway station in New York with a sign that read: Ask the Philosopher!

After some time, a woman in her sixties stopped, with all the features of seriousness on her face. She took off her coat and the shawl covering her neck, and said firmly: "I am a woman in my sixties, retired with a comfortable pension. I hold a master's degree, and I have no family, as I am divorced. I have recently risen from the bed of recovery after a serious operation, the scars of which remain on my neck, and I want to know what I should do for the rest of my life. I want a reason to live for!"

Doesn't the question of this woman remind you of your own life? Do you know a reason to live for? What should you do for the rest of your life to give your life and existence true meaning? Isn't it the reality that our lives are covered by "thin layers" that can easily be removed, revealing the truth that we are immersed in this life without purpose, awareness, or genuine questioning, waiting to retire only to discover that our working life, which has occupied the majority of our time, taken our youth and the prime of our lives, and in which we have merged into crafting our career path, professional advancement, economic prosperity, shopping, trips, weekend getaways, watching the latest movies, and following sports championships, or even striving to overcome hardship and difficulty, securing a livelihood, and paying bills, only to find that all of this—with the enjoyment accompanying everything within it, or the toil, and despite its importance in our lives—is not the true reason we should live for or the reason we were created in this universe, which has been designed and calibrated with precise, controlled, and extraordinary measures to allow life to exist and to be hospitable to it, and to bring into existence this intelligent being capable of questioning, exploring, understanding, and perceiving,

When those "delicate veils" are lifted and their glow that accompanied them in the journey of youth and building the future, and looking forward to a comfortable and pleasant life, is no longer present, the question that this woman asked becomes urgent, with all its force: What should I do with the time I have left in my life? What is the reason for which I will live? At that moment, memory returns to the past to ask: Was I living for the reason that I should have lived for in the past of my life? Did I live my life correctly?

One may wait a long time to discover that they are living without purpose, that they are completely devoid of the elements of human existence, and that they are afflicted with spiritual emptiness, running through their entire life aimlessly, plagued by anxiety, confusion, and psychological fragmentation, not knowing the answer to the question: What is the meaning of life? Although this question is intrinsic to human thought and a fundamental condition for understanding human existence, so that one does not fall into intellectual emptiness, moral void, and behavioral deviance!

So what about you while you are on a journey like this in this worldly life? Do you not see the importance of answering similar questions to explain to you the reason for your existence in life, who brought you here? And what comes after? And what is required of you? Why did you come to this life?
