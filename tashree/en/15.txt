Their golden opportunity, which they paved for over a century and a half, was the fall of Turkey along with its ally Germany, which lost in World War I. The English, Greek, Italian, and French armies entered the territories of the Ottoman state and took control of all its lands, including the capital Istanbul. When negotiations for the Lausanne Conference began to establish peace between the warring parties, England stipulated to Turkey that it would not withdraw from its lands until the following conditions were met:
The abolition of the Islamic caliphate and the expulsion of the caliph from Turkey and the confiscation of his assets.
Turkey must commit to suppressing any movement by the supporters of the caliphate.
Turkey must sever its ties with Islam.
Turkey must choose a civil secular constitution instead of its constitution derived from the rulings of Islam.
Mustafa Kemal Atatürk implemented the above conditions, and the occupying countries withdrew from Turkey. When Curzon, the British Foreign Minister, stood in the British House of Commons to review what had happened with Turkey, some English MPs protested vehemently against him, expressing astonishment at England's recognition of Turkey's independence, which could potentially unite the Islamic countries again and attack the West. Curzon responded: "We have eliminated Turkey, which will not rise again from today. Because we have eradicated its strength represented in two matters: Islam and the caliphate." All the English MPs applauded, and the opposition fell silent(16).
