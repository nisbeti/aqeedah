If the values themselves are erroneous or impossible to realize (such as basing elections on the opinion of the majority), then the laws are also erroneous for being based on falsehood. Therefore, there must be standards that can be achieved, and values to which society can refer to organize social behavior, preserve the legitimate rights of each individual, and resolve conflicts between objectives when they collide (such as when legislation leads to the realization of one objective at the expense of another, like privacy and security when they conflict, leading the state in the West to spy on individuals and violate their privacy and freedom under the pretext of maintaining security).
Or some laws may realize certain values while contradicting other laws that undermine these values, such as achieving freedom of expression and protecting a person's rights to a fair trial, which clash with the law on secret evidence in America that deprives the accused of knowing their crime and defending themselves under the pretext that presenting evidence of their crime threatens national security; neither they nor the judge knows it, nor do they have the right to defend themselves. Thus, the laws contradict the objectives and contradict each other, and this contradiction is fundamental(499) and undermines the beginning and the end(500), not a superficial contradiction that can be resolved).
We can summarize some of the objectives and goals that legislation aims to achieve in order to organize the relationships of individuals with themselves, with others, with society, and with the state, according to binding normative rules that achieve balance and harmony among the various interests of the members of society. The ultimate goal of legislation or codification in all laws and in every time and place is to create an organization for various legislative or legal relationships in a permanent and continuous manner, in order to achieve justice among the members of society, for which that law was brought forth only to organize their lives and regulate their behavior with the authority of the state to ensure the stability of their community.

Correct legislative policy must rely on elements that are harmonious with the environment it regulates; if harmony does not unite them, conflicting elements will separate them, losing the connection between the texts and their intended meanings, such that the intended goal is not achieved, considering that any legislative organization is not intended for itself.
