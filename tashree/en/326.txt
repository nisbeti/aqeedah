The sciences and arts, literature and philosophy, history, jurisprudence (fiqh) and language, the advancement and elevation of humanity, the civilizations it has established, and knowledge in its essence, are all the product of the mind and thus the product of thought. Knowledge is the essence of human existence and the fruit of its intellectual output, as well as its exploration of the depths of existence and the challenges of life. These fruits either elevate the noble status of a human being to become the best of creatures, or degrade him to be worse than animals; they allow him to be in harmony with himself and find peace in the course of his life, or they shake his being from the depths and cause him anxiety, doubt, and suspicion; they explain the world to him or leave him in confusion.
Likewise, human beings are central in this existence; the universe, with all its celestial bodies and systems, was created to be ready to receive life, and life, with all its wonders and miracles, was created to be ready to receive humans. The highest aspect of the cycles of life is the rational human being, and this human ascends through the intellect that he possesses!
The intellect is the tool of man for knowing his Creator and understanding the secret of his existence in life, and for unlocking many of the mysteries of this existence and interpreting them through intelligent connections, understanding, and perception, as well as generating ideas, researching, deducing, and inferring. The intellect is the basis of obligation.
Whereas reason has this immense status in the "plan of existence," and in the distinction of rational human beings from other creatures, relying on reason to perceive the connection with the Creator; there is no doubt that it is an extremely important tool, of great capacity, and there is no doubt that its judgment, which it will render in the process of inferring the existence of the Creator or in interpreting existence, that judgment based on evidence and connected to the parameters that provide certainty is a judgment that is undoubtedly correct, and we must accept it without hesitation. However, when examining the ability of reason to issue judgments on matters of lesser significance than issues of belief, such as some rulings related to organizing human life, like the judgment of whether an action is good or bad, or whether it is a judgment of good or a judgment of evil, or whether it entails reward or punishment, or whether it is suitable for human livelihood or corrupts it? We will find that reason cannot issue such judgments. It is worth noting that reason cannot issue judgments not because it is prohibited from doing so, but rather because it has not been endowed with the tools, capacities, and information upon which its ability to issue them depends. If reason were able to determine good and bad, or to judge what is good and evil, it would necessarily have to agree with the judgment of the Sharia (Islamic law); however, since they differ, this clearly indicates that reason does not possess the ability to issue judgments. Therefore, in this discussion, when we say at any point that reason can err in judgment, or that it varies, or that judgment does not belong to reason, we mean desires or inclinations, not reason itself, because reason does not have the capacity to judge. However, humans do not cease to issue legislations and judgments; humans judge their desires, not their reason(481), and Western civilization has elevated the status of reason to the extent that it governs everything in the realm of nature. Therefore, as we place the issue here in its correct context, we may use the word reason when responding to secular liberal rationalism, but we mean desires and inclinations.

The intellect, without the guidance of divine revelation, cannot encompass the vastness of human experiences and the complexities of life. It is inherently limited in its capacity to understand the full scope of moral and ethical dimensions that govern human behavior. Thus, relying solely on reason for legislation would result in a narrow and potentially flawed understanding of justice and righteousness.
