When a person wishes to meet the king, he prepares himself in the finest attire, and he fabricates his words in his mind, embellishing them, and he reviews them repeatedly until he reaches a formulation that befits the rank of the one he addresses, and conveys his need to him with the best expression that reaches the heart of that king. And to Allah belongs the highest example, for He is the One who created to be worshiped, and He provides and bestows, so it is necessary to thank Him. He is the One who takes the sinner by their sin with a severe grip, and rewards the doer of good with deeds many times greater than what the doer deserves, out of generosity and grace. Those who praise Him cannot reach His praise, nor can anyone, no matter how precise, truly worship Him as He deserves to be worshiped, except for the effort of the one who is lacking. Therefore, it is essential for this obligated servant, in order to elevate his actions and improve them, to present them before the rank of the Lord of the heavens and the earth. He must seek assistance to reach that lofty rank through incentives that aid him and fears that drive him, in order to attain what helps him achieve that difficult and arduous goal. It is necessary to invoke faith, vigilance, fear, and hope so that he may elevate his deeds as much as possible, presenting them before Allah, the Blessed and Exalted, desiring to attain His acceptance. This is the rhetorical point in linking piety (taqwa), faith (iman), and good deeds (amal), because what is required of every action is to bear its fruits and benefits in transforming a person's life and elevating it to the highest rank befitting this honored being among all creatures. For this reason, a person elevates themselves by combining faith, piety, and good deeds.
