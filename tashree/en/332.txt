First: A set of major foundational values upon which legislation relies, including:
Comprehensive justice, which is achieved by ensuring that legislation establishes equality in rights and duties, and by organizing them. Comprehensive justice does not rest on absolute equality among individuals, but rather on equality regulated by abilities, responsibilities, and natural roles in life; Islamic legislation acknowledges equality between men and women in shared rights and obligations, but distinguishes between them concerning natural characteristics, functions, and abilities, assigning each according to their nature and role, in pursuit of justice rather than mere similarity.
Among comprehensive justice is the equal opportunity, and the prohibition of discrimination among individuals for any reason. Thirdly: the balancing of punishment with sin and reward with action.
Restoration of rights and redress of grievances, which is achieved by protecting rights, safeguarding honor and life, and enabling rights holders to obtain their rights through clear and effective means, and thirdly by prohibiting injustice in all its forms, whether material, moral, or institutional.
Preservation of the eight necessities: religion, life, intellect, wealth, lineage (family), community security, state preservation, and the protection of human dignity, and legislation must be in harmony with the establishment and service of these objectives.
The reliance of legislation on a supreme reference to achieve these values, and to regulate the concepts upon which this legislation is based regarding good and evil, beauty and ugliness, reward and punishment, so that they are not subject to bargaining or change, and so that they possess the motivation that makes adherence to them self-driven or voluntary, because their source or reference is acceptable to the individual and represents them.
Second: A set of basic societal and organizational values:
Achieving societal and political stability, as legislation must be a factor of stability, not a factor of provocation or discord.
Protecting the family as the nucleus of society, such that legislation organizes the social relations arising from the union of male and female, and the rights and rulings that result from it, and combating what undermines it, such as adultery, abortion, and homosexuality.
Responsibility, roles, and accountability, and the establishment of obligations, so that legislation pertains to the role of women as mothers, their role as wives, and their role in community life, along with the responsibilities, rulings, and priorities that arise from each role, similarly concerning the responsibilities of the ruler and the state. As for accountability, no one is above the law, and every decision has legal consequences.
Protecting public security and tranquility, ensuring the physical, psychological, and moral safety of all individuals, and combating crime, drugs, chaos, corruption, and public disorder.
Third: A set of economic and developmental values:
Legislation related to wealth ownership and distribution, and preventing exploitation, such as legislation that limits monopolies and prohibits usury, exploitation, and fraud, and supports economic balance and social solidarity.
