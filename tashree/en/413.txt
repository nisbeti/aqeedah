Shura (consultation) means that the ruler should not act unilaterally in matters of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) or public interest without listening to the scholars and those with opinions. It is obligatory in certain matters specified by the jurists (such as issues that concern the entire community), and advisory in the sense of counsel in other matters. However, in all cases, the decision of shura must not exceed the limits of Sharia; shura is a mechanism of ijtihad in permissible matters and prioritization, and it is not a vote on the permissibility of the forbidden or the prohibition of the permissible. Shura existed during the time of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) and the caliphs after him (the council of the Companions and those with the authority to make decisions), and its forms have recently evolved into the National Assembly and advisory bodies, all within the scope of the means left to organize it in a way that achieves the objective of the Legislator in involving the community in opinion and accountability.
These concepts form the foundations of political theory in Islam. It is important to understand them as they came in revelation and in the applications of the early period, so that we can remove the confusion caused by the incorrect translation of modern Western terms. For example, Western democracy speaks of "the sovereignty of the people" and gives legislators free rein, while Sharia-based politics speaks of "the sovereignty of the Sharia" and that the community chooses those who govern them by it. Also, the modern concept of "the state" is associated with nationalism and secularism, while the Islamic state is of a religious universal origin and purpose (a caliphate based on the methodology of prophethood). The concept of "freedom" in Sharia is constrained by servitude to Allah (for the servant of Allah alone is free in relation to anything else) unlike the liberal notion of freedom whose consequences we have witnessed.
Precisely defining these concepts prepares us for the next step: reviewing the foundational Sharia rules that regulate the realm of politics and define the role of reason within it.
Sharia rules in politics: Limits of obligation and the scope of reason
The comprehensiveness of political obligation and the hadith "You know best about your worldly affairs"
One common misconception is the belief that Islam is only concerned with acts of worship and ethics, while matters of governance and worldly affairs have been left to human experience and intellect. They mistakenly cite the hadith "You know best about your worldly affairs," and expand its meaning to justify the separation of religion and politics. In reality, this hadith was mentioned in a limited context (the pollination of date palms) teaching us not to rigidly adhere to human opinions in worldly technical matters, not that it allows for whims to legislate as they wish in matters of governance! Scholars have clarified its understanding by stating: worldly matters are of two types: those that pertain to art and experience, such as agriculture and industry, which should be referred to their experts; while those that pertain to judging actions as permissible or forbidden and organizing relationships are purely Sharia matters that should not be left to intellect. Thus, the hadith does not encompass legislative or political matters, as indicated by the continuation of his (peace and blessings be upon him) words: "If I command you with something from your religion, then adhere to it, and if I command you with something from my opinion, then I am but a human." The Companions understood this, so they did not consider the hadith as permission to create whatever systems they wished, but rather remained committed to referring to revelation in every matter. On the contrary, the texts affirm that a person is not left without guidance: ﴾Does man think that he will be left alone?﴿ [al-Qiyama 36]. That is, without obligations or commands and prohibitions that organize his life. The message of Islam encompasses all aspects of life, including politics and governance.
