Justice, meaning not being apparent in immorality, is required in comparison to the witness, because the witness informs about the incident, while this informs about the legal ruling; thus, both are informing about something, and justice is a condition for both. Moreover, Allah has forbidden accepting the statement of the immoral and has commanded that one should verify his statement. Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾O you who have believed, if there comes to you a disobedient one with a report, investigate, lest you harm a people out of ignorance and become, over what you have done, regretful﴿ [al-Hujurat 6]. The indefinite article used with "disobedient" and "report" indicates that any immoral person who brings any report must be met with caution, and people should refrain from accepting it and seek clarification of the matter and the truth, and they should not accept the statement of the immoral. The opposite implication of this verse is that the statement of the just is to be accepted for inquiry, learning, and other matters.

However, in the era following the corruption of speech, the process of inquiry must be regulated by the same condition we stipulated for the follower (muqallid), which is that the follower should only follow someone whose reasoning (or informing him about the legal ruling) is connected to a disciplined knowledge of the principles (usul) (for example, he should follow a jurist of a school of thought, or follow a scholar whom he knows has complete and disciplined principles and adheres to them in his reasoning in a structured manner, and that he is a jurist in all rulings of the specific issue he is being asked about and follows him in all rulings of that issue (like prayer, for example)). The difference between him and the general follower is that the follower asks about the evidence and the principles, while the general layman follows without needing to learn the evidence or the principles. However, he must know from whom he takes his religion, so he should ask the people of knowledge about a scholar of principles who meets the conditions for issuing fatwas and take him as a mufti or follow him, not just ask any mosque imam, or any fatwa website on the internet, nor those who engage in fatwa merely by reading a few books, as is the case today. The opinion I see is that the best he can do is to adopt a school of one of the four schools of thought, and then always inquire of the mufti about the legal ruling for that issue only in that school, due to the rarity of mosque imams or jurists who have knowledge of all the issues that a Muslim needs in various matters, such that they encompass all their detailed rulings.
