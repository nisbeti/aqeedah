The third manifestation: consideration of practical vital standards surrounded by a fence of responsibilities:

For example, the philosophy of inheritance in Islam is based on precise criteria that have nothing to do with masculinity and femininity at all. When a deceased person leaves behind a daughter, a mother, and a father, the daughter receives half of the inheritance, and the mother receives one-eighth. There is a difference between one female and another in the share, and the father's share is less than the daughter's. When he has two daughters, they receive two-thirds of the inheritance, even if any number of males (not sons) share the inheritance with them. As for the criteria of inheritance in Islam, the first is the degree of kinship; the closer the degree of kinship, the greater the individual's share in the inheritance. The second criterion is the position of the inheriting generation; the younger the inheriting generation is, which is expected to face life and its obligations, the greater their share in the inheritance. Therefore, the son of the deceased inherits more than his father, even though they are both males, as the son will typically bear the burdens of a longer life than his elderly grandfather. The third criterion is the obligation and financial burden and responsibilities, where the only case in which a male receives the same share as two females is when they agree in the degree of kinship and the position of the inheriting generation from the deceased, due to the resulting difference in financial burden. The law has placed upon the male the responsibility of caring for the interests of the woman close to him, so the male shares with the female in what he has received from his share of the inheritance when she is in need, which is obligatory upon him, and not the other way around unless it is a favor from her. Maintenance is obligatory on the man, while the woman's wealth is saved for herself even if she is wealthy. In Islam, a woman inherits like a man in more than thirty cases, or she inherits while her male counterpart does not inherit in several cases. She inherits half of what a man inherits in only four cases out of more than forty cases, and a woman inherits more than a man in ten cases. This is the philosophy of inheritance in Islam(285).
