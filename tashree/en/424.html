The invalidity of the liberal "Sharia of Maslaha" approach: the perspective that places "maslaha" (public interest) as a rational judgment above the texts is scientifically and practically flawed. Scientifically, it ignores the limitations and biases of human reason, and it overlooks the fact that revelation came to regulate public interest, not to neglect it. Practically, it has led to dangerous concessions in the constants of religion without achieving the desired renaissance; Western democracy has brought nothing to the Islamic world except strife and chaos under the guise of false freedom, and the dilution of legal rulings has only resulted in further weakening of identity and loss of rights. In summary: there is no substitute for the supreme reference of revelation; any pursuit of guidance outside of it leads to misguidance.

In conclusion, we present a textual map summarizing clearly what falls under the fixed legal rulings in the system of Islamic politics, and what falls under the changing means and methods of implementation:

Unity of leadership and establishment of the caliphate: It is a definitive legal ruling that Muslims must have one Imam (Caliph) through whom their word is united. As for the method of choosing the caliph (consultation of those capable of making decisions, general election, etc.), it is an organizational means of ijtihad (independent reasoning) to achieve the legitimate pledge of allegiance.

Source of legislation and sovereignty of Sharia: The legal ruling is that sovereignty belongs to the Sharia, and it is not permissible to legislate anything that contradicts the Book and the Sunnah; any law that contradicts a text is legally void. However, enacting procedural and administrative laws is permissible if it is based on a legal principle (such as organizing the interests of the people) and does not contradict a fixed ruling.

Form of the state and authorities: The legal ruling is that the system of governance is a caliphate based on the methodology of prophethood with a general presidency for the Muslims, not republics or hereditary monarchies outside the framework of the pledge of allegiance. The division of the state into provinces and the distribution of powers between the central authority and localities (decentralized administration) is an administrative means permitted to achieve good governance, provided that the supreme authority remains with the caliph and the unity of the state. Likewise, the existence of a council of ministers, and the organization of security and military apparatuses, are all means to achieve the objectives of security and administration mandated by the Sharia.

Consultation and political participation: Consultation (shura) and enjoining what is good and forbidding what is wrong in relation to the ruler is a legal obligation; the ruler must establish a consultative council and consult those of opinion as a ruling. As for the details of parliamentary councils (the number of members, methods of their election or appointment, duration of their term, their detailed powers), these are means subject to ijtihadi maslaha. It is only required that these means do not negate a legal principle (such as the opinion of the majority becoming binding in contradiction to a definitive text, which is prohibited).

Judiciary and execution of rulings: Appointing judges to settle disputes among people is an obligation, and establishing an independent judiciary is a legal ruling to achieve justice. However, the system of litigation and judicial procedures (the number of courts, levels of appeal, procedural law) is organized by the state as a procedural means. For example, the Sharia commands the establishment of justice and the redress of the oppressed, but it does not prohibit the establishment of an appellate court or cassation as stages to verify rulings; this is a permissible procedure to achieve a legal objective, which is to ensure justice and prevent judicial error.

Foreign policy and jihad: The ruling of the Sharia is that spreading Islam and protecting the nation through jihad is an obligation, and that loyalty to the believers and disavowal of the aggressors is a fixed principle. It is prohibited to ally with disbelievers against Muslims or to empower them militarily.
