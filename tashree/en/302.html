The first section: The meaning of judgment and ruling linguistically, technically, and legally:

The term "judgment" has many meanings in the Qur'an and Sunnah, including(439):

sovereignty, or kingship, or authority over matters, or the power that enforces rulings ﴾And judge between them by what Allah has revealed, and do not follow their desires, and beware of them lest they entice you away from some of what Allah has revealed to you﴿ [al-Ma'idah 49], and its implications encompass every matter in which a legal ruling has been revealed in the Qur'an and Sunnah to which they must refer!

And it means prophethood and what it entails of a Sharia that has been revealed to adjudicate between people: ﴾And We gave Lot judgment and knowledge, and We saved him from the city that was committing abominations. Indeed, they were a wicked people﴿ [al-Anbiya 74], ﴾And We gave understanding to Solomon, and to each We gave judgment and knowledge﴿ [al-Anbiya 79], "and it refers to what must be done, or prophethood; for the prophet is a judge over his nation or arbitrates between disputants in judgment"(440). If we gather these verses from Surah al-Anbiya about Lot, Solomon, and David (peace be upon them) regarding their receiving of judgment, along with Allah's statement ﴾Indeed, mankind was one community, and Allah sent the prophets as bringers of good tidings and warners, and He sent down with them the Scripture in truth to judge between the people in that in which they differed﴿ [al-Baqarah 213], we find that their laws were revealed to judge between people in what they differed about, so either their people respond and their calls are victorious, or their people disbelieve and do not judge by their books, thus establishing the proof against them with what Allah has revealed of the truth!

And it means Allah's command and His Sharia ﴾That is the judgment of Allah; He judges between you, and Allah is Knowing and Wise﴿ [al-Mumtahana 10], and authority over matters, which includes what is related to the management of affairs and the facilitation of interests according to specific rulings, ﴾Obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you﴿ [an-Nisa 59]. Ali ibn Abi Talib (may Allah be pleased with him) said: "It is a right upon the imam to judge by what Allah has revealed and to fulfill the trust; if he does that, it is a right upon the subjects to listen and obey!"

And judgment means adjudication in disputes, ﴾But no, by your Lord, they will not believe until they make you judge concerning that over which they dispute among themselves, and then find within themselves no discomfort from what you have judged and submit in [full, willing] submission﴿ [an-Nisa 65], and adjudication, meaning the resolution of disputes, and the final decision in conflicts in a binding manner, and the judge is also called a ruler, and what he pronounces is (a ruling), ﴾And Allah judges with truth, and those whom they invoke besides Him do not judge with anything. Indeed, Allah is the Hearing, the Seeing﴿ [Ghafir 20].

And adjudication includes what is beyond disputes, such as determining benefit and harm as a result of the ruling! Allah says: ﴾And He is Allah; there is no deity except Him. To Him belongs praise in this world and the Hereafter, and to Him belongs the judgment, and to Him you will be returned﴿ [al-Qasas 70](441),

and the judgment and ruling over people's actions on the Day of Judgment, and the resolution of their disputes and differences in an eternal manner. ﴾Indeed, your Lord will judge between them by His judgment, and He is the Exalted in Might, the Knowing﴿ [an-Naml 78].

(439) Refer to the book: Al-Hakimiyya and the Sovereignty of the Sharia by Dr. Abdullah al-Mas'ari, pp. 42-43. With significant modifications.

(440) Ruh al-Ma'ani, Tafsir al-Alusi.

(441) Ibn Ashour said in al-Tahrir wa al-Tanwir: And His statement ﴾And to Him belongs the judgment﴿ the "lam" here also indicates sovereignty. The precedence is for specification as well. And ﴾the judgment﴿ is the adjudication, which is the determination of benefit or harm for others. The omission of the related object of the judgment is indicated by His statement ﴾in this world and the Hereafter﴿, meaning He has judgment in both realms. The specification is used in its literal and metaphorical sense because the judgment in this world is established for others metaphorically, while the judgment in the Hereafter is exclusive to Allah.
