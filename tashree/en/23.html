It is needless to say that the types and number of doubts raised regarding the issue of relying on the Sunnah are very numerous. However, we can summarize the most important of them in terms of types of doubts, categorizing them into four classes or types(41), and responding to them with a methodological rebuttal that is applicable to every doubt that falls under each of these categories:

The first type: relying solely on the Qur'an instead of the Sunnah, denying the authority of the Sunnah, denying that it is revelation, denying that it clarifies the Qur'an, rejecting the independence of the Sunnah in legislation, not referring to the Sunnah or relying on it, claiming that the hadiths are not suitable for our time, differentiating between the status of prophethood and messengership in terms of acceptance and obedience, denying the existence of a specific obedience to the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him), permitting error and ijtihad (independent reasoning) on the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), denying infallibility, and doubting the Sunnah based on conjecture and certainty.

The second type: attacking the chain of narration (isnad): which branches into: attacking the integrity of the Companions or some of them, claiming the fabrication of chains and the distribution of texts upon them, doubting the attribution of the Sunnah to the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him), claiming that the early critics were lenient in authenticating narrators, claiming that there is much fabrication and that the prevalence of fabrication has weakened trust in the Sunnah, and claiming that it is difficult to distinguish between the authentic and weak Sunnah.

The third type: attacking the texts (mutun), claiming that the criticism of hadith scholars focused on the chain of narration rather than the text, and claiming that the texts contradict reason and certainties.

The fourth type: doubting the collections of the Sunnah, questioning the attribution of the Sahih al-Bukhari in its current form to Imam Bukhari, weakening some of its hadiths or those of Imam Muslim, doubting the documentation of the Sunnah, claiming that the documentation of the Sunnah was delayed, claiming that some of the Sunnah has been lost or misplaced, claiming that the Sunnah has not been preserved, asserting that the Sunnah was documented in the palaces of princes, and claiming that the bearers of the Sunnah were liars and the jurists of the Sultan.

Thirdly: attacking the system of Islam and its legislations, doubting the heritage in general and in detail, and attacking jurisprudence and jurists and principles, and questioning the validity of Islam:

Either by "doubting the validity of the legal rulings to apply to reality, or attacking concepts of Sharia such as the concept of jihad by limiting it to defensive jihad only, polygamy, inheritance, and others, with the aim of pushing Muslims to adopt capitalist solutions instead of Islamic legislations in all issues, or Muslims doubting the ability of legislations to reform reality, or the possibility of applying them, which has led to a regression in concepts, and a widening gap between reality and the legislations with which Islam addressed this reality, "and has led to a complete feeling of helplessness among many in facing the current of Western life, resulting in the total adoption of the rulings of Western legislations without considering their contradiction with the rulings of Islamic Sharia"(42)!

Or by presenting capitalist ideas and asking Muslims about Islam's stance on them, such as the system of usurious banks, insurance, and the commercial relations between countries, what is the legal ruling on them: does it advocate freedom of exchange or does it advocate protection?
