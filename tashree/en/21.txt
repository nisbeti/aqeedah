On the conference, it is said: "However, the most dangerous recommendation adopted by the conference is its call to purify the religious heritage from the noble prophetic hadith, relying solely on the texts of the Qur'an as the only reference, confronting the ideas of institutions that monopolize hadith in the name of religion, and creating a new school of ijtihad (juridical reasoning) in the current century."
We can say: These recommendations are almost a reproduction (in form and content) of Cheryl Bernard's recommendations in the RAND Corporation report, especially regarding her views on the Sunnah and the dangers of prophetic hadith. This simply means that the conference organized by the Ibn Khaldun Center and funded by the United States was a practical application of the recommendations of the RAND report on democratic civil Islam. What is particularly striking is that this conference did not stop at issuing these highly controversial recommendations, but took a further step with significant implications; when the conference statement considered the attendees as a core nucleus for a new movement called "Democratic Islamists in the Arab and Islamic worlds," and they are invited in their personal capacity twice a year to follow up on the final recommendations of the conference. This means that the recommendations of the report published by the American RAND Corporation were implemented in the name of democratic Islamists as Cheryl Bernard recommended; when she called for unifying the ranks of modernist Islamists, and even some traditional Islamists, who agree to modernize the Islamic religion based on the American vision.(31)
Among the features of the fierce attack on the pure Sunnah to exclude it from Islamic legislation is the claim of its non-binding nature, or through questioning the hadith and attacking it - in terms of its chain of transmission (isnad) and content (matn), its narrators, their justice and memorization, the methodology of the hadith scholars, and its documentation as well.
This includes questioning the authenticity of attributing the Sunnah to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him): the orientalist(32) Coulson sees: "That the Sunnah in the Hijri century refers to the totality of the legal opinions agreed upon among the scholars of a particular jurisprudential school... and eventually, the matter reached attributing those opinions to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) himself."(33)
And questioning the documentation of hadith: the orientalist Goldziher said: "Most of the hadith is merely a result of the religious, political, and social development of Islam in the first and second centuries, and it is not correct to say that it is a document of Islam in its early period - the period of childhood - but rather a remnant of the efforts of Islam in the era of maturity."(34)
And this includes questioning the narrators and attacking them: the orientalist Guinepl(35) said: "The trust in some of the major companions was not a given for everyone at first, which is why we find that the trust in Abu Huraira was a subject of fierce debate among many people."(36) Goldziher said: "Abdul Malik ibn Marwan prevented people from performing Hajj during the fitna (civil strife) of Ibn al-Zubair and built the Dome of the Rock."
