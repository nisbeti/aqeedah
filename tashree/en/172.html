Fourth: Establishing precise criteria that form standards for the mujtahid:

The legislator has established precise criteria (signs) that can be applied to various things, to serve as standards for the mujtahid (jurist) so that he can issue rulings related to them based on new discoveries of things. For example, he has set criteria for what constitutes wine (intoxicant), what is a narcotic, what is a drug, and what is vinegar. It is essential to study the concept of wine in Islam: what makes something wine from a legal perspective? And the rulings related to each of these, and so on, until the mujtahid has a clear understanding of what qualifies as wine in the legal concept. It is said: the definition of wine in legal terms is: "that which obscures the mind without the senses"(278) with intoxication and exhilaration, or it produces foam and has an intoxicating strength(279). The quantity that causes intoxication is: "that which intoxicates a large amount is also prohibited in a small amount" (and this refers to a vessel in which the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) used to wash, and linguistic sources say that this vessel holds three sa's (a measure), and one sa is approximately 3.1 liters, thus the capacity of the vessel is 9.3 liters). "A small amount" (a handful of it) "is prohibited," and so on (there are other criteria for wine), the mujtahid continues his research until he accurately defines the criteria for the concept of wine legally(280),

so that the mujtahid studies any newly discovered plant such as tobacco, khat, and hashish (cannabis), or any newly introduced drink such as nabidh, whiskey, and others, or any narcotic medicine that has been discovered, to determine which category any of these falls under after laboratory research reveals its properties, components, and how it interacts with humans (whether it affects the mind, the body, or both), and to apply the appropriate ruling directly. Thus, we say: whiskey is among the intoxicants, and therefore it is prohibited. Hence, it suffices to establish the ruling that "what intoxicates a large amount, a small amount of it is also prohibited" for wine, to apply it to any newly introduced intoxicating drink.

Similarly, it is stated in what Abu Dawood narrated: Umar ibn al-Khattab once mentioned the spoils of war and said: "I am no more entitled to this spoils than you are, and none of us is more entitled to it than anyone else, except that we are based on our positions in the Book of Allah, Exalted is He, and the division made by the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him). A man and his seniority, a man and his needs, a man and his family."

This is the ruling on the division of spoils, and this is a sign of the ruling regarding the circulation of wealth among the wealthy alone; and this is the ruling regarding the grazing of livestock, and this is a sign of the ruling regarding what belongs to the community. Or this is the ruling regarding the state's allocation to its subjects of what does not belong to anyone, and this is a sign of the ruling regarding minerals. He states regarding the division of spoils among the emigrants without the helpers: ﴾What Allah has bestowed upon His Messenger from the people of the villages, it is for Allah and for the Messenger and for those near of kin and the orphans and the needy and the wayfarer, so that it does not become a commodity between the wealthy among you﴿ [al-Hashr 7], and he states in what Ahmad narrated in the remaining Musnad of the Helpers: "Muslims are partners in three: water, grazing, and fire," and he states, as mentioned in what was narrated by al-Tirmidhi and Abu Dawood: "From Abyaḍ ibn Hammal that he came to the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) and requested a portion of salt, and he granted it to him. When he turned away, a man from the assembly said: 'Do you know what you have granted him? You have granted him the water of the 'idd!' He said: So he took it back from him, and he asked him about what is heated from the arak?"

(278) This distinguishes it from narcotics: a narcotic, in language, is that which causes numbness in the body and heaviness and incapacity in the limbs. An intoxicant is anything that causes lethargy and numbness in the extremities. Umm Salamah (may Allah be pleased with her) said: The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) prohibited every intoxicant and narcotic. An intoxicant has a legal penalty, while a narcotic has discretionary punishment. An intoxicant is impure and its small amount is prohibited, and it has a legal penalty, while a narcotic does not prohibit its small amount and is not impure. Thus, as you see, it is necessary to understand reality to accurately determine legal rulings.
