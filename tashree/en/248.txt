"The foundation of the function of 'arbitration' is social, while the foundation of the function of 'judiciary' is political. Therefore, we find that the most important characteristic that distinguishes 'arbitration' is: will and freedom, a will and freedom that grant the individual the right to choose their judge, the right to determine the guarantees of litigation, the right to innovate the procedures that they find most suitable for themselves, and finally the right to enforce the judgment. These are four main rights that individuals do not enjoy in the ordinary judiciary"(383) in the modern legal state.
Human systems have suffered from a lack of the components necessary to ensure their proper application in reality, so they worked to present them in the form of laws, legalizing the process of the supremacy of laws, and imposing penalties for misapplication. Therefore, we see that "the technical meaning of positive law (al-qanun al-wad'i) - as most legal scholars have agreed upon - is 'a set of rules applied in a specific society, at a specific time, and binding on individuals in that society by virtue of the coercive power of the public authority.'" Thus, the importance of law arises from its being a fundamental necessity for organizing the behavior of individuals in society in a binding manner, and for organizing the relationships between them on one hand, and between them and the public authority on the other hand. In addition to that, it is a necessity for organizing relations between states and groups. The legal system in the state reflects the state's strategy and defines its declared economic, social, and political programs for its society. Therefore, they paid attention to the element of drafting laws in a manner that leads to the organization of the behavior of individuals, groups, and entities in a binding manner. Then, a constitutional court oversees the constitutionality of the laws"(384).
The possession of systems and legislations constitutes the elements of respect and mechanisms that ensure the existence of self-application and obedience by individuals, or rebellion against them. Or are they imposed by the force of power? In other words: how is the sovereignty of the state (law) over conflicting interests in social relations achieved in reality? (Judge Muhammad Wafiq Zain al-Abidin says: "The law never possesses in itself the reasons for its respect, and thus all elements of its respect always and forever fall if the surveillance cameras are disabled or disappear!
As for the components of respecting the Sharia (law), they are inherent in itself; due to the precedence of knowledge, it is established from a power that surpasses all of humanity, and no exceptions, knowledge, relationships, or intermediaries are suitable with it.
The obedience that the law enjoys and which calls people to adhere to it is due to the respect imposed by an authority that has the right to use force always and forever. However, just as it is present, it can also be absent, and whoever possesses the ability to use it can also suspend it!
As for the obedience that the Sharia (Islamic law) enjoys and calls people to adhere to voluntarily, its cause is the moral commitment that finds its foundation in religion without the need for a force to impose it, except in the case of denying, rejecting, or rebelling against it.
Many people do not refrain from violating the law except when it affects them and they fear its power, and this does not constitute true respect for legislation.
