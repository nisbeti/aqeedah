Secondly: A set of fundamental societal and organizational values:
Achieving societal and political stability, as legislation must be a factor of stability, not a factor of provocation or discord.
Protecting the family as the nucleus of society, so that legislation organizes the social relationships that arise from the union of male and female, and the rights and rulings that result from it, and combating what undermines it, such as adultery, abortion, and homosexuality.
Responsibility, roles, and accountability, and establishing obligations, as legislation pertains to the role of women as mothers, their role as wives, and their role in community life, along with the responsibilities, rulings, and priorities that arise from each role, similarly: the responsibilities of the ruler and the state, and as for accountability, no one is above the law, and every decision has legal consequences.
Protecting public security and tranquility, ensuring the physical, psychological, and moral safety of all individuals, and combating crime, drugs, chaos, corruption, and public disorder.
Thirdly: A set of economic and developmental values:
Legislation related to the ownership of wealth and its distribution, and preventing exploitation, such as legislation that limits monopolies and prohibits usury, exploitation, and fraud, and supports economic balance and social solidarity.
Regulating labor relations, rights, ownership, and responsibilities.
Fourthly: Sovereign and civilizational values:
Achieving the legal foundations of the state and its legitimacy and principles of governance, and the relationships between the state and the subjects, for sovereignty belongs to the Sharia, and authority belongs to the nation.
Highlighting the civilizational identity of the nation, so that legislation produces a nation with culture and civilization.
Legislation should encompass the systems of society and the state, such as economy, governance, social matters, penalties, judiciary, and others, and regulate individual affairs.
