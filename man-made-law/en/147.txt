The Sharia indicates the consideration of it or its kind (i.e., it is not contrary to a legal origin).
It was established, not imagined.
It was estimated by the measures of the Sharia, not by abstract rational interests.
It is not permissible to avert a harm by causing a greater harm, nor by disregarding a definitive principle.
An invalid applied example: the permissibility of usury to purchase a house.
Usury is prohibited in itself by a definitive text.
Buying a house is not a necessity, but an imagined interest, especially since financing entities only provide loans to those who prove their ability to repay the monthly value, which is usually more than the rental value.
It is not permissible to commit a definitive harm (usury) to achieve a potential interest, especially if an alternative exists.
"When an interest contradicts a definitive text, it is an imagined interest, and it is not considered legally."
Every subsidiary ruling is linked to its detailed evidence, not to an abstract rational cause.
Interest and harm are measured through texts and regulated causes, not solely through general objectives.
There is no preference between rulings unless there is a real conflict that cannot be reconciled.
This is a serious attempt to establish foundational rules upon which a jurisprudence of balances can be built in real cases that require preference.
Thus, the problem with what is called "the jurisprudence of balances" lies in how it is applied and its limits. Some have used this "jurisprudence of balances" to justify conceding some rulings under the pretext of achieving higher objectives or averting greater harms, and we have found a repetition of the methodological errors they followed in "the jurisprudence of minorities," which led to preference and the determination of interests being dominated by desires, whims, and rational interests that do not meet the standards of interests accepted in principle.
We will reiterate the controls of interest and balance according to Imam al-Shatibi (al-Muwafaqat 2/6-25) to clarify them and provide a framework for examining the methodology of deviating from them in some applications of the jurisprudence of balances:
The foundational condition:
It must be regulated by the five objectives of the Sharia (religion, life, intellect, lineage, wealth).
It must be based on the extrapolation of the texts of revelation, not on abstract reason.
The procedural conditions:
Universality: it must encompass the community, not individuals (such as alleviating hardship for all Muslims in the West, not just for a privileged group).
Non-contradiction with the text: any "balance" that permits what the Qur'an has explicitly prohibited is rejected.
Certainty: the harm being averted must not be imagined (such as "fear of Islamophobia").
