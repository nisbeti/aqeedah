I am surprised: "Is it by the opening of Khaybar, or by the arrival of Ja'far?" [Sirah of Ibn Hisham] And it was added to that: "Or by the Islam of the Negus," but he did not mention the Negus in this hadith, even though the situation would require it if he had responded to his call and embraced Islam.

7 – Those who claimed that the Negus for whom the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) performed the funeral prayer is the same Negus to whom the Muslims migrated and sought refuge, and the same Negus to whom the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) sent the letter inviting him to Islam at the end of the sixth year of the Hijra, have erred in their assertion. This misconception arose from the fact that the Negus to whom the Muslims migrated was praised and commended by the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) when he described him to those who were asked to migrate to him: "He is a king under whom no one is wronged, and his land is a land of truth" [Sirah of Ibn Hisham]. And from the fact that he provided good protection to those Muslims who migrated to him, allowing them to worship Allah without fear of anyone, and because he refused to hand them over to the Quraysh envoys after they requested him to do so, contrary to the wishes of his nobles, and he protected them from both of them, saying to them: "You are safe in my land; whoever insults you will be liable." And because he commented on Ja'far's response to him when he asked him about what the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) had brought to them, saying: "This and what Jesus brought come from the same source." And on Ja'far's response the next day when he asked him what they say about Jesus, while he had taken a stick from the ground, "By Allah, I do not say anything about Jesus son of Mary that I do not say about this stick" [Sirah of Ibn Hisham]. They were misled by all of this into thinking that he had embraced Islam, even though the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) did not announce that he had converted, just as Umm Salama, the wife of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), who was among those who migrated to Abyssinia, did not mention that he had converted when she spoke about him and what happened to them in the land of Abyssinia, where she said: "When we landed in the land of Abyssinia, we were under the protection of the best neighbor: the Negus. We were safe in our religion, and we worshipped Allah, the Exalted, without being harmed, nor did we hear anything we disliked... And she said: By Allah, we were in that state when a man from Abyssinia came to dispute with the Negus over his kingdom. She said: By Allah, we did not know sadness that was greater upon us than the sadness we felt at that time, fearing that this man would prevail over the Negus and that a man who did not know our rights as the Negus knew them would come to rule over us. And she said: After Allah granted victory to the Negus over his enemy, and established him in his land, by Allah, we did not know joy that was greater than that joy. She said: And the Negus returned after Allah had destroyed his enemy, and established him in his land, and he secured the affairs of Abyssinia, so we were with him in the best of conditions until we came to the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) while he was in Mecca" [Sirah of Ibn Hisham]. Thus, Umm Salama's narration does not indicate that the Negus had embraced Islam.

On one hand, and on the other hand, it seems that those who said that the Negus for whom the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) performed the prayer is the same Negus who sent the emigrants and the Negus to whom he sent the letter inviting him to Islam, seem not to have seen the hadith of Anas ibn Malik which was narrated by Muslim in his Sahih, which states: "The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) wrote to Khosrow and to Caesar and to the Negus and to every tyrant inviting them to Allah, and it is not the Negus for whom the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) prayed."

As for the two letters mentioned by Muhammad Hamidullah in his book (Political Documents of the Prophetic Era), one of them states that the Negus wrote to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) a letter in which he declared his Islam and his readiness to come to the Prophet if he commanded him to do so, and that he sent to him his son Arha ibn al-Ashham ibn Abhar, and this letter was sent while the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) was in Mecca. As for the second letter, it was reported that the Negus sent it to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) with those returning from Abyssinia from the companions of the Prophet while he was in Medina.
