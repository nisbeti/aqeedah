From this perspective, the Sharia (Islamic law) guarantees non-Muslims in the Islamic state the freedom to remain on their beliefs, without acknowledging their validity, and prohibits coercion to enter Islam. All these rights are inseparable from obligations and are not isolated from the system of divine values; rather, they are regulated by the measures of truth, justice, and wisdom, governed by the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) and its values in achieving the welfare of the individual and society, without making the individual himself a reference in estimating benefits and harms, but rather the reference is the revelation, and the honor lies in the obligation to follow guidance.

Therefore, Tariq Ramadan's argument regarding what he calls the objective of religious freedom in Western societies is a Western objective, not an Islamic one, and his comparison is fundamentally flawed.

The third mechanism: Distorting the "balance" by inflating benefits and minimizing harms.

The correct approach: The legal harms are established by revelation (such as usury and adultery), not by reason. They are not measured by worldly gains (al-Qurtubi, al-Jami 4/160).

The deviation in the jurisprudence of balances: Reducing legal harms to "worldly detriments" and "their material effects" only!

Example: Justifying a Muslim woman's marriage to a non-Muslim under the pretext of "the benefit of الدعوة (da'wah, invitation to Islam)" (London seminar 2005), even though the harm of losing her religion and her children is greater.

Example: Justifying dealings with usurious financing companies, the entity: "International Islamic Fiqh Academy" (2016, Resolution 245).

The justification: "A balance between the harm of usury and the benefit of developing the economy of Muslims." Even though those who advocate the objectives see the preservation of wealth as one of the greatest objectives of the Sharia, and the system of wealth preservation in the economic system is based on a fundamental principle that cannot be separated from it, which is the prohibition of usury due to the severe danger of its effects on society and life! Their reasoning contradicts itself, invalidating the beginning with the end, all of it is contradictory!

Critique: Usury is prohibited in itself (an-Nisa: 161), not just for its material harm, especially with the availability of alternatives (Islamic bonds - crowdfunding), and its harm to society is firmly established.

Example: Permitting commercial insurance: the entity: "Council of Senior Scholars in Saudi Arabia" (the minority in the decision, 1978). The justification: "A balance between the risks of insurance and the benefit of social security."

Critique: The alleged benefit does not permit the forbidden (Ibn Uthaymeen, al-Sharh al-Mumti 8/243).

Commercial insurance is one of the prohibited risks (Sahih Muslim: 1513).
