Third: The Arguments of Those Who Claim that Sharīʿah Aims to Bring About Benefits and Prevent Harms
Classification of Benefits into Levels:
Essential (ḍarūrīyyāt): e.g., preservation of religion, life, intellect, lineage, and property.
Non-original essentials: e.g., prohibition of even small amounts of intoxicants.
Primary needs (ḥājiyyāt): e.g., marriage of a minor by the guardian.
Non-original needs: e.g., consideration of social parity in marriage.
Embellishments (taḥsīniyyāt): e.g., denying legal competence to slaves in giving testimony.
From these they infer that these interests are the causes (‘ilal) behind all legislation.
Evidence from the Qur’an and Sunnah:
﴾And We have not sent you, [O Muhammad], except as a mercy to the worlds﴿.
﴾My mercy encompasses all things﴿.
The ḥadīth: “There shall be neither harm nor reciprocating harm.”
Claim of Consensus (Ijmāʿ):
They claim that scholars unanimously agree that God's rulings all have underlying wisdom and objectives.
Inductive Survey of Sharīʿah:
They argue that a comprehensive survey of Sharīʿah shows that its details revolve around realizing benefits and avoiding harms, especially with regard to the three well-known categories: necessities, needs, and embellishments.
Claim that in Customs (ʿĀdāt), Meanings Matter:
They maintain that in areas of customs and transactions, rulings fluctuate with perceived benefits and harms. They cite ﴾And for you in legal retribution is [the preservation of] life﴿.
Using Maṣlaḥah When No Text Exists:
In the absence of a text, they argue that rulings may be built on maṣlaḥah, if supported by comprehensive inductive reasoning or reason enlightened by the objectives of Sharīʿah.
Fourth: Refutation by Taqī al-Dīn al-Nabhānī of These Arguments
Invalidity of Using Maṣlaḥah as a Legal Cause Based on Reason Alone:
The fact that God is Wise and Just does not justify deriving legal causes based on benefit:
This pertains to kalām (theology), not to uṣūl al-fiqh.
Belief in God's justice does not entail identifying the legal reasons for His rulings.
A valid legal cause (‘illah) must be deduced from revelation, not from divine attributes.
