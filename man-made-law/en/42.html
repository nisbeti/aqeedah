Summary:

It is clear that any human attempt at legislation – no matter how advanced in technique or methodology – will remain deficient, influenced, prone to contradiction, and perpetually unjust, because it simply originates from the limited mind that is incapable of determining good and evil, or comprehending all the consequences and outcomes of laws, or resolving disputes among the various issues related to the matter that affect it or are affected by it or are intended to be achieved. In contrast, the divine legislation revealed by the Creator, Exalted is He, possesses all the qualities that guarantee its justice, permanence, and absolute suitability, making it the sole true reference for organizing human life at all times and places, as it contains complete knowledge of human conditions and the outcomes of actions, balances rights and duties with precision, and combines wisdom, mercy, facilitation, and the alleviation of hardship simultaneously. Therefore, legislation must be a pure right of the Creator, Exalted is He, for the divine law, comprehensive, just, eternal, is the only valid framework for guiding humanity. Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾Indeed, judgment belongs to Allah﴿ (the Noble Qur'an).

There is no judgment before the arrival of the law, and the mind is not a judge. What did the scholars of the ummah (community) of usul (principles of jurisprudence) say?

Humans are obligated, and the meaning of obligation (taklif) is to bind the obligated person to what the law requires to do or refrain from, or the equivalence of doing and refraining, according to the conditions of obligation. ﴾So be steadfast as you have been commanded, and whoever has repented with you, and do not transgress. Indeed, He is All-Seer of what you do﴿ [Hud 112]. There is no judgment before the arrival of the law; al-Shawkani says in Irshad al-Fuhul (p. 6): "There is no disagreement that the judge is the law, and this is after the prophethood and the arrival of the call." He said in Irshad al-Fuhul, p. 14: "The mind does not determine what is good or bad in terms of legal obligation, but rather it helps a person know what he must or must not seek in the law." Al-Ghazali says in al-Mustasfa (p. 40): "The subject of judgment is the action of the obligated person, and there is no judgment before the arrival of the law, and the mind is a recognizer of the judgment, not a judge." Al-Ghazali also says in al-Mustasfa, vol. 1, p. 61: "The legal ruling is the address of the law concerning the actions of the obligated, and the mind has no role in its establishment." And al-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said in al-Majmu' Sharh al-Muhadhdhab, in the Book of Purification: "And the books of the school state that the origin of things before the arrival of the law is permissibility or prohibition, or there is no judgment before the arrival of the law? And there are three well-known opinions, the correct one among them according to the scholars is that there is no judgment before the arrival of the law, and a person cannot be judged for something he does with prohibition or hardship, and we do not call it permissible because the ruling of prohibition and permissibility are among the rulings of the law, so how can this be claimed before the law? Our school and the school of all the people of the Sunnah is that rulings are not established except by the law, and that the mind does not establish anything." Al-Shatibi, may Allah have mercy on him, said in al-Muwafaqat 4/200: "The origin of actions before the arrival of the laws is the nullification of obligation, for there is no judgment upon him before knowing the ruling, as the condition of obligation according to the usuliyyin (scholars of usul) is knowledge of what one is obligated to."(15)

(15) Imam Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani, may Allah have mercy on him, in Islamic Personality, part three: (Usul al-Fiqh), states that the origin of human actions after the prophethood of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) is not permissibility, but rather adherence to what the law has brought, because a person is obligated and accountable, and cannot be excused for neglecting adherence under the pretext of the absence of harm or corruption. He supports this with numerous verses that require returning to the law: ﴾So if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger﴿, and ﴾And whatever you differ over, its judgment is with Allah﴿, as well as the hadith: "Every matter not in accordance with our command is rejected," which indicates that the origin is to seek the legal ruling, not to rely on rational assumptions. He also rejects the claim that the origin of actions is "to refrain from judgment," because refraining means suspending the law, and the Qur'an and Sunnah command asking and seeking the ruling: ﴾So ask the people of knowledge if you do not know﴿, "Did they not ask when they did not know...". There must be a legal evidence for the ruling, and it is not permissible to issue a judgment from the mind without a text or evidence. As for material matters, the origin in them is permissibility, because the texts have come generally permitting all of them, such as Allah's saying: ﴾He has subjected to you what is in the earth﴿, ﴾Eat from what is in the earth lawful and good﴿, ﴾And walk in its slopes and eat from His provision﴿, and these generalities imply the permissibility of everything unless there is a text that prohibits it. Therefore, prohibition can only occur with an explicit text that specifies this permissibility, as in the prohibition of carrion, blood, and wine, which are exceptions to the origin of permissibility. In summary: there is no judgment before the arrival of the law, and any judgment issued by the mind without legal evidence is rejected.
