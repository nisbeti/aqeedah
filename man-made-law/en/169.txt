Reason that is not disciplined by revelation inevitably deviates. Opening the gates of unbounded ijtihād without adherence to the established methodological safeguards is the beginning of the secularization of Sharīʿah, turning divine guidance into man-made speculation governed by societal norms and personal whims.
Sources of Knowledge in Islam: Revelation, Reason, and Sensory Perception
Human knowledge arises from three primary sources:
The External Senses: These are man’s portals to perceive and receive information from the tangible external world (such as hearing, sight, and touch).
The Intellect: This is the faculty responsible for analyzing sensory data, engaging in abstraction, and drawing logical inferences.
Revelation: This refers to that which God conveys to humankind—be it information or commandments—either directly to the prophets or through divine scriptures.
In the domain of religious knowledge—pertaining to beliefs and laws—revelation plays the foundational role. It is the only source that provides knowledge of the unseen and of God’s commands, which the unaided intellect has no independent access to. Reason’s role is to receive, comprehend, and apply revelation. It acts as a servant to revelation: deriving legal rulings from it, applying it to real-world contexts, and using it to construct arguments for the faith.
Reason: Its Role and Limits in Sharīʿah
Reason is a tremendous gift, and God has made it the basis for moral responsibility (taklīf). There is no legal obligation upon the insane or those lacking sound cognition. God has honored the intellect by frequently calling upon it in the Qur’an: “Will you not use reason?” “That they may reflect,” “Indeed in that are signs for those of understanding,” and many more. In Islam, the intellect is a means to faith, contemplating the universe and the self to infer the existence and oneness of the Creator. It is also a means to understand revelation and ponder its meanings. One cannot conceive of understanding the Qur’an and Sunnah without an intellect engaged in extracting and interpreting their meanings. This unique faculty was bestowed upon humans to elevate them in both knowledge and faith.
However, the All-Wise Lawgiver—who created reason—also knows what nurtures or corrupts it, and has outlined clear boundaries for its use so it does not stray:
First, the intellect is limited. It cannot access the unseen realms or comprehend human welfare in full independence. Therefore, revelation came to supply what reason cannot reach. For example, the mind may affirm the existence of a Creator, but it cannot define God's attributes or the realities of the unseen. Revelation completes this gap.
