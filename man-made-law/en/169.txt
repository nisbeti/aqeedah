Understanding this issue is essential for regulating the methodology of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning): there is some confusion among some regarding the use of reason to understand texts - which is mandated by Sharia - and the application of reason to accept or reject texts - which is categorically prohibited. In this chapter, we will clarify the pillars of knowledge in Islam, and their ranks (senses, reason, and revelation), the role of reason and transmission in producing religious knowledge, and we will conclude by outlining the correct methodology for legal ijtihad as established by the usuliyyin (founders of Islamic jurisprudence).
Sources of Knowledge in Islam: Revelation, Reason, and Sensation
The sources of human knowledge are three:
The external senses: they are the window through which a person receives information about the external, tangible world (such as hearing, sight, and touch…).
The intellect: It is the tool for analyzing sensory information, abstraction, and deduction.
Revelation: It is what Allah conveys to humans in terms of news and legislation, either directly to the prophets or through the heavenly books.
In the field of religious knowledge (creeds and laws), revelation (wahy) plays the fundamental role, as it is the source that brings us the truths of the unseen and the commands of Allah that pure reason cannot independently comprehend or produce. As for reason, its role is to receive revelation, understand it, and apply it; it serves as a servant to revelation, deriving rulings from it and applying them to reality, and it comprehends the arguments of faith through it.
The intellect: its function and limits in the Sharia
The intellect is a great blessing that Allah has made the basis of legal responsibility; there is no legal obligation on the insane or the one who lacks understanding. Allah has honored the intellect by frequently urging reflection and contemplation in the Qur'an: ﴾Do you not reason?﴿, ﴾Perhaps they will reflect﴿, ﴾Indeed, in that are signs for those of understanding﴿… and other verses. The intellect in Islam is a means to faith; it looks into the horizons and the selves to deduce the existence of the Creator and His oneness. It is also a means to understand the Sharia and contemplate its meanings, and it is inconceivable to understand the Qur'an and the Sunnah without an intellect that works in the text for indication and derivation. Humanity has been endowed with this gift to elevate itself in the ranks of knowledge and faith together.
But the Wise Legislator – who is the Creator of reason – knows best what rectifies and what corrupts, so He has clarified for us the limits of its use so that we do not go astray:
It has been established that the human intellect is limited; it cannot independently comprehend the unseen nor the interests of humanity. Therefore, revelation has come with what the intellect cannot reach on its own. For example, the intellect acknowledges the existence of a God, but the details of God's attributes, actions, and the realms of the unseen cannot be invented by the intellect; thus, revelation has come to clarify them.
