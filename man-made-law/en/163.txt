Some of them have become so bold as to state that "what was considered right a thousand years ago is no longer acceptable to remain prohibited today in light of the developments of this era," and he continues, "Because social relationships and their conditions have completely changed, some forms of rulings that represented the truth according to the standards of religion a thousand years ago no longer fulfill the requirements of religion today, nor do they meet the objectives that it seeks, because the possibilities have changed, the causes of life have developed, and the results that arise from enforcing a specific ruling in its form have completely reversed(45)."
One of them says: The jurisprudence of the surrounding reality concerning the incident: This principle means that the one who is looking into incidents when exercising independent reasoning (ijtihad) should take into account the changes in the surrounding reality of the incident, whether it is a temporal or spatial change, or a change in conditions and circumstances. Accordingly, the one looking into it must consider this change in his fatwa and ruling, as many of the juristic rulings are affected by changes in temporal and environmental conditions; the rulings are an organization mandated by the Sharia that aims to establish justice, bring about benefits, and avert harms. They are closely linked to the temporal conditions and means, as well as to public morals. How many rulings were a successful arrangement or remedy for an environment at a certain time, but after a generation or generations, they no longer achieve their intended purpose, or they lead to the opposite due to changes in conditions, means, and morals. One who reflects on the contemporary reality of women notices the changes that have affected their lives and the transformation that has occurred in their current situation. Clinging to fatwas that were issued in a time that has changed without altering this fatwa is a mistake against the Sharia and an encroachment on its validity in every time and place. Based on this, the juristic principle was established: "The change of rulings is not denied by the change of times," his words have ended(46).
Notice the stark contradiction; for Islam to be suitable for every time and place, it must change according to time and place. Its suitability lies in its dilution, and the one who judges that it is no longer suitable and determines the alternative ruling for this old ruling is the human intellect.
