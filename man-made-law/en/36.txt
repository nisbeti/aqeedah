Limitations of the Human Mind's Understanding of Reality and Its Complexities:
Summary:
Human reality is complex and multidimensional.
Reality is not formed solely from material data, but from psychological, spiritual, social, economic, and political factors that are difficult for the mind to grasp all at once.
The mind's inability to comprehensively understand reality and its interactions.
The mind cannot perceive all elements of reality or accurately link causes to outcomes, which weakens its ability to enact precise and just legislation.
Legislative reasoning suffers from a lack of balance between values and interests.
The mind fails to achieve a balance between individual and collective needs, between rights and duties, and between justice and facilitation, leading to legislation that is either inadequate or contradictory.
Relying on experimentation in legislation leads to catastrophic errors.
Laws are often enacted through trial and error, being adopted before their effects are understood, resulting in deep social harms that are difficult to rectify later.
The reliance on limited material measures excludes the ethical and spiritual dimension.
The success of laws is often measured by economic indicators or relative satisfaction, while the value impact is ignored, leading to societal disintegration despite apparent progress.
The mind alone is unqualified for legislation without the guidance of revelation.
Its inability to grasp the total complexities of human beings and society necessitates the need for revelation to guide it, as it has no light except that which Allah, Exalted is He, has revealed.
A number of intertwined issues are related to the matter, both in terms of influence and being influenced, making resolution difficult:
Or resolving disputes among the intertwined issues related to the matter that affect it, are affected by it, or seek to achieve it.
Detail: One aspect of the structural shortcomings of the human mind is its inability to fully comprehend the reality for which it claims to legislate. Human and social reality is inherently complex, multidimensional, intertwined in its influences, variable in its manifestations, and linked to biological, psychological, economic, political, intellectual, ethical, material, and moral factors, which makes the attempt to "regulate this reality with purely human legislation" a venture doomed to structural failure.
While the human mind can analyze a part of reality through observation and study, it is incapable of encompassing all its elements and long-term interactions, unable to penetrate the depths of various problems and what influences them.
