Sixth: Important Jurisprudential Alerts
The ability to abstain nullifies the rule: if a third way exists that has no prohibition, it must be chosen, and it is not permissible to take the easy way out under the title of necessity.
Relying on facilitation without detailed evidence is merely following concessions without a standard, and scholars have deemed it a path to destruction.
Public interest (maslaha) does not abrogate texts: rather, it is regulated by what is indicated by the Sharia. If it contradicts definitive evidence, it is not to be acted upon.
Seventh: Conclusive Summary
The rule of "the lesser of two evils" is not a general justification for bypassing rulings, nor is it suitable as a cover for normalizing prohibitions. It is a precise rule, regulated by its conditions, and should only be resorted to in cases of complete necessity, after exhausting efforts to seek other lawful alternatives. Using it inappropriately is human legislation disguised in the name of Sharia, opening the door to desires, and wasting the distinction between the lawful and the unlawful.
Imam al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam said: "Harm is not removed by a greater harm unless it is necessary, and its removal is preferred."
Thus, the greater harm is not in committing the forbidden act, but in cloaking it in the garb of legitimacy. Participation does not prevent evil but rather transforms it from an "external obligation" imposed by force on society to an "internal choice," after dressing it in the guise of legitimacy, which is far more harmful.
And Allah, Exalted is He, knows best. May peace and blessings be upon our Prophet Muhammad and upon his family and companions altogether.
Conclusion and Recommendations:
I have presented some manifestations of "the jurisprudence of minorities" that have made dangerous methodological concessions in established rulings, especially in the area of marital relations and the stance towards secular systems, under the pressure of reality and under the pretext of public interest. The concept of "public interest" in these applications has shifted from a Sharia-based concept regulated by principles to a purely rational interest, beholden to Western reality and its pressures, leading to:
Contradictions with definitive texts and consensus.
Waste of major doctrinal principles (such as referring to the law of Allah, loyalty and disavowal).
Weakening of the legal sensitivity towards definitive prohibitions.
Presenting a distorted image of the flexibility of Islam and its susceptibility to absolute interpretation.
