The strength of the evidence (criteria for preference):
Presenting the explicit textual evidence over the ambiguous or general. (Such as the text on the apparent(42), and the apparent over the interpreted (because it is closer to the speaker's intent unless the interpretation is based on valid evidence or a supporting indication), the definitive over the ambiguous, if they cannot be reconciled, or if there is no indication strengthening one over the other, and the correspondence over the implication, and the explicit over the hinted, and so on. What is in accordance with the apparent text is preferred over what requires carrying or interpretation, and what has definitive evidence is preferred over what has ambiguous evidence.) In all of this, the context, the nature of the address, and the generality or specificity of the ruling are considered indicative of the strength of the evidence.
Presenting what is clearer in ruling, or broader in application, or stronger in the context of the address.
Considering the contextual indications, the explicit versus the implied, and the correspondence versus the implication.
The strength of the connection to the issue (criteria for relevance):
Presenting the specific evidence over the general in cases of conflict(43), and the clarifying over the ambiguous, (because the ambiguous cannot be acted upon except after its clarification, thus the clarifying is an explanation of it.)
Presenting the restricted over the unrestricted if there is a valid connection (i.e., provided there is unity in the ruling and the cause or the aspect, which is referred to as "unity in ruling and cause").
Presenting the evidence related to the specific incident over the general evidence in cases of specification. (If it is established that the general was not intended for that incident).
These criteria are not based on tastes or unregulated interests, but on definitive rules in understanding and applying the texts.
Thirdly: The stage of acting upon the preferred evidence
The preferred evidence must be acted upon after completing the consideration of the previous aspects.
As for the less preferred evidence, it should be interpreted in a way that does not contradict the preferred evidence if possible, or it should be abandoned if reconciliation is not possible.
Thus, the process of preference in the science of usul (principles of jurisprudence) is a scientific balance between legally accepted evidences, considering their ranks in authenticity, evidence, and connection to the issue, not based on imagined interests or utilitarian estimates.
