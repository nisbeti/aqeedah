From the Sharia of Allah to the Sharia of Maslaha: The Roots of Modern Liberal Islam and Its Role in Producing "Minority Jurisprudence"

Thaer Ahmad Salama.

Executive Summary:

Praise be to Allah, the Lord of the worlds, and peace and blessings be upon the noblest of messengers. To proceed... This is an extensive scholarly research on the principles of Islamic legislation, discussing the reference of legislation between reason and revelation. This is a fundamental topic and a delicate issue that should only be approached with utmost precision in foundational work and awareness of methodological errors to avoid them.

This book presents an in-depth critical study on the issue of the roots of the shift in the reference of legislation in Islamic thought, from the Sharia of Allah to the Sharia of Maslaha, and discusses the roots of modern liberal Islam and its role in producing "minority jurisprudence," or the role of "minority jurisprudence" in producing liberal Islam, starting from the foundational relationship between reason and revelation, and addressing the intellectual currents that sought to present reason—especially in its modern Western form—as a reference above the legal text or as a competitor to it.

Main Objective of the Book

This study aims to re-establish the reference of revelation as a fundamental source for legislation and to refute claims that elevate reason isolated from the text, or that prioritize "maslaha" (public interest) in its modern sense over legal rulings, illustrating the impact of these trends on Islamic thought and society, and the deviations in understanding and ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) that resulted from them.

General Structure and Key Themes

Reason and Revelation in Islam: The first chapter addresses the philosophical and legal foundations of the status of reason in Islam, emphasizing that reason serves the text and does not govern it. It distinguishes between reason connected to revelation and reason influenced by materialistic and secular philosophies, demonstrating its inability to produce legislation.

The Usul Methodology and the Status of the Text: The second chapter presents the foundations of usul (principles of jurisprudence) reasoning among usul scholars and the importance of connection to revelation from the Qur'an and Sunnah in establishing rulings, highlighting the status of the text and prioritizing it over other speculative meanings such as maslaha when they conflict with it.

Deviant Currents from the Reference of Revelation: The third chapter discusses the historical deviation of some doctrinal and intellectual currents that undermined the reference of the text, such as modernists and contemporary thinkers. It addresses how the abstract rationalist inclination infiltrated Islamic thought during periods of weakness and fascination with the West.
