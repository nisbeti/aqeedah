Critical Conclusion
The price has been heavy; the rationalist school of thought has excessively been influenced by the Western model to the extent of diluting the constants of religion, introducing foreign Western concepts into the understanding of Islam. More dangerously, it has altered the way of thinking among many Muslims and their scholars, leading them into a chaotic foundationalism, a methodological chaos, disconnected from any link and any grounding.
Indeed, "some fortresses have collapsed from within," as Dr. Muhammad Hussein described in the title of his book. The nation now faces a new challenge, more dangerous than that of external colonizers: the challenge of incoming concepts that have infiltrated through its children while cloaked in the guise of ijtihad (independent reasoning) and renewal. Generations of Muslims have emerged, dazzled by the West to the extent of adopting its approach to dealing with religion—an approach that arose from a specific conflict between the church and science in their context, which does not necessarily apply to us. In conclusion, we can say that the reference for Islamic legislation has shifted—for those influenced by the rationalist school without scrutiny—from being the reference of infallible revelation to being the reference of relative human reason. Human desires have begun to infiltrate in the form of "interest," "interpretation," and "development," rising above the words of Allah, Exalted is He, and the words of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him), which is a reversal of the equation and a loss of direction. Hence, the writings of Islamic critics—such as Muhammad Hussein, Anwar al-Jundi, and al-Nadwi, among others—have served as a warning cry for the nation not to be deceived by the allure of modernity at the expense of the essence of its religion. They called for "renewal according to the methodology of Prophethood," not according to the methodology of the West. Finally, the historical experience itself has vindicated much of the criticism of these scholars; we have witnessed with our own eyes what the exaggeration in unrestrained rational thought has led to: an increasing disconnection between Muslim societies and their Sharia, and a wide admiration for the Western civilizational model, even in its negatives, alongside a decline in the reverence for godly scholars in the face of the noise of "modernist thinkers." Conversely, the balanced return to the Book and the Sunnah—as called for by political Islamic movements in the past and present—has yielded a blessed awakening that has restored balance, albeit partially. The struggle continues between a current inspired by the West and a current inspired by revelation, and perhaps this detailed report will help in understanding the roots of this intellectual conflict and its early pioneers and their systematic critique. The contemporary Muslim is in dire need of distinguishing between the worthless and the valuable in what is presented under the names of ijtihad and renewal, so that he can take the means for true revival without undermining the constants of his identity and religion. We ask Allah to inspire us with guidance and to protect our nation from intellectual factors of destruction; indeed, He is All-Hearing, Responsive.
