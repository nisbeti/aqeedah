A benefit that is not explicitly mentioned, but rather a benefit that the Sharia has considered through a number of texts, and a collection of evidence, and the description of the writers of usul (principles) of the appropriate benefit (maslaha) as "the general analogy" is merely a term intended to differentiate between general analogy and analogy (qiyas) (18), in contrast to other definitions that clarify its generality beyond detailed evidence. The first indication of the idea of general analogy appeared with Imam al-Juwayni in his book Al-Burhan, through terms such as "istislah" (public interest) and "ra'y mursel" (general opinion) and "ma'ani mursala" (general meanings), without using the direct phrase "mursala benefit". Imam al-Ghazali, who passed away in 505 AH, was the first to formulate the term general analogy and combined it with the phrase "mursala benefit" in his books Al-Mankhul and Al-Mustasfa, defining it as: benefits not mentioned in the text, neither by negation nor by affirmation, but which are in accordance with the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) and require the consideration of the mujtahid (jurist). He did not leave it open to all cases, but imposed legal controls on it, and indicated that if there is a legal evidence (text, consensus, or valid analogy), then the preferred option is to agree with it, not to exceed it. Al-Ghazali discussed the topic of the mursala benefit under the section of "istislah" within the discussion of "illusory evidence", and although the transmission from Imam Malik by al-Juwayni was incorrect, there is no evidence that Malik actually used this term. (In fact, it can be said that none of the Maliki jurists before Ibn al-Arabi (d. 543 AH) mentioned the mursala benefits in their principles, although Ibn al-Arabi did not mention the mursala benefit in the summary he prepared in the principles of jurisprudence titled Al-Mahsul fi Usul al-Fiqh, but he discussed in his other books that the benefit is a principle of deduction according to Malik. Likewise, Abu al-Walid al-Baji, the Maliki scholar who passed away in 474 AH, did not mention the issue of mursala benefits in his book Ihkam al-Fusul fi Ahkam al-Usul.) Later, expressions such as "istihsan" (juridical preference) and "qiyas mursel" (general analogy) appeared in the jurisprudence of the Malikis and Hanbalis, but these are considered later expansions influenced by the thoughts of al-Juwayni and al-Ghazali. Thus, the term "mursala benefit" is a product of the thought of the al-Juwayni–al-Ghazali school, and not from Maliki jurisprudence or the predecessors (19). It is essential to recognize that some reliance on issues such as compiling the Qur'an or reinforcing the limits as representations of the mursala benefit in the original Maliki jurisprudence is a late conceptual introduction to the earlier juristic efforts, and not an accurate representation of their methodology, meaning that they found interpretations in Malik's (may Allah be pleased with him) efforts that they interpreted as using mursala benefits, due to their lack of access to its detailed evidence, and because Imam Malik did not clarify the evidence upon which he based those interpretations.

The topic of the objectives of the Sharia and the way of thinking in deriving objectives for the Sharia and the methodology that resulted from it was invented by al-Juwayni and al-Ghazali, and it seems that when they stumbled upon this "treasure" in the view of the Sharia, they began to explore its applications, one of which was the idea of mursala benefits.
