The detailed or adherence to the legal boundaries of the foundational principles, nor to the established methods of deduction and reasoning in the science of usul al-fiqh (principles of jurisprudence), has led to serious methodological concessions.

Manifestations of this methodological deviation in the jurisprudence of minorities:

Dissolution from the controls of legitimate interest:

The scholars – such as Imam al-Ghazali and al-Shatibi – have elaborated on the conditions for considering an interest, clarifying that it is only valid if it is genuine, comprehensive, legally recognized, not opposed to a definitive text, derived from the general evidences of the Sharia, and not based on whims at all. However, in the applications of minority jurisprudence, these conditions have been neglected, and the "pragmatic worldly rational interest that does not rely on detailed evidence, but rather on circumstantial estimates and political or social accommodations" has replaced the regulated interest.

Partial selection from the schools of thought and fatwas without regard to the foundational principles that established them:

The danger of moving between the schools of thought – as explained in the book The Miracle of Islamic Legislation – lies in the fact that every subsidiary ruling is based on a complete foundational structure. For instance, one who takes the opinion of al-Shafi'i regarding the nullification of ablution by touching a woman, and then takes the opinion of Abu Hanifa regarding the invalidation of prayer due to excessive movement, has combined two opposing foundations that do not converge in methodology, leading to "the destruction of the foundational structure from its very roots." This is because the principles of each school ensure the consistency of their results with the way those principles interact with texts and evidences in a methodologically disciplined manner. Disrupting these methodologies by jumping between schools and principles is prohibited in usul, as it leads to the interference of whims and desires in taking the legal ruling. Additionally, the disruption of the disciplined methodologies of each school occurs when they are mixed with those of other schools, as some schools may adopt methods of preference and application of different evidences related to the issue that contradict the methods of other schools that have chosen different approaches in preference and application of evidences. Therefore, one must always adhere to disciplined and homogeneous foundational methodologies.

Transcending the rules of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) to eclecticism and selection:

Legal ijtihad is not based on selecting fatwas from the depths of ancient jurisprudential books without considering the context in which they were said, or the principles upon which they were based, as some who take the lead in minority jurisprudence do when they justify a fatwa by a deviation in jurisprudence that was said in a different context, found in a book of one of the schools or scholars! Without possessing the tools of ijtihad that place that fatwa within a serious foundational research framework that includes it among the evidences related to the subject and studies it in a foundational manner aimed at deriving the legal ruling. Moreover, they have not constructed a methodology that allows for this dangerous expansion and regulates it foundationally! Their goal has been to prove the legitimacy of a fatwa they initially deemed appropriate and sought to find a basis for it.
