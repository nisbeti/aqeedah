Adhering to the constants and not compromising them under any pressure.
Regulating the use of the concepts of necessity (darura), need (haja), and public interest (maslaha) with their strict foundational controls.
A serious focus on developing practical legitimate alternatives (true Islamic financing, recognized Islamic arbitration courts, effective marriage programs).
A clear distinction between "facilitating" for Muslims in applying the law of Allah, and "conceding" the law of Allah to ease their lives in the Western environment.
Bold self-criticism from within the institutions of minority jurisprudence regarding these methodological deviations.
Minority jurisprudence (fiqh al-aqalliyat) will not succeed in its religious and social mission unless it strongly relies on definitive texts (qat'iyat), and presents a sound jurisprudential vision that combines legal authenticity with disciplined practical realism, without pure rational interest being the version that abrogates established rulings.
