Is it really derived from the evidence? Let us analyze the most important examples they used to argue for public interest (maslaha) that is not explicitly mentioned in the texts:

They said: The compilation of the Qur'an during the time of Abu Bakr was not explicitly stated by the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), but the Companions understood that preserving the religion – as a necessity – required this compilation. Thus, the work was completed with the intention of fulfilling the overall objective derived from the texts.

The alleged inference: There is no text on this, so they did it for the sake of public interest (maslaha).

The response and investigation:

First: Compilation does not mean legislating a new legal ruling, but rather it is a matter of preserving a revelation that has been established through continuous transmission (tawatur), meaning it is not a new narration or an assertion based on opinion, but rather a preservation and documentation of what has been established through continuous transmission from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him). "The compilation of the Qur'an was not a writing by the memorizers, but rather it was a gathering of the pages that were written in the presence of the Messenger, and comparing what the memorizers had of the Qur'an against them, and placing them in one location."

Secondly: A general text has been reported urging the preservation of the religion, such as: ﴾Indeed, We sent down the Reminder, and indeed, We will preserve it﴿. To analyze this from a jurisprudential (usuli) perspective, we say: The text and its implication: the implication of entailment, inclusion, and necessity:

The verse indicates that the preservation of the Scripture is a definitive divine commitment, and the divine address comes in a form of exclusivity and emphasis: "Indeed, We sent down the Scripture" is a self-affirmation of the revelation, "and indeed, We will preserve it" is a verbal and meaningful affirmation of preservation. This indication encompasses: the preservation of the wording and structure (letters, words), and the preservation of the meaning (meanings, commands, beliefs). The continuity of transmission and the guarantee against distortion are of such importance that the affirmation and continuity include the possible means of preservation, including documentation, inferred from the verbal indications of the active participle form and the lam of emphasis, and a regulated rational indication which is the principle that whatever is necessary for the fulfillment of an obligation is itself obligatory, based on the actual practice of the Companions. In this, there is a reference to what Allah opened the heart of Abu Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) to after he contemplated the matter, with his eagerness not to contradict or innovate!

But this preservation required a visible human action over time, because Allah, Exalted is He, implements His laws through the actions of His servants. Thus, the actions of the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) were part of the implications of this divine commitment, not a human initiative or a rational interest. This is exactly like the action of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) in writing on papers and parchments.

This rule is derived from the concept of command in the Book and the Sunnah. Since the preservation of the Qur'an is a religious obligation that Allah has commanded the entire community, and the danger of losing the Qur'an is present with the death of the memorizers, its preservation can only be achieved by compiling it in writing. Therefore, compiling the Qur'an is a religious obligation because the preservation of it depends on it.
