Political Recommendation
Emphasis must be placed on the “power of rejection” (i.e., boycott)—an effective strategy that compels regimes to concede, as seen in campaigns to boycott products that support Israel. This approach proves far more successful than participation, which is invariably marred by concessions.
This analysis thus shows that participation in secular systems of governance is not “the lesser of two evils.” Rather, it is a recurring evil—one that legitimizes disbelief and implicates the Muslim in the sin of legislating against divine law. And God knows best.
Contemporary Deviant Applications
1. Participation of Islamic Movements in Democratic Governance Claim: Participation prevents the dominance of secularists.
Response: Such participation constitutes a legislative mandate; mere entry into the system affirms and entrenches it—it is not a necessity.
Deconstructing the “Penetration” Argument:
The democratic system is built on “institutional equilibrium,” meaning that participation within it signals recognition of its legitimacy, not resistance to it. According to David Easton’s theory of Institutional Legitimacy, “the participant in a system becomes a guardian of it, not a challenger.”
Contemporary Example: Tunisia (2011–2021)
Participation of Ḥarakat al-Nahḍah (The Ennahda Movement) in parliament did not prevent the passage of laws violating the Sharīʿah—such as equal inheritance and legalizing adoption by homosexuals.
Outcome: The movement transformed from a “reformist movement” into a “supporter of the status quo,” according to the International Institute for Strategic Studies (IISS, 2022).
New Jurisprudential Argument:
Participation entails swearing allegiance to a secular constitution, which signifies a shift in loyalty from divine law to man-made law. “And do not obey one whose heart We have made heedless of Our remembrance and who follows his own desires.” (Sūrat al-Kahf, 18:28)

Analysis of the Modern Social Contract:
In a democratic system, voting is not merely the “selection of an individual,” but a delegation of authority to form the 'General Will’, as per Rousseau. This renders the voter a co-author of every law passed by parliament during its term.
