Examples: The claim that the prohibition of a Muslim woman marrying a non-Muslim is specific to the environment of the Arabian Peninsula, source: Khaled Abou El Fadl, "Fiqh and Minorities" (2003, p. 77). Justification: "The verse was revealed concerning the polytheists of the Arabs, and does not apply to the West."
Critique: The reason for the prohibition is disbelief (kufr) ﴾And do not marry polytheistic women until they believe﴿ (Al-Baqarah: 221), which exists in every era.
Consensus on the generality of the prohibition (Ibn Hazm, Al-Muhalla: 7/97).
Example: Restricting women's hijab to the time of "fitna" (trial): source: Muhammad Shahrour, "The Book and the Qur'an" (1990, p. 500). Justification: "The verse on hijab was revealed to protect the wives of the Prophet specifically!"
Critique: The verse is general: ﴾And tell the believing women﴿ (An-Nur: 31).
Consensus on its obligation (An-Nawawi, Al-Majmu: 3/169).
Mechanism 5: Replacing Western terms with Sharia concepts:
The correct approach: Sharia terms have specific connotations (such as "hardship", "necessity").
Deviation in the jurisprudence of balances: Translating Western terms ("integration", "human rights") into fatwas!
Examples: Transforming "personal freedom" into a justification for permitting unveiling: source: Amina Wadud, "The Qur'an and Women" (1999, p. 63). Justification: "Hijab restricts women's freedom in the West."
Critique: Servitude to Allah takes precedence over "Western freedom" ﴾Indeed, the believers are those who have believed in Allah and His Messenger﴿ (Al-Hadid: 4).
Consensus that revealing one's nakedness is prohibited (Ibn Abd al-Barr, At-Tamhid: 23/331).
Example: Replacing "Sharia justice" with "Western equality" in inheritance: source: The National Council for Fatwa in Australia (2018). Justification: "Aligning inheritance laws with the principle of equality in the West."
Critique: The distribution of inheritance is fixed by text ﴾There is a share for men what is equal to that of two women﴿ (An-Nisa: 11).
Absolute "equality" contradicts the wisdom of Sharia (Al-Tahir Ibn Ashour, At-Tahrir wa al-Tanwir: 4/250).
These mechanisms demonstrate the transformation of "jurisprudence of balances" from a legitimate ijtihad tool into a method of undermining constants, through the hijacking of Sharia terms (such as maslaha, necessity) and emptying them of their foundational meanings. The remedy lies in restoring the foundational controls of ijtihad and distinguishing between ijtihad in branches and steadfastness in fundamentals.
Jurisprudence of reality:
As for "jurisprudence of reality," it should be a conscious understanding of reality to apply rulings to it. However, for some, it has become an excuse to dilute rulings under the pretext of keeping up with the complexities of the age. How often have we heard the saying: "The fatwas of the predecessors do not fit our reality."
