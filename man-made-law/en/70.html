First: The connection between public interest (maslaha) and revelation:

After the Imam al-Nabhani presents a number of criteria that the Malikis relied upon in their attempt to connect public interest with revelation, which are:

That it does not contradict an established legal text (definitive or speculative):

This means that the interest is not acted upon if it conflicts with the Qur'an, Sunnah, or consensus.

This condition is used to prove that they do not prioritize public interest over the text.

That the interest is legally recognized:

That is, it must be consistent with the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), such as preserving the five necessities: (religion, life, intellect, lineage, and wealth).

That it is not based on the whims or mere desires of the souls:

They require that the interest be free from inclination and passion, and that it does not stem from personal desires.

That the interest is general or collective, not partial or personal:

That is, it should benefit the community as a whole, not a specific group or individual.

That it does not lead to a greater harm:

An interest is not considered an interest if its outcome results in greater harm, according to the standards of rational weighing.

That it is compatible with the objectives of the Sharia, not contradictory to them:

If the interest leads to the neglect of a legal objective (such as the necessities), it is not recognized.

That it is derived from a general understanding of the texts, not from a specific text:

This is the basis for calling it "unrestricted" (mursala), meaning it is detached from a specific proof, and is understood in light of the overall objectives.

Despite mentioning these criteria, al-Nabhani believes that the rational regulation of public interest does not prevent personal inclination from entering into ijtihad (juristic reasoning), nor does it substitute for explicit texts; therefore, he considers that these criteria are not sufficient to grant public interest the status of "legal proof," because:

Anything that is not indicated by the speech of the legislator is not considered a legal proof.

Regulating interests with these criteria does not remove them from being rational judgments that are not legal.

Al-Nabhani precisely clarifies that personal inclination enters from the estimation of the interest itself, as it ultimately returns to a human perception that may be influenced by the environment, emotion, or culture, which makes jurisprudence susceptible to politicization or uncontrolled modernization. He said: "The fact that it is considered an interest according to the estimation of reason, as a legal proof, is the point of danger; because it means making reason the legislator, not revelation."
