Sixth: Emphasizing the necessity of regulating the methodologies of ijtihad (jurisprudential reasoning) and thinking according to the Sharia, and clarifying the meaning of renewal (tajdid). If there is a lesson to be learned from this research, it is that contemporary scholars must adhere to a disciplined methodology in ijtihad: which means engaging in ijtihad within the principles of Sharia and not outside of them. It is essential to revive the jurisprudence of principled ijtihad based on the Qur'an and the Sunnah to effectively address modern issues through serious and constrained deduction, not through wishful thinking and the imposition of opinions. True renewal is not about abolishing established rulings, but rather about wisely applying them to new realities and highlighting their rulings and legal objectives (maqasid) if they exist. Whoever believes that renewal equates to facilitation by dropping obligations and permitting prohibitions has gone astray and led others astray. The renewal we need is to remove the dust of stagnation from our hearts so that we truly understand the intent of the Legislator, and then apply the Sharia as it is with the strength of faith and the insight of reason. It is high time for those engaged in Islamic thought to realize that empty means of reconciling with modernity have only led us to dependency and weakness. The latter part of this Ummah will not be rectified except by that which rectified its first part, as Imam Malik said; that is, by a sincere return to the Qur'an and the Sunnah, and their application in a rightly guided Caliphate based on the methodology of Prophethood.
