The directions: This trend often emerges implicitly in the literature and fatwas of many institutions and scholars working in the West who focus on "coexistence" and "realism"; it is rarely explicit in a fatwa, but it is a practiced reality and often tolerated in jurisprudence.

Finally, we will address one of the most important issues that generates discussions during parliamentary or presidential elections in the West, and we will elaborate on it as well, which is:

Tenth: Participation in Western elections and voting for the ruling secular parties:

The direction: "Al-Rifa'i" in the Path of Islam:

The fatwa: "It is permissible to vote and run for office in non-Islamic systems if it achieves a 'significant legal interest' such as alleviating injustice and supporting the truth."

The justification: The principle of "achieving legal interest" was used through participation.

The original principle in legislation is that sovereignty belongs to Allah, and participation in secular systems contradicts this principle.

The saying of Allah, Exalted is He: ﴾Indeed, judgment belongs only to Allah﴿ [Yusuf: 40], ﴾And whoever does not rule by what Allah has revealed - then it is those who are the disbelievers﴿ [Al-Ma'idah: 44].

The point of evidence: Judgment - meaning legislation and the establishment of laws - is a right that belongs solely to Allah, and no human shares in it, as the Quran states in the saying of Allah, Exalted is He: ﴾Indeed, judgment belongs only to Allah﴿ [Yusuf: 40], meaning that legislation is not a human function, but a divine prerogative that cannot be delegated or assigned to anyone.

As for in Western democratic systems, the constitutional principle that establishes the state grants the parliament - as a representative of the general will - the supreme authority in legislation, and considers the legitimacy of the state in ruling by secular laws to be derived from the people's delegation through elections. This means that when a voter participates, they legally and constitutionally authorize the candidate to legislate in their name and on their behalf, and to reflect their "will" as a citizen, whether these laws conform to the Sharia or contradict it. This is not merely political representation, but rather an explicit contract of authorization to legislate instead of Allah, meaning granting humans the authority to create what is lawful and unlawful outside of Allah's revelation.

This is precisely what the verse of condemnation indicates: ﴾They took their scholars and monks as lords besides Allah﴿ [At-Tawbah: 31], and the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) explained it in the hadith of Adi ibn Hatim - when he found the verse perplexing and said: "We did not worship them, O Messenger of Allah" - he (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "Did they not make lawful for you what was unlawful, and you made it lawful, and they made unlawful for you what was lawful, and you made it unlawful?" He said: Yes. He said: "Then that is your worship of them." (Narrated by al-Tirmidhi and graded as good).
