The Jurisprudence of Reality and Drowning in the Quagmire of Reality

The method of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) consists of three stages, the first of which is understanding the reality for which the legal ruling is sought. This part of ijtihad has no relation to transmitted texts. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) asked Ma'iz when he confessed before him to adultery: "Perhaps you kissed, perhaps you touched?" He continued to question him and verify until the reality of what he had done became clear in a way that allowed for the appropriate legal ruling to be applied to that reality. The reality of adultery necessitates the punishment of stoning for Ma'iz, whereas if the reality were merely touching or kissing, it would not lead him to stoning. Hence, the importance of accurately knowing the reality in order to apply the appropriate legal ruling.

The legal ruling is a judgment that is applied to realities, and each incident has its own specific legal ruling. From this, we see that the reality of the solar eclipse prayer differs from that of the funeral prayer. If someone intentionally performs two bowings in the funeral prayer, his prayer is invalidated, while if someone does not perform them intentionally in the solar eclipse prayer, his prayer is invalidated. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "Whoever among you sees a wrongdoing, let him change it with his hand; if he cannot, then with his tongue." Although the term wrongdoing here is mentioned in an indefinite form to include any wrongdoing, the wrongdoing of a ruler, if it does not reach blatant disbelief, does not entail the same ruling as it would for others. Therefore, it is not permissible to change it by hand unless blatant disbelief is evident. Thus, the reality of the wrongdoing in relation to its perpetrator differs, which makes the legal ruling in both cases different.

Therefore, it is essential to understand the reality, meaning to comprehend it and be aware of it from all aspects, and then to apply the legal ruling derived from the Qur'an and the Sunnah and what has been guided by the consensus of the Companions and analogy, so that the Muslim adheres to the legal ruling.

Judging things, as established by scholars, is a branch of understanding them. If a scholar does not accurately perceive what is in this reality, then his judgment on it will be somewhat deficient. Ibn al-Qayyim (may Allah have mercy on him) stated in "I'lam al-Muwaqqi'in" that a scholar must be knowledgeable about religion and knowledgeable about reality, and he used the term reality and then said he must know how to apply and relate one to the other. All of this has been explained in our method of ijtihad.
