These values that legislations seek to achieve and establish can be divided into foundational rules and practical applied values, the most prominent of which are:

First: A set of major foundational values upon which legislation cannot stand without, including:

Comprehensive justice is achieved through legislation that ensures equality in rights and duties, and through its organization. Comprehensive justice does not rely on absolute equality among individuals, but rather on equality regulated by abilities, responsibilities, and innate roles in life. Islamic legislation acknowledges the equality between men and women in shared rights and obligations, but it distinguishes between them concerning natural characteristics, functions, and abilities, assigning each according to their nature and role, in pursuit of justice rather than mere similarity.

Among the comprehensive principles of justice is the equal opportunity for all, and the prohibition of discrimination among individuals for any reason. The third principle is the balancing of punishment with the sin and the reward with the action.

Establishing rights and redressing wrongs is achieved through the protection of rights, the safeguarding of honor and life, and enabling the rightful owners to obtain their rights through clear and effective means. Thirdly, it involves prohibiting injustice in all its forms, whether material, moral, or institutional.

By preserving the eight necessities: religion, life, intellect, wealth, lineage (family), community security, state preservation, and the protection of human dignity, it is essential that the legislations are in harmony with the establishment and service of these objectives.

The reliance of legislations on a higher reference to achieve these values, and to regulate the concepts upon which these legislations are based, such as good and evil, beauty and ugliness, reward and punishment, so that they are not subject to negotiation or change, and so that they possess the motivation that makes a person's commitment to them intrinsic or voluntary, because their source or reference is acceptable to him and represents him.
