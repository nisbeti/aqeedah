A matter is imposed unless there is a legal proof for it in the Sharia, either by acceptance or rejection." After discussing the types of Sharia rulings in terms of how to know their ruling, he concluded by saying: "It follows that every benefit (maslaha) that is imagined in a situation surrounded (i.e., surrounded and encompassed by conflicting principles) by conflicting principles must be supported by the principles for its rejection or acceptance. As for the estimation of its occurrence being neglected, disregarded (from neglect), it is impossible to imagine."(20) Thus, Imam al-Ghazali denies that the Sharia is devoid of detailed proofs addressing any benefit; the Sharia has not overlooked anything, nor has it been negligent.

Ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) is defined as: "Exerting effort to achieve a preponderance of belief regarding some of the legal rulings in a manner that the mujtahid feels incapable of going beyond it" ((21)), through the extrapolation of the detailed proofs related to the issue for which the legal ruling is to be derived, from the general proofs (the Qur'an, the Sunnah, consensus, analogy).

It can be asserted that the Malikis clearly distinguished between rulings based on direct texts, that is, detailed proofs directly related to the issue (verses, hadiths, actions, approvals, consensus, etc.), and rulings based on the unregulated benefit (maslaha mursala) which is derived from the extrapolation of general proofs (objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), general or universal principles, and a collection of texts), because they did not find anything to support it from the detailed proofs. They said that the legislator has sent it, so it is not among the recognized or abrogated benefits (i.e., those that have been considered or abrogated by the detailed proofs).

First: The distinction between specific proof (detailed) and general proof among the Malikis:

The Malikis define "unregulated benefit (maslaha mursala)" as the benefit that does not have a specific proof (a direct text on its own), but is supported by general proofs (objectives of the Sharia, or general or universal principles, and a collection of texts).

Specific proof: is the direct text (detailed proofs) on the ruling (verse, hadith, consensus).

General proof: is the overall reasoning derived from the objectives of the Sharia and the entirety of its proofs and principles.

Imam al-Qarafi (d. 684 AH) says: "The unregulated benefit is that which is not supported by a specific principle in terms of consideration or abrogation, but is supported by the genus of the principles of the Sharia. For example, the compilation of the Qur'an does not have a specific text, but it falls under the general meaning of Allah's saying: ﴾You are the best nation brought forth for mankind﴿, and the saying of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), 'There should be neither harming nor reciprocating harm,' because the division of the Qur'an causes harm. (Anwar al-Burooq fi Anwa' al-Furuq, vol. 4, p. 29). This clarifies that the Malikis seek a "general framework" in which the unregulated benefit is placed, not a direct detailed proof.
