The Dangers of the Modern Approach to Using “Maslahah” (Benefit)
From what has been presented, it is clear that the modern trend in deploying maslahah—under its various labels such as “Fiqh of Reality,” “Maqāṣid” (Higher Objectives of the Sharīʿah), “Balancing of Interests,” or “Fiqh of Minorities”—entails grave risks if it is not strictly governed by sound Islamic knowledge and sincere religious scrupulousness (waraʿ). The most prominent of these risks include:
Undermining the Sanctity and Authority of the Texts: When it becomes commonplace to prioritize a perceived rational interest over the apparent meaning of the scriptural texts, a dangerous notion takes root—that the commands of the Qur’an and Sunnah are “secondary” and can be bypassed. This gradually erodes reverence for the Sharīʿah in people’s hearts and makes human desire a hidden partner in the processes of declaring things lawful or unlawful, even if such people do not explicitly state this.
Opening the Door to False Interpretation: Under the pretext of pursuing maqāṣid, one may come along and claim that every definitive text was only revealed for a temporary benefit which has since expired. This has already occurred. There are contemporary writers who openly claim that the rulings of inheritance, criminal punishments (ḥudūd), and polygamy were appropriate for 7th-century Arabian society, but today they conflict with “human rights” and must therefore be “updated.” They falsely argue that the objectives of Sharīʿah, such as justice and equality, require such changes (see: middle-east-online.com). We seek refuge in God from such ruin. Jurisprudential Incoherence and Contradiction:
Those who follow imagined interests are inevitably inconsistent, for they lack any stable standard. They may expand a legal dispensation here while narrowing it there based on nothing more than speculation. In contrast, one who follows the revealed texts remains consistent and grounded. The glaring divergences among contemporary theorists of maqāṣid on fundamental legal questions stem from their abandonment of a firm reference point. Their intellects and whims have diverged, leading to erratic outcomes.
Dilution of Muslim Identity and Assimilation into Western Civilization: Many of the fatwas stemming from the so-called “Fiqh of Minorities” and “Fiqh of Balancing” have facilitated Muslims’ assimilation into the Western model—even when it contravenes core tenets of Islam. Due to these fatwas, many Western Muslims today feel no shame if their wife works unveiled in public, or if they tolerate their children engaging in unlawful relationships, or even if they drink alcohol “in moderation” all under the pretext of “integration” and “benefit”!
