The dangers of the modern approach to using public interest (maslaha)
It is clear from the above that the modern trend in employing public interest—under various names: the jurisprudence of reality, objectives (maqasid), balances, minorities—entails numerous dangers if it is not regulated by authentic religious knowledge and piety. The most prominent of these dangers are:
The undermining of the sanctity and authority of the texts: When it becomes customary to prioritize the interest perceived by reason over the apparent text, it becomes ingrained in minds that the commands of the Qur'an and the Sunnah are "secondary" and can be overlooked. This weakens the reverence for the Sharia in hearts and allows whims to become partners in what is permissible and impermissible, even if they do not explicitly state this.
Opening the door to false interpretation: Under the pretext of objectives, someone may interpret every definitive text as having been intended for a temporary interest that has now ceased! This has indeed occurred: there are contemporary writers who openly declare that the rulings on inheritance, punishments, and polygamy were all suitable for the society of the seventh century CE, but today they contradict "human rights," so they must be updated! They falsely argue that the objectives of the Sharia, such as justice and equality, necessitate what they do. We seek refuge in Allah from failure.
Contradiction and juristic confusion: Those who follow imagined interests will inevitably contradict themselves because they have no fixed standard. They may broaden a concession here and then restrict it there with no basis other than conjecture. Meanwhile, those who follow the text remain consistent and stable. The severe differences we see among contemporary theorists of "objectives" on fundamental issues stem from their loss of a solid reference and the divergence of their minds and whims.
Diluting the identity of Muslims and merging into Western civilization: Many fatwas on minority jurisprudence and balances have facilitated Muslims' engagement with the Western model, even in ways that contradict their religion. For example, thanks to those fatwas, many Muslims in the West see no issue with their wives being unveiled, or with their children engaging in forbidden relationships, or with drinking alcohol "with caution"! Under the pretext of integration and interest! This is a dangerous deviation from Islamic morals and a concession of one principle after another, until Islam remains merely a name without substance.
