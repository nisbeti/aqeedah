It is not permissible in any case to find solutions in Islam that align with what the capitalist system says; how could it be, when the capitalist system itself has drowned in cycles of failure and catastrophic results for society and the general public!
Of course, these solutions cannot exist for the evident contradiction between the capitalist system and Islam, for Islam does not call any ruling a ruling of Islam unless it is derived from revelation. Thus, even if a ruling is brought that aligns with what is found outside of Islam and is not derived by the jurist from the Book and the Sunnah and what they have guided to, it would not be a legal ruling nor would it be called Islamic.
These distorted concepts mean that adhering to them is equivalent to abandoning Islam and following the capitalist system, because Islam contradicts the capitalist system and it is impossible to find reconciliation or harmony between them. Therefore, any call for reconciliation or harmony between Islam and disbelief is a call to adopt disbelief and abandon Islam. This poses a significant danger to Muslims and to Islam.
The jurists of today are judging Islamic law!
All praise is due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon the Messenger of Allah.
There is no doubt that it has become firmly established in your mind that the mentality of many contemporary jurists, and those who deal with balances and minorities, hides under the umbrella of flashy slogans that some Muslims may find appealing, such as the slogan of renewal, opening the door to ijtihad (independent reasoning), the spirit of the Sharia (Islamic law) and its objectives (maqasid al-sharia), achieving interests (masalih), bringing them about and repelling harms, and the necessities permit the prohibited, and choosing the lesser of two evils. However, when you look into the substance and the methodology used in applying these truly important principles and issues, the matter is entirely different; it does not align with the raised slogan, nor does it adhere to the foundational principles that these matters are based upon.
Then, the matter develops for some of them to raise destructive tools, such as development, flexibility, modernization, and not being rigid regarding the literal text, as well as the jurisprudence of reality, balances, rational interests, and so on. Under these slogans, Islam is subjected to a fierce campaign to transform it from a religion whose foundation is revelation, and its most prominent characteristic that makes it unique is that it is divine, godly, into what they attempt to portray as a collection of opinions brought forth by jurists who have long since passed, and whose jurisprudence no longer suits humanity in the age of space.
The legal rulings (ahkam shar'iyya) are subjected to scrutiny by those who promote these calls, aiming to dismantle any connection between Muslims and the texts, so that Muslims may venture into a vast space, where they coexist according to frameworks that align with the Western way of life.
Indeed, issues arise and emerge, and the Islamic Ummah is in need of deriving legal rulings related to these new developments from the Book and the Sunnah. There is a significant difference between this and the judgment of practical legal rulings. What is truly sorrowful is that these individuals have not limited themselves to judging, rejecting, and distorting the jurists' ijtihadat (independent legal reasoning), but some of them have gone beyond that to judge the hadiths and verses, rejecting and ignoring them to assert that they are not suitable.
