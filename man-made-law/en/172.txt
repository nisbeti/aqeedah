We conclude this chapter with a quotation from the words of scholars throughout the ages, affirming their consensus on the role of reason subordinate to the Sharia, and correcting those who prioritize it over the latter:
Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal said: "It is not to be criticized for one who follows the narrations and forbids analogy" (meaning the rejection of personal reasoning that does not rely on a narration).
Imam al-Shafi'i said: "Everything that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) legislated is obligatory for us to follow, and it is not permissible to oppose it," and he said: "Whoever deems something good has legislated."
Ibn Abd al-Barr said: "Whoever seeks his religion through reason has gone astray, and whoever seeks it through authentic transmission has succeeded." (meaning the legislations).
Al-Ghazali said in the Mustasfa: "There is nothing more perfect than what has been," indicating that the Sharia of Allah has reached the utmost perfection, and it is inconceivable for reason to conceive a better system than it.
The scholar Ibn Taymiyyah said: "Sound reason is in agreement with religion, and reason cannot independently grasp all benefits and harms; rather, it must be accompanied by prophetic clarification." We have previously refuted the existence of what is called sound reason.
His student Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyya said: "The Sharia is based on judgment and the interests of the servants… but not every interest is known to us; sometimes the judgment may be hidden, so we are obliged to submit."
These statements and many others collectively indicate the agreement that legislation is the right of Allah and His Messenger, and that reason is a tool for understanding this legislation, not a legislator. Even the Mu'tazila – who are the most famous for prioritizing reason, but in the realm of belief since they are not a jurisprudential school, rather they were followers of the jurisprudential schools – limited this to beliefs and some rational commendation and condemnation. However, they do not attribute the obligation of legal rulings and their prohibition except to Allah. We do not endorse their methodology in the realm of belief and rational commendation and condemnation, of course, but the point is that "the arbitration of reason in legislation without revelation" is a rare idea that no one among the recognized people of the Qibla has stated. If there are deviant opinions, they are rejected by the consensus of the rest. Today, it is promoted under modern slogans such as "human rights" and "renewal of jurisprudence" and the like, thus it is necessary to expose its falsehood as we have done.
In summary: Reason is a luminous essence created by Allah for humans to know their Lord, understand His Sharia, and cultivate their worldly affairs according to His methodology. It is a means, a servant, not a master. The Sharia holds a higher status than reason, as it does not burden it with what it cannot bear, and it relieves it from obligations that its limited nature is not qualified for, thus sparing it the toil of delving into the unseen and comprehensive legislation, and granting it ample space for reflection and contemplation in the dominion of Allah and for deriving applications of the texts. Whoever employs his reason in obedience to Allah has understood it and succeeded, and whoever worships his reason and desires has gone astray and failed. We ask Allah to inspire us with our guidance and protect us from the evil of ourselves.
