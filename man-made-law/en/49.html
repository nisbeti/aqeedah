The nature of public interest (maslaha mursala) and its criteria for consideration by the jurists

Definition of public interest and the difference between it and benefit:

The definition of "public interest" in language dictionaries:

In Lisan al-Arab (root: صلَح): "Public interest (salah) is the opposite of corruption. It is said: something has become good (salih) when it is in a state of goodness... and public interest is that which brings about goodness for people and benefit." In Al-Qamus Al-Muhit (al-Firuzabadi): "The good (salih) is the opposite of the corrupt. Public interest is that which improves a person's condition or organizes their affairs." In Taj al-Arous (al-Zabidi): "Public interest is that which contains goodness, or that which leads to goodness, or that which brings benefit to people." In Al-Mu'jam Al-Wasith: "Public interest is that which returns goodness or benefit or safety to people or individuals. Its plural is مصالح."

As for the relationship between public interest and benefit, the scholars of usul (principles of jurisprudence) have expressed this relationship by saying: "Public interest is that which is accompanied by goodness in its outcomes and means, whereas benefit may be based on desire, passion, or exploitation." For example, usury (riba) has immediate financial benefit, but it is not a public interest, as it leads to corruption, and thus Islam has strongly opposed it. There is a clear text prohibiting it: ﴾Allah has permitted trade and forbidden usury﴿, and Allah has described it as one of the works of Satan.

Indeed, we find in contemporary reality that many modern economic systems have justified usury in the name of reason and public interest, considering it a foundation for development and financing, establishing banks and institutions based on it, formulating international laws for it, and encouraging people to borrow based on it. Many minds have found no fault in it; nevertheless, the Sharia has ruled it as a great corruption, indicating that the assessment of something as a public interest does not rely on pure reason but on the measure of Sharia.

Another example: gambling and sports betting are justified by many as a means of entertainment and profit, and in some societies, they are viewed as tools for stimulating the economy (especially in the fields of games and sports). However, in the measure of Sharia, they are a great corruption, as the Sharia has described them as abominations. Therefore, Allah said: ﴾Indeed, intoxicants and gambling... are an abomination of the work of Satan﴿.

The sale and marketing of pornography is considered by some as "personal freedom" or an "economic interest" based on supply and demand, and laws are even established to protect it in some countries. However, the Sharia criminalizes it severely, as it falls under the statement of Allah: ﴾Indeed, those who love to see immorality spread among those who have believed will have a painful punishment﴿, which makes the claimed "public interest" here nothing but a great corruption.

Profit from monopolies may be seen by the merchant as an economic interest, as it yields a surplus in profits, but in the measure of Sharia, it is a crime that leads to general harm to the poor and consumers, disrupting the balance in the market. Therefore, the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said, "Whoever engages in hoarding is a wrongdoer."
