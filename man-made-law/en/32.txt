Contradictory ideologies stem from ignorance of human nature:
Capitalism treats man as a pleasure-seeking consumer.
Communism suppresses individual spirit and freedom.
Man is a complex being knowable only to his Creator: He consists of body, mind, soul, and instincts, all requiring balance and discipline.
Only Islam comprehensively understands this complexity:
Balances lawful pleasure with spiritual purification. Links labor and wealth with charity and solidarity.
Encourages worship while affirming dynamic social life.
True happiness lies in loving God and following His law: Fulfillment arises from closeness to the Creator, not material benefit. Divine law must not be judged by reason: Since reason is ignorant of man's true purpose, it cannot evaluate revelation; it must submit to it.
Details:
Human reason is most deficient in its understanding of man’s nature and existential purpose. No matter how brilliant or experienced, the intellect alone cannot answer life’s ultimate questions: Was man created solely for physical enjoyment? Or does he possess a higher calling that transcends worldly existence?
These unresolved philosophical questions led to conflicting ideologies:
Capitalism defined man as a profit-seeking, pleasure-maximizing individual, fueling selfishness, anxiety, and social breakdown.
Communism, in reaction, suppressed
individuality, abolished private property, and stifled faith, leading to spiritual emptiness and state oppression.
Even Western legal schools differ:
Legal Positivism disconnects law from morality, anchoring it in political authority regardless of ethics.
Natural Law Theory ties law to universal moral principles.
Critical Legal Studies rejects fixed values and views law as ideological. Sociological Jurisprudence adopts pragmatism and considers ethical values only if they support social cohesion.
