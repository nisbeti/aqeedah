The later Malikis, such as al-Qarafi, al-Amidi, and al-Shatibi(9). "Al-Shatibi was one of the most ardent defenders of public interest (maslaha) and considered it an extension of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), but he nonetheless sought to regulate it with strict conditions so that it would not become a pretext for personal desires or independent rational legislation."(10)

However, the reality is that this door has gradually transformed in the modern era into a gateway for launching a new way of thinking based on the estimation of "rational interest," meaning that which does not rely on studying the overall or detailed objectives of the legal proofs, nor on the legal texts. It has thus become an independent source of legislation, with interest ruling over the text, abrogating it, and being prioritized over it. In fact, it has been employed to undermine definitive texts, as is the case with those who claimed that usury (riba) is a contemporary economic interest, thus permitting it explicitly or indirectly. The proponents of this approach have freed themselves from the old constraints for considering public interest, "that it should not contradict a text, consensus, or analogy," and they have disregarded the texts entirely. Consequently, "interest" has become a tool for demolishing legal rulings based on clear and accepted evidence in Sharia!

The matter did not stop there; rather, vague principles—having no basis in the Sharia—were used to justify this deviation, such as their saying: "What is widespread is not denied," and "The rulings change with the change of time and place," and "Wherever there is interest, there is the law of God." These phrases have become a slogan for bypassing and diluting the Sharia, rather than for understanding it.

We are facing a phenomenon of the intellect overwhelming revelation, and the expansion of rationalist thought in undermining sectors of the Sharia under the pretext of development, or interest, or keeping pace with the times. This idea is an old one, finding its roots in periods of decline, and its manifestations are present in contemporary "rationalist" discourses. It has been and remains one of the most dangerous causes of intellectual and legal deviation, and it has found its way to become a mode of thinking for many (scholars and laypeople alike). In fact, it is one of the greatest obstacles to the revival of the Islamic nation.

This research will, with God's permission, prove that legislation is a pure right belonging solely to God, and that the role of the intellect is not to create or judge, but to understand revelation within the disciplined methodological framework of usul al-fiqh (principles of jurisprudence). We will also affirm that ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) in Islam is not a free intellectual act without constraints, but rather a disciplined extraction based on evidence, governed by the methodology of the principles of jurisprudence, and built upon the general rules of the Sharia, not on the whims of the mujtahid (jurist) or on imagined interests.

This is because the source of good and evil, reward and punishment is indeed revelation, and it is what will determine accountability on the Day of Judgment. If the legislator left a "legislative vacuum" for minds to fill, and the perspectives of those minds contradicted each other regarding those solutions, then by which of the solutions imagined by one mind or contradicted by another will Allah hold His servants accountable? If they oppose the opinion of one in favor of the opinion of another, which of these will be the object of God's pleasure, rewarding them for it, and which will be the object of His wrath, punishing them for it?
