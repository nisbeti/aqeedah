3. Killing a Group for the Murder of One Person The Incident: ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb (may Allah be pleased with him) ordered that a group of individuals who collectively murdered a single man be executed.
Claimed Justification: Some argue this violates the literal text, which mandates only the individual killer be punished. They thus frame it as an instance of maṣlaḥah mursalah, to: Deter collusion in murder,
Close the door to exploitative schemes where individuals conspire to share guilt to avoid retribution.
Al-Nabhani’s Analysis:
This ruling is not an extra-textual maṣlaḥah but rather an analogy (qiyās) based on group theft.
Al-Nabhani explains that ʿUmar based his ruling on the Qur’anic punishment for cooperative theft, where all involved thieves are subject to the same penalty: “As to the thief, male or female, cut off their hands…” (Qur’an 5:38)
The analogy here rests on the fact that the crime was perpetrated jointly. Even if only one hand carried out the act, all participants are culpable, just as in group theft.
Conclusion:This ruling is not an instance of maṣlaḥah mursalah, but rather a valid partial analogy (qiyās) based on an act explicitly covered by revealed text.
4. Pouring Out Adulterated Milk
The Incident:
ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) ordered that milk mixed with water for purposes of deception be discarded.
Claimed Justification:
Some argue this is a rationally-driven punitive measure not explicitly legislated in the texts—thus an example of maṣlaḥah mursalah—since it entails destruction of private property.
Al-Nabhani’s Rebuttal and Clarification: This act falls under taʿzīr (discretionary punishment), a recognized category in Islamic law.
Taʿzīr is delegated to the ruler’s discretion in all matters where no fixed ḥadd punishment is specified.
It includes a variety of penalties: rebuke, imprisonment, public shaming, physical punishment, or destruction of goods—depending on what the imam (ruler) deems effective for deterrence and justice.
Imām Mālek himself stated:
“Whoever causes harm to the Muslims, the imam must discipline him in whatever way he sees fit.”
Conclusion:The disposal of the milk was a legitimate disciplinary act under the domain of taʿzīr, not an instance of unrestricted, rationally-derived maṣlaḥah in the legal-theoretical sense. 5. Lashing for Alcohol Consumption Beyond the Prescribed Forty
