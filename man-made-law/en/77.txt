Not killing the group for the individual in some cases: This ruling is constrained by the consideration of general deterrence and closing the door to collusion in murder, based on the principles of retribution (qisas) and justice mentioned in the Qur'an.
The incident: Umar ibn al-Khattab (may Allah be pleased with him) ordered the killing of a group who killed a single man.
The alleged reasoning: This is a public interest that goes beyond the text because the text indicates the killing of the murderer only.
The response and investigation:
This issue is based on analogy to collective theft.
Al-Nabhani said: Umar analogized it to the killing of multiple thieves if they participated in the theft, so they are all to be cut off, as in the verse: ﴾As for the thief, the male and the female, amputate their hands﴿ [al-Ma'idah: 38].
The analogy here is based on the fact that the crime occurred through cooperation and participation, just as those who cooperate in the crime of theft are punished even though one hand may have executed the act.
In summary: This is not among the public interests, but rather a partial analogy to an act that has a text.
Spilling adulterated milk
The incident: Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) ordered the spilling of the milk that its owner mixed with water as a fraud.
The alleged reasoning: This is an unmentioned public interest, which is a penalty for the destruction of property.
The response and investigation:
This ruling falls under the category of ta'zir (discretionary punishment), and ta'zir in Islam is entrusted to the imam in all matters that do not have a fixed limit.
Ta'zir includes various types of punishments, such as: reprimand, imprisonment, destruction, and flogging... as the imam sees fit to deter and appropriate for the violation.
Imam Malik said: "Whoever causes harm to the Muslims, the imam must punish him as he sees fit."
In summary: Spilling the milk is a discretionary punishment within the authority of the imam, not based on "public interest" in its usuli meaning.
Flogging for drinking alcohol beyond forty:
The incident: Ali ibn Abi Talib and other companions flogged for drinking alcohol eighty lashes.
The alleged reasoning: They exceeded the limit mentioned by the Prophet (forty), indicating "legislation by public interest."
The response and investigation:
Muslim narrated from Ali ibn Abi Talib that he said: "The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) flogged for alcohol forty, Abu Bakr forty, and Umar eighty, and each is a Sunnah, and this is more beloved to me."
