A comprehensive study on the foundation of the principle of "the lesser of two evils" or "the lighter of two harms":
Introductory foundation:
The principle of "the lesser of two evils" or "the lighter of two harms" is one of the principles mentioned by jurists, and it should be understood accurately within its established legal parameters, rather than being used in a loose manner to justify deviations and political participation in systems of disbelief, as some modern Islamic movements do. This study reviews the origin of the principle, the conditions for its application, the evidence for it, and examples from the works of the usul scholars such as al-Ghazali and Izz al-Din ibn Abd al-Salam, then it clarifies the distorted understanding of the principle in contemporary reality and responds to its misuse.
First: Definition of the principle and its legal scope
The principle: It means that if the obligated person has no choice but to commit one of the two prohibitions (i.e., cannot avoid both), then he commits the one that is less harmful.
Its origin is from the Qur'an:
﴾Allah does not burden a soul beyond that it can bear﴿
﴾So fear Allah as much as you are able﴿
Its original condition: That it is impossible to refrain from both prohibitions at the same time, and that the obligated person is inevitably falling into one of them.
Second: Legal controls of the principle
The impossibility of avoiding both harms: That is, the obligated person cannot avoid both prohibitions.
The determination of the lesser harm must be based on the rulings of Sharia, not on whims or abstract rational interests.
The obligated person must be unable to refrain from both except by incurring a greater harm.
Documenting the conditions for applying this principle(40):
First: Texts of the jurists regarding the condition
Hanafi: Ibn Nujaym al-Hanafi in "al-Ashbah wa al-Nazair" (p. 96): "Principle: If two harms conflict, the greater harm is considered by committing the lesser of them, and there is no way for him but to commit one of them."
