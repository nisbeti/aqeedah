Even these necessities are understood according to different perspectives; for example, Christians do not see wine as harmful to the intellect.

The difference between the legal cause (illah) and public interest (maslaha):

The cause: derived from the text, it has a form of justification.

The interest: a description of reality, which may or may not exist, and does not affect the ruling.

Example: Selling fresh dates for dried dates, the cause is "the decrease when drying" not "the financial harm."

The prohibition of following results in legal reasoning:

What is achieved from applying the ruling is not part of the ruling, nor is it a cause for it.

The application of the ruling may lead to an interest or not, but it does not enter into the structure of the ruling, nor its indication, nor its cause.

The intellectual conclusion according to al-Nabhani:

What he affirms:

The Sharia has brought about commendable results (bringing benefits and preventing harms).

The legal cause is what is stated by the Sharia or indicated by evidence that implies justification.

Understanding the texts must follow the legislative meaning, not the tangible interest.

What he denies:

That the interest (in its rational or real sense) is a legal cause, meaning a motivator for legislation.

That rulings be based on an interest derived from a text that does not indicate it.

That the results are part of the structure of the ruling or its indication or a cause for it.

Fifth: The criterion for justification is "the relevant influencing factor," not mere rational conjecture.

The legally accepted cause must be a clear, understandable, defined description that influences the ruling. The usul scholars have set precise conditions for the cause that prevent the intellect from uncontrolled expansion and guard against following whims. Al-Amidi said:

"The relevant factor is a clear, defined description that necessitates the arrangement of the ruling according to it, resulting in what is appropriate to be the objective of the legislation of the ruling." (Al-Masalih Al-Mursala, Mahmoud Faour), and Ali Al-Shashi said: "If we see a person giving a poor person a dirham, it is likely that the giving is to meet the need of the poor and to obtain the reward," which makes the description (meeting the need) relevant and influential.) (The previous reference).

Sixth: The intellect understands the texts, but does not create them.

The legislator establishes the causes of rulings that are indicated by the wording and opens the door for the intellect to reason within the framework of the text, not outside it. The intellect perceives the relevance when indicated by the text or understood from the context, not when it invents it on its own.
