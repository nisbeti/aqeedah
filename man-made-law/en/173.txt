Chapter Five: Modern Rationalist Schools from the Era of Abduh and al-Afghani to Today: A Methodical Critical Analysis
Executive Summary of Chapter Five: Modern Rationalist Schools from the Era of Abduh and al-Afghani to Today:
This chapter focuses on a historical and analytical study of the emergence and development of what is known as the "reformist rationalist school" in modern Islamic thought, which emerged in the late nineteenth century with figures such as Jamal al-Din al-Afghani, Muhammad Abduh, and Rashid Rida. The chapter aims to demonstrate that this school—despite its reformist slogans—was the intellectual precursor to empowering "reason" and "public interest (maslaha)" as governing sources over the legal text, rather than being subordinate to it, which led to radical changes in the structure of Islamic thought among large segments of intellectuals and thinkers later on.
The chapter opens with a presentation of the key ideas of Muhammad Abduh: the interpretation of texts, the focus on ethical and social objectives, and provocative fatwas claiming public interest, such as permitting bank interest, modifying marriage and divorce laws, and considering the conditions of endowments. It also discusses the influence of his teacher al-Afghani, who was more politically oriented but instilled intellectual courage and skepticism about heritage in his students.
The chapter then transitions to Rashid Rida, illustrating how he inherited and expanded Abduh's project, but leaned towards a form of Salafism in the later years of his life. It then traces how this school divided after him into two currents: one relatively conservative (such as Mahmoud Shaltut) and another progressive liberal secular (such as Ali Abd al-Raziq and Taha Hussein), which later expanded in the twentieth century with intellectual projects like "liberal Islam" and "civil Islam."
The chapter highlights the influence of the rationalist school on contemporary currents such as:
Yusuf al-Qaradawi and his fatwas based on public interest regarding usury, working in banks, congratulating on holidays, music, and others.
Ahmed Raissouni and his maqasid-based approach that elevates the status of public interest.
Shahrour, Jamal al-Banna, Tariq Ramadan, Jassim Sultan, and others among the advocates of contemporary readings, who sought to adapt texts to the logic of the age.
It also discusses the role this school played in re-reading Sharia in the spirit of the age, influenced by Western modernity, which opened the door wide to Western rationalism under the guise of "maqasid-based ijtihad," where the text was no longer governing over reality, but rather reality governed over the text.
The chapter concludes with a stern critique of this trajectory, affirming that what is called the "reformist rationalist school" has paved the way for a form of partial secularization of Sharia in the name of renewal, and has created a serious rift in the understanding of the Ummah of its revelation, necessitating a return to the established usul (principles) methodology that positions the text as the foundation and reason as its servant, not its ruler.
