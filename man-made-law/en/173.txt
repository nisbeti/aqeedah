Chapter Five: Modern Rationalist Schools from the Era of Abduh and Afghani to the Present — A Methodical Critical Analysis
Executive Summary of Chapter Five: Modern Rationalist Schools from the Era of Abduh and Afghani to the Present
This chapter offers a historical and analytical study of the emergence and development of what has become known as the “Reformist Rationalist School” in modern Islamic thought. This school began in the late 19th century with figures such as Jamal al-Din al-Afghani, Muhammad Abduh, and Rashid Rida. The chapter aims to demonstrate that—despite its reformist slogans, this school intellectually paved the way for elevating "reason" and "utility" as sovereign sources over the revealed text, rather than being subordinate to it. This shift led to a radical transformation in Islamic thought among wide sectors of intellectuals and later thinkers.
The chapter opens by presenting Muhammad Abduh’s major ideas: reinterpretation of scriptural texts, emphasis on moral and social objectives, and controversial fatwas in the name of utility (such as the permissibility of bank interest, modifications to marriage and divorce laws, and revisions to the conditions set by endowment donors). It also examines the influence of his teacher, al-Afghani, who was more focused on politics but instilled intellectual audacity and critical skepticism toward tradition in his students.
The chapter then explores Rashid Rida’s role as inheritor of Abduh’s project and how he expanded it—before leaning toward a limited form of Salafism later in life. It traces how this school then split into two currents: a relatively conservative one (e.g., Mahmud Shaltut) and another progressive, liberal, and secularist (e.g., ʿAli ʿAbd al-Raziq and Ṭāhā Ḥusayn). The school later expanded through the 20th century with intellectual programs such as “Liberal Islam” and “Civil Islam.”
The chapter highlights this school’s influence on contemporary currents, including:
Yusuf al-Qaradawi, with his utilitarian fatwas on riba (usury), working in conventional banks, festive greetings, music, etc.
Ahmed al-Raysuni, with his maqāṣid-based methodology that places high weight on maslaha (interest/utility).
Thinkers like Muhammad Shahrour, Jamal al-Banna, Tariq Ramadan, and Jasim Sultan, who all promote “contemporary readings” that seek to adapt revelation to modern paradigms.
The chapter also analyzes how this school played a key role in reinterpreting the Sharīʿah in light of modernity, under the influence of Western philosophical trends. This effectively opened the floodgates to Westernizing rationalism under the guise of "maqāṣid-based ijtihād", wherein the scriptural text no longer judges reality—instead, reality judges the text.
The chapter concludes with a strict critique of this trajectory, asserting that the so-called “Reformist Rationalist School” laid the groundwork for a partial secularization of the Sharīʿah under the name of renewal, producing a dangerous fracture in the ummah’s understanding of divine revelation.
