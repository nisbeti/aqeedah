The Jurisprudence of Balancing or Priorities, and the Jurisprudence of Reality: Between Methodological Discipline and Intellectual License
Executive Summary:
This chapter focuses on tracing the development of the jurisprudence of balancing and priorities, and clarifies that this concept, although its origin is found in the books of usul (principles of jurisprudence), its contemporary usage often strays from its foundations. The book demonstrates that contemporary jurisprudence of balancing, as promoted by some reformist movements, has deviated from the established methodological and usuli controls, and has been used to justify extensive concessions in creed and Sharia under the banner of "the lesser of two evils" or "preventing harm".
The chapter dismantles the epistemological foundations of this trend and presents examples of its contemporary applications where the utilitarian intellect prevails over the texts of Sharia, including: acceptance of participation in secular systems, legalizing the forbidden under the pretext of necessity, or prioritizing an imagined interest over a definitive text. It shows that this expansion in the jurisprudence of balancing has led to the dilution of the Sharia reference and the exclusion of jurisprudential controls in favor of political or utilitarian justifications.
The chapter concludes that a disciplined jurisprudence of balancing is part of Islamic jurisprudence, but its fundamental condition is not to exceed definitive texts or contradict the major objectives of Sharia (maqasid al-sharia). However, its usage as practiced in some circles is considered a deviation from the correct methodology, opening the door for the intellect to escape the controls of revelation and undermining the constants of Sharia in the name of "flexibility".
The main themes of the chapter:
Definition of Balancing and Its Controls in Usul: The book clarifies that balancing between interests and harms is not an alien methodology, but in the principles of jurisprudence, it is constrained by conditions:
That it does not contradict a definitive text,
That the interest is certain and not imagined,
That it does not open the door to neglecting Sharia.
Contemporary Application Deviations: The chapter presents several examples of using balancing to contradict texts:
Justifying work in secular systems under the title of "preventing the greater harm",
Participation in liberal elections under the pretext of "preventing secularists",
Fatwas allowing the abandonment of Sharia rulings in media or politics under the claim that "reality does not permit",
Alliances with entities hostile to Islam under the justification of "preventing imminent danger".
