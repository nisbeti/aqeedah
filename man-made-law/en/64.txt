Ibn Ashour (d. 1393 AH):
"The inference from the mursal (unattributed) is the derivation of the ruling from the general objectives of the Sharia, not from a specific evidence... It relies on the moral induction of the texts of revelation." (Maqasid al-Sharia al-Islamiyya, p. 125).
Al-Qarafi (d. 684 AH):
"The difference between qiyas (analogy) and mursal: qiyas has a specified origin, while mursal has an origin derived from a collection of evidences." (Nafa'is al-Usool, vol. 9, p. 4231).
Methodological Summary:
The Maliki distinction between the ruling with a direct text, that is, derived from detailed evidences, and the ruling based on mursal maslaha (public interest) is based on:
The mursal from the specific evidence: the absence of a direct text or detailed evidences on the specific issue.
Linking to the general evidence: searching for a purposive framework or a general principle that supports the type of interest.
Comprehensive induction: gathering scattered texts to derive the consideration of the interest.
Referring to the evidential system: it is not permissible to bypass the specific texts (i.e., detailed) or the established objectives of the Sharia.
Thus, the ruling derived from mursal maslaha remains connected to revelation through the bridge of comprehensive induction, not a mere opinion. As al-Shatibi said: "The Legislator did not leave any interest except that He clarified it, either in detail or in general." (Al-Muwafaqat, vol. 2, p. 290).
Conditions and Regulations of Mursal Maslaha According to Imam al-Shatibi (d. 790 AH) in "Al-Muwafaqat"
Imam al-Shatibi sets strict regulations for relying on mursal maslaha, preventing its transformation into a rational ruling or whim, and ensuring its connection to revelation. Here are the details:
The first regulation: inclusion under the major objectives of the Sharia
The interest must fall within one of the five objectives (preservation of religion, self, intellect, lineage, and wealth), or be related to them.
"Any interest that is not supported by the foundations of the Sharia in terms of consideration is not considered... Rather, it must be regulated under a general principle." [Al-Muwafaqat, vol. 2, p. 284], for example: prohibiting the circulation of drugs (preservation of intellect) – even if there is no explicit text regarding "heroin."
