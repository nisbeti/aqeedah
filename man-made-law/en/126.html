These two messages are not mentioned in the authentic hadith collections. The author of "Political Documents in the Prophetic Era" stated that he took these documents from the historical books of al-Tabari, al-Qalqashandi, Ibn Kathir, and others, and he did not mention that he took either of them from any of the hadith books. Historical books are not reliable, as they do not concern themselves with the authentication of hadith like the hadith books do; they gather their reports like a night gatherer who does not know whether he is gathering sticks or snakes. Therefore, these two messages have no value, especially since they contradict the hadith of Anas reported by Muslim, and the narration of Umm Salama regarding the Negus, and about the emigrants in Abyssinia, and the lack of mention of the emigrants to Abyssinia, the last of whom to return was Ja'far. There is no report that indicates that the Negus embraced Islam, even though Ja'far returned to the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) in the seventh year after the conquest of Khaybar, and after the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) sent letters to the kings and princes. Thus, these two messages are not authentic, and it is not appropriate to use them as evidence, so they are rejected. From all of this, it becomes clear that the Negus who embraced Islam and for whom the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) performed the funeral prayer is not the same Negus to whom the emigrants to Abyssinia migrated, nor is he the Negus to whom the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) sent a letter inviting him to Islam at the end of the sixth year of Hijra and the beginning of the seventh with Amr ibn Umayyah al-Dhamri, but rather he is the Negus who ascended to the throne of Abyssinia after the death of the Negus to whom the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) sent the letter inviting him to Islam.

This Negus who embraced Islam had assumed kingship in the seventh year, because the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) had sent his messengers to the kings and princes, including the Negus, after his return from the Battle of Hudaybiyyah, which was at the end of the sixth year of Hijra in the month of Dhul-Qi'dah. This Negus must have died in the seventh year, during which the Negus who embraced Islam, for whom the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) performed the funeral prayer, took over, and his death was before the conquest of Mecca in the eighth year of Hijra, as mentioned by al-Bayhaqi in "Dalail al-Nubuwwah."

Thus, the period between his ascension to kingship and his embracing Islam, and his death was a short period during which he embraced Islam secretly, and no one knew of his Islam, not even the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him). The Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) was informed through revelation of his death and Islam on the day of his death, as indicated by the wording of the six hadiths in Sahih al-Bukhari that were narrated about his death. The short time he spent as a Muslim before he died did not allow him to learn the rulings of Islam. The Prophet's (peace and blessings be upon him) lack of knowledge of this did not prevent him from sending him what he was required to do.

Thus, this cannot serve as evidence for anyone who permits himself to participate in the rule of disbelief that governs by other than what Allah has revealed, and his claim in this regard is invalidated.
