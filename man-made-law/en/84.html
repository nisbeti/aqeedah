The difference between the legal "suitable" (munasib) and the claim of modern "rational interest" (maslaha 'aqliya)

Cheryl Bernard wrote for the Rand Corporation, which is supported by the U.S. Department of State, in her book: Civil Democratic Islam: ((29)) "There are other solutions as well, as modernity applies the principle of interest, or the public good, which dates back to the reformist Muslim jurists of the thirteenth century, as something believed to be able to transcend or even abrogate the Qur'an itself."

After this account of the concept of maslaha mursala in the inherited usuli perspective, we move to the second part of this chapter: comparing the regulated legal interest with what some contemporaries see as interest through their abstract minds, and clarifying that there is a vast gulf between them.

The legally effective suitable description (mislaha mu'tabara)

While some usuli scholars were researching the paths of causation in qiyas (analogy), they invented the concept of "suitable" to refer to the description that the legislator makes a cause for the ruling because it achieves a desired interest or prevents a harm. "We find among many usuli scholars the view that the suitability between the ruling and the description is a cause, and this was the entry point for the discussion of maslaha mursala, and the research on suitability, causation, and its paths, or the indicators of it from the research of qiyas. Al-Qarafi(30) said: 'The indicators of causation are eight: text, implication, suitability, analogy, rotation, examination, exclusion, and refining the basis.' Al-Razi mentioned in Al-Mahsool that they are ten with the addition of consensus and division. Many consider suitability to be one of the indicators of causation. So what is suitability? Or: what is suitable?"(31) Imam Ibn al-Hajib said: "The suitable is a clear, defined description that is rationally derived from the arrangement of the ruling upon it, which is appropriate to be intended for achieving an interest or preventing a harm." The condition is that the description be clear and defined (not hidden or variable) and that the intellect perceives that linking the ruling to it achieves an interest recognized by the Sharia or a harm that the Sharia dislikes.
