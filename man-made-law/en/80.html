An applied example:

The term "sale" in Islamic law encompasses all forms of exchange, whether it is traditional sale, electronic sale, or barter, as long as the contractual element (transfer of ownership in exchange for compensation) is present.

General rules and legal definitions

A. Definition:

General rules (such as: harm must be removed, certainty is not overruled by doubt) are not proofs in themselves, but rather general legal rulings derived from detailed evidences, upon which similar analogies are based.

B. The ijtihad function:

They are used to regulate rulings in the absence of texts, or to codify subsidiary applications.

They are utilized in general jurisprudence as an interpretive and organizational tool.

C. An applied example:

The rule "the means to the unlawful is unlawful" leads to rulings prohibiting the sale of grapes to someone who will turn them into wine, or blocking pornographic sites despite the absence of a direct text.

Analogy and rationale: the controlled expansion of rulings

A. The pillars of analogy:

The original: it has a stipulated ruling.

The branch: it is not explicitly stated.

The rationale: a common attribute between the original and the branch.

The ruling in the original.

B. Conditions of the rationale:

The rationale has eight conditions:

It must be a genuine cause for the ruling

Not merely a descriptive or indicative attribute, but it indicates the reason for legislation, meaning it is the motivation for which the ruling was established.

It must be a clear, defined, and appropriate description

Clear: meaning not hidden.

Defined: it can be identified and controlled.

Appropriate: meaning it conveys the reasoning behind the ruling, not an incidental description that does not indicate causality.

It must be influential in the ruling.
