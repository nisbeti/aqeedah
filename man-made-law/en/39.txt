“The lawful is what Allah has made lawful in His Book, and the unlawful is what He has forbidden in His Book. And what He has remained silent about is part of His pardon.”
And the narration of Abū al-Dardāʾ: “Whatever Allah made lawful in His Book is lawful, and whatever He forbade is unlawful. And whatever He was silent about is a pardon—so accept from Allah His pardon. For Allah did not forget anything.”Then he recited: “Your Lord is never forgetful.” (Maryam 19:64), Also reported by al-Bazzār and al-Bayhaqī via Abū Thaʿlabah: “Indeed Allah has laid down obligations, so do not neglect them; and He has set limits, so do not overstep them; and He has forbidden things, so do not violate them; and He has remained silent about things as a mercy for you—not out of forgetfulness—so do not seek after them.” These ḥadīths are aḥād reports, meaning they are solitary narrations and do not override definitive Qur’anic proofs. Moreover, these reports do not imply that Allah failed to legislate on certain matters. Rather, they indicate that there are things He did not prohibit—out of mercy—so He abstained from declaring them harām, not due to negligence or omission.
When they asked about ghee, cheese, and furs—these were permissible items and Allah did not prohibit them, as a mercy to the people, not because He forgot to rule on them. Hence, the subject of these ḥadīths is not silence regarding legislation, but rather refraining from prohibition.
Prophetic Silence Meant to Avert Undue Hardship In Fatḥ al-Bārī, it is reported from Muslim (also in al-Bukhārī, Kitāb al-ʿIlm), from Thābit on the authority of Anas:
“We were forbidden from asking the Messenger ﷺ too many questions. We loved it when a desert Arab—ignorant of social restraint—would come and ask, so we could listen.” And in the story of liʿān (mutual cursing) narrated by Ibn ʿUmar: “The Prophet ﷺ disliked excessive questioning and censured it.” Also from Muslim, via Nawwās ibn Samʿān: “I stayed in Madinah for a year, and the only thing that stopped me from making hijrah (full migration) was fear of being forbidden from asking the Prophet ﷺ questions.”
The meaning is clear: the Prophet ﷺ advised his Companions not to excessively inquire into issues for which no command or prohibition had been revealed—lest something becomes prohibited through their questioning. As in the authentic ḥadīth: “O people! Allah has made ḥajj obligatory upon you, so perform it.” A man asked, “Every year, O Messenger of Allah?” The Prophet ﷺ remained silent. He asked a second and third time, and the Prophet ﷺ replied: “Had I said ‘yes,’ it would have become obligatory—and you would not have been able to bear it.” Then he said: “Leave me as long as I leave you. Those before you were destroyed by their excessive questioning and disagreements with their prophets. So if I command you to do something, do as much of it as you can. And if I forbid you from something, then avoid it.”
