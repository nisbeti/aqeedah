Because the ignorant mind regarding the purposes of existence is not permitted to evaluate divine legislations according to its whims or what seems to it as interests, rather revelation must be accepted with devotion and submission.
Detail: One of the most severe shortcomings of the human intellect is its compounded ignorance of the nature of humanity itself and the purposes of its existence in life; thus, it is incapable of constructing a legislative system that achieves its happiness and the completeness of its humanity. The human mind, no matter how intelligent and experienced it becomes, cannot – independently, that is, on its own – comprehend whether humans were created merely to eat, drink, and enjoy? Or were they created for a higher wisdom and a deeper mission that transcends their momentary existence in this world? And is their happiness found in physical gratification? Or in the purification of the soul and achieving spiritual perfection by realizing their connection with the Creator? This conceptual confusion regarding the essence of humanity and its greater purpose has caused positive systems to oscillate between excess and deficiency, and these profound questions have not been answered by human thought in a definitive legal manner, leading to the emergence of contradictory legislative systems in their objectives, conflicting in their vision of humanity:
Some material philosophies – such as capitalism – have made humans into consumer beings, measuring their happiness by the extent of their physical pleasures and financial gains, leading to an inflation of individualism, anxiety, and frustration, while collective values collapsed and family ties disintegrated.
Meanwhile, communism emerged as its opposite, confiscating private property, stifling individual initiative, and persecuting every manifestation of religion, resulting in a suffocation of the spirit, economic collapse, state tyranny, and the exclusion of human nature.
Even Western legal schools have differed in their philosophical approach to building law; the positivist school (Legal Positivism) completely separates law from morality, considering law to be what is determined by political or legislative authority, regardless of its moral content, while the natural law school (Natural Law Theory) connects law to morality, deriving its legitimacy from higher rational and ethical principles such as justice, and considers its violation of natural moral laws to nullify it. The critical legal studies school (Critical Legal Studies) views morality as relative and sees law as an ideological tool, not believing in fixed values, while the sociological jurisprudence school (Sociological Jurisprudence) adopts a practical pragmatic perspective, considering ethical values to the extent that they serve social cohesion.
All of this resulted from ignorance of the complex reality of humanity: it is not merely a body, nor a separate spirit, but an integrated entity of mind, body, spirit, and soul, with mentality and psychology, inclinations and desires, and vital energy, whose condition can only be rectified if each aspect is given what it needs in terms of education, purification, and disciplined fulfillment. Achieving happiness in both this world and the Hereafter together. This understanding cannot be attained by the mind alone; rather, it must come from the Creator of humanity itself, who said: ﴾Does He not know who created, while He is the Subtle, the Acquainted?﴿ [al-Mulk: 14].
