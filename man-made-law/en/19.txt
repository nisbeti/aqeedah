The methodology for reaching the correct creed separates freedom from its existential dimension and dismantles the system of: (reason/evidence that fills the corners of the universe/the intellectual process that examines it), -which will necessarily lead to the truth of the Islamic creed-, and this dismantling can only occur by disabling this system and encroaching upon it, which is rejected as it will inevitably lead to separating the empowerment of research without coercion from its existential dimension, and its obligatory rational methodology that requires following the evidence and submitting to it, which is a matter of great danger.
Islamic law (sharia) guarantees freedom of belief before one even believes in Islam itself; however, once the argument is conveyed, it obliges one to adhere to the truth that has been clarified, and it does not permit the use of freedom of belief as a pretext to undermine or retreat from the truth, because when the truth is known through evidence, it becomes obligatory to adhere to it, not to deny it. From this standpoint, the sharia guarantees non-Muslims in the abode of Islam the freedom to remain on their beliefs, without acknowledging their correctness, and it prohibits coercion to enter Islam, but at the same time, it prevents serious intellectual inquiry from being trivialized in a way that undermines respect for reason and thought. It is only fitting that these fundamental ideas should be the product of thought, scrutiny, and proof, and whatever results from that should not be retracted after confirming it; thus, apostasy from Islam is not accepted.

From here, this chapter came to clarify that legislation cannot be correct, valid, or obligatory unless it meets essential conditions in its source and content, the most important of which are:
It must be based on a precise understanding of the reality of human beings, including their needs, instincts, and existential purposes, not on an incomplete conception of them as materialistic or positivist philosophies do.
It must be issued by a knowledgeable, wise, and just authority, not by a biased, limited, and fluctuating human intellect.
It must be stable in its major objectives, achieving specific societal goals, with its systems harmonizing and integrating in achieving them, and not contradicting each other to the point of undermining one another.
