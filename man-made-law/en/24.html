Fifth: The necessity of obligation and the supreme authority

There must be a supreme entity with authority that imposes legislation and obliges adherence to it (either through personal conviction or executive power).

Divine legislation grants legislation a character of self-obligation: because the individual believes he is tasked with a divine command that is not open to debate.

Modern societies, even if they claim freedom, impose strict laws on people regarding taxes, trade, banking, identity, and security... which demonstrates that obligation is a societal necessity.

The second axis: Legislation must be correct, as errors in it lead to grave evils and widespread harm:

Legislation, in its essence, and by virtue of its nature and function, is not a circumstantial legal activity, nor a bundle of social guidelines or viewpoints that can be experimented with or modified according to political, cultural, or historical circumstances; rather, it is a comprehensive, integrated system intended to regulate human behavior, define one's stance towards actions, organize relationships with other individuals, state institutions, and society, and regulate the systems of public and private life and various state systems. Based on this, affairs will be managed, public interests will be determined, and societal problems will be resolved. Thus, legislation transcends being mere temporary or customary laws to become a governing discourse over actions, relationships, and systems, establishing obligations, defining rights, regulating duties, organizing the management of affairs, and establishing order, justice, security, and mercy.

Legislation is not a technical matter that can be compensated for later; it should not be a field for human experimentation, nor should it be entrusted to a relative and variable source like human reason.
