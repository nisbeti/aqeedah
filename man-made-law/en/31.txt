Sociality and political affiliation through intellectual upbringing. This structural submission to personal desires and interests, changing agendas, emotions, and sometimes to selfishness, instincts, and passions, along with other tendencies, renders the human mind unqualified to establish just and comprehensive legislation free from bias.
The absence of desire and inclination is impossible at the collective level just as it is at the individual level. The impossibility at the individual level arises because a person cannot detach from their feelings and inclinations, as they are part of their being. The gathering of individuals does not remove desires and inclinations; rather, it amplifies them, necessitating a process of reconciliation among the multiple desires. Hence, legislation based on "the will of the public" or collective will cannot be free from desire and inclination, and thus the first and most important pillar for achieving the purpose of legislation (justice) is completely absent.
A person may believe they are ruling by their intellect, while this "intellect" is governed by what has been ingrained in it from acquired interests, a specific ideological background, a nationalistic or class-based inclination, or even by momentary utilitarian considerations, or by responding to the interests of a powerful class that brought them to power to achieve gains at the expense of society. Therefore, we see that the legislation produced by human minds often reflects the interests of the ruling elite or influential groups, rather than expressing the public interest or objective justice.
This deviation is not always intentional; it may arise from the unconscious, where the legislator is influenced by a prevailing intellectual current, media pressures, or a dominant public mood, without realizing the impact of that on the value system upon which their legislation is based. Laws may form that reflect a purely materialistic vision—as in capitalist countries—that prioritize economic benefit even at the expense of family, virtue, or the environment, or adopt a specific nationalistic or class-based inclination, or enact legislation in the name of "personal freedom" that unleashes sexual desires or deviant behavioral patterns, due to the corruption of the intellectual foundation adopted by the legislator. Later, those societies may find themselves unable to confront the consequences of moral collapse and social disintegration that arise from such legislation, resulting in the prioritization of those considerations in their laws at the expense of absolute justice.
The Noble Qur'an warns against this legislative deviation arising from desire, stating: ﴾So judge between them by what Allah has revealed and do not follow their desires﴿, indicating that following desire in the realm of legislation leads to the governance of something other than Allah in one of the most significant matters, which is the matter of the lawful and unlawful, right and wrong. The matter can even reach the point where desire itself becomes a deity to be worshipped, as He, Exalted is He, said: ﴾Have you seen the one who has taken as his god his own desire, and Allah has led him astray despite his knowledge﴿. Thus, following desire in the context of legislation means making desire a deity worshipped besides Allah, and we seek refuge with Allah.
From here, the danger of human legislation that submits to desire becomes evident, where personal interest is disguised under the guise of "freedom," instincts are clothed in the garb of "rights," and deviation is legitimized through what is called "the right to choose," resulting in human intellect producing biased, unjust legislative systems that serve one group at the expense of another, or entrench destructive values under shiny slogans.
