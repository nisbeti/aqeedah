Here, the concept of "objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia)" emerged as an intellectual tool for them: they revived the term maqasid with vigor but with a different understanding from that of previous scholars; they made it a basis for developing and changing rulings rather than a criterion for understanding their fixed reasons.
The result of prioritizing reason and public interest (maslaha) over text manifested in a dangerous qualitative shift: revelation became subordinate to reason in many instances for them. Thus, the door opened to many transgressions, as we will see, under the pretext of "upholding the objective" or "achieving the interest," even at the expense of the definitive rulings of the Sharia.
Interpreting texts with a focus on public interest and symbolically under the influence of the age
Alongside practical fatwas, the influence of rational thought appeared in the interpretation of the legal texts themselves. Reformists adopted a method of free interpretation of the texts, especially the verses of the Holy Qur'an, in accordance with the spirit of the age and modern science. Muhammad Abduh and Rashid Rida authored "Tafsir al-Manar," which represented a clear model of this trend. In his interpretation, Abduh sought to highlight the ethical and social aspects of legislation, linking the verses to general rational objectives even if this required diverting the wording from its apparent meaning. He was the one who interpreted the disjointed letters at the beginning of the surahs with symbolic rational interpretations, contrary to the method of the scholars of interpretation before him, who entrusted their meaning to Allah or explained them in other ways.
It was also reported that he interpreted some of the Prophet's ﷺ miracles with purely rational explanations; for example, regarding the event of Isra and Mi'raj, it was said that he denied the occurrence of the Mi'raj in body to the heavens, considering it a spiritual vision or a dream. In summary, Abduh attempted - according to the expression of some researchers - "to narrow the gap between the world of the unseen and the world of the witnessed" through bold interpretative steps. He relied on modern Western scientific discoveries to interpret the unseen phenomena mentioned in the Qur'an and Sunnah, in an attempt to make religion consistent with the data of material science.
Examples of rational interpretations
Ghazi al-Touba - a researcher in their biographies - mentioned that Muhammad Abduh interpreted many of the unseen matters mentioned in the Qur'an with symbolic material interpretations. Among these: he interpreted the jinn mentioned in the legal texts as microbes that are seen under magnifying glasses; and he interpreted ﴾the whisperers in the knots﴿ (mentioned in Surah Al-Falaq) as gossipers who sever the bonds of affection between people; he also interpreted the birds of Ababil as a type of mosquito or fly, and he explained the stones of Sijjil that were thrown at the companions of the elephant as germs of smallpox or measles that destroyed them. His audacity even reached the point of interpreting the miracle of the birth of Jesus, son of Mary (peace be upon him), with two rational interpretations: either it resulted from a strong belief that overwhelmed Mary's heart, causing the pregnancy in her womb - meaning a psychological and physical effect! - or that a "gentle spirit" sent by Allah caused the fertilization without a father. Both statements deviate from the apparent meaning of the Qur'anic text, which explicitly stated that Gabriel blew into her garment by the permission of her Lord and the glad tidings of Allah to her of a son without a father.
