Critique of the School of Rational Interest: The fourth chapter reviews the roots of the thought that elevates the status of "interest (maslaha)" and "objectives of the Sharia (maqasid)" at the expense of the text, and it highlights the danger of interpreting texts or bypassing them under the pretext of changing time and place, especially in contemporary proposals that originated from the sayings of al-Tufi, which were then reproduced and expanded upon by some modern scholars.

The modern rationalist school: Afghani, Abduh, and Rida: In the fifth chapter and beyond, the book detailed the trajectory of the reformist school represented by Jamal al-Din al-Afghani, Muhammad Abduh, and Rashid Rida. It showed how this school redefined Islam with a modern Western logic, promoting the idea of "modern Islam," and opening the door for rational interpretations and pragmatic juristic efforts that deviate from the established foundational methodology. The book also discussed the impact of this school in establishing the jurisprudence of minorities, schools of balance, tolerance towards positive laws, the nation-state, and the influence of national and nationalist thought.

Contemporary manifestations of deviation from the authority of the text: The book addresses famous contemporary fatwas that demonstrate the extent of the infiltration of the rationalist and utilitarian approach, such as:

The permissibility of bank interest,

the permissibility of working in usurious institutions,

the marriage of a Muslim woman to a non-Muslim,

equality in inheritance,

denial of the punishment for apostasy and stoning,

defending Western democracy as a legislative system.

Critique of Western modernist thought: The final chapters presented a critique of the ideas of Western modernity as they were passed through the gateway of religious renewal, focusing on the book "Civil Democratic Islam" as a clear example of efforts to engineer Islam in accordance with Western values, through tools such as the interpretation of texts, the dominance of public interest (maslaha), and the promotion of individual liberal Islam instead of legislative Islam.

Conclusion and Results

The book concludes that the detachment of Muslims from the reference of revelation, and the subjugation of texts to public interest (maslaha) and Western rationality, poses a real danger to the identity of the nation and its legal constants, as well as its way of thinking. It recommends returning to the established usul (principles) methodology that is based on the Qur'an and the Sunnah, which regulates reason and public interest within their framework, not the other way around.
