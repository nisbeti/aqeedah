Analysis of the methodology of concession:

An unjustified expansion of the concepts of "necessity" (necessity is measured by its extent) and "need" (which is not equivalent to necessity): The level of social and economic difficulties in the West is elevated to the level of "necessity" that permits prohibitions, which is an expansion that violates the strict foundational controls of these concepts, especially in light of the reality that banks do not approve a mortgage contract unless the applicant is financially capable of fulfilling the contract in the foreseeable future. This study presented by the bank undoubtedly proves the applicant's ability to rent in principle, as if they are not capable of renting, which is usually cheaper than the monthly payment for the house they own, they will not approve the contract for them. So where is the need that has been equated with necessity?

The use of "unrestricted public interest (maslaha)": Unrestricted public interest (which is not indicated by specific evidence but does not contradict a legal principle) is used in cases that contradict definitive texts, which is rejected by the usul scholars (even those who accept public interest, like al-Ghazali and Ibn Qudamah, stipulate that it does not oppose the text).

Ijtihad in the presence of text: The attempt to exercise ijtihad and find loopholes (tricks or false distinctions) in issues where there is a definitive text or consensus, which effectively means disabling the text in practice.

Separating public interest from the controls of revelation: Transforming "public interest" from a legal concept regulated by evidence and the overarching objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) into a purely pragmatic worldly concept, whose main goal is to facilitate life in the Western environment and avoid conflict, even at the expense of the constants of religion.

Western centrality in ijtihad: Making the Western reality and its values (individualism, absolute integration, secularism) the primary driver and main determinant of the direction of ijtihad, instead of making the legal texts and principles the criteria by which reality is evaluated and solutions are prepared within its limits.

Ibn al-Qayyim says: "The public interest that is not acknowledged by the Sharia is not a public interest" (I'lam al-Muwaqqi'in). These fatwas have made "desire" (owning a luxurious house) equivalent to "necessity"!

Contradiction with consensus: The definitive consensus on the prohibition of usury (Ibn Abd al-Barr: al-Tamhid 4/60).

The scholar Ibn Taymiyyah: "Definitive texts are not abrogated merely by public interest" (al-Fatawa 20/58).

The European Council for Fatwa issued 30% of its decisions based on the invocation of "necessity" to permit prohibitions.
