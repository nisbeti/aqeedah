Sheikh Bakr Abu Zaid in his message "The Ruling on Belonging": "It is not permissible for a Muslim to participate in the democratic system because it is an acknowledgment of a rule other than what Allah has revealed."

Sheikh Abdul Aziz bin Baz and Ibn Uthaymeen: They rejected participation in elections that involve man-made legislation or empower those other than the Law of Allah.

Sheikh Abu Ishaq al-Hawaini (Egypt): He said in one of his recorded lessons: "Democracy is based on the idea that what is lawful is what the parliament has permitted, and what is unlawful is what the parliament has prohibited, and this is explicit disbelief in Allah, for Allah alone is the legislator… So how can I be a Muslim and acknowledge this disbelief?" He emphasizes that elections contribute to entrenching the rule of tyranny.

Sheikh Abdul Salam Yasin (Morocco) – Founder of the Justice and Spirituality Movement: He said in the Prophetic Methodology: "Participation in man-made legislative institutions is an implicit acknowledgment of the legitimacy of their laws, which are based on principles that grant sovereignty to humans rather than to Allah." Despite his educational and political approach, his stance on parliamentary elections under man-made rule was explicitly rejecting.

Sheikh Omar Flatah (Tunisia) – From the scholars of Zaytuna: He clarified in one of his fatwas: "Running for office or voting in democratic systems that refer to humans rather than to revelation is a form of referring to tyranny, and Allah has forbidden us from it in His saying: ﴾They wish to refer to tyranny﴿."

Sheikh Muhammad Ahmad Hussein (Jerusalem – Mufti of Palestine): He issued several fatwas through the Palestinian Fatwa House affirming that: "Any system that makes the people a source of legislation in place of divine revelation is a tyrannical system in which it is not permissible for a Muslim to participate," with the exception of elections that do not involve legislation (such as those related to purely service-oriented institutions).

Sheikh Abdul Qadir Auda (Egypt): In his book Islamic Criminal Legislation: "Legislation is a pure right of Allah, Exalted is He, and any human intervention in legislation is considered a serious transgression that the Sharia does not accept, and participation in it is an acknowledgment of it."

Sheikh Muhammad al-Bashir al-Ibrahimi (Algeria) – Association of Muslim Scholars: He said in one of his political messages: "Whoever is content with a rule that does not stand on the Book of Allah and the Sunnah of His Messenger has nullified the essence of loyalty to Allah and has accepted to be ruled by tyranny."

Sheikh Said Hawwa (Syria): In his book Soldiers of Allah in Culture and Morality: "Democracy is not a suitable Islamic methodology, as it transfers sovereignty from the Sharia to the people, which is unacceptable in Islam that grants sovereignty solely to Allah."

Sixth: Necessities do not justify fundamental prohibitions

Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾So whoever is forced, neither desiring to transgress nor exceeding the limit, there is no sin upon him﴿ [al-Baqarah: 173]
