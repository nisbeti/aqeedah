Similarly, human beings are central to this existence; the universe, with all its celestial bodies and systems, was created to be ready to receive life, and life, with all its wonders and miracles, was created to be ready to receive humans. The highest aspect of the cycles of life is the rational human being, and this human ascends through the intellect they possess!
The intellect is the tool of human beings for knowing their Creator and understanding the secret of their existence in life, unraveling many of the complexities of this existence and interpreting them through intelligent connections, comprehension, and understanding, as well as the creation of ideas, research, deduction, and induction. The intellect is the basis of obligation.
Whereas reason has this immense status in the "plan of existence," and in the distinction of rational human beings from other creatures, and reliance on reason in perceiving the connection with the Creator; there is no doubt that it is an extremely important and powerful tool. There is no doubt that its judgment, which it will make in the process of reasoning about the Creator or in interpreting existence, that judgment based on evidence and connected to the determinants that provide certainty, is a judgment whose correctness is indisputable, and we must accept it without hesitation. However, when examining the ability of reason to issue judgments on matters of lesser significance than issues of belief, such as some rulings related to organizing human life, like the judgment of the goodness or badness of an action, or whether it is a good ruling or a bad one, or that it has a reward or punishment, or that it is suitable for human livelihood or corrupts it? We will find that reason cannot issue such judgments. It is worth noting that reason cannot issue judgments not because it is forbidden to do so, but because it has not been given the tools, abilities, and information necessary for it to be able to issue them. If reason were able to determine goodness and badness, or to judge what is good and what is evil, it would certainly have to agree with the judgment of the Sharia (Islamic law). However, since they differ, this clearly indicates that reason does not possess the ability to issue judgments. Therefore, in this discussion, when we say at any point that reason can err in judgment, or that it varies, or that judgment does not belong to reason, we mean desires or inclinations, not reason itself, because reason does not have the ability to judge. However, humans do not cease to issue legislations and rulings; humans judge their desires, not their reason(11), and Western civilization has elevated the status of reason to the point that it governs everything in the realm of nature.
But can the limited human intellect be an independent legislator apart from divine revelation? This is what we strongly deny in this chapter, by demonstrating the inherent shortcomings of the intellect and reviewing the inability of human experiences (both ancient and modern) to establish legislation that achieves justice and righteousness without relying on the guidance of heaven.
