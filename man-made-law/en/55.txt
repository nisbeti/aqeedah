Moral and spiritual purification: He purifies them and teaches them the Book and Wisdom.
Trial and differentiation: And We will surely test you...
Eschatological (afterlife-related) aims e.g., praying or giving charity for the deceased.
Identity-defining and distinguishing aims: e.g., distinguishing Muslim practices from others.
Hence, understanding Sharīʿah must adopt a comprehensive analytical approach – not reduce it to utilitarian aims. The human being is not just a body, and Sharīʿah is not merely a worldly legal code, but a divine system that purifies, tests, reforms, and guides. Reducing it to a tool for achieving interests risks emptying it of its essence as a system of obligation and submission and risks conditional compliance based on perceived benefit.
First: Maṣlaḥah and the Social Necessity of Regulation
Society is not maintained by individuals chasing personal interests, but by a unified structure that prioritizes collective benefit over individual desire and enshrines justice above chaotic competition. If every person pursued only what they considered beneficial, unregulated by a shared system, conflict would prevail.
The book The Miracle of Islamic Legislation: Characteristics and Foundations points to this by stating: “Society cannot be upright if each individual merely seeks personal interests; rather, they must unite upon an accepted system that advances the group’s interest over the individual’s, with individuals conceding to the needs of society.” Hence, the need for divine legislation.
Second: Reason as a Servant of Revelation, Not an Independent Legislator
Reason is a means of understanding and inference, not a source of legislation. The usūl scholars differentiate between reason’s ability to comprehend the suitability of certain rulings and its inability to independently legislate based on presumed benefits.
The Qur’ān says: ﴿Say: I only follow what is revealed to me from my Lord﴾ [al-Aʿrāf: 203], clearly affirming that the source of legislation is restricted to revelation, and reason’s role is in interpretation and deduction, not origination.
Taqī al-Dīn al-Nabhānī affirms: “The understanding of rulings by a mujtahid must be based on revelation, not mere opinion or desire. If a ruling is deduced solely from perceived benefit without supporting evidence, the person has made their opinion a source of legislation – which is prohibited.” (The Islamic Personality, vol. 3)
