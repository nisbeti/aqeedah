Introduction: The Roots of the Rational Reformist School
The Islamic nation has witnessed since the late nineteenth century the emergence of an intellectual current known as the "modern rational trend" or "reformist school," which is associated with prominent figures such as Jamal al-Din al-Afghani (1838–1897) and his student Muhammad Abduh (1849–1905), and later extended with Muhammad Rashid Rida (1865–1935) and others. The pioneers of this school raised the banners of revival and renewal, calling for the elevation of the authority of reason and the liberation of thought from stagnation, to the extent that reason prevailed for them over all else, including texts. Thus, their movement represented an attempt to reconcile Islam and the legal tradition on one hand, and the spirit of modernity and Western civilization on the other. We will review below the role of this school in several critical axes:
Establishing the precedence of reason and public interest (maslaha) over texts in legislation.
Interpreting legal texts in a new way (whether pragmatic or symbolic) under the influence of the spirit of the age and Western thought.
Laying the groundwork for dismantling the traditional legislative foundational structure and opening the door for renewal on unregulated bases.
Adapting to nationalist, liberal, and socialist tendencies and attempting to color Islam with them.
The influence of the pioneers of the school by Freemasonry and the project of the Islamic University, transforming it from a religious association into a national political one.
Their direct connection to the modern Egyptian intellectual elite, and then their influence on contemporary currents such as liberal Islam, the jurisprudence of minorities, and the jurisprudence of unregulated balances and objectives.
Criticism of their methodology and concepts as seen in the writings of Dr. Muhammad Muhammad Hussein - especially the national trends and our fortresses threatened from within - and the studies of Anwar al-Jundi, Abu al-Hasan al-Nadwi, Ziyad Salama in his study on Sheikh Hassan al-Banna, and other Islamic critics.
Each axis will be addressed in detail, analyzing the impact of this school on changing the way Muslims think about texts, revelation, and legal reference. We will also compare the legal concepts as understood by traditional jurists with what the modern rational school has presented, supported by tables and scientific documentation.
Prioritizing reason and public interest over legal texts
One of the most prominent features of the rational reformist school is that it elevated human reason to be the primary arbiter in legislation. Its symbols believed that revival could only be achieved through the exercise of free thought and the rejection of dependence on inherited legal tradition. Muhammad Abduh even famously stated: "Islam is the religion of reason and science, and everything that contradicts reason is not established."
