From here, Islamic law (sharia) came with a unique balanced perspective on humanity:
It combined spiritual, material, moral, ethical, and human rights.
It took into account the need for permissible enjoyment, but regulated it through marriage, family, and chastity.
It opened the doors to work and striving, but regulated it through zakat (almsgiving) and mutual support (takaful).
It directed individuals towards worship and humility, but did not abolish their social or economic lives.
This objective is based on a noble relationship of love between the human being and their Creator, which is not solely based on fear and compliance, but on awareness of Allah's bounty, mercy, and generosity, and what that requires in terms of fulfilling the duty of love and drawing closer to Him, as stated in the Qur'an: ﴾Say, "If you love Allah, then follow me, and Allah will love you"﴿.
This reciprocal relationship in love is not merely feelings, but is based on embodying the qualities that Allah loves – such as benevolence, piety, patience, generosity, and mercy – qualities that the sharia instills and nurtures through obligations, trials, righteous deeds, and continuous purification of the self. For Allah breathed into humanity from His spirit, and endowed them with the readiness to know Him and draw near to Him, and this closeness can only be achieved through striving against the self and purifying it to embody some of the divine attributes, such as mercy and benevolence, so that divine affection grows in the heart of the believer, and this becomes the pinnacle of happiness in this world, and the key to eternal bliss in the Hereafter.
Thus, it becomes clear that divine legislation alone – not man-made laws – is what takes into account this complex reality of humanity, directs its instincts correctly, achieves balance between its material and spiritual needs, and leads it to its highest purpose of existence: servitude to Allah, attaining His love, achieving tranquility in this world, and success in the Hereafter.
With this precise balance, divine legislation fulfills the ultimate purpose of human existence: the worship of Allah, the cultivation of the earth, and achieving balanced happiness in this world and the Hereafter, a balance that no man-made philosophy has succeeded in attaining, because it simply ignored humanity and its purpose. Therefore, legal rulings should not be subjected to the scrutiny of minds to grasp their wisdom and reasons and to realize what they seem to those minds as interests and desires; if they do not grasp that, they will move on to human legislations, thinking that they achieve those interests, desires, and benefits, which is never correct. Rather, legislations should be derived from revelation in worship, love, satisfaction, submission, commitment, application, and surrender!
Society should not be a field for legislative experiments:
Human laws are tested and modified or abolished after social, moral, or economic harms occur. Society is turned into a laboratory for uncertain experiments. The examples are numerous: laws regarding drug use, abortion, or financial transactions are constantly amended after their negative effects appear. This threatens the stability of the legal system and harms individuals and communities.
