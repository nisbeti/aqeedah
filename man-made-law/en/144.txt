The methodology of preference between detailed evidences in the principles of jurisprudence: a scientific alternative to the jurisprudence of balances.
When the juristic scholar (mujtahid) deals with seemingly conflicting evidences, he does not resort to preferring one based on presumed "interest" or subjective inclination, but rather follows a strict, foundational path grounded in the science of the principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh), according to the regulated rules of equivalence and preference that reflect the depth of understanding and the presence of a disciplined juristic mentality anchored in text and implication.
The process of preference goes through three central rational stages:
First: The stage of equivalence (investigation of apparent conflict)
First, verifying the existence of conflict: for there are two evidences that may seem to conflict, but investigating their implications may lead to reconciliation, specification, or restriction.
Reconciliation is preferred over preference whenever possible, because acting on both evidences is better than discarding one of them.
And since it is possible to reconcile the two evidences on the basis that one is general(41) and the other is specific, or that one is unrestricted and the other is restricted, the rule is that restriction is preferred over unrestrictedness, specification over generality, and interpretation over abrogation if possible (provided that the cause or subject is the same). The preference here does not mean preference between two conflicting evidences, but rather a mechanism for reconciling evidences.
If it is established that reconciliation is impossible, the juristic scholar moves to the stage of preference.
Second: The stage of preference between conflicting evidences
In this regard, regulated preferences are taken into account, which we summarize in three main axes:
Strength of authenticity (supply preferences):
Preference is given to mutawatir (consecutively transmitted) over ahad (individually transmitted).
Preference is given to sahih (authentic) over da'if (weak).
Preference is given to what has been agreed upon as accepted over what is disputed.
Preference is given to what has been narrated by trustworthy and just imams over others, and what has been narrated from two sources over what has been narrated from one source, and so on.

(41) Unrestrictedness (itlaq): is what indicates the reality without restriction, such as His saying: ﴾So the freeing of a neck﴿. Generality (umum): is what indicates the inclusion of all individuals, such as His saying ﴾So kill the polytheists altogether﴿. Restriction occurs in descriptions, and specification in individuals. Therefore, we do not say that "restriction is stronger than unrestrictedness" in isolation, but we say: "restriction is preferred over unrestrictedness when they appear to conflict in one ruling, due to the unity of the cause and the subject."
