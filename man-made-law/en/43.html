Knowledge – that is, the principles of jurisprudence (usul al-fiqh) – is only utilized in conjunction with textual evidence, and the mainstay is indeed the textual evidence, for it is the foundation... Thus, the primary reliance is on the legal evidences. "Al-Muwafaqat, vol. 3, p. 53." And Imam al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam said in Qawaid al-Ahkam fi Masalih al-Anam, vol. 1, p. 16: "Rational improvement and disapproval have no place in legal rulings, for the ruler is Allah, not reason. If reason had independence in improvement and prohibition, there would have been no need for the sending of messengers." This is a decisive statement and a compelling proof. Al-Amidi said in al-Ihkam fi Usul al-Ahkam, vol. 1, p. 105: "Reason does not impose anything upon Allah nor prohibit anything from Him, and the ruler in the Sharia is the Sharia, not reason." And the scholar Ibn Taymiyyah said in Majmu' al-Fatawa, and in the explanations of the principles: "There is no ruling for things before the Sharia... Whoever does not agree with the Mu'tazila on improvement and disapproval... the actions of rational beings have no ruling before the arrival of the Sharia," ended.
