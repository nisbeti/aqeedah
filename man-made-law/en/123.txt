Did the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) approve of the rule of disbelief by the Negus?
We quote this section from the very valuable book: The Call to Islam, by Ahmad al-Mahmoud:
"Whoever carries Islam sincerely and works to restore it to the reality of governance and life, whether as an individual or as a group, cannot participate in a rule of disbelief while claiming to work for its destruction, because participation in a rule of disbelief that implements the systems and laws of disbelief is a reinforcement of those systems, not a destruction of them. Any argument presented to justify participation in a disbelieving rule is nothing but self-deception before it is deception against Allah and the believers, especially when that argument contradicts the definitive textual evidence, both in terms of its authenticity and its meaning.
It is indeed a severe calamity and a great sin for a bearer of the call to resort to the interests he perceives with his mind, which have not been considered by the Sharia, as evidence to justify for himself the violation of the definitive textual evidence, both in terms of its authenticity and its meaning. Or to resort to what does not even reach the level of a questionable proof to take it as a justification for participating in a rule of disbelief that governs by other than what Allah has revealed, even though this participation in a disbelieving rule contradicts the definitive textual evidence, both in terms of its authenticity and its meaning, which necessitate ruling by what Allah has revealed and which prohibit ruling by other than what Allah has revealed.
This is similar to taking the story of the Negus, whom the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) announced to the Companions on the day of his death and prayed the funeral prayer for him, as a justification for participating in a disbelieving rule that governs by other than what Allah has revealed, going so far as to claim that the Negus had embraced Islam during the time of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) and continued to rule by the system he had been ruling by before he embraced Islam, even though it was a non-Islamic system. They have cited six hadiths reported by al-Bukhari, related to his death and the prayer for him, three of which were narrated by Jabir ibn Abdullah al-Ansari, and three by Abu Huraira. However, these six hadiths do not suffice as evidence to justify participation in a disbelieving rule that governs by the systems and laws of disbelief. The clarification of this is as follows.
1 – When al-Bukhari narrated these hadiths, he titled five of them under (The Chapter of the Death of the Negus), and the sixth under (The Chapter of Funerals). The six hadiths relate to the death of the Negus, the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) informing the Companions of his death, that he was a righteous man, that he was their brother, and the request for them to seek forgiveness for him and pray the funeral prayer for him. This indicates that he was a Muslim.
2 – Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani commented in his book Fath al-Bari, the explanation of Sahih al-Bukhari, on al-Bukhari's title (The Death of the Negus) and his failure to title it with his Islam, saying: "It is puzzling that he – meaning al-Bukhari – did not title it with his Islam – meaning the Negus – and this is its place, and he titled it with his death because when the story of his Islam was not established for him, which is explicit in his death, he titled it with that so that it could be understood from the prayer for him that he had embraced Islam."
3 – The wording of the hadiths that al-Bukhari reported indicates that the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) learned of the death of the Negus and his Islam on the day of his death through revelation, and it also indicates that the Companions did not know of his Islam and death until the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) informed them.
