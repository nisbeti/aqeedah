The honest merchant reveals the defect in his goods and incurs a loss, thus losing a material benefit, while the usurer is prevented from guaranteed profit, and the public interest (maslaha) necessitates the prohibition of usury, which takes precedence over individual interest. Therefore, it is not left to reason to determine what is beneficial; rather, it is what has been decreed by the Sharia.

Similarly, we see that the organization of satisfaction leads to prioritizing moral values and worship over material interests, as in the honesty of the merchant or the Muslim's abstention from lying or false oaths or being lax in a prohibited matter to preserve worldly gain.

If interests are diverse and the organization may prioritize some over others, how can reason comprehend all this and build upon it a foundation through which it defines a specific concept of interest and then makes this definition or specification a basis to apply to another relationship derived from detailed evidence and build upon it a legal ruling? It is fundamentally incapable of grasping how to resolve the contradictions between the many factors influencing societal relationships and the optimal legislation that is prescribed for them! How can the human mind, for example, balance the interest of fatherhood in compassion and tenderness against the interest of justice in testifying against a son or enforcing a punishment upon him? Or prefer the spiritual benefit of night prayers over physical comfort? Or regulate the marital relationship in divorce, abstention, and waiting periods without divine inspiration that organizes these details? The mind may mistakenly perceive benefit in what is harmful or perceive harm in what is good, and it cannot discern "the most beneficial paths" except through the guidance of the Legislator who created the soul and its needs and legislated to organize them.

Therefore, it is very important to distinguish between organization and reasoning; many rulings are not reasoned but are divine regulations for human life. Not everything that Allah, Exalted is He, has legislated is reasoned; rather, the majority of rulings have come devoid of explicit reasoning, and their purposes are understood only through submission and compliance. Similarly, there is a distinction between what motivated a ruling or the purpose of its legislation (if clarified by the Legislator) or even what we have not discerned as the motivation for its ruling, nor the purpose related to the interest of human affairs that it brings or the harm it averts. In all this, we find that the Sharia encompasses broader categories than merely bringing about benefits and averting harms: as for the purposes and objectives of legislation, they include:

Human organizational purposes related to satisfaction and relationships:

Necessities: that without which life cannot be sustained (religion, self, intellect, lineage, wealth).

Needs: that which alleviates hardship and difficulty.

Improvements: that which relates to dignity, nobility, and taste. All of these are life organizations, but they should not be understood as "the motivation for legislation," rather they are results that follow unless there is an explicit or appropriate legal reasoning.
