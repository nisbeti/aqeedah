Therefore, it is sheer and unacceptable error to compare reason (aql) and revelation (naql) in legislation. In this regard, what the scholar Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him) said is pertinent: "Sound reason does not contradict authentic revelation; rather, it testifies to it and supports it… Yes, there may be texts that are beyond the grasp of reason, but not those that it rejects." Many matters of religion transcend our understanding without contradicting our intellects. The notion that sound reason does not contradict authentic revelation is a false premise with no basis in reason or revelation. Reason does not have this role in Islam, nor does it possess the ability to arrive at a truth only to later find that it aligns with authentic revelation! Except in the realm of the beliefs previously mentioned, which are based on rational evidence.
The Sharia has established, beyond any doubt, that the sole reference for legislation is revelation, not reason. Allah, Exalted is He, says: ﴾Follow what has been revealed to you from your Lord and do not follow other than Him as allies. Little do you remember﴿ [al-A'raf: 3]. Thus, following the revealed (revelation) through rational deduction to understand the texts (reason) is the desired stance, and adopting this as an undisputed principle is something that the wise should remember ﴾Little do you remember﴿.
Imam al-Shatibi provided us with a beautiful example of the relationship between reason and revelation. He said, in essence, that the wisdom of legislation and the knowledge of the reasons for rulings are too great for individuals to grasp independently. Allah has taken it upon Himself to preserve the objectives of His Sharia by sending messengers. Our role is to understand those objectives and act upon them, not to invent objectives from ourselves. For this reason, the texts of the predecessors abound in warnings against mere opinion in religion and condemn those who follow their intellect without regard for the text. Ali ibn Abi Talib (may Allah be pleased with him) said: "If religion were based on opinion, the bottom of the shoe would be more deserving of wiping than its top, and I have seen the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) wipe over the tops of his shoes" (narrated by Abu Dawood with a good chain). This means that the worshipfulness of the rulings may come contrary to what reason perceives as preferable, as a test of obedience and a break from intellectual arrogance. Reason says: ablution cleans the bottom of the shoe more, but the legislator wiped the top as a symbol of the comprehensiveness of purity, and the believer obeys. Therefore, we find our scholars saying: "Ijtihad (independent reasoning) is exerting effort in seeking the presumption of a legal ruling" - they did not say "in creating the ruling"! Rather, it is in seeking it and deriving it. This is very clear in the previous hadith of Mu'adh ibn Jabal: "I exert my opinion and do not spare any effort." Mu'adh did not say, "I legislate by my opinion," but rather, "I strive to search within the texts if I do not find a specific text, then I resort to analogy," and he called it his opinion. We have elaborated on the interpretation of this narration and studied it in our book: "The Miracle of Islamic Legislation: Characteristics and Foundations," so refer to it.
The scholars have established precise criteria for the realm of analogy. Analogy is purely an intellectual activity, but it is constrained by pillars: the original case, the branch, the reason, and the ruling. It is not analogy for a person to invent a ruling and attach it to religion. There must be a prescribed original case with a reason that can be analogized, and the reason must be a common factor between the original case and the branch that the Sharia has indicated should be considered. Any analogy that does not meet these conditions is rejected because it is an invalid analogy. Thus, we see that reason here operates, but in the service of the text; it seeks the commonality between two issues to equate their ruling in execution of Allah's intent, not to unleash the imagination of man.
