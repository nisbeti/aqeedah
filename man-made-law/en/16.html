Chapter One: The Limitations of Reason and the Impossibility of It Being a Reference for Legislation

Executive Summary of Chapter One: Establishing the Authority of Revelation in Islamic Legislation:

This chapter is considered the cornerstone of the entire book, as it lays the methodological framework for addressing the issue of authority in Islamic legislation, clarifying that the foundation of the Islamic perspective is that the ultimate authority returns to divine revelation as represented in the Holy Qur'an and the authentic prophetic tradition (sunnah). The chapter opens by stating the necessity of establishing the legislative perspective on the concept of servitude to Allah, and the implications of complete submission to Allah, Exalted is He, as the sole legislator, rejecting any notion that places reason or personal interest as an independent source of legislation.

The chapter also discusses the difference between human-derived legislation and Islamic legislation that originates from revelation, explaining how the former inevitably leads to relativity and legislative chaos, while the latter achieves stability, justice, and consistency. It presents a detailed exposition of Qur'anic verses that affirm Allah's exclusivity in legislation, and responds to some doubts raised about the role of reason in Islam, highlighting that reason in the Islamic methodology serves revelation, rather than governs it.

Among the most important topics covered in the chapter is a methodological study that proves the complete inability of reason to produce correct legislation, detailing the necessary specifications for legislation to be suitable for guiding human life and societies, and the destructive impact of placing legislation in the winds of relativity, utilitarianism, and change on societies. It provides the necessary arguments to clarify the reasons that decisively rule out the suitability of reason and desires for legislation.

The chapter also critiques modern thinking methodologies that have established a human authority under various names such as democracy and social contract, pointing out the fundamental contradictions between them and the Qur'anic perspective. It concludes by affirming that liberating the nation from cultural and intellectual dependency first requires liberating its legislative authority, meaning a return to revelation as the sole source of governance.

Content of Chapter One:

Indeed, the sciences and arts, literature and philosophy, history, jurisprudence and language, the advancement of humanity and its elevation, the civilizations it has established, and knowledge as knowledge, are all products of reason and thus products of thought. Knowledge is the essence of human existence, the fruit of its intellectual output, and its exploration of the depths of existence and the struggles of life. These fruits either elevate the noble status of humanity to become the best of creations or degrade it to be worse than animals; they either align it with its essence and provide reassurance in the course of its life, or they shake its very core, causing anxiety, doubt, and uncertainty; they either explain the world to it or leave it in confusion.
