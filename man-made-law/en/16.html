Preserve public morality, stability, and societal integrity.

Be rooted in a correct understanding of human nature and existential purpose.

Address changing realities without compromising foundational values under the sway of interests or passions. Emanate from a supreme, legitimate source that commands binding authority, not from arbitrary majorities or unauthorized powers. This chapter, therefore, demonstrates that legislation cannot be valid, beneficial, or binding unless it meets essential criteria in both source and substance:

It must be based on a deep and accurate understanding of the human being's needs, instincts, and existential aims, not on an incomplete view as seen in materialist or positivist philosophies. It must originate from a source that is all-knowing, just, and wise, not from the limited, biased, and ever-changing human intellect.

It must be fixed in its overarching purposes, coherently oriented toward societal well-being, and harmonized in its legal mechanisms, free of contradiction and internal conflict. It must realize authentic higher values, such as justice, dignity, and equality of opportunity, and not merely serve the interests of the powerful or codify moral decay.

The chapter further explains that the absence or corruption of legislation leads not only to legal dysfunction but to widespread societal ruin: family disintegration, moral anarchy, economic injustice, political paralysis, and spiritual emptiness. Therefore, it is essential to define the qualities of valid legislation and expose the inadequacy of man-made systems based solely on reason without revelation.

Methodological Foreword:

Legislation in any society or state is not an end in itself, but a functional tool designed to regulate human behavior and relationships, organize public and private interests, and establish a framework for organizing the procurement of benefits and repelling harm. This role is only fulfilled when legislation reflects an accurate moral scale, differentiating between truth and falsehood, good and evil, benefit and harm, and aims to secure human well-being by guiding conduct and establishing an order of values and objectives that ensure personal rectitude, societal stability, and political justice.
