Sheikh Ata Abu al-Rashta expressed this by saying: "The appropriate descriptive attribute is one that has a significant relationship with the ruling that follows from it, and in this case, it is suitable for reasoning, such as anger in judicial matters and killing in inheritance."

Methodological Summary:

The Sharia was revealed to organize the interests of humanity according to the will of Allah, not according to their intellect.

The intellect is a tool for understanding and deriving rulings, not a tool for legislation.

The interests (masalih) are derived from the texts, not invented by the jurist.

The reason ('illah) is only accepted if it is a suitable, clear, and impactful description that is understood from the text.

The use of reason in deriving legal rulings must not exceed the texts of the Sharia or its principles.

It is essential to close every avenue that leads to the dominance of desire over the legislative process in the name of public interest (maslaha).

Thus, ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) becomes a regulated legal process that protects the Sharia from distortion and ensures that reason plays its legitimate role without becoming a master over revelation.

Definition of Public Interest (Maslaha Mursala)

In language, "maslaha" is a noun meaning benefit or something that is beneficial. When we say "maslaha mursala," we mean the absolute benefit that is not restricted by a specific text. The scholars of usul (usul al-fiqh) have defined maslaha mursala as: "Any benefit that is not supported by a specific legal text either in recognition or in abrogation, and that is inherently compatible with the general objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia)." The term "mursala" indicates that it is an unrestricted benefit, meaning it is a benefit that is free from a specific evidence. However, it should be noted that the absence of specific evidence does not mean there is no legal basis for it; it may have general evidence or a legal principle that supports it.

An example of this is the collection of the Qur'an during the time of Abu Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) following the martyrdom of many of the memorizers. This is a clear public interest (maslaha) (preserving the Qur'an from loss), for which there is no specific text (the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) did not command this before his death), but it has a valid general basis, which is the command to preserve and protect the religion. Similarly, Umar ibn al-Khattab established the system of the diwan and recorded the names of the soldiers for salaries; this is an administrative interest for which there is no specific text, but it falls under the general principles of the Sharia in organization, justice, and giving everyone their due right. Thus, the key here is that the public interest (maslaha mursala) must be in accordance with the spirit of the Sharia and its general objectives (maqasid al-sharia), not a fictitious interest or one that contradicts another text.
