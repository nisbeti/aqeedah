“Muhammad is not the father of any of your men, but he is the Messenger of Allah and the Seal of the Prophets” ﴾ [al-Ahzab 40] ﴿. This indicates the necessity of maintaining a complete and perfect proof for those addressed by the religion of Islam - in both belief and law - until the Day of Judgment, and that it must reach them - necessarily - as a clear, preserved, and unaltered evidence, both in the Book and the Sunnah! Thus, the system of Islam, with its belief and law, is complete, and no issue is devoid of legislation connected to revelation, so that there remains no avenue for human legislation to be a source of injustice in that matter. This necessitates that the Sharia be preserved with its Qur'an and Sunnah to complete the blessing of establishing the balance of justice and equity among creation until the Day of Judgment.
Imam Ibn Hazm al-Andalusi said: "﴾Today I have perfected for you your religion and completed My favor upon you and have chosen for you Islam as religion﴿, [al-Ma'idah 3]. He said: So we say to those who permit that what Allah commanded His Prophet regarding the clarification of the Sharia of Islam for us is not preserved, and that it is permissible for it to be altered, and that it may be mixed with fabricated lies in such a way that it can never be distinguished: Inform us about Allah's completion - Exalted is He - of our religion, and His pleasure with Islam as our religion, and His prohibition of accepting any religion other than Islam. Is all of that remaining for us until the Day of Resurrection? Or was it only for the Companions - may Allah be pleased with them? Or neither for the Companions nor for us? There must be one of these possibilities... If the answer is necessarily: all of that remains for us until the Day of Resurrection, it is valid that all the laws of Islam are complete, and the favor of that upon us is complete, and this is a necessary and conclusive proof that everything the Messenger of Allah - (peace and blessings be upon him) - said regarding the religion, and in clarifying what is required of us, is preserved and can never be mixed with anything that is not from it."(16)
There is no legislative vacuum: ﴾Today I have perfected for you your religion﴿:
We often hear some Muslims, when deriving the legal ruling on a particular issue, say: "In matters where there is no text," and we hear others say: "The interests according to the Sharia (Islamic law) are of three types: interests that the Sharia has recognized as valid, interests that the Sharia has recognized as invalid, and interests that are unqualified and have not been recognized by any text as valid or invalid." We also hear a third group say: "This falls within the realm of concession that the Sharia has remained silent about regarding its ruling." Is there, then, a legislative vacuum that requires rational rulings? Upon closer examination, we find that the notion of a legislative vacuum contradicts the texts of the Qur'an and the Sunnah, which clarify that the religion has been completed and that there is no obligatory matter that our Sharia has not addressed. The Sharia has not remained silent about things without clarifying their rulings; rather, it has specified the ruling for everything. There is no incident without a ruling, nor any event without a place for a ruling, and it is not permissible for a Muslim to say that the legislator has remained silent about something and has not clarified its ruling after reading His saying: ﴾And We have sent down to you the Book as a clarification for all things﴿ and His saying: ﴾This day I have perfected for you your religion and completed My favor upon you and have chosen for you Islam as religion﴿. This clarification of rulings may be explicit by naming the thing and its ruling, such as the prohibition of carrion, blood, and the flesh of swine, or it may be through a general text like: ﴾He created for you what is in the earth all﴿, which encompasses everything in the earth.
