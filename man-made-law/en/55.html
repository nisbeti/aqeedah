Purposes of spiritual and educational purification: ﴾He purifies them and teaches them the Book and wisdom﴿. The rulings are legislated for the purification and education of the soul, not for the direct acquisition of benefit.

Purposes of testing and sifting: ﴾And We will surely test you...﴿, so that Allah may distinguish the truthful from others.

Purposes related to the Hereafter and the unseen: Actions that yield fruits in the Hereafter, not in this world, such as praying for the deceased and giving charity on their behalf.

Purposes of identity and distinction: Distinguishing the nation in its rituals, morals, and appearance from others.

Therefore: The construction of an understanding of the Sharia (Islamic law) must be based on integrative analysis, not merely reducing it to "achieving interests." A human being is not just a body, and the Sharia is not merely a worldly law; rather, it is a divine system that educates, tests, purifies, and guides. Any attempt to confine the Sharia to the realm of interests alone may strip it of its essence as a law based on obligation and transform it into a terrestrial law that does not reflect the greatness of revelation. On the other hand, it may lead the obligated person to adhere to this Sharia only when they perceive the expected benefit from it, and if it conflicts with interests they may mistakenly assume, their commitment to it may weaken!

First: The interest and social necessity for organization:

Relationships in society are not based merely on mutual interests, but on an organization that regulates these interests within a comprehensive framework, prioritizing collective interest over individual whims and establishing justice rather than chaotic competition. If matters are left to each individual to pursue what they see as beneficial for themselves, without a system that defines their rights and limits their actions, disputes will prevail, and social life will turn into a jungle where individuals clash without balance.

The book "The Miracle of Islamic Legislation: Characteristics and Foundations" pointed to this essential point, stating: "Society cannot be established by each individual pursuing their own interests; rather, there must be a consensus on an acceptable organization that prioritizes the interests of the community over the individual, in which the individual concedes for the benefit of the collective." Hence, there was a need for legislation to organize these relationships, which is what the Sharia brought from Allah, who knows what is best for humanity in this world and the Hereafter.

Second: The intellect is a servant of revelation, not an independent legislator:

The intellect is the basis of understanding and the means of inference, but it is not a legislator. The jurists differentiated between the intellect's understanding of the relationships derived from the legal rulings and its positioning itself as a legislator in the absence of text, in the name of interests.

When Allah says: ﴾Say, "I only follow what is revealed to me from my Lord"﴿ [al-A'raf: 203], this indicates that the source of legislation is confined to revelation, and that everything else is merely subordinate to it in understanding and deduction, not a foundation upon which legislation is built.

This meaning was affirmed by the scholar Taqi al-Din al-Nabhani, who said: "The understanding that the jurist has of the rulings must be based on revelation, not merely on opinion or desire. If he derives a ruling from an interest devoid of evidence, he has made his opinion a foundation for legislation, which is what Allah has prohibited by saying: ﴾And judge between them by what Allah has revealed﴿" (Islamic Personality, Volume 3).
