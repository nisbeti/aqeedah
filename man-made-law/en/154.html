Example: The call to abolish the punishment for apostasy under the pretext of "freedom of belief," by: Tariq Ramadan in the book "Islam and Modernity" (2015, p. 112) justification: "Achieving the objective of religious freedom in Western societies."

Critique: The punishment for apostasy is established by consensus (Ibn Qudamah, al-Mughni: 12/266).

"Freedom" in Islam is constrained by the prohibition of apostasy (Ibn Taymiyyah, al-Sarim al-Maslul: 3/967), and Islam does not give any weight to what is called freedom of belief among Westerners.

First: Freedom in Islam: servitude that realizes dignity: and freedom means negating servitude to anyone other than Allah:

Freedom in the Islamic perspective does not mean a release from constraints, but rather liberation from the servitude of humans, desires, and authority, to achieve servitude to Allah alone, and this is the essence of human perfection and dignity: Umar ibn al-Khattab (may Allah be pleased with him) said: "When did you enslave people while their mothers bore them free?" (Narrated by Ibn Abd al-Hakam in "Futuh Misr"), he said this in denial of the unjust enslavement of the Copts, linking freedom to the dignity of creation, not to the dissolution of rulings.

And servitude to Allah is the pinnacle of liberation: a person cannot be without servitude, but rather must either worship Allah or worship their desires, as He, Exalted is He, said: ﴾Have you seen the one who takes as his god his own desire?﴿ [al-Jathiya: 23], and from here, true freedom is in submission to an All-Knowing, Wise God, not in submission to oneself or to others. When a person submits to laws established by another human who has no authority to legislate them, this is a form of servitude to humans. The right to legislate is confined to revelation, and adherence to divine legislation is what achieves freedom, for when a person submits to the command of Allah, they submit to a command that guarantees their dignity, preserves their interests, and fulfills the purpose of their existence. Thus, they are truly free, "My servant is free as long as he is content, and a servant to people as long as he is greedy" [Narrated by Ibn al-Mubarak in al-Zuhd, as a mursal narration], meaning that greed for what is in the hands of people is humiliation, even if a person thinks of themselves as free. Therefore, the term freedom in Islam encompasses the liberation of a person from human legislation and submission to humans, to become a servant of Allah committed to His legislations. When one achieves this, they are free, thanks to the implementation of their Lord's legislations that elevate them from being a servant. Thus, it is essential to establish the criteria that distinguish between freedom (the opposite of servitude) and liberation (which is promoted by Western freedoms), which prevents the understanding of freedom in the manner of Western thought.

Second: Liberation in Western thought: a release from values and constraints, for freedom to them means liberation from every restriction. The Western liberal thought sees freedom as the individual's ability to do as they wish, whenever they wish, as long as it does not conflict with the freedom of others. This results in extreme individualism that strips values of their sanctity and produces servitude to desires and whims. Furthermore, we notice in analyzing Western thought that freedom is separated from responsibility. In Western philosophy, freedom is often divorced from moral or legal responsibility, whereas in Islam, legislation is inseparable from accountability and obligation: ﴾And stop them; indeed, they will be questioned﴿ [as-Saffat: 24].
