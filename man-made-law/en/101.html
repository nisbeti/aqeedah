Practical examples that demonstrate the danger of building on the jurisprudence of minorities:

We will expand on the first example, with God's permission, so that we understand the methodological errors, and then we will present many other examples that clarify this jurisprudence:

First: The fatwa permitting the taking of usurious loans to buy a house or a car:

Some jurisprudential councils in the 1990s, such as the European Council for Fatwa and Research (ECFR), Dr. al-Qaradawi, and the Fiqh Council of North America (FCNA) in 1999, and others, issued a fatwa permitting Muslims residing in the West to take usurious loans to buy a home for living, under the pretext of need, necessity, civil coexistence, and public interest (so that they do not continue to pay high rents that harm them). They relied on the concept of lifting severe hardship.

The official text (decision 15/5 in 2001):

"It is permissible for a Muslim residing in the West to borrow with interest to purchase a residence when the following conditions are met:

(a) That he does not find a lawful alternative.

(b) That he fears for himself or his family moral or religious deviation due to not owning property.

(c) That housing is a necessity, not a luxury."

Source: European Council for Fatwa magazine (issue 8, p. 183).

The contradiction with the legal evidence:

The Almighty's saying: ﴾Allah has made trade lawful and prohibited usury﴿ - a definitive prohibition.

The hadith: "May Allah curse the one who consumes usury and the one who pays it" (Sahih Muslim).

Sheikh Yusuf al-Qaradawi's fatwa: In his book "Fiqh of Muslim Minorities":

"Necessity permits the prohibited, and not owning housing in the West leads to family disintegration and exposes children to deviation, so this is a necessity that permits dealing in usury under regulated conditions."

The American Islamic Society (ISNA) at its 2009 conference: "It is permissible for Muslims in America to take usurious loans for housing and cars when no alternative exists, because need is equivalent to necessity."

How was "rational public interest" transformed into an abrogator of definitive prohibition?
