It should be noted that we have just derived many legislations that have no relation to public interest (maslaha), and we said that divine legislation encompasses the organization of bringing about benefits and repelling harms, and it extends beyond that in other types of legislation, meaning it is not limited to it.
The point here is that the appropriate description considered in the Sharia is, in reality, a "legitimate interest" that evidence has indicated is to be considered, even if it is also recognized by reason through its sound nature. As for the modern rational interest that some promote, most of it does not adhere to these criteria; rather, it is an interest measured by human thought influenced by its culture and desires, and it may be illusory, partial, or contrary to the true interests of the Sharia.
"Interest" in modern rationalist trends
In the modern era, some Muslim thinkers – under the influence of Western modernity – have adopted a proposition that the Sharia came to achieve the interests of people, so whatever the mind perceives as an interest is undoubtedly the law of Allah, and their saying became famous: "Wherever there is interest, there is the law of Allah." At first glance, one might think this statement is beautiful, and those who advocate it might argue against the denier by questioning the concept of contradiction in their statement, asking: If this statement is not correct, then its opposite would be to say: Rather, some laws came to avert interests? And to achieve harms? This is a weak argument, but the matter is not so; rather, scrutiny reveals a very serious deviation in understanding and application among these individuals from the methodology of the scholars of the Ummah, and this statement is found to be in direct opposition to the Holy Qur'an!
Islamic scholars have established that the principle of bringing about benefits and repelling harms is an important principle: "The Sharia is based on bringing about benefits and repelling harms." The five essential objectives (maqasid) of preserving religion, life, intellect, lineage, and wealth are clear evidence of the Sharia's care for the necessary interests of the servants. However, there is a significant difference between saying that the Sharia achieves interest and taking the interest itself, as determined by reason without deriving it from the detailed or general texts of the Sharia, as a source of legislation!
The new rationalist trends – starting from the school of Muhammad Abduh and al-Afghani up to some contemporary figures – have fallen into the following confusion:
They considered that every legal ruling must be rationally justified by an apparent interest, and if it does not appear to them, they said it should be amended or suspended.
They expanded the concept of "interest" to include what modern minds see as beneficial according to the data of Western civilization, even if it contradicts texts or higher legal interests.
