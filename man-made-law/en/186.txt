Ahmad al-Raysuni also expanded on the activation of the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) to the extent that some have considered him the "Tufi of the era" (referring to Tufi's theory of public interest (maslaha)). The danger here is that some who identify with this trend have created what resembles a "new version" of the Sharia that focuses on the general principles and marginalizes the details. They have come to see that any ruling whose objective (maqsid) is not apparent to them is subject to change. This is a repetition of the logic of the early rationalist school that "the ruling is based on its benefit," but with their own understanding of benefit, not based on textual evidence.
Muslim Minority Jurisprudence: This is a form of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) that emerged in the 1990s to address the dilemmas faced by Muslims in the West. Two trends appeared within it: one is disciplined, taking into account necessity and urging Muslims to adhere to their principles as much as they can, while the other was adopted by some individuals lacking sufficient qualifications, who began issuing fatwas (legal opinions) that allowed significant concessions under the pretext of reality. We have seen fatwas permitting, for example, the purchase of homes through interest-based loans in Europe on the grounds of necessity, and allowing behaviors among Muslims that lead to their integration. Some even reached the point of discussing the permissibility of a Muslim woman marrying a non-Muslim, considering the pressing Western reality – and this is an anomalous fatwa that contradicts both the texts and consensus. This second trend is a contemporary version of Abduh's approach but under different circumstances: they prioritized the outcome over the evidence, and formulated a "jurisprudence" that justifies leniency in definitive matters such as the prohibition of riba (usury) and the family system, under the pressure of Muslims being a minority. It has become evident that some fatwa institutions in the West that promoted this approach (the jurisprudence of balances and minorities) were receiving conditional funding from entities seeking to shape a domesticated Islam that aligns with Western values.
