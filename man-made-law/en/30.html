Submission of the intellect to desires, interests, and biases:

Summary:

The human intellect is inherently non-neutral.

The intellect is influenced by emotions, interests, and intellectual and social affiliations, making it incapable of producing legislation that is fair and free from bias.

The impossibility of stripping individuals and communities of desires.

Desire is inherent to the individual, and it is exacerbated in groups, which makes collective legislation (such as democratic legislation) necessarily subject to multiple desires.

The dominance of class and elite interests in the creation of legislation.

Many laws are enacted in favor of influential groups, not to achieve social justice, thus turning the intellect into a tool for dominance rather than public interest.

The impact of collective unconsciousness and intellectual currents on the deviation of legislation.

Deviation need not be intentional; it may occur due to societal, media, or ideological pressure, which undermines the balance and fairness of legislation.

The predominance of materialistic or individualistic perspectives in the legislative system.

Laws are sometimes enacted to serve immediate pleasures or materialistic tendencies, such as desire or utilitarianism, which disrupts the value system of modesty, family, and the environment.

Transforming desire into a legislative reference in the name of freedom and rights.

Deviations are legitimized through slogans like "personal freedom" and "the right to choose," leading to the legalization of evils and moral and social dilution.

The Qur'anic warning against following desires in legislation.

The Qur'an highlights the danger of making desire a legislator, warning against the authority of anything other than Allah in the most significant matters: the lawful and unlawful, and the truth and falsehood.

The danger of human legislation when subjected to desires.

It results in laws that serve specific groups and destroy values under flashy slogans, highlighting the urgent need for a divine legislative reference.

Detail: The human intellect is not a pure entity free from subjective influences and psychological and social impulses; rather, it is an entity affected by psychological and social influences, where desire intertwines with interest, and personal inclinations with culture.
