Liberalism (civil and constitutional liberties) can even accommodate the justice of social socialism without adopting its atheism. They saw this adaptation as a necessity so that Muslims would not lag behind in the march of progress aljazeera.net.

It is also important to mention their stance on the issue of women within this modernization context; Abduh supported the relative liberation of women and their education, and his student Qasim Amin endorsed his famous call for the removal of the hijab (in the book The Liberation of Women 1899) aljazeera.net. Rashid Rida wrote a translation of Qasim Amin in al-Manar, indicating attention to his ideas. It is true that Abduh himself did not call for complete unveiling, but he paved the way with his intellectual campaign for the emergence of voices calling for greater liberation of women and their engagement in public life, all under the banner of "renaissance" and keeping pace with advanced societies.

In summary, the rationalist reformers adopted an approach of adapting to modern ideas and currents as much as possible. They attempted to give an Islamic coloring to nationalism, liberalism, and others, believing that this would affirm Islam's ability to keep pace with development and prevent its stagnation. They succeeded to a large extent in forming a class of "enlightened Muslim" intellectuals who adopted the values of Western modernity within a formally Islamic reference framework. However, on the other hand, many critics believe that this approach gradually stripped Islamic identity of its specificity and transformed religion for some generations into mere flexible spiritual slogans that fit any intellectual trend, after it had been a comprehensive system with its own constants and foundations. This will be elaborated upon in the final critique.

The connection of Afghani and Abduh with Freemasonry and the Islamic University Project

A wide debate has arisen regarding the relationship of Jamal al-Din al-Afghani and Muhammad Abduh with Freemasonry. Historically, it is known that Afghani joined a Masonic lodge in Egypt around 1876, and even founded the Masonic lodge "Kawkab al-Sharq" with a French orientation aljazeera.net. Muhammad Abduh also entered Freemasonry alongside his teacher Afghani aljazeera.net. Sources indicate that Afghani invited Khedive Tawfiq to join him in order to promote the idea of consultation!!!, and they pledged within the lodge to work towards a constitution that would limit the tyranny of Khedive Ismail. In any case, Afghani and Abduh's affiliation with Freemasonry remains a clear mistake in the eyes of most scholars – it is inconceivable that such as they would be unaware of the connection between Masonic lodges and colonial powers and their opposition to the Ottoman Caliphate aljazeera.net. Contemporary writers such as Sheikh Yusuf al-Nabhani and Dr. Muhammad Muhammad Hussein have criticized them for this, and Dr. Ghazi al-Toubi concludes in his study on Muhammad Abduh with a negative assessment in this regard, considering it one of the three major sins committed by the Imam (in addition to his loyalty to the occupation on one hand, and his excessive interpretation of the unseen on the other) aljazeera.net aljazeera.net.

As for the project of the "Islamic University" (Pan-Islamism) that Afghani called for and which was later adopted by Sultan Abdul Hamid II, it was originally an idea to unify Muslims under a single political/religious bond in the face of colonial division. Afghani traveled to many countries calling on Muslims – both Sunnis and Shiites alike – to forget their differences.
