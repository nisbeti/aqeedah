Laws based on environmental perceptions may be considered "beneficial" in one context and "harmful" in another, leading to conflicting laws and societal disputes.
Human legislation is biased by its temporary cultural context.
What a materialistic or culturally deviant society legislates may be catastrophic if generalized, such as legislations that permit deviance or undermine the family in the name of freedom.
Islamic law addresses humans as humans.
In contrast to relative legislations, divine law is based on human nature (fitra), emanating from the Creator of humanity, suitable for all times and places, and unifying people under a single ethical system.
Detail: The human mind does not grow in a vacuum; rather, it is shaped and nourished by the cultural and social environment in which it lives, and is influenced by customs, traditions, prevailing concepts, and perceptions of good and evil, beauty and ugliness in its society. Therefore, the mind, while producing its legislations, carries within it this environmental and customary bias, which makes its rulings tied to a narrow local context that cannot be generalized to all humanity or considered a universal reference.
Thus, one who grows up in a materialistic society that venerates utility will legislate laws that elevate profit even at the expense of ethics. One who grows up in a culturally deviant society may legislate laws that permit incestuous marriages or assault on the family in the name of freedom. What one particular society sees as "a significant benefit" may be viewed by another society as "a pure harm," transforming law from a means of justice into a source of conflict between nations and peoples, as is currently observed in disputes over abortion laws, or deviance, or the death penalty.
This confirms that a mind influenced by its environment cannot produce legislation that is comprehensive for humanity, as it is bound by its context. Meanwhile, Islamic law has come with legislations issued by the Creator of all humans, directed at all people, and based on the human nature that all humans share, as He, Exalted is He, said: ﴾The nature of Allah upon which He has created mankind﴿ [al-Rūm: 30], thus its rulings consider the human being as a human, not as a child of a specific environment or subject to a temporary local culture.
From this, it follows that divine law possesses the capacity for application in every time and place, as it addresses the human in his essence, not in his color, gender, or geographical location, and unifies humanity under a just ethical and legislative system that preserves the dignity of all.
