Therefore, it was not surprising for anyone to claim that Christians and Jews are believers, and that they should stand with Muslims united against secularists. He says in the magazine (Political Readings), issue 11/12 of the year '93: "We must reclassify the previous divisions of religions and place them within a new equation: 'Religious and non-religious'." He also states in the same magazine, indicating the separation of religion from life: "Religion is universal, and God is for everyone; God did not draw the natural political boundaries because all religions are universal." Is this not the same statement made by secularists: "Religion is for God, and the homeland is for everyone," or "Render unto Caesar what is Caesar's, and unto God what is God's"? Does his statement that God is for everyone not imply that the faith of Christians in God is acceptable, and that we Muslims do not have the right to claim that we are the only believers in Him, Exalted is He?

He said: "The distancing from the partisanship of religion and liberation from sectarian fanaticism is the gateway to genuine dialogue between religions. If the people of religions abandon the fanaticism of their sects and turn to studying religions with an open mind, it is more likely that the single origin of these religions will be revealed to them, along with their shared fundamental values that they advocate. This is our call today: to establish a front of the People of the Book, and the Book, in our view, refers in the Qur'an to every scripture that came from God."

Thus, al-Turabi calls for the establishment of a front of the people of faith (Muslims, Christians, and Jews) based on the Abrahamic faith. He states in an interview with the magazine Al-Mujtama (issue: 736, dated 8/10/1985): "National unity is one of our greatest concerns, and we in the Islamic front achieve it through Islam based on the principles of the Abrahamic faith that unite us with Christians through a shared historical heritage and a historical record of beliefs and morals. We do not want religion to be a partisanship of enmity, but rather a bond of brotherhood in the One God"(47).

What he conceals in his call for renewal becomes evident when he openly rejects the rulings of Islam and instead demands, as stated in the magazine Al-Wasat on 07/11/94: "I am one of the most concerned people about freedom compared to my brothers in the Islamic movement. If I were to rule, I would leave others their freedom, and if they ruled, I would want them to leave me my freedom. And you know my opinion on the freedom of those who leave the religion, despite the criticism and disagreement it has brought upon me." He says in an interview with the newspaper Al-Muharrir (issue: 263, August 1994): "And I add to all this my opinion, which is my personal view: even if a Muslim completely apostatizes and leaves Islam and wants to remain where he is, let him remain where he is. There is no compulsion in religion... there is no compulsion in religion... and I do not say that he has apostatized or not; he has the freedom to say whatever he wants, provided that he does not corrupt what is common between us in terms of the system."

Thus, al-Turabi began theorizing about the Abrahamic religion before his time, and then the United States seized upon it to attempt to establish the Middle East based on it, making it a framework for recognition and normalization with the usurping entity!
