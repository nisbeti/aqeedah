﴿ [Al-Ma'idah 101]. Thus, the meaning of "what He remained silent about" is what He did not prohibit, so do not ask about it, for Allah has forgiven it and lifted it from you. Do not burden yourselves or seek it out!
Take a step back and ask yourself: If some matters related to organizing life have not been addressed by the Sharia in terms of forgiving people, neither by prohibition nor by permissibility, and there is a prohibition against asking about them, what is the stance on them? When you encounter one of these actions, what do you do? If the legislator has not legislated, nor permitted you to legislate, nor allowed you to inquire about them, and these matters pertain to your livelihood, what is the appropriate action? This means that those who search for areas of "legislative vacuum," if the legislator has not legislated, out of mercy and forgiveness for the people, will they be able to do better than that? To impose rulings based on reason! This essentially means that a person will not be able to act because there is no address, nor permission for reason to legislate, nor permission to act! And this is impossible. In the hadith of Abu Thalabah about the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him): "Indeed, Allah has prescribed obligations, so do not neglect them, and He has set limits, so do not transgress them, and He has prohibited certain things, so do not violate them, and He remained silent about things out of mercy for you, not out of forgetfulness, so do not ask about them." Here it is clear that the prohibition against asking about those things He remained silent about, such as whether we should perform Hajj every year, when he said, "O people, Allah has made Hajj obligatory upon you, so perform Hajj," then there is an important clue for understanding in the text that says: "And He remained silent about things out of mercy for you, not out of forgetfulness, so do not ask about them." Thus, what is left unaddressed out of mercy for the people means that either He did not prohibit them or that He did not impose burdensome obligations upon them, such as using a miswak for every prayer, performing Hajj every year, or coitus interruptus: Jabir (may Allah be pleased with him) said: "We practiced coitus interruptus while the Qur'an was being revealed, and the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) did not prohibit us from that," meaning he did not make it unlawful, and the example of the Jews in the story of the cow is not far from us!
And it may be that the time for its prohibition has not yet come, such as when the companions asked Allah to clarify a clear ruling regarding wine, and the time for prohibition came later; it is a ruling that Allah did not forget, but He legislates it whenever He wills. Also, this is part of the prohibition against asking, as the Jews used to do, which caused the legislator to impose burdensome legislation upon them, so He prohibited them from asking. Thus, its meaning is: Obligations have been prescribed for you, so do not ask more lest it becomes difficult for you, as happened with the questioner who asked about Hajj, and He remained silent about prohibiting certain things out of mercy for you, so do not ask about them lest it becomes difficult and He prohibits them upon you. They did not ask about coitus interruptus, for example, nor can it be considered a legislative vacuum because the legislator cannot leave us without guidance in any matter, as has been clarified by the definitive texts in both authenticity and meaning, and He has not permitted us to legislate on our own, which means that the meaning of the hadith cannot indicate a legislative vacuum.
It may come to mind the issue of organizing traffic, and it is said that it is one of the matters that the Sharia has not addressed with permissibility or prohibition. We say: Rather, the Sharia has addressed it with permissibility. It is known that traveling on roads is permissible, and the roads are public spaces, and it is permissible for a traveler to walk on the right side of the road or the left side, and he is allowed to choose this road or that. This reality was evident before the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) and he approved it, and there are many hadiths regarding the etiquette of the road. If the imam sees fit to organize traffic by prohibiting some permissible actions and mandating others, he is entitled to do so, because the Sharia has commanded.
