The interest must be general, covering the community, not specific to an individual or a passing case. “A partial interest is not counted; it must be a universal one that suits the kind of interests the Lawgiver has safeguarded.”
[al-Muwafaqat, vol. 2, p. 301]. The nearest contemporary example that occurs to me is the obligation of vaccination to prevent the spread of infection that may harm others, under the rule “no harm and no reciprocating harm.”
The third control: certainty or strong probability (not guesswork)
The interest must be certain, or strongly probable, in bringing a benefit or averting a harm, not mere conjecture.
“The unrestricted interest (<em>al-mursal</em>) is acted upon only if the interest is realized or preponderant; as for the imaginary, it is not part of the religion.”
[al-Muwafaqat, vol. 2, p. 295]. Example: prohibiting monopoly (to avert the harm of famine), on the basis of established economic expertise.
The fourth control: no conflict with the texts or consensus
If the interest conflicts with a transmitted proof (a verse, a hadith, a consensus), the textual proof takes precedence. “The unrestricted interest is rejected if it contradicts the text, because the text takes precedence over everything derived.” [al-Muwafaqat, vol. 4, p. 145],
The fifth control: an organic connection with the objectives of the Shari‘a
The interest must be a natural extension of the objectives of the Shari‘a, not an innovation detached from them. “It is not valid to introduce an interest cut off from the objectives of the Lawgiver; it must be joined to them as a branch is united with its root.”
[al-Muwafaqat, vol. 2, p. 289].
The sixth control: agreement with the rules of legal induction
The consideration of an interest must be established through a comprehensive induction of the texts of the Shari‘a, not by a lone inference: “the authority of the unrestricted interest rests on complete induction of the legal proofs, so what agrees with their sum is the one that counts.”
[al-Muwafaqat, vol. 2, p. 286].
The results of applying these controls according to al-Shatibi
Preventing human legislation:
Turning interest into an instrument that carries out the objectives of revelation, not a rival to them.
“The Lawgiver left no interest without explaining it, in detail where it is detailed and in summary where it is summary.” [al-Muwafaqat, vol. 2, p. 290]
Eliminating whim:
Requiring generality and induction prevents individual whims from ruling.
“Every interest whose matter is not governed by the Shari‘a is an opinion of whim.” [al-Muwafaqat, vol. 2, p. 305]
Protecting the religion from innovation:
Linking every interest to the universal texts shuts the door on “rational innovations.”
