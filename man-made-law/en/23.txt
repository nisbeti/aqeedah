The first axis: The necessity of legislation to regulate behavior and societal relations:
First: The nature of humanity necessitates the existence of legislation:
Humans are social beings who do not live in isolation, and their interactions with others involve conflicts of interest and disparities in power and ambitions.
Without legislation, individuals resort to their whims and justify their actions according to their immediate interests, leading to the prevalence of chaos.
Selfishness, greed, power, and cunning may drive a person to exploit others if not restrained by an overarching authority.
Second: Regulating societal relations is essential for the continuity of society
Every human relationship (marriage, sale, partnership, management, governance, politics...) requires regulations that govern it, protect the rights of the parties involved, and establish a framework for resolving their disputes.
Without legislative regulations, the logic of dominance and power prevails, the weak are oppressed, and justice is undermined.
The absence of regulations leads to the spread of injustice, fraud, betrayal, and the loss of rights.
Third: The catastrophic consequences of the absence of legislation or its corruption:
Family disintegration: Without provisions regulating marriage, divorce, and inheritance, rights are lost, threatening the family unit, which is the cornerstone of society.
Social injustice: The lack of regulations for the distribution of wealth and financial dealings creates a class that monopolizes wealth, while another class suffers increasingly.
Moral disintegration, where illicit relationships and births outside the family framework become widespread.
Loss of security: The absence of deterrent penal legislation leads to the proliferation of crime, turning society into a jungle.
State failure: If the ruling authority does not regulate itself with binding contracts and provisions, it devolves into tyranny or authoritarian disintegration, and its institutions fail.
Fourth: Legislation cannot be left to the whims and desires of people
Experience has shown that societies that legislated according to their whims or interests fell into contradictions, and most often, they were dominated by the influential class in establishing biased laws for themselves, requiring continuous amendments.
