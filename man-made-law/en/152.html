Establishing the legal punishments without implementing the Islamic economic system that guarantees mutual support (takaful) and justice, and without the social system that protects the family and society from collapse, is a form of futility; indeed, it is an injustice to impose punishment without removing the causes of crime, while the existing environment today—the liberal capitalist one—fuels instincts, promotes corruption, and impoverishes nations, making crime a natural outcome of this deviant system. As for the claim that legal punishments are not applied if they lead to "fitna" (sedition), this has been misinterpreted; for here, fitna does not mean societal rejection or media uproar, but rather it means that the implementation contradicts the Islamic way of applying it in life, which establishes and applies the systems of life to yield their fruits, and in which the systems of life are integrated and interdependent. In the absence of this application of Sharia, imposing a legal punishment is an encroachment on the authorities of the Islamic state, as individuals are not authorized to implement the legal punishments. To use this as a pretext to abolish the legal punishments altogether is to invalidate definitive texts in the name of public interest (maslaha), and a dangerous camouflage that employs exceptions to undermine the original principle.

The Usul (Principles) Discussion:

Transforming "suspicion" into "necessity": "distortion of the image" is a fictitious suspicion that cannot be measured against the conditions for averting legal punishments based on doubts (such as coercion or uncertainty in evidence).

Contradicting the definitive consensus: The consensus of the Companions (as in Sahih al-Bukhari: 6830) on applying the punishment of stoning (rajm) to the married adulterer.

Violating the condition of certainty: The prevented harm (distortion of the image) is not realized, while the committed harm (suspension of a limit from the limits of Allah) is definitive.

The righteous scholars have responded to them that the legal punishments are among the greatest rituals that achieve justice and deterrence and preserve the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) (such as the sanctity of life, property, and honor). How can their suspension be mercy and justice?! Yes, it has been reported that Umar suspended the punishment for theft during the year of famine (the year of Ramada) due to the suspicion of public necessity, and this is acceptable because suspicion and necessity avert the punishment. However, to suspend the punishment for the whims of rulers or to please disbelievers is a vile falsehood. Islam did not come to satisfy people's tastes but to guide them, whether they like it or not. So where is the public interest in their claim?
