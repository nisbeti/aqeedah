Then al-Nabhani emphasizes that everything considered a legal proof must be based on the speech of the Legislator (the Qur'an or the Sunnah or what derives from them through a valid legal proof), and thus, public interest (maslaha) cannot be a legal proof because it:
It is not a speech from the Legislator.
There is no specific legal proof indicating it.
It relies on independent rational reasoning to estimate the interest, not on text or legal deduction from it.
Secondly: The error of saying that the Sharia was brought to achieve interests and avert harms.
He believes that the statement "the Sharia was established to achieve interests and avert harms" is a comprehensive teleological description of the Sharia, not a legislative reason that can be measured against. Texts such as ﴾And We have not sent you except as a mercy to the worlds﴿ indicate the result of the Sharia, not the reason for every ruling within it. Therefore:
It is not permissible to consider every legal ruling as justified by interest.
Not every ruling must result in a tangible benefit; it may be purely devotional.
Thus, every legal ruling is merely a legal speech, deriving its legitimacy not from the rationality of the interest but from the text of revelation: "The Legislator did not consider the type of interests in the type of legal rulings; because He did not make them the reason for legislating the Sharia, nor the reason for legal rulings in general, so public interests have no legal consideration."
As for al-Nabhani's position on the objectives of the Sharia:
Al-Nabhani believes that what is called "the objectives of the Sharia" cannot serve as a legal proof, because:
The objectives may either be partial rulings (like prohibition), and thus it is incorrect to describe them as objectives.
Or they may be a general ruling like "mercy," which is a wisdom and not a reason, and wisdom cannot be the basis for a legal ruling.
Thirdly: Refuting the proofs of those who advocate public interest.
Al-Nabhani responds to those who argue that the Companions acted upon public interest (such as compiling the Qur'an, flogging the drinker, pouring out adulterated milk, etc.) by saying:
The science of Usul al-fiqh had not yet developed among them, and they did not use what is called "interest," but understood the rulings from the texts.
All these incidents can be referred back to specific (detailed) legal proofs or known analogical reasons.
Fourthly: Public interest introduces rational legislation and opens the door to whims.
Al-Nabhani warns that reliance on public interest leads to:
The supremacy of reason in legislation, which contradicts definitive texts such as ﴾And whatever the Messenger has given you, take it﴿ and ﴾And whoever does not judge by what Allah has revealed, those are the disbelievers.﴿
