al-Zailai in "Tabyin al-Haqaiq" (6/22) when quoting al-Kasani: "One commits the lesser of two harms in a state of necessity, if one cannot escape from both."

The Malikis: al-Qarafi in "al-Furuq" (4/204): "If a person is compelled to choose between two prohibitions, and he cannot avoid both, he must commit the one that is less harmful."

Al-Mawaq in "al-Taj wa al-Iklil" (8/218): "It is permissible to commit the lesser of two corruptions in a state of necessity, provided that one cannot escape from both corruptions together."

The Shafi'is: Imam al-Ghazali in "al-Mustasfa" (1/286): "If two corruptions conflict, and one must commit one of them, then one commits the lesser of the two."

Al-Suyuti in "al-Ashbah wa al-Nazair" (p. 117): "Rule: If two corruptions occur together, and one cannot escape from both, the lesser of the two is committed."

The Hanbalis: Ibn al-Qayyim in "I'lam al-Muwaqqi'in" (3/14): "The Sharia permits committing the lesser of two harms if one must choose one, and to avoid the greater of the two corruptions."

Al-Buhuti in "Kashaf al-Qina'" (6/298): "If one is compelled to commit one of the two corruptions, and he cannot avoid both, he commits the lesser of the two."

Secondly: Scholars' explanations of the condition

The meaning of "necessity":

Ibn Abidin in "Hashiyah" (5/45): "What is meant by necessity is that one fears for the destruction of oneself or a limb if he does not commit the prohibition."

Condition of "no escape": al-Qarafi in "Anwar al-Buroq" (4/200): "It is not permissible to commit the lesser of two harms except if it is impossible to escape from the harm entirely; if it is possible to escape from both harms together, it is not permissible to commit either."

Verification by Ibn Taymiyyah: in "Majmu' al-Fatawa" (20/58): "Whoever is compelled to commit prohibitions, he is permitted only what is absolutely necessary... If he can escape from both prohibitions together, he must do so."
