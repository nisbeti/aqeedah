No matter how intelligent and astute a person may be, their understanding of what the outcome of an action will be in the future remains limited, as it is confined to their personal experience and temporal horizon. A person cannot encompass all the factors influencing reality, nor can they accurately predict how proposed laws will interact with human behavior, or with the constantly changing economic, social, and psychological contexts, nor how the laws will interact with those factors that may be complex and intertwined, requiring coordination among them so that they do not conflict or cause the failure to achieve the objectives of legislation.

Thus, human legislation suffers from short-sightedness; it is often established to address immediate problems, becoming situational laws that fit a specific moment in time, but soon become inadequate or conflicting when the data changes. From here, amendments and modifications to positive legislation follow in succession, leading to laws being constantly altered and amended, and sometimes conflicting, resulting in the legal system losing its stability and effectiveness, and social behavior becoming disturbed as a consequence.

Legal and social studies have indicated that human legislation—as a human product subject to time and change—cannot remain in a single state, nor can it maintain permanent stability; therefore, we see countries suffering from an endless cycle of "legal patchwork," where the need to amend laws becomes a continuous state and an indicator of their failure to encompass, which deprives them of stability and prestige, and the ability to provide social security and legal stability.

As for divine legislation, it has been issued by the All-Knowing, the Wise, who encompasses the conditions of His creation in the past, present, and future, and it has been established to address human life as such, without being tied to a specific time or special circumstance. Rather, the Sharia has come with comprehensive principles and supreme objectives that enable it to adapt to new developments without losing its essence or requiring fundamental abrogation or any amendment. The law of Allah has been complete since its revelation, suitable for all times and places.

The limitations of the mind in terms of place (environment and culture):

Summary:

The mind is influenced by the cultural and social environment.

The mind does not operate in a vacuum; rather, it is shaped under the influence of the norms, customs, and prevailing concepts in its society, which limits its objectivity.

The bias of the local mind makes its legislation relative and not universal.
