He attempted to twist the meanings of metaphysical texts to align with the discoveries of natural sciences in his era. His stated motivation was to eliminate what he considered irrational or "superstitions" in people's minds, and to present religion in a manner acceptable to Western thought. For instance, Abduh launched a campaign against many Sufi practices and popular beliefs under the pretext of purifying the creed from what he termed superstitions and irrational imaginations. This is a positive point in its reformative aspect (purifying monotheism from the impurities of polytheism and innovations), but it expanded for him to include the reinterpretation of established matters based on the correct texts of revelation, claiming that they do not conform to "reason" by modern standards.
The influence of Western thought on interpretation
It is clear that the spirit of the age (especially secular Western thought) had a significant impact on the interpretative approach of the reformists. Ghazi al-Touba states: "Muhammad Abduh tried to narrow the gap between the world of the unseen and the world of the witnessed through interpretative steps, relying mostly on what Western civilization had achieved in material discoveries in the fields of nature and science." This was reflected in the attempt to explain miracles and metaphysical matters through a material experimental logic. For example, when Darwin's theory of evolution emerged, some of Abduh's students (such as the Egyptian judge Muhammad Farid Wajdi) leaned towards the idea of reconciling it with the story of the creation of Adam, interpreting what was mentioned in the Book and the Sunnah about our master Adam and Eve in a symbolic rather than literal manner.
This trend in symbolic reformist interpretation led to a dangerous outcome pointed out by critics: it opened the door to emptying texts of their certainty and reverence. Any meaning could be debated, interpreted, or nullified if it did not align with the "reason" of contemporary humans or the "public interest" as they see it. Dr. Muhammad Muhammad Hussein – may Allah have mercy on him – warned of the danger of this approach by saying, "Some well-meaning thinkers tend to stretch Islamic values to the utmost limits that the texts can bear towards Western values, thus falling into the traps of the West. They, fearing the accusation of rigidity against Sharia – as Orientalists label it – deviate to the opposite extreme to demonstrate its flexibility, until this flexibility reaches the point of laxity and loss of identity, such that Sharia becomes a tail to any system and subject to whims. Thus, they effectively end up canceling the function of religion, because instead of correcting the distortions of life with the texts of Sharia, they manipulate the texts of Sharia to justify the distortions of contemporary life." These words – written by Dr. Hussein decades ago – eloquently summarize the outcome of unrestrained rational interpretation: transforming religion into a tool for justifying reality instead of being a standard for evaluating reality.
It is fair to mention that Rashid Rida himself noted in the later years of his life the danger of some interpretations that spread after Abduh. He observed the results of excessive intellectual Westernization in Egyptian society after Abduh's death (such as the writings of Qasim Amin advocating for women's liberation and the spread of a form of atheism), and he retracted some of his teacher's ideas and called for moderation.
