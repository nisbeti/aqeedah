Detail: The intellect is not a legislator, as its ability to establish legislations and issue rulings on actions, interests, consequences, good and evil, beauty and ugliness, reward and punishment, justice and injustice depends on tools, information, and capabilities that are not available to it, such as knowledge of the consequences of an action or future realities: will it be good for him or bad for him?

As for why the intellect cannot issue judgments (legislations), it is because the intellect is undoubtedly limited and constrained; it is limited to thinking within a specific circle, and it can only contemplate what is perceived by the senses or what results from it, provided there is a clear connection (relationship) between it and its effect. Otherwise, it would fall into contradiction and error. If the intellect ventures outside this circle, it requires tools to safeguard it from attributing to its judgment the qualities of conjecture or error.

and limited by his need for prior information that explains reality, so he cannot generate ideas or judgments except in the presence of that prior information. He is constrained by his need for a standard to evaluate the validity of ideas and the truth of their results, as well as their degree of strength, which prevents him from falling into fallacies regarding facts,

And upon a discerning and thorough examination, we find that when the intellect searches through the evidence that fills the corners of the universe, it engages in the realm where it can judge the truthfulness, correctness, and definitiveness of its conclusions. It possesses prior knowledge, axioms, and foundational principles to which it refers, elevating its inquiries to the level of certainty and definitiveness, and truthfulness in their application to the reality being investigated, or leaving them within the realm of conjecture, or ruling them as false. Therefore, it is capable of issuing judgments on matters of belief, such as the inquiry into the existence of the Creator and the validity of the relationship between the Creator, the universe, and humanity, whether that relationship is one of organization, creation, or the commands and prohibitions conveyed through the messengers.

In the context of issuing judgments on actions, interests, consequences, good and evil, beauty and ugliness, reward and punishment, justice and injustice, it possesses some tools (such as a partial understanding of the nature of reality), but it lacks other necessary tools for this inquiry (such as the future consequences of this reality, whether this reality will be good or bad for it, whether it contains its interest or harm? And since the interests resulting from performing certain actions may lead to a conflict in the type of interests that will be realized or that may be nullified or pushed aside due to their conflict with other interests, does the interest lie in material benefit, for example? Or in moral, humanitarian, or spiritual value? And how is one to weigh them when they conflict? Thus, these are locks that the mind cannot unlock). Therefore, it must refrain from issuing judgments and legislations due to its lack of those characteristics and information necessary to carry out the intellectual process that results in judgment, legislation, and codification. Thus, when a person allows themselves to legislate, they are not assigning their mind the task of legislation, but rather they are giving it to their whims that drive them to delve into matters where the mind has no say, and to their inclinations that suggest to them that the interest is whatever aligns with them. And when we expect the mind to legislate, it is because it is one of the greatest tools and most astonishing creatures that Allah has created.
