Chapter Three: The Evolution of the Concept of Public Interest (Maslaha) in Jurisprudence and the Danger of Its Contemporary Use in Minority Jurisprudence, Balancing, and Reality
Executive Summary of Chapter Three: Public Interest in the Islamic Perspective and Its Considerations:
This chapter addresses a highly significant topic in the principles of Islamic jurisprudence, which is the concept of public interest (maslaha), as it is one of the tools of ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning), but at the same time, it is one of the most concepts that has led to intellectual and juristic deviation among some contemporary movements.
The book first clarifies that public interest in the Islamic perspective is not an independent reference; rather, it is subordinate to the legal text and is derived from its rules and evidence. It is not permissible to separate it from revelation or to make it a criterion by which the text is judged.
Then, the chapter explains the difference between the legitimate public interest that is based on the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia) and its controls, and the absolute rational interest that some contemporary thinkers have used as a pretext to bypass texts or to interpret them in a way that serves their personal views.
The book also discusses the precise foundational concepts of public interest and clarifies the conditions for its consideration, such as: it must not contradict a text, it must be real and not imagined, it must be general and not specific, and it must not open a door to corruption greater than the anticipated benefit.
The chapter presents historical examples of deviations that occurred when the concept of public interest was unleashed without controls, as happened with al-Tufi in his preference for public interest over the text, and among some modern jurists who permitted usury or adjudication according to man-made laws under the pretext of achieving interests.
At the end of the chapter, the book emphasizes that the true public interest lies in applying the Sharia and not in suspending it, and that Allah's law is more knowledgeable of the interests of His servants than their limited minds. Any attempt to make public interest a judge over the text leads to the secularization of religion and the distortion of the Sharia.
Content of Chapter Three:
The concept of "public interest" has evolved in its usage throughout the historical stages of Islamic jurisprudence. In the flourishing eras of jurisprudence, the use of public interest was regulated by the principles of the Sharia and did not raise significant issues; scholars would apply it within the framework of qiyas (analogy) in matters where there is no text, or in ways that achieve a general objective without contradicting a text, as we have seen with the Malikis, for example. However, over time, with the onset of periods of stagnation and then the interaction with Western modernity, new and expanded readings of public interest emerged that sought to create unprecedented applications, especially within what is today referred to as "the jurisprudence of Muslim minorities."
