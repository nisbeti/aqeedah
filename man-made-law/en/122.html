A monarchical system based on sovereign administration relies on "ma'at" as a comprehensive principle for achieving balance and peace in the universe and society, rather than on fixed legislative texts or a written constitution, nor on comprehensive laws to organize life, but rather on scattered legislations such as the legislation on the punishment for theft, for example. The king was the highest source of legislation, issuing orders according to his perceptions of "truth" and "justice," which were implemented through a bureaucracy of ministers, priests, and deputies, without any original legislative support. This means that the laws were based on an authoritarian system rather than a legislative system, that is, through an executive order in specific matters that produced organizational effects, but did not rest on a religious foundation or a constitution, but rather on the king's absolute authority. Consequently, the delegation and empowerment that the king granted to Joseph (peace be upon him) when he made him the chief overseer of grain storage and crisis management allowed him to manage it as he wished. He could violate the situational royal sovereign legislation as he pleased, as he did without review from the king. Therefore, this situation cannot be applied to modern legislative systems that enact laws applicable in life and society across various life systems.

In summary:

The story of Joseph (peace be upon him) proves that he was empowered to govern, that he applied God's law in judgment and politics, and that he was not part of a tyrannical system, nor of positive laws that organize life and society, but rather a sovereign administration with a situational character. He did not submit to the orders of an unjust king, but acted as he wished according to what his Lord commanded him. For this reason, Allah thanked him and praised him, and indeed his story was a source of comfort for His Prophet Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him) when he faced great harm, especially in the Year of Sorrow. The lesson at the end of the surah is that his patience for the truth and for the path based on insight from Allah, which he did not deviate from, would lead him to governance just as our master Joseph reached before him. This precise analysis shows that the inference against the context of the surah and the reason for its revelation is entirely misplaced! It is not correct in any way to use his story as evidence for the permissibility of entering into positive parliaments, or for the permissibility of acting contrary to God's law.
