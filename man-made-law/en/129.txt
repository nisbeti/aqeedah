The summary: The original condition – as established by the majority of jurists – is:
"That the obligated person must be compelled to commit one of the prohibitions, while being unable to avoid both."
This is based on principles:
Necessities permit prohibitions (to the extent of necessity).
Harm cannot be removed by a similar or greater harm.
The greater harm is removed by the lesser harm (only in cases of necessity).
Thirdly: Correct examples from the books of scholars
Difficult childbirth:
If the life of the mother or the fetus is threatened, and it is necessary to sacrifice one to save the other, the lesser harm is prioritized, which is saving the mother, for example, as determined by the scholars.
Killing under coercion:
Al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam said: "If a person is coerced into killing a Muslim, then it is obligatory for him to endure the killing and not kill another; because killing himself is less severe than unjustly killing another Muslim."
Preventing a wrongdoing that causes a duty to be missed:
Preventing fornication or murder if it can only be done by missing an obligatory prayer at its time; then, the denial of the wrongdoing, such as preventing murder here, takes precedence because it is more emphasized in Sharia.
Fourthly: Refuting the distorted political use of the principle
Using the principle as a pretext for participating in governance with disbelief:
Some preachers today justify their participation in parliamentary elections, or support secular governments or parties, or passing projects that contradict Sharia, claiming that they "prevent a greater evil."
The response: This is invalid for two reasons:
The condition of inability does not apply; for a Muslim can choose not to vote or participate in governance with disbelief, and abstaining from the action is permissible in Sharia.
It is not permissible to delegate someone who rules by other than what Allah has revealed, for parliamentary representation is a legislative delegation, meaning the appointment of the representative to enact laws, and this is polytheism if legitimacy is conferred upon it.
