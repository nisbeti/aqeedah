Chapter Two: Critique of Maslaha Mursala and the Distinction Between Appropriate Description and Modern Rational Interest
Executive Summary of Chapter Two: The Status of Reason in Islamic Thought and Its Regulatory Framework:
In this chapter, the book addresses the issue of reason as one of the most contentious topics in contemporary Islamic thought, where confusion often arises regarding the status of reason, its function, and the limits of its operation. The chapter clarifies from the outset that Islam does not exclude reason as some closed religious currents do, nor does it elevate it above revelation as modern rationalist currents do; rather, it grants it its true status: a tool for understanding the text, not an independent source of legislation.
The book elaborates on the distinction between "reason guided by revelation" and "independent reason detached from it," indicating that the former is relied upon in the methodology of established usul scholars, while the latter is the root of the deviations of theological and rationalist sects. The chapter presents a series of examples from Islamic history, theological thought, and philosophy to illustrate how prioritizing reason over revelation has led to serious deviations in beliefs and rulings.
It also discusses the most common objections raised in the name of reason against certain legal rulings, demonstrating that these objections are fundamentally based not on sound reasoning but on erroneous perceptions and concepts imported from Western civilization. It clarifies how contemporary Western rationalism assumes complete independence of reason from revelation, a hypothesis that the chapter discusses in detail, highlighting its contradiction with human nature and the limitations of human understanding.
The chapter concludes by outlining the legal regulations for using reason, such as submission to the text, acknowledgment of the limits of human perception, and the prohibition of opposing definitive texts with speculative rational concepts. It emphasizes that every intellectual or legislative deviation begins when reason is prioritized over the text, or when the text is made subordinate to the interests perceived by reason.
Content of Chapter Two:
The term "maslaha mursala" or "istislah" has emerged in the science of usul al-fiqh to express a specific type of interests that lack a specific legal evidence regarding their establishment or cancellation. Scholars have differed on the validity of relying on it as a source for legislative reasoning. In this chapter, we will undertake two tasks: first, to explain the concept of maslaha mursala as understood by prominent usul scholars and to clarify its legal regulations; second, to critique the misuse of this concept in modern thought to justify the legislative authority of reason, by comparing what is known as "the appropriate description that influences" in legal reasoning with what is termed "maslaha" by some contemporary thinkers who claim to prioritize reason over the text under the pretext of achieving the objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia), within a newly introduced methodology unprecedented in the history of Islamic jurisprudence!
