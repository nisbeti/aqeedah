Eighth: The permissibility of a Muslim woman marrying a non-Muslim (the People of the Book) in the West:

The established legal ruling: The definitive texts categorically prohibit it. Allah, Exalted is He, said: "And do not marry polytheists until they believe" ﴾Al-Baqarah: 221﴿, and His saying: "And if you know that they are believing women, do not return them to the disbelievers; they are not lawful for them, nor are they lawful for them" ﴾Al-Mumtahana: 10﴿. There is a consensus on the prohibition.

The contradictory fatwa of "minority jurisprudence": The "European Council for Fatwa and Research" (ECFR) issued a fatwa (Decision No. 4/19 in Session 19, 2003) permitting a Muslim woman in the West to marry a non-Muslim (specifically a Christian) under the title of "civil marriage" only, provided that the marriage is civil and not religious, and ensuring her right to practice her religion and raise her children in Islam.

Their justifications based on public interest (maslaha):

The necessity of integration into Western society and avoiding the isolation of Muslims.

The difficulty of finding a suitable Muslim partner in the Western environment, which leads to spinsterhood, adultery, or apostasy from the religion.

Protecting the Muslim woman from falling into unlawful relationships if marriage is not available to her.

Considering that civil marriage is not a legitimate marriage in the eyes of Islam, and thus does not entail the full legal rulings of legitimate marriage! (A dangerous methodological justification).

Critique of the methodological concession:

Contradiction of the definitive text and consensus: Rational public interest cannot abrogate a fixed ruling established by definitive text and consensus, no matter the pressures. This undermines a fundamental principle of the religion.

Pure rational interest: The alleged necessity (difficulty in finding a Muslim husband) does not rise to the level of a legal necessity that permits prohibitions (like dying of hunger to permit consuming carrion). Spinsterhood or difficulty is not a justification for permitting a definitive prohibition. The interest here is "purely rational," considering comfort and social integration at the expense of a fixed legal ruling.

The artificial distinction between "civil" and "legitimate": This distinction has no basis in Sharia. A civil contract that permits enjoyment and seclusion is a marriage that entails legal rulings. Sharia does not recognize a "civil" marriage devoid of rulings.

Reversing a specific ruling (a Muslim marrying a woman of the Book) inappropriately: This is an analogy with a difference, as the ruling in the Qur'an is specific to the Muslim man and not the Muslim woman, and it cannot be generalized.
