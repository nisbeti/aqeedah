The jurisprudence of minorities between legal necessity and methodological concession
At the end of the twentieth century, with the expansion of Islamic communities in the West, urgent calls emerged to formulate what is known as "the jurisprudence of minorities" – a form of ijtihad aimed at adapting Sharia rulings to the reality of Muslims in non-Islamic societies. There is no doubt that the conditions of minorities impose unique issues, from dealing with secular systems and their laws, to complex personal status matters such as marriage and arbitration, as well as food, financial transactions, insurance, and more.
