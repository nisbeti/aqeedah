The Sharia (law) that came to protect the morals of society and safeguard its innate nature. The judgment on something being "beneficial" should not be left to the human mind burdened by desires, propaganda, media, and material culture, but rather it should be referred to the balance of the Sharia. Allah, Exalted is He, said: ﴾And He makes lawful for them the good things and prohibits for them the evil﴿. What Allah has prohibited is certainly harmful, even if some people perceive it as beneficial or pleasurable. Alcohol, drugs, prostitution, and gambling yield enormous profits for their owners today, to the extent that they argue it is "economically beneficial"! However, they remain, in the balance of Sharia and true benefit, harmful evils that destroy individuals and societies, even if it takes time.

In conclusion to this chapter, we say: the benefit that is considered valid in Sharia does not contradict any text, but rather exists where the texts of Sharia or its general objectives, derived from a correct extrapolation of the Sharia, are found. As for the benefit in its relative sense among humans, it requires the criteria of Sharia to be accepted or rejected. If benefits conflict or oppose harms, then they are prioritized based on the Sharia itself, not on the whims of people. Thus, the confusion is resolved: the mind observes and weighs, but within the framework of Sharia and with reference to its texts and correct objectives. This is the approach of the scholars of Islam. As for the other approach that makes the mind the arbiter over Sharia in the name of benefit, it is rejected and dismissed because it is a surrender to human ignorance and biases, and a denial of Allah's profound wisdom in His legislation.
