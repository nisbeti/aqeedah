The Maliki opinion: Some Maliki jurists permitted swearing in the case of a murderer if there are indications and no evidence has been established, as a precaution to protect lives.
The source: Ibn Rushd mentioned in "Bidayat al-Mujtahid" that Malik allows in matters of blood what he does not allow in matters of wealth, in terms of severity or indications of circumstances, and he linked this to public interest (maslaha).
The methodological flaw: This introduces reason as a criterion in the matter of blood money (diya), which is a fixed category that should not deviate from the established rules of evidence in the texts, such as: "The burden of proof is on the claimant, and the oath is on the one who denies."
Abolishing the punishment for adultery for an ignorant woman
The Maliki opinion: It has been reported in some Maliki books (such as "al-Taj wa al-Ikleel" and "al-Sharh al-Kabir") that ignorance of the prohibition or the ruling removes the punishment, and this is supported by public interest to avert the harm resulting from imposing the punishment on the ignorant.
The methodological flaw: Introducing general ignorance in the chapters of punishments is an unregulated expansion, because punishments are fixed and can only be removed by a legally prescribed doubt, not merely by a rational estimation of public interest in removing them.
Suspending the punishment out of fear of sedition or political disruption
The Maliki opinion: It has been reported from some Malikis that it is permissible to delay the imposition of punishment out of fear of public sedition or political rebellion, as was said about Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) during the year of famine.
The methodological flaw: The difference is that Umar (may Allah be pleased with him) suspended the punishment for theft not based on a general rational interest, but due to the removal of the reason for the ruling: which is wealth, because in the year of famine, it was likened to general hunger, not based on the application of rational interest. Thus, we see the impact of the absence of a strict inductive methodology in deriving interests on its applications.
