Under the generality of this text, it is permissible for us except for what other texts have specified as exceptions, and this clarification may be established by setting a sign, which is the reason ('illa). Thus, wherever the reason is found, the ruling applies by analogy (qiyas).

As for what Ibn Majah and al-Tirmidhi narrated from Salman al-Farsi, he said: The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) was asked about ghee, cheese, and fur, and he said: "What Allah has made lawful in His Book is lawful, and what Allah has made unlawful in His Book is unlawful, and what He has remained silent about is what He has pardoned." And what Abu al-Darda reported from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) that he said: "What Allah has made lawful in His Book is lawful, and what He has made unlawful is unlawful, and what He has remained silent about is pardon, so accept from Allah His pardon, for indeed Allah does not forget anything." Then he recited this verse: ﴾And your Lord is not forgetful﴿. This was narrated by al-Bazzar. And what al-Bayhaqi narrated through Abu Thalabah from the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) that he said: "Indeed, Allah has prescribed obligations, so do not neglect them, and He has set limits, so do not transgress them, and He has prohibited certain things, so do not violate them, and He has remained silent about certain things as a concession for you, not out of forgetfulness, so do not seek them out." These hadiths are singular reports in one regard, so they do not contradict the definitive texts, and in another regard, these hadiths do not indicate that there are things about which the legislator has remained silent in the sense of not clarifying them, but rather they indicate that there are things that Allah, Exalted is He, has not prohibited out of mercy for you, so He pardoned them and remained silent about their prohibition. (They asked about ghee, cheese, and fur, which are lawful as is known, and Allah has not prohibited them for you out of mercy for you, not out of forgetfulness.) Thus, the subject of these hadiths is not the silence about legislating rulings for them, but rather the silence about prohibiting them(17). The intended meaning is the prohibition against seeking them out, not that there are matters whose rulings Allah has not clarified.

In Fath al-Bari: Muslim has narrated, and its origin is in al-Bukhari as previously mentioned in the Book of Knowledge, through a chain from Thabit from Anas who said: "We were forbidden to ask the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) about anything, and we liked it when a man from the Bedouins would come and ask him while we listened." And it has been mentioned in the story of the li'an (the mutual cursing) from the hadith of Ibn Umar: "The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) disliked questions and condemned them." And Muslim has narrated from al-Nawwas ibn Sam'an who said: "I stayed with the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) for a year in Medina, and what prevented me from migrating was only the question; one of us, when he migrated, would not ask the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him)." His intention was that he came as a visitor and continued in that state to obtain questions, fearing that he would transition from the status of a visitor to that of a permanent resident, thus becoming a migrant and being prevented from asking. The meaning of the hadith is that the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) addresses the Companions, saying to them: "What the text has brought, take it, and do not ask about anything else lest it becomes prohibited for you." This is similar to what he (peace and blessings be upon him) said, as narrated by Imam Muslim from Abu Huraira, who said: "The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) addressed us and said: 'O people, Allah has made Hajj obligatory upon you, so perform Hajj.'" A man said: "Every year, O Messenger of Allah?" and he remained silent until he repeated it three times. The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "If I had said yes, it would have been obligatory, and you would not have been able to do it." Then he said: "Leave me as long as I have left you, for those who were before you perished due to their excessive questioning and their differing with their Prophets. So if I command you with something, then do of it what you can, and if I forbid you from something, then avoid it." And it was added in Sahih Ibn Hibban: and it was mentioned that this verse in Surah Al-Ma'idah was revealed regarding that: ﴾O you who have believed, do not ask about things that if they were shown to you would distress you﴿.
