Fourth: The concept of "public interest (maslaha)" in Islam, according to those who advocate it (and we are not among those who support the notion of unregulated public interests), is a regulated legal concept, not merely what individuals desire or what cultures find favorable. We have clarified the difference between the recognized legal public interest among its proponents (which is the genuine benefit that the texts of the Sharia aim to achieve) and the illusory modern public interest that some influenced by Western standards may claim. The former is only considered a public interest if it is acknowledged by the Sharia, even if there is no specific detailed evidence; it suffices that it falls under a general principle or overarching rules. As for the latter (such as the rational public interest claimed by advocates of secularism, for example), it is a benefit by a human, variable standard, and is not recognized by the Sharia at all. We provided examples for this: such as the delusion of the public interest in "women's liberation" through the abolition of the hijab or by encouraging illicit relationships, which in the balance of the Sharia are destructive evils, even if some find their adornment appealing. On the legislative front, we clarified that the Malikis – who are the most expansive in their understanding of unregulated public interests – have never considered it a reference that supersedes the texts, but rather a means to address an emergent deficiency or to bring about a greater benefit without violating a definitive rule. Therefore, any modern legislation enacted under the banner of "public interest" that contradicts established texts is invalid and rejected, and cannot be attributed to Islam. It is erroneous in research methodology to conflate "objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia)" with "the whims of people"; the former is a framework for understanding the rulings of religion, not a tool for abrogating or altering them as some contemporary figures do under the pretext of progress.
Fifth: Critique of modern rationalist schools and two centuries of "Islamic Enlightenment". The research concluded that the trends which championed the supremacy of reason since the era of Muhammad Abduh and thereafter were based on a methodology that prioritized public interest (maslaha) over textual evidence, which led them into clear contradictions and mistakes.
