On one hand, sincere scholars have sought to establish a framework for necessity and concessions with strict foundational controls, without infringing upon the definitive rulings.

On the other hand, those who can be described as "pseudo-scholars" have taken the lead in issuing fatwas – those who have expanded the interpretation of "public interest (maslaha)" and "objectives of the Sharia (maqasid al-sharia)" and "averting the lesser of two evils" in a manner that is unrestrained by legal controls, making it a basis for abrogating even the established definitive rulings with the weakest arguments and justifications.

This division has deepened the problem to the point where the breach has widened beyond repair: slogans of "facilitation" and "realism" have opened the door to ideas that have made pure rational interests – not those derived from revelation – the dominant factor. The result has become prioritized over the evidence: a "jurisprudence" is formulated that justifies leniency in definitive rulings (such as marriage, usury, and arbitration) under the pretext of minority circumstances, through justifications that reduce the Sharia to merely accommodating Western realities, within a framework that attempts to find a compatibility between derived rulings and Western laws and legislations, placing issues of integration and coexistence with Western societies as a prominent goal for which any legislation, no matter how far-fetched, is adapted!

Therefore, this research addresses the fundamental contradiction between some fatwas of minority jurisprudence and the definitive legal evidence, revealing:

The mechanism of methodological concessions in the areas of marital relations, finance, and confronting secular systems.

The transformation of public interest from a legally regulated concept to a mere rational justification that abrogates established rulings.

The entities and exemplary fatwas that embodied this deviation, and how they transformed "the necessity of integration" and "social pressure" into an abrogator of revelation.

Thus, this study begins with a central question:

How has "rational interest" – in some representations of minority jurisprudence – become a tool for dismantling constants, instead of being a framework for applying them within the complex reality?
